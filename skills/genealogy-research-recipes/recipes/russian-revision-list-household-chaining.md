# Chain a Russian-Empire revision-list household across copies, sections and revisions (Urals evidence)

Use this recipe for a non-Jewish revision list (ревизская сказка) of the Russian Empire. The evidence behind steps 1 to 7 comes mostly from the Urals and Trans-Urals (the Ekaterinburg administration, the Perm, Shadrinsk, Kamensk, Katai and Zlatoust areas), with Tula-province and Vyatka cases for owners, transfers, women's ages and the last revision. The method carries over to other provinces. The fond numbers, the age-bias pattern and the boilerplate formulas do not, so check them locally before relying on them. The recipe covers mainly 18th- and 19th-century returns for state, factory-ascribed, monastery and seigneurial peasants, plus town-estate (мещане) lists. Steps 8 to 11 add four checks outside the Urals evidence: whether 1897 census sheets survive, the published estate inventories that come before the first revision in former Grand Duchy lands, which later revisions exist for an Orenburg Cossack household, and what the 1926 census lists of a village record.

Use it in any of these situations:

- the first copy you find does not show the family
- the village seems to be missing
- the ages do not add up
- a wife's origin is unknown
- you are about to count a central and a local copy as two confirmations

Each revision is one registration episode, and that episode can survive as several copies and sections in different fonds. Read each copy field by field, then chain the household back one revision at a time.

## 1. Fix who held the village, and under which unit, on the revision date

- Identify the estate category and owner on the revision date: state, factory, monastery or private owner. The reported gap in RGADA f. 614 op. 1 d. 73 for 1744 missed the Dalmatov monastery’s own records [S1]. For that monastery, check Shadrinsk f. И-224 op. 1, dd. 3157 (1719), 3155 (1727), 3238 (1742), 282, 3155 and 3156 (1744–1745), and 3144 (1757) [S1]. Verify the file titles and dates in the inventory.
- Search under the administrative unit in use that year, including the volost for Barnevskoe in 1850 [S14]. For factory-ascribed peasants, check the factory and its owner or supervising administration [S6]. The phrase «уволенные от личных заводских работ» in an 1811 destination tale leaves the former status open [S22]. For Perm-to-Orenburg migrants of 1802–1806, compare the name with GASO f. 24 op. 2 d. 286 [S22].
- Check how the census grouped settlements. The 1710 Nevyanskaya census used tithings (десятки), so a village could be present without its own heading [S21]. Check neighbouring settlements, other slobodas’ sections and «вновь заведенная» entries for a newly founded settlement [S1].

- **Weigh the chance of a private owner first.** Troinitsky's count from the 10th revision, as given in Brockhaus and Efron's article «Крестьяне», puts the serfs' share of the population at 69% in Smolensk and Tula guberniyas and 62% in Kaluga, but at 23% in Vologda, 4% in Olonets and 3% in Vyatka, with none in Arkhangelsk. [S60] Use the share to decide where to start looking for an owner [S59]. Establish the family’s status from its own records.
- **Read a post-1861 «временно-обязанный» entry as the former owner's name.** Temporarily obligated peasants were former landowners' peasants, free since 1861, who had not yet bought out their land; two thirds had gone over to the buyout by 19 February 1870, and it became compulsory from 1 January 1883. [S61] Take the owner named in a post-emancipation entry back to the 1858 tale [S62]. One Tula village’s records named a second landowner before its buyout, so check entries across the interval and search under each owner [S63].
- **Find the owner in the 1860 estate survey.** «Сведения о помещичьих имениях», published in 1860 in six volumes as appendices to the works of the Editing Commissions, tabulates each estate's owner and name, its serfs and households, its land and the peasants' obligations, but only for estates of 100 souls or more; volume IV covers Kostroma, Smolensk, Tver, Tula and Yaroslavl guberniyas. [S58] Look up the estate to identify its owner before emancipation [S59]. A holding below the survey threshold requires another source.
- **Search a village under each of its names.** Check the church dedication, owner’s name and any «тож» alias in the revision, index and inventory. For example, search both Покровское and Потемкино for the Krapivna-uezd parish recorded under those names [S64], [S65].

## 2. Map every copy and section of the revision, and give each its own locator

Record one row per copy. Do not stop at the first copy you find.

- **Central copy:** RGADA f.350, op.2 for the 1st–3rd revisions. Examples:
  - d.908 (1762; peasants of the Ekaterinburg administration ascribed to the Verkh-Isetsky works, with the Beloyarskaya and Vyshminskaya slobodas)
  - d.909 (1763; Kamenskaya sloboda)
  - d.1632 and d.1636 for one factory's 2nd- and 3rd-revision lists

  The Shartash citation for a 1747 tale points to d. 899, catalogued as a 1742 census, and the factory citation for 1747 points to d. 1632, catalogued as 1748 [S2], [S3], [S55]. Check the cover, heading and household before accepting either date. For early censuses, reported leads in RGADA f. 214 include op. 1 d. 1587 (1719, Tobolsk district) and op. 5 d. 434 (1710) [S9], [S10]. Check the Kamenskaya heading in d. 909 for the 1763 tale [S6].
- **Local originals and parallel copies:** participants cited the following:
  - GASO f.24 for household tales: op.2, d.74 for the 1719 household-tale census and op.1, d.548a for 1747
  - district-level returns in other GASO fonds: f.38, op.1, d.29 (1743), f.42, op.1, d.86 (1735) and f.27, op.1, d.22 (1745)
  - GAPK f.111 for 19th-century revisions
  - GASO f.24, op.23, d.6625 for the 1850 Zlatoust factory list

  [S2], [S10], [S11], [S12], [S25], [S3]
- **County-treasury and volost fonds:** the GASO inventory of the Irbit county treasury (f.674, op.1) lists revision tales for 1816, 1834 and 1850 by volost, town and factory. It also has supplementary tales (дополнительная) and separate tales for individual landowners' household serfs. Check these fonds as well as the provincial chamber. [S4]
- **Supplementary and special lists:**
  - Books of those who left after the 1st revision exist as a type. RGADA 350-2-1153 is one, for the Arsk road of Kazan district (1748). It is not a Urals file. [S5]
  - In an alphabet of leavers (алфавит выбывших), do not assume the number by a name is a leaf number; columns can differ between files. Read the column header and test one entry before you search by number. Also look the village up under its "тож" second name.
  - Supplementary tales for 1850–1853 for Shadrinsk district are filed by volost in GAPK f.111, op.1, d.2536. [S14]
  - For the 1858 town-estate tales, a participant cited GASO f.8, op.1, d.1776–1779, including "private" tales (частные) and tales of newly enrolled townsmen (вновь причисленные). The same participant cited lists of factory people discharged into the town estate (d.1922, 1922a) and enrolment into the town society (d.1935a). [S8], [S20]
  - In the Ekaterinburg area, Old Believers were also counted in separate schismatic series, which you must search alongside the revision. Participants cited the 1742 schismatic census (GASO f.24, op.1, d.1000), a 1752 list of Old Believer children born after the revision (d.1370) and the 1765 schismatic tales (GASO f.34, op.1, d.187). [S2]
- **Owner and monastery fonds:** see step 1. [S1]

- **Clergy statements:** among the first-revision files, also look for the parish clergy's statements about their parishioners. RGADA's inventory describes f.350, op.2, d.1649 as clergy statements about parishioners, filed with statements about landlords' and monastery peasants of three станы of Kurmysh uezd, 1722–1723. [S55] One file can cover several станы, so where such a statement names a village known only from later sources, keep every стан in the file's title as a candidate until the statement's own headings or another record place the village. Give each clergy statement its own row, and do not use one as a household list until you have read it.

Mark each copy as inspected (images or original), extract only, or unlocated. Ask for the fond, opis, delo and leaf, and compare the cover with the pages. An archive reply led to an alphabetical list of household heads with revision numbers, which could establish neither the other members nor their absence [S7].

## 3. Use the volume's own finding aids, then check that the file is what its title says

- Use the front реестр to find the settlement’s leaf range, then turn to the body for names. Check the back for a summary and geographical index, as in RGADA f. 350 op. 2 d. 909, ll. 312 ob.–313 [S55]. For 1816 Perm, Okhansk and Cherdyn, check the register in GAPK 111-3-40 [S15].
- Record the separate «умершие и убылые» (dead and departed) ranges and gaps for each village. In the 1744 Shadrinsk index, new leaf 1 corresponds to old leaf 34, so the new numbering conceals missing leaves at the start [S1]. Check gaps farther into the file against both sequences.
- Look for sub-sections inside a village's run. They include:
  - people entered under a later order or after the census
  - солдатские дети (soldiers' children)
  - рекрутские дети (recruits' children)
  - children of factory workers not assessed for the poll tax
  - people "who moved of their own accord"

  [S11], [S25], [S10]
- Before reading, check what the file really contains:
  - A file catalogued for one volost in 1858 held supplementary tales from several districts and years (GAPK 111-1-2198) [S15]. Check the heading and date of each bound item.
  - The word «сказка» can also describe an autobiography [S13]. Read the opening and columns to identify the document.
  - One requested file named the right hamlet but covered only one family, possibly as a supplement [S5]. Check the return’s scope, foliation and catalogue number against the images before calling anyone absent.
- Note whether the volume covers males only. The 1st (1719), 2nd (1744–1747) and 6th (1811) revisions did not record women; the others did. [S37], [S38] A wife or daughter missing from an 1811 tale was not recorded, so her absence says nothing about her. Some local copies of the 1st revision name women anyway (step 6). [S10], [S25]

- **Look for wives and daughters in the 1710 census books.** The 1710 Yarensk-uezd study counted both sexes by age [S77]. Search the household entries, including hired workers’ families, for women’s names, ages and relationships [S78]. Check what the particular book recorded before treating a missing woman as absent.

## 4. Transcribe each entry field by field

Keep every field as written, with the leaf (л./об.) for each household. Do not merge fields.

- **Numbers.** Copy every number before a name. Distinguish the running soul number from household numbers, and check for a separate sequence for women [S2]. Where a later entry gives two numbers, test each against the preceding revision [S3].
- **Age pairs (3rd revision and later).** Read the headings for the last revision, departures and people present. A son under «написанные в последнюю ревизию» could have a single age, so one figure cannot identify a post-revision birth [S6]. Look for «рожденной после ревизии» or an equivalent status formula [S6], [S2]. Copy the earlier age, current age or fate, and date. Exits include death, recruitment, removal from assessment for mint work, and appointment as a church clerk [S6]. Preserve fractional ages and exit notation:
  - "(41,5) 49" or "22/38 4/12", with fractions of a year [S3], [S12]
  - "26/ум.1836" or "Помер в 1818 г." when the second value is an exit [S3], [S12]

  Where the totals are split by sex, reconcile previous, departed and present separately for each (GAPK 13-1-36, 1782) [S15].
- **Wives and daughters.** Transcribe a wife’s stated origin and father («взята … дочь») and a daughter’s destination and husband («выдана в замужество …») wherever the tale gives them [S6], [S2]. Preserve blanks, the original columns and fractions. A wife’s «/38 4/12» in the 1850 Zlatoust tale occupies the current-age column with the earlier column blank [S12].
- **«Старинная» wife.** The word was taken to mean a woman from the same village, yet transcribed 1763 Kamenskaya entries use it for wives from other places [S22], [S6]. Follow the origin clause to her father’s household in the previous revision. If it names no place, the current village is one candidate.
- **Status and origin formula.** Record it word for word as its own field. Examples:
  - "написан в прежнюю перепись", with any other place named ("в Камышевской слободе", "в Алапаевском заводе")
  - "после переписи рожденный"
  - entries made under the 1735 order
  - bought or transferred people ("купленной … из деревни …, написанного в прежнюю перепись за оным помещиком")
  - "в рекруты", "в бегах" or "умре"

  [S25], [S3], [S11]
- **Transferred serfs.** Check the person counted in the previous revision for a «transferred from» place. A child born later may have no origin recorded. Keep neighbouring families’ origins as leads and leave the family’s origin open until a record identifies it [S20].
- **Converts.** A baptised dependant’s second name can suggest a godparent as well as a father. Compare it with the names and roles in the baptism entry before interpreting the revision-list name as a patronymic [S19].
- **Local narrative, signatures and confession.** In the 1747 local tales, an entry can give:
  - the birthplace
  - the year the person left an earlier home on a passport
  - the earlier census he was assessed in
  - who signed for an illiterate head

  The central summary of the same entries leaves this text out. Copy it in full. [S2] The town-estate lists of 1858–1867 usually note confession at the foot of the page. Members of one household could be partly Orthodox and partly Old Believer, so record confession per person. [S8]
- **Later layers.** Transcribe corrections and move notes separately from the original entry, with the hand and date where identifiable. Check both original and corrected district names when searching the finding aid [S15], [S23].

- **Trace a married daughter from both households.** A departure note can name a village or estate without naming her husband [S79]. Look for «выдана» in her father’s household and «взята» in her husband’s [S80], [S81]. Compare names, patronymics and ages at the dates of their respective columns. A previous-revision age in her father’s entry and a current age in her husband’s may imply different birth years [S79]. Leave her unmatched if place and age fit several women.

- **The compiler's attestation.** A tale ends with the signatures of those responsible; in villages it was compiled from the elder's statement, and landowners' peasants were enumerated by their managers. [S73] Copy the closing lines with the compiler’s name, office and date. They can document a village elder in his own right [S74].

## 5. Test formulas, placements and ages before using them

**Take each age column's reference date from its heading, not from the year on the file's label or cover.** A heading can reckon ages at the previous revision, at an additional list or at another named year. Copy the heading's wording with the ages. If the headings leave the reference date open, copy the ages as written and derive no birth year from them. Once the heading settles that a column gives the current age, compute from the compilation date below.

- **Boilerplate origins.** The «native of the same» formula in 1743 Katai tales was reported for men counted elsewhere in 1719 [S11], [S10]. Compare the named household with the earlier census under both places.
- **Official registration versus residence.** One 1834 tale records a family as already living there «с давних лет», and a 1790 confession list places it there decades earlier [S23]. Search the former registration place as well as the actual residence, and date each from a record that names it.
- **Compilation date.** When the column gives current age, calculate from the dated entry for that place [S12], [S2], [S6]. A return known as the 1743 Katai census reportedly includes an entry dated April 1744 [S11]. Inspect dated entries before computing a birth range.
- **Declared ages.** Compare successive entries for unchanged or rounded ages and for jumps around recruitment age. One man reportedly gave his age as 14 in both 1710 and 1719 [S17]. Test exceptional ages of older men against the earliest return [S16].

  The legal recruiting ages changed during the century. Reference accounts give about 20–30 for the 1708–1726 levies, then a period without fixed limits, with 17–35 applying only from the 1766 General Establishment to 1831. Take the cut-off from the rules in force in your year, not a fixed 35.

  Compare a man's age across every census from 1710 and 1719 onward. Prefer a metrical-book birth entry when one exists. An age mismatch lowers a candidate's rank. It does not exclude the candidate. [S17]
- **Exits and recruits.** The paired figure shows a recruit as taken, with the year. [S6] Check whether his wife and children are marked "выбыла в [volost]". If they are, open that volost's list for the same year and look for a heading such as "взятого в рекруты … крестьянина … жена". Copy the destination and check for the wife and children under the recruit’s name there [S9].

  A participant noted that in the Urals some recruits were sent to state factories as artisans and were not counted as soldiers. Early soldiers could stay in a local garrison and live at home outside the taxable population. So a man missing from the 1719 list while his son appears is not proof of death. Recruit lists survive only in part. [S9]

- **Check a cluster of identical birth years.** After a revision that omitted women, an estimated age could be carried forward. Tula tales reportedly entered some women as 25 in 1763 and 57 in 1795, reproducing the same apparent birth year in both returns [S71]. Compare parish entries and independently stated ages before giving a birth range.

- **A soul on the roll is not a man known to be alive.** Every revision soul was counted as present, even after death, until the next revision. [S82] A blogger adds that men taken as recruits between revisions were also still counted, and taxed, until the next revision corrected the list. [S83] So a man counted among a village's revision souls in any document made between two revisions is not shown alive by it: look for his death or recruitment in the metrical books and recruit lists, and in the next revision's exit remarks.

## 6. Chain the household one revision at a time

- **Go backwards.** Match the previous-revision age, named prior place or owner, and household position. A purchase statement can name both the village and the former owner [S3]. For the factory chain, reported leads are GAPK 111-3-260 and 111-3-256, then RGADA 350-2-1636 (catalogued 1762–1764) and 350-2-1632 (1748) [S3], [S55]. Verify every link.
- **Find wives through their fathers’ households.** Match the marriage-out note to the entry naming the same father in the husband’s household. Compare the ages and named places, then take the daughter back in her father’s household [S2].
- **Compare copies line by line.** Check surname spelling, settlement grouping, ages, taxes and the inclusion of women in the local and central copies [S10], [S2]. Only the local 1719 Katai copy reportedly named the women, so the central copy’s silence cannot exclude a wife or daughter [S10]. Check each copy’s date and compare its ages with the next revision’s previous-age column. Record each copy as a separate observation of one episode, never as corroboration.
- **Bridge gaps with confession lists.** To chain revisions through the confession lists between them, and to use lists of people absent from confession, follow [reading confession lists, steps 3 and 5](russian-orthodox-confession-list-reading.md#5-check-the-household-against-revision-lists-and-metrical-books).

- **Follow a transfer to the receiving village.** Copy the issuing body, date, order number and everyone included in a transfer note, then search the receiving village’s tale for the same revision [S68], [S69], [S70]. A reported note named sons omitted from the confession lists, so check the family accompanying the named man [S68].

- **Record unlocated revisions as unlocated.** For a gap in the Irbit district, check the 1800 confession lists for Bobrovskaya (GASO f. 6 op. 3 d. 345б, l. 69 ob.) and Kirginskaya (d. 345а, l. 153 ob.) [S24].

- **Iset-line fortresses.** Chelyabinsk fortress was founded in 1736, after the 1719 sloboda lists; its 1740 census book is published by I. V. Degtyarev in «Челябинская старина» (Chelyabinsk, 1996, pp. 56–96). [S52 @2026-11-08] V. V. Pozdeev published the 1739–1740 first-settler lists of the Miass fortress in «Ветер времени», 2007, no. 1 (14), pp. 67–74. [S53 @2026-11-08] A local-history page cites the Miass census as RGADA f.248, op.3, d.144; confirm the locator in the RGADA inventory and check each edition against its original. [S54 @2026-11-08]

- **Bashkir and Tatar households.** Do not chain households by an -ов/-ев/-ин name as if it were a hereditary surname. Among Turkic-speaking peoples, Bashkirs included, propertied families took surnames from the 17th century but ordinary people only in the 19th [S49]; a Familio guide says many Tatars and Bashkirs fixed theirs only after 1917 [S48]; and the Orenburg Cossack surname handbook notes that the revision tales of some Muslim settlements record no surnames [S43]. Chain such households by the links in the first bullet of this step, and do not use a surname as a link in revisions earlier than the one where it first appears for that household; search earlier tales for the name only as a lead. Check the printed volumes «История башкирских родов» (Ufa; vol. 14, «Тазлар», 2015, prints shezhere, archival documents and 1850 revision data) [S50], cite volume and page, and test each name against the revision.

  The household cards and fragmentary householder lists of the 1920 agricultural census are a separate route: NA RB f. Р-472, op. 1 (Central Statistical Administration of the Bashkir ASSR). [S51 @2026-11-08]

## 7. Bound every absence

Start with the list's own coverage. From its title and headings, name whom it leaves out by construction: women in a male-only revision (step 3) or in a male-only family list made for the call-up, and people registered in another society or outside the taxable population. A wife or daughter missing from a male-only list was not counted, so her absence is no evidence of her death or of who the mother was. The checks below apply only to people the list was made to include.

A person, household or village that is not found counts only as a gap with stated bounds. Never record it as a death, a non-existent village or a lost revision until you have checked all of these:

- the other copies (central, local, treasury, owner)
- the dead-and-departed section and any missing leaves
- the supplementary and Old Believer lists
- the administrative unit and owner on that date
- the next revision's exit remarks

[S1], [S5], [S12] Check other copies of incomplete image sets. A reported Zlatoust entry missing from the online leaves was located at И-138-2-59, ll. 266–266 ob. [S12]. Expand НАРБ in the citation. In the И-138 inventory it denotes the Ufa archive [S34]. Check transfers, registration at a former place and exemptions from the taxable population [S9], [S23].

- **Search every village of the owner.** Serfs’ tales can be grouped by owner, with moves among the changes since the previous revision [S85]. A family missing in 1858 under its later village was reported in another village of the same landowner [S72]. Check the owner’s estates across guberniya boundaries [S84]. The 10th revision was the last [S73], so use later record series for a subsequent move.

- **Look for a person added after the revision.** People left out of a revision were entered in supplementary tales: the inventory of RGADA f. 350, op. 2, as mirrored on NashiPredki, lists tales «о прописных и утаённых» peasants as dela of their own [S86], and a NashiPredki copy of an Astrakhan archive's inventory lists a file on supplementary tales for people «ошибочно пропущенных во время ревизии». [S87] A blogger adds that the next revision marked such a person «приписанный из утаенных» or «пропущенный в прошлую ревизию». [S88] Before you explain a son missing from his father's household as an omission, look for supplementary tales, which may be filed apart from the main return, and for those marks in the next revision; without either, keep the gap open.

Route a revision for present-day Bashkortostan, Chelyabinsk or Orenburg oblast by the guberniya on its date. The offices of Orenburg guberniya moved from Orenburg to Ufa in 1802, and Ufa became the seat of a separate Ufa guberniya in 1865. [S45 @2026-11-08] The Orenburg Treasury Chamber's revision tales are fond И-138, op. 2 of the National Archive of the Republic of Bashkortostan in Ufa, per NashiPredki's copy of the inventory [S47 @2026-11-08], and hold the 5th–10th revisions (1795–1858) according to a Familio guide [S48 @2026-11-08], so an 1816 or 1834 tale for a village now in Chelyabinsk or Orenburg oblast can be in Ufa, and for this fond NARB means the Bashkortostan archive.

Conversely, the agreement of 20 March 1919 on Soviet Bashkir autonomy moved to Bashkiria volosts of eleven uezds: Orenburg, Orsk, Verkhneuralsk, Troitsk, Chelyabinsk, Shadrinsk, Ekaterinburg, Krasnoufimsk, Zlatoust, Ufa and Sterlitamak, and one volost of Buzuluk uezd. [S46] Shadrinsk, Ekaterinburg and Krasnoufimsk uezds belonged to Perm guberniya. [S56] So for a village that was in Bashkiria after 1919, look for its pre-1919 records under the guberniya and uezd it then belonged to, Perm guberniya included, not only in the Orenburg and Chelyabinsk regional archives.

## 8. Check that 1897 census sheets survive before routing anyone to them

Search-engine summaries of a Rosstat release report that the household sheets of the 1897 census were ordered destroyed after tabulation, with the order not carried out everywhere. The PDF did not load, so this account of destruction and uneven survival remains unconfirmed from the full release [S26]. Check the particular locality's surviving sheets before you send anyone to "the 1897 census":

1. Check the survival catalogues. The volunteer catalogue at metrics.tilda.ws/perepis1897 is arranged by country and archive, sometimes down to fond, delo and uezd, and marks some entries as unconfirmed. [S27] Altai State University's arch1897.histcensus.asu.ru is a catalogue lead: its title and address were seen in a search-result bibliography, but the site refused the connection, so its contents and usability remain unconfirmed [S28].
2. Then read the holder's own inventory. A census-commission fond does not mean that named sheets survive, and an uezd absent from a catalogue is not proven lost.
3. Even in a set known to survive, check the delo for the settlement. Two examples: the State Archive in Tobolsk holds the sheets of the Tobolsk guberniya statistical committee (ф. И-417, оп. 2, д. 1–4175): second and trial copies for eight uezds and their towns, all except Berezov and Surgut, digitised with paid online access. [S39], [S40] The State Archive of Yaroslavl Oblast keeps an 1897 census database and electronic indexes for the residents of some towns of the guberniya, Yaroslavl among them. [S41]

Search the FamilySearch catalogue by place for filmed 1897 household sheets, including for Turinsk uezd, and verify the film and delo numbers against the archive’s inventory [S90].

NashiPredki's mirror of a FamilySearch catalogue record lists filmed 1897 sheets of Tara uezd from the State Archive in Tobolsk, ф. 417, оп. 2, with a FamilySearch film and digital number for each run of dela. [S89 @2026-11-16]

Rootera's 1897 index (rootera.lv) covers only places in present-day Latvia and is still being extended. [S30]

The census forms differed by the kind of place enumerated: А for peasants, Б for private land and estates, В for urban areas, with separate forms for military units, student boarding houses and charity homes; the cover pages differ, but the household returns record the same items. [S75] Select the form series by actual residence. Look for a peasant household living on an estate or in town under Б or В [S76].

Read the absence or temporary-stay column and follow any named destination, including for children working away from home [S91]. The literacy question asked «Умеет-ли читать?» [S75]. A positive response establishes reported reading ability only [S92].

## 9. Before the first revision in former Grand Duchy lands, read the published estate inventories

For 16th- to 18th-century estates of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, now in Belarus and Lithuania, the Vilna Archaeographic Commission's «Акты» volumes publish estate инвентари (inventories). These are economic descriptions of starosties, estates, folwarks and villages, with their peasants and dues, not archival описи (finding aids). [S31], [S32], [S33]

- **Choose the volume by the documents' dates, not the publication year.** Vol. XXV (1898) holds inventories and boundary acts catalogued as 16th–17th century, with indexes on pp. 517–614. [S33] Vol. XXXV (1910) holds inventories of 1751–1789. [S31] Vol. XXXVIII (1914) holds inventories of 1720–1798. [S32]
- **Start from the contents and the indexes,** and search the estate's name as well as the village's. [S31], [S32]
- **Map printed pages to the PDF for each location** instead of carrying one offset across the volume or to another copy, and check the errata list.
- **Read the neighbouring pages** for the document's heading, start and continuation.
- **Bound what an inventory shows.** It is not a full list of residents, and its content depends on the document. An empty OCR search of the scans is not a negative.
- Link the online copies; do not redistribute them.

## 10. For an Orenburg Cossack household, confirm which later revisions exist

- **Do not assume 9th or 10th revision tales exist for a Cossack household.** Check the holding archive's inventory for which revisions were taken and survive, and search the Host's own records for the later years.
- **Read the surname handbook as an index.** Vishnevsky's handbook of Orenburg Cossack surnames is a surname index built from revision tales in the Bashkortostan, Samara and Orenburg archives, not a set of family lists. [S36] A hit does not prove residence in that place and year. A miss does not disprove a later document.

- **Use the handbook for non-Cossack families of the Host's territory too, and take spellings from the tale.** After its Cossack lists of 1816–1834, it indexes groups later enrolled into the Host: the peasants and soldiers of the volosts attached to it, the soldiers and odnodvortsy of the line settlements, and retired soldiers with their children. It gives surnames in modern spelling with alternative readings separated by a slash, so search and cite the form written in the revision tale. [S43]

- **After 1834, follow the Host's own series.** The handbook's introduction states that the 8th revision (1834) was the last taken for the Host, so do not expect 9th or 10th revision tales for a Host household. [S43 @2026-11-08] The Chelyabinsk Oblast United State Archive (OGAChO) lists formulary, service, family and name lists in its stanitsa-board fonds, for example f. И-31 (Kremenkul, 1853–1856, 1865, 1866) and f. И-10 (Emanzhelinsk, 1833, 1842–1849, 1854, 1855, 1865), and orders of the Orenburg Cossack Host for 1850–1858 in f. И-10. [S42 @2026-11-08] Cite a delo only after finding it in the opis.

- **Fix both jurisdictions on the event date:** the Host's (stanitsa or settlement; from the 1840 Statute one of ten regimental districts, later a military department) and the civil one (guberniya, uezd and volost). [S44] The Statute of 12 December 1840 and later acts enrolled the population of the Host's territory, state peasants and retired soldiers among them, and some state peasants only in 1853, so a family entered as peasants or soldiers in 1834 can be Cossacks later. [S44], [S43] Search the civil series up to the enrolment and the Host series after it, and record the change as a dated transition ([estate registration status, step 3](russian-estate-registration-status.md#3-date-when-the-person-acquired-the-status)), not as a clerk's error. The handbook adds that some peasants moved away to avoid enrolment. [S43]

- **Read a Cossack's service category as a stage of his service.** Under the Military Service Statute and the regulation of 1876, a Cossack of the Orenburg Host served from 18 to 38: three years in the preparatory category learning military service, four on active service in units of the first очередь, four on leave (льгота) in the second and four in the third, living in his stanitsa, with a yearly camp in the second, then five years in the reserve category, and then the militia. [S93] In a stanitsa list, transcribe the очередь, regiment and retirement status where given [S94]. Interpret the category using the statute for that Host and year [S93].

## 11. Read a 1926 village list as a list of householders

For the 1926 rural «поселенный список домохозяев», copy the named householder, nationality and counts of men and women [S95], [S96]. Use the counts to describe the household’s size. Individual members were recorded on personal sheets (личные листки), while family cards covered the permanent urban population [S97]. Check the heading before treating a town yard form (владенная ведомость) as a rural householder list [S95], [S97].

The volunteer blog vpn1926.blogspot.com collects поселенные списки домохозяев: about 80 posts from May 2019 to May 2020, mostly of Belarusian villages, with a few places in Russia, and no newer post when checked [S98 @2026-11-16]. A place missing there says nothing about what the regional archive holds.

   - the leaf range and any dead-and-departed range
   - missing or renumbered leaves
   - the status: inspected, extract only, or unlocated
2. **Entry transcript.** Include:
   - the numbers
   - the age pair or exit remark
   - the status formula
   - the wife's-father clause and daughters' departure notes
   - per-person confession

3. **Household chain.** List each step back with the field that links it (age, named place or owner, daughter–wife link). Mark parallel copies as one episode.
4. **Bounded gaps.** For each gap, name the next series to check: a supplementary list, the owner or monastery fond, confession lists, or the destination volost.
