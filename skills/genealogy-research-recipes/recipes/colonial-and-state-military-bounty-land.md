## Separate the issuing programs

According to the presenter, check the authority and period before identifying a military tract on a map [S1†]. Copy the authority from the warrant or grant. Use that authority to decide whether to follow a colony, state or federal series. NARA microfilm M829 contains federal Revolutionary War warrants [S3].

According to the presenter, check federal patents and state awards separately for the same veteran [S8† @2026-11-21]. Search the Bureau of Land Management’s General Land Office index for completed federal entries and use the federal warrant series when a patent search is empty [S11 @2026-11-21]. For state awards, use Lloyd Dewitt Bockstruck’s *Revolutionary War Bounty Land Grants: Awarded by State Governments* as a finding aid [S7 @2026-11-21] [S12 @2026-11-21].

According to the presenters, read the introduction to Bockstruck’s compilation for the state’s award procedures before interpreting a name search [S35†] [S36†] [S37]. Use the state’s procedure to choose the next record in the award trail.

Run the federal search if the state award is missing and the service could qualify under federal rules [S7] [S3]. Check the terms of a cession before switching issuing authorities. Virginia reserved land in what is now Ohio for its own Revolutionary War veterans [S13].

According to the presenter, use earlier warrant acreage as a lead to additional bounty [S45†]. If an earlier federal warrant shows fewer than 160 acres, check for a later application for the balance under the act of 3 March 1855. Record the authorizing act, acreage and warrant number separately and verify eligibility under that act [S3] [S54].

For a bounty under the Continental Congress’s resolution of 16 September 1776, compare the warrant acreage with the schedule for the soldier’s documented rank [S7] [S3]. Confirm the issuing program before using acreage to infer rank.

According to the presenter, identify the veteran, the person who claimed the bounty and the eventual patentee separately [S29†]. Check whether the issuing program allowed a widow or other heir to claim, then look for the proof of relationship in the application or patent papers [S30] [S31].

According to the presenter, check for assignments before using a bounty tract to place the veteran [S29†]. Follow transfers on the back of the warrant or survey to the final assignee. Establish the veteran’s residence from a record that places the veteran there [S30].

## Trace a Virginia claim or award

For an Ohio tract based on a Virginia award, trace the warrant in the Virginia Military District entry and survey books in federal Record Group 49. Check the separate warrantee and patentee indexes and carry the Virginia issuer through to the patent [S7 @2026-11-21] [S17 @2026-11-21].

According to the presenter, use Clifford Neil Smith’s *Federal Land Series*, volume 4, parts 1 and 2, for the Ohio Virginia Military District [S25† @2026-11-22]. Record the federal patent and its underlying Virginia warrant separately. Use Smith to recover the survey number and warrant number when the patent search stalls [S26 @2026-11-22].

According to the presenter, identify the unit before choosing Virginia’s award provisions [S8†]. For general Virginia state or Continental service, start with the 1779 Virginia act [S22]. For George Rogers Clark’s Illinois Regiment, check Virginia’s 1781 resolution and 1783 act, then trace the allotment in the plat of Clark’s Grant in present-day Indiana [S23].

According to the presenter, check both the Virginia and Illinois Regiment coverage when using Bockstruck for a Virginia lead [S27†]. For Clark’s Illinois Regiment, check the separately listed Virginia-Indiana coverage as well as Virginia and follow the lead to the original allotment [S28] [S23].

According to the presenter, look up Virginia soldiers and sailors in Samuel Mackay Wilson’s *Catalogue of Revolutionary Soldiers and Sailors of the Commonwealth of Virginia* [S8† @2026-11-21]. Follow a positive result to the Library of Virginia’s *Bounty Warrants* files [S12 @2026-11-21] [S22 @2026-11-21].

According to the presenter, look for a Virginia Land Office grant image when the lead identifies a Virginia grant [S36† @2026-11-22]. Search the Library of Virginia’s *Virginia Land Patents and Grants* for the recorded grant, including Kentucky grants recorded by Virginia before Kentucky statehood in 1792 [S46 @2026-11-22] [S22 @2026-11-22].

According to the presenter, check William Lindsay Hopkins’s *Virginia Revolutionary War Land Grant Claims, 1783–1850 (Rejected)* if the expected award is missing [S8† @2026-11-21]. According to the presenter, look up 1852 Virginia warrant exchanges in Margie Brown’s *Genealogical Abstracts, Revolutionary War Veterans, Scrip Act 1852* [S8† @2026-11-21] [S12 @2026-11-21]. Keep the Virginia warrant and the federal scrip as separate records [S24 @2026-11-21] [S22 @2026-11-21].

According to the presenter, use documented service to choose the 1852 scrip route [S36†]. Check section 1 of the 31 August 1852 act for a Virginia military warrant issued or allowed before 1 March 1852 that remained wholly or partly unsatisfied. ## Trace a French and Indian War warrant

According to the presenter, compare a French and Indian War bounty to the rank schedule in the proclamation of 7 October 1763 [S47†]. For qualifying claimants, the schedule specifies 50 acres for a private, 200 for a noncommissioned officer, 2,000 for a subaltern or staff officer, 3,000 for a captain and 5,000 for a field officer. Verify the authority before treating acreage as a rank clue [S48].

According to the presenter, search Philip Fall Taylor’s *A Calendar of the Warrants for Land in Kentucky, Granted for Service in the French and Indian War* by warrant recipient or assignee [S47†] [S49]. In the combined *Kentucky Land Warrants for the French, Indian, and Revolutionary Wars*, use Taylor’s portion for this search and Wilson’s for Revolutionary War warrants [S50].

According to the presenter, start with the bounty land section of *The Virginia Land Office*, the Library of Virginia’s Research Notes Number 20 [S47† @2026-11-22]. For Virginia Regiment service, use the guide to follow county court proof of service into the Land Office’s filmed land certificates. Use the grant date and service period separately when records from the two wars overlap [S51 @2026-11-22].

## Read a colonial group grant

For a colonial New England town, start with its town history to identify the original grant name, granting colony and any service named in the settlement account. Use those clues to find the petition or grant, as with Suncook, also called Lovewell’s Town, in present-day Pembroke, New Hampshire [S7] [S9].

Keep a soldier who served on a single expedition in the colonial grant search when the grant names that expedition, as Massachusetts’s 1728 Suncook grant did for Lovewell’s Pigwacket company and the legal representatives of deceased men [S7] [S10].

According to the presenter, look for group petitions or grants and distinguish a Narragansett settler from the soldier whose service qualified the claimant [S2†]. For a Narragansett township associated with King Philip’s War, record the original qualifying veteran or heir, the proprietor and any later settler as separate roles. Trace any assignment or inheritance before attributing service [S6].
