# Identify the issuer of a colonial or state military bounty grant

The grant's location alone leaves the place of enlistment, service and residence open.

## Separate the issuing programs

According to the presenter, check the authority and period before identifying a military tract on a map [S1†]. Copy the authority from the warrant or grant. Use that authority to decide whether to follow a colony, state or federal series. NARA microfilm M829 contains federal Revolutionary War warrants [S3].

## Follow a state Revolutionary War award

According to the presenter, search Kentucky for a Virginia Revolutionary War military tract [S1† @2026-11-20]. Use the Kentucky Land Office warrant index by veteran, warrant number or immediate assignee. Follow the authorized patent references separately and distinguish the person awarded the warrant from the eventual patentee [S4 @2026-11-20].

According to the presenter, search Pennsylvania under the label *Donation Land* [S1† @2026-11-20]. Use the Pennsylvania State Archives’ guide to find the Pennsylvania Line entitlement return and follow the named soldier into the land records. Keep Donation Land distinct from other military compensation [S5 @2026-11-20].

## Read a colonial group grant

According to the presenter, look for group petitions or grants and distinguish a Narragansett settler from the soldier whose service qualified the claimant [S2†]. For a Narragansett township associated with King Philip’s War, record the original qualifying veteran or heir, the proprietor and any later settler as separate roles. Trace any assignment or inheritance before attributing service [S6].
