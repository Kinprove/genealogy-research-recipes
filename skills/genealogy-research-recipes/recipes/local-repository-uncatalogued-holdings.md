# Ask for a local repository's uncatalogued holdings before reporting a negative

Use when a local, county or state library, a historical or genealogical society, or a county or state
archive has returned nothing in its catalogue or online search for the target surname or locality. Its
uncatalogued holdings have still not been inspected. This recipe covers asking about those holdings,
recording each one's access state, and citing what a compiled file contains. The presenters work in
and describe US repositories, so the categories and terms below follow US practice. Use
[research documentation](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/research-documentation.md)
for the onsite plan that separates finding an index from retrieving files. Use
[evidence/proof judgment](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/evidence-proof-judgment.md)
to weigh a compiler's knowledge and conclusions.

## Bound what the empty catalogue covers

Treat an empty catalogue or online search as covering only the repository's processed, catalogued
holdings. Do not write "not held", "nothing found" or "searched [repository]" on the strength of it.
The presenters describe vertical files as donated material that often never enters the card catalogue
or indexed collection [S6], [S9]. They also describe accessioned donations waiting on shelves without
being processed, filmed or put online [S1], [S2], [S3].

## Ask about four categories by name

Ask staff about each category by name, on site or by email or phone. Melissa Barker presents these
four as collections that patrons usually cannot see and that staff usually do not volunteer, so you
have to ask for them [S1], [S3]:

1. **Vertical files.** These are folders arranged by surname or subject [S1]. Ask for the target
   surname and also for the locality, church and relevant topics [S6], [S8]. They can be held by local
   and state libraries, historical societies and state archives [S8], [S10]. Ask whether the repository
   also keeps family files. A presenter describes one county library that puts everything asked about
   a family into one file, including a past librarian's own search notes [S4].
2. **The index to manuscript-collection finding aids.** Ask for the index itself, then the relevant
   finding aid [S1].
3. **Loose records.** These are court working papers such as affidavits and subpoenas, filed
   separately from the bound minute books that are usually brought out [S1]. Ask for them by name
   when only the minute books have been offered. A presenter also mentions boxes of court records
   that went unexamined until a local group indexed them [S4]. Ask whether there are unexamined boxes
   of court records, or boxes a local group has indexed.
4. **Accessioned but unprocessed donations.** Ask whether there are recent or unprocessed collections
   for the surname or place. Ask what the archivist's cursory accession inventory records about each
   one [S1], [S3]. Barker says that asking shows interest in a collection and may move it up the list
   for processing [S3]. Staff can describe this material by email or phone, but plan an onsite visit to see
   it: these donations are not filmed or online, and Barker says you will most likely have to travel
   [S2].

When remote, ask staff to check a named family and area and to say what they checked [S9].

## Log each category's access state

For each category and repository, log: what was asked, the date, who answered, and the state. The
state is one of: inspected; described only (for example from the accession inventory); access refused;
not held; unanswered. The presenters say some archives allow patrons to view unprocessed collections
and others refuse outright [S2], [S3]. A refusal or no reply is not absence. Keep that material on the
worklist as unsearched, with the collection named as staff described it [S2]. An accession inventory
tells you what the archivist recorded on intake, not what the boxes actually contain.

Check whether the repository has digitized any vertical files, but do not assume it has. The
presenters say most remain on site only, with some exceptions [S6], [S10].

## Cite the file layer, not the document it copies

Cite a vertical-file item by repository, file heading (surname or topic) and item description. Name
the compiler where known: a donating researcher, a past librarian's search notes, or an earlier
relative's inquiry [S4], [S5], [S7]. Record the item's creator and date where visible.

Lisa Lisson reports finding photocopies of deeds, family Bible pages and photographs, as well as
newspaper clippings, in compilers' surname files, and says vertical files hold copies rather than
original records [S5], [S10]. Label such items as copies held in the compiler's file. Do not cite them
as the Bible, the deed or the newspaper. Clippings may be undated [S5]. Leave a missing date blank
rather than inferring one.

Lisson also describes Bible photocopies carrying citations to the original Bible [S5]. When a copy
carries such a citation, transcribe it into the log and list the original as a separate item still
to locate. Also list the sources the compiler's notes say were used, as retrieval targets [S5].

## Keep the compiler's conclusions as clues

Put pedigree charts, margin notes, conclusions, oral-history notes and family lore on a clue list
marked unverified until the cited sources have been checked [S4], [S5], [S7], [S10]. The presenters
stress that such notes can contain mistakes [S7]. Two compilers telling the same family story are
not independent confirmation; record each version separately [S4].

## Return

- A coverage log separating catalogued holdings searched from uncatalogued categories asked about,
  each with its access state.
- Vertical-file citations giving the heading, the compiler and a copy/original label.
- A list of cited originals still to locate.
- An unverified clue list.

If any category is refused or unanswered, the repository search stays open. Say so in the report.
