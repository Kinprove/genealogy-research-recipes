# Trace a historical Helsinki household across parish, tax and address records

Start with a deceased person, a date range and one historical address, parish or household. If your lead is a street
address, match it to the period lot first. If you have a household or parish lead, start with the parish volumes and
use the annual rolls to track the household from one property to the next.

## Contents

- Choose the parish and volume
- Bridge a gap with the annual henkikirjat
- Match an address to a property and its occupants
- Check the declarations behind the 1806 roll

## Choose the parish and volume

Check the surviving communion-book years before reading a missing household as an absence. The Helsinki
Swedish-Finnish parish’s early communion books resume in 1785 after the earlier runs ending in 1741. Use
[Scandinavian parish-record recovery](scandinavian-parish-record-recovery.md) to follow the household to another
parish when a record points there. [S1] [S2]

Where the Helsinki Swedish-Finnish parish keeps separate communion books for workers, choose by marital status
at the date sought. Compare I Aa:10, married workers, 1827–1839, with I Aa:14, unmarried workers, 1827–1837.
Transcribe the occupation from the individual’s line and record the dated communion marks and changes of title separately.
Kyläkoski’s Schoultz example uses the unmarried workers’ volume I Aa:16, 1844–1860, pp. 141 and 145, and
*Rippikirjat I*, 1856–1869, p. 982, to track communion marks in 1851–1853 and 1861 and the change from sailor
to sea captain. [S3] [S9]

If a daughter disappears from her mother’s entry, check the marriage date and search later household entries
under the married designation. In Kyläkoski’s Burtz example the daughter married in 1779, is absent on p. 20
and appears as *fru maj Rosenqvist* in the book begun in 1795, p. 229. The earlier omission gives little
support for a lasting family break. Check a couple’s communion entry for an out-of-town marriage date and place,
then search the marriage register there. In the same case, p. 435 points to Stockholm on 7 September 1799. [S2]

For an earlier Lutheran event in a German-speaking Helsinki family, check the period parish. The separate
German Lutheran congregation began in 1858. For a marriage or baptism before then, search the Helsinki
Swedish-Finnish parish and any other parish supported by the residence and family records. Bound any negative
HisKi search by the congregation, event type and indexed years. Check the surviving parish books directly
when coverage is uncertain. [S4] [S5] [S6]

### Follow a fortress household

For a household linked to the fortress, search the Helsinki and Viapori (Sveaborg) parish runs separately.
Read a child’s artillery-parish baptism for the parent’s military title and company. Match the household and
test discrepancies in the child’s dates. In Kyläkoski’s example, the baptism of a boy born on 19 April 1780
places the father as *hantlaakari* in von Post’s company, while the apparent Helsinki children’s-register entry gives
1782. Keep the link tentative if the discrepancy remains. Follow a named Swedish-period unit through
[Swedish military record routing](swedish-military-record-routing.md). [S7] [S8]

Use the named workplace in the children’s register (*lastenkirja*) to locate the worker. Compare it with parental
titles and service status in the children’s dated baptisms and burials to bound the likely period of work.
Kyläkoski uses Helsinki’s 1789–1804 children’s register to place a gardener at the Schwartz plantation near
the west end of present-day Aleksanterinkatu. A burial on 8 September 1791 describes the father as having left
the army, while the baptism of a child born on 28 January 1793 calls him a master gardener. The author uses
these records to estimate at most a couple of years of plantation work in the 1790s. [S8]

Prefer the occupation on the individual line when the page grouping suggests something different. In Helsinki’s
1795–1805 communion book, p. 307, Kyläkoski finds a garden hand on the seamen’s page. Compare an
“artilleryman’s widow” label with discharge and later civilian records before assigning the husband’s last
occupation. The author reports a widow so described in the 1806 roll whose husband had left the artillery
before his death (*Rippikirja* 1806–1819, p. 271), and the same label in a separate Helsinki death entry
on 5 March 1809. [S8]

## Bridge a gap with the annual henkikirjat

In Astia, choose the county accounts (*läänintilit*) through 1808. For later years start with the Uusimaa
provincial *henkikirjat*. In rolls arranged by district, block and lot, use the period parcel and check the
villas and islands section for places outside the city plan. Check every relevant volume for the year and look
under owners, tenants, children and servants. Carry the occupations and co-residents as written into the
comparison at the next property. [S10 @2026-11-22] [S11 @2026-11-22]

Under an early “other residents” heading, compare the order of names across years. Treat recurring consecutive
lines as a shared-building lead and test it against co-residents, a property or a church locator. Kyläkoski’s
Burtz comparison uses KA 8448:14 (1789), KA 8452:7 (1790), KA 8463:7 (1793) and KA 8485:6 (1798). If a household
has a separate communion-book page, check whether it follows the related household in the annual roll. In the
1801 example (KA 8503:7), the order supports the possibility that the parish used an extra page for the same
building, and the precise residence remains uncertain. [S2] [S11]

If the person has no entry of their own, inspect the lines for servants and other household members for an
indirect reference by name or role. In Kyläkoski’s example a servant’s entry names the gardener at the plantation
in 1853, before the gardener’s own 1877 entry. Use [tax-roll observation](tax-roll-observation-workflow.md) to log
which years and categories you checked. [S12] [S11]

## Match an address to a property and its occupants

Open Helsinki City Archives’ Sinetti catalogue and search for *kiinteistökortisto*. Choose the district and block,
then confirm the property from the lot number and street address at the top of the card. Copy the buyer’s name as
written, occupation or title, and transaction date where shown. Use the buyer as an ownership candidate and test
residence in the annual poll-tax roll. [S16 @2026-11-22]

For the Helsinki municipal rolls from 1844, find the dated *henkikirja* entry by district, block and lot. Read all the
households at the property. List the recorded occupants who fit the surname and other clues, then compare their
titles and other details with the property-card buyer and the original address lead. [S11 @2026-11-22] [S16 @2026-11-22]

In Kyläkoski’s memoir example, the card for Uudenmaankatu 7 records the 1861 buyer as G. A. Lindström, a town hall
caretaker. In the 1866 roll (U58b:1761) he appears as *Kommissions Handlanden* and is the only adult man of that
surname recorded at that address. Keep the candidate comparison bounded to the people listed at the property
in the year checked. [S15]

### Check named villas and property interests

When a newspaper names a building after a person, record owners, tenants and employees in separate fields. In
Kyläkoski’s Fjälldal example, the villa names in *Hbl* on 30 April 1878 and *HD* on 20 November 1878 differ.
The 1878 roll (U82:1913) lists Böckerman as owner, Tång as assistant and Andstén as tenant. Check the probate
inventory for another property reference. The author found no property entry for Andstén in HRA 11488. [S17]

Re-read the property headings each year. In 1878, *Villan Fjelldal* appears separately from the workers’
barracks. In the 1886 comparison (U105:1121–1135), *Fjelldal* heads the barracks and *Lilla Fjelldal* has
separate sections owned by Tallgren and Böckerman, each with tenants. If a listed owner is absent from the
occupants, keep the ownership attribution and find the owner’s own registration elsewhere in the roll. [S17]

If a directory lists a bankrupt estate under only one part of a property, check the auction notice for other
interests. Compare the 1890 and 1891 Fjälldal address calendars with the notice in *Finland*, 1 October 1891,
which Kyläkoski reports as offering one quarter of the large villa and one half of the two-storey villa nearer
the city. Record each fraction as offered at auction and check a sale or conveyance before assigning a new
owner. Use [city-directory observation](city-directory-observation-workflow.md) for the listing date and
[land index and book series](land-index-book-series-recovery.md) for the conveyance. [S17]

## Check the declarations behind the 1806 roll

For the 1806 *Helsingfors stad* entry, use the Uusimaa and Häme county accounts, KA 8529. Kyläkoski reports
that the digitized original declarations follow the order of the names in the roll. Use that order to find
the likely declaration, then confirm the match by name, property or neighbouring entries. [S18] [S19]

If the roll omits given names, check the declaration before accepting or rejecting the identification (reported
examples at pp. 158 and 252). Compare the declarations for details omitted in the tabulated roll, such as rent
(p. 151), household birth years (p. 158) or sons’ schooling (p. 178). Behind a luxury-tax line, check who smoked
or used silk and whether the declaration describes the taxable item, such as a dog’s size and colour (p. 237). [S18]

## Recover reporting dates and bound the move

Find both properties in the successive rolls. Read the date written at the end of each volume and log it as the
closing date. Kyläkoski suggests late spring as a rough estimate when a volume closed in summer. Keep that estimate
provisional when the reporting day is unknown. Search the contemporary press for that year’s *henkikirjoitus*
notice and find the assigned reporting day for the block or outlying locality. Log the publication date, local
reporting day, any stated reference day and the volume’s closing date separately. [S20] [S21]

Use the latest dated evidence at the old address and the earliest dated evidence at the new one. An earlier
return can also supply a lower bound if its coverage and contents establish that the household had yet to arrive.
When a late or missing entry leaves that uncertain, check a dated directory, notice or advertisement and keep the
interval as broad as the evidence requires. Use [newspaper section search](historical-newspaper-section-search.md)
for dated address evidence and [evidence and proof judgment](evidence-proof-judgment.md) to test a proposed
absence. [S20] [S21]

For Taipale, start with *Suomalainen Wirallinen Lehti* on 13 February 1897 and 15 February 1898. Kyläkoski uses
the local reporting dates, 6 April 1897 and 14 April 1898, and the corresponding household evidence to bound
arrival after the first date and by the second. These newspaper locators come from the cited study. Independent
verification requires checking the notices and the matching returns. [S21]

Test a proposed customary moving day against the local deadline. Kyläkoski reports that the 1884 Helsinki volume
closed on 22 December, although reporting for central blocks began on 21 April. The household in her Makrilli
example had already left by that block’s 8 May reporting deadline, before the proposed 1 June moving day. The
example uses the deadlines as reported in the community study and comment. [S20] [S21]

## Cross-check police address cards

For a deceased subject, use the City Archives’ *Poliisilaitoksen osoitetoimiston osoiterekisteri* guide. Search
nearby phonetic spellings and check the spouse’s card. Transcribe the previous address, new address, reporting
date and any host name. For a potentially living subject, follow the [Living people rule](../SKILL.md#living-people)
and stay with the historical materials supplied by the user. [S13 @2026-11-22]

Check the incorporation date before taking a first city entry as the start of residence. Search the earlier
jurisdiction when the locality was outside the city. For Fredriksberg (Pasila), Kyläkoski reports a 1913 first
card entry a year after annexation and treats residence in Helsingin pitäjä in 1907–1912 as a possibility.
Use [historical place-name resolution](historical-place-name-resolution.md) to track the period locality and
jurisdiction. [S13] [S14]

Compare the card with the same-year *henkikirja* at the exact property. Keep a card-only address as a separate
observation. Match a legible host surname to the recorded occupants and keep a match as a candidate household.
In the 1913 Fredriksberg villa 6 case, Kyläkoski reports a possible Kiriloff host match (U304:7216) while the
resident named on the card is missing from that roll entry. If a host field is unreadable, check for a pasted
clipping and record an obscured field as such, as in the spring 1914 Vaasankatu 6 example. [S13] [S14] [S11]

Keep temporary addresses separate from longer stays and use successive rolls to identify the years recorded at
the later address. Kyläkoski distinguishes a temporary entry at Pursimiehenkatu 25 from Kankurinkatu 4, listed
in 1916–1918 (U340:3362, U355:3077 and U369:3130). After a gap, use the origin of the first recorded move as a
lead back to the previous jurisdiction. In the same example, the card’s 1930 move to Pääskylänkatu 7 A 81
points back to Tapanila in Helsingin pitäjä after the 1919–1923 Punavuorenkatu 20 roll entries
(U383:2001, U393:1975, U407:1527, U420:1394 and U432:1399). [S14]

## Return the household timeline

Give one observation per record. Include the name and role as written, the historical address and lot or parish,
and the creator, series, volume and page, image, notice or card locator. Label communion, transaction, closing,
reporting and move dates by what each record actually dates.

Give the best-supported bounds for an inferred move, state when a bound is open and cite the records and
reporting schedule behind the interval. Explain what links an identity across records, which matches remain
candidates and what an absence is bounded by. For each unresolved gap, give the next unsearched series,
property or jurisdiction.
