# Rebuild a Russian Orthodox cleric's career and household from directories, eparchial gazettes and clergy registers

Use this recipe when the person you are researching was a Russian Orthodox priest, deacon, psalmist (дьячок, пономарь, псаломщик), prosphora-maker or clergy widow or child, and you need a dated chain of posts and a household. Use it before you search metrical books or a present-day oblast archive, and before you merge or split clergy who share a surname. It is built on Ural forum work in the Perm, Yekaterinburg, Tobolsk and Orenburg dioceses. The order of work and the record types carry over to other dioceses. The editions, fonds and boundaries listed below do not, so re-establish them for each diocese.

For dating jurisdiction and logging custody in general, use [research documentation](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/research-documentation.md). For auditing directory editions and reading each entry as a dated observation, use the [city-directory observation workflow](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/city-directory-observation-workflow.md). For merge and split rulings, and for the rule that a different name does not by itself mean a different person, use [evidence/proof judgment](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/evidence-proof-judgment.md). For 1930s arrest and repression files, use [Russian military and repression record episodes](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/russian-military-repression-record-episodes.md). For case boundaries and pages actually inspected, use [archival image coverage verification](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/archival-image-coverage-verification.md).

## 1. Date the diocese, consistory and deanery for every post

- Do not route by the present-day oblast. The Yekaterinburg diocese was formed in 1885 from the eastern part of the Perm diocese and covered Verkhoturye, Yekaterinburg, Irbit, Kamyshlov and Shadrinsk uezds. [S6] The Ural forum's reference article adds that Krasnoufimsk uezd stayed in the Perm diocese until 1917, and that Turinsk uezd and part of Tyumen uezd belonged to Tobolsk. [S6] For each post, write down the diocese, uezd and deanery (заказ, благочиннический округ) in force on that date.
- For the 18th century, look in Tobolsk first. Participants found trans-Ural clergy in the Tobolsk consistory fond (Tobolsk archive, Ф. И-156) [S7], [S12], [S17], [S25], and found Kungur zakaz clergy in a 1755 statement at RGADA (Ф. 439, оп. 1, д. 27) [S17]. A participant cited a list of transfers made by the consistory in 1755–1758 as Ф. 155, оп. 1755, д. 246 of the Tobolsk archive. [S12] The consistory fond is otherwise cited as И-156, so confirm the fond number before you request it.
- Expect one family's records to be spread over several archives under the names those archives use now. The threads cite metrical books and confession lists for parishes in the Shadrinsk and Kurgan area held in Chelyabinsk (ОГАЧО), Omsk (ГАОО), Shadrinsk (ГАШ), Dalmatovo, a municipal archive at Artemovsky, Perm (ГАПК) and Sverdlovsk (ГАСО). [S1], [S4], [S11], [S12], [S15], [S17] Log each custodian you checked.
- Same-faith (единоверческие) churches were grouped in their own deanery, not under the civil uezd. The 1915 Yekaterinburg reference book lists them in a separate единоверческий округ, and the clergy registers for that deanery are filed separately (1912: ГАСО ф. 6, оп. 4, д. 334). [S10]
- Use dated consistory files on clergy appointments to show which zakaz and diocese a parish belonged to in each period. A participant warned that these files will name few or no parishioners. [S19]
- Keep look-alike place names apart before you assign an entry. In one thread, Теченское поселье (later Теченское село) and Теченская слобода were different places. [S12] When civil gazetteers do not show a village with a church, gazette service notices that name the church can confirm it existed. [S9]

## 2. Build the draft service list from the full run of directories

- Before you go to an archive, read every surviving diocesan address-calendar (адрес-календарь) and reference book (справочная книга/книжка). Each one lists the clergy by uezd, deanery and parish, with name, patronymic, rank, office, education, length of service and awards. [S6] The reference article lists these editions: Perm 1870, 1877, 1882, 1885, 1894, 1896, 1899, 1909, 1912; Yekaterinburg 1886, 1887, 1890, 1900, 1902, 1903, 1904, 1905, 1906, 1909, 1915; Tobolsk 1884, 1895, 1897, 1901, 1903, 1908, 1913. Full clergy lists without education or awards also appear in the Perm provincial address-calendars for 1896 and 1897. [S6] Participants also used a Perm clergy list for 1898 and a Yekaterinburg list for 1899. [S7] Mark each edition as inspected, unavailable or not yet seen.
- Record every age together with the date it refers to. According to the reference article, the 1915 Yekaterinburg book gives ages as of 1 January 1914, not as of the title year. [S6] For other editions that print ages (the 1912 Perm book does [S5], [S8]), keep the reference date as unknown unless the edition states it. Where no age is printed, estimate a birth range from the school-leaving year. The article puts the end of the clerical училище at about 14–15 and the end of seminary at about 20–22. [S6] An age mismatch makes a candidate less likely. It does not exclude him.
- Decode the abbreviations from the edition itself, for example the higher division of a named училище plus a year, a seminary rank, or "сверх штата" (supernumerary). A man listed as supernumerary at a parish may never have held a staff post there. [S1]
- Read the special sections. The article reports that the 1885 Perm calendar lists men who left the diocese over the previous 20 years: those who died, moved to other dioceses or were dismissed from clerical status. [S6] A participant used it to date a priest's dismissal. [S18]
- Add the 1900 reference book of all Perm seminary graduates from 1800 to 1900. It gives the graduation year, class, rank and later post. The article warns that it was compiled in haste and contains errors, and that many clergy never finished seminary. [S4], [S6], [S8]
- Use a directory staff list to check a hard-to-read metrical entry. Note the directory's reading and its page next to the original reading, and correct only the element in doubt. [S13]
- Same surname, compatible patronymics and neighbouring parishes only suggest kinship. The article notes that until 1867 a post often passed to a son or son-in-law. [S6] Record these as hypotheses.

## 3. Add exact dates from the eparchial gazettes, then page the whole run

- The runs are Perm EV 1867–1919, Yekaterinburg EV 1886–1917 and Tobolsk EV 1882–1919. [S6] The reference article adds that Известия Екатеринбургской церкви (1917–1918) and Известия Екатеринбургской епархии (1919) followed the Yekaterinburg EV. [S6] A participant cited a 1919 issue of the Известия Екатеринбургской церкви for a transfer. [S20]
- Gazette notices usually omit patronymics, and the gazettes have no name index. Fix full names from the directories first. Then page through every issue for the relevant years. Do not rely on a sample. [S6] A participant resolved patronymic-less salary-list and transfer notices against the 1912 Perm reference book, parish by parish. [S5]
- Collect these notice types: appointment, ordination, transfer, award, ban, retirement and death [S6], [S11], [S22]; grants to retired clergy, widows and orphans [S6], [S1], [S13]; a widow's appointment as prosphora-maker [S1]; orphan-capital accounts that name the children year after year [S1], [S22]; decisions on girls' school places, which can mention siblings [S22]; membership lists of church societies [S6], [S20]; the yearly class and rank lists of seminaries, clerical училища and diocesan girls' schools [S3], [S5], [S6]; and jubilee articles and obituaries in the unofficial section (неофициальный отдел, Н.О.) [S6], [S11]. A participant also cited an award in the Synod's Церковные ведомости. [S16]
- Cite each notice by year, volume, issue number and date, and page, including supplement pagination where it applies ("прил."). [S1], [S11]
- A death notice and a later widow's appointment name different churches. Do not put the death at the church where the widow later served. In one thread a participant first did exactly that, then corrected it from the death notice. [S1] Treat one-off gazette name readings that clash with the run as possible misprints, and flag them. [S22]

## 4. Retrieve the clergy registers (клировые ведомости) and record which years exist

- Clergy registers, also called ведомости о церквах, were compiled every year for every church from the late 18th century. They can give origin, age, birth date and place, education, a dated career, conduct, awards and penalties, property, wife, children (including adult children living elsewhere), sons-in-law, and kinship with the other clergy of the church. [S6] Participants used them for exact dates and households. [S2], [S5], [S8], [S10], [S16], [S23]
- They are filed in two ways. Some are year volumes covering all churches of a deanery, or of a whole uezd in earlier years, in consistory and deanery fonds. The article gives ГАСО ф. 6, 251, 252 and 698 for these. Others are multi-year volumes for a single church in that church's own fond. [S6] Examples cited: one church for 1871–1899 (ГАШ ф. 387, оп. 1, д. 51) [S1]; deanery volumes for 1894 and 1900 (ГАСО ф. 698, оп. 1, дд. 328, 340) [S11], [S16], [S23]; ГАСО ф. 6, оп. 2, д. 577 (1860) [S18]; ф. 6, оп. 2, д. 643 (1882) and оп. 4, д. 228 (1907) [S2]; ГАСО ф. 603, оп. 1, д. 695 (1909) [S5]; ГАПК ф. 542, оп. 1, д. 3 (1916) [S8].
- The gazettes did not replace the clergy registers. The reference article points out that they had different readers, and states that the registers went on being compiled through the gazette era and at least into the early 1930s. [S6] Search for late registers on that basis, but log any year you cannot find as a coverage gap.
- Follow the church's registers after the cleric died. A 1907 register kept a section on a deceased archpriest's children, with their ages, occupations, places of residence and marital status. [S2] Registers also record wards (воспитанница). [S10]
- Read the entries for the cleric's colleagues too. Court and complaint notes in a fellow cleric's entry can name the person you are looking for and date a dispute. [S11], [S23]
- Use the stated social origin (for example "из крестьян" of a named works) to link a cleric to his birth family. [S8]
- Record which years each series actually holds. Participants found one church with only an 1860 deanery volume and later registers in the church fond [S22], and another parish with no surviving registers for the period needed [S1]. A missing year is a coverage gap, not a negative result.

## 5. Where registers are missing, use clergy revision lists, confession lists and 18th-century statements

- Clergy do appear in revision lists, despite the common belief that they do not. The article gives ГАПК ф. 111, оп. 1, дд. 2418, 2712, 2764 and 2981 for the 1833 lists of Yekaterinburg, Kamyshlov and Irbit uezds and the 1795 list of Verkhoturye. [S6] Entries show the previous revision's household and where a man left to, which links a cleric under a new surname to his father's household. [S22] A participant cited the 1833 Yekaterinburg list as ф. 111, оп. 1, д. 418. Check that locator against д. 2418 before you request it. [S22] Clergy revision lists from 1811 and 1834 also survive for Orenburg diocese parishes. [S5], [S12]
- Confession lists (исповедные росписи, духовные росписи) were compiled yearly. They list families with ages, starting with the clergy, and give reasons for absence such as seminary study. [S6] Most are in church fonds. Bound runs for whole uezds are in ГАСО ф. 6, оп. 2 and ф. 24, оп. 23, дд. 6987, 7078–7082, 7168. [S6], [S14], [S18] Other runs cited are ГАСО ф. 6, оп. 3, дд. 638 and 638а (1800) [S11] and a combined metrical and confession volume for 1849–1851 [S15]. Read successive years, not one.
- For the 18th century, use the Tobolsk consistory's periodic statements on clergy and their sons, which give name, patronymic, age and sons: И-156, оп. 1, д. 2226 (1748, 1751); оп. 2, д. 2857 (1772); оп. 3, д. 335 (1773–1774); оп. 3, д. 1192 (1777); and a 1754 confession report, оп. 1, д. 1965. [S12], [S14], [S17], [S25]
- Find staff rosters inside other files. Metrical books carry periodic lists of the church's clergy [S6], [S1], [S22], and a case on fining people who missed confession lists each parish's clergy by year (ГАСО ф. 24, оп. 1, д. 2293). [S25]
- To test whether a man served in a gap, compare a church ledger for one year (1805: ГАСО ф. 6, оп. 2, д. 421) with a later roster (1820: ГАСО ф. 6, оп. 3, д. 21). Then find the family in the next surviving confession list (1849, in a combined metrical and confession volume for 1849–1851: Artemovsky municipal archive, ф. 13, оп. 1, д. 1, л. 1–1об.), and report the years in between as unresolved. [S15] A marriage may sit in the bride's home parish even after her father stopped serving there. [S15]
- Before you accept a cleric as a child's father, check his age and his death window at the child's birth, the mother's age, and the nearest confession-list household. An Orthodox priest or deacon could not marry a second time while he remained in holy orders, so a late child of a widowed priest or deacon cannot come from a second marriage while he keeps that rank. [S14] Judge by rank, not by the post he fills: a deacon on a psalmist vacancy is still a deacon. [S1] The rule does not bind дьячки, пономари and psalmists: a serving дьячок married a second time in 1866. [S13] A widowed priest or deacon who remarried had first left holy orders; participants documented such cases. [S17], [S18]

## 6. Separate namesakes and test surname changes

- Separate same-named clergy by patronymic, school and graduation year, and household. [S1], [S8] Record a variant patronymic in a metrical entry as a conflict to resolve, not as a second man. [S1]
- A transfer list without patronymics can point to the wrong man. Check the "ordained to this church" date in the registers of the sending and receiving churches. Unbroken service at one church since before the transfer points to a namesake. [S12]
- Test a surname change before you reject a kinship, and do not invent kinship from a shared surname. Participants documented these mechanisms: a seminary-assigned surname replacing the family one [S16]; a son taking a surname formed from his father's given name, found where the patronymic and surname coincide and the entry states his origin as a sexton's son [S21]; a surname taken from the home village, shown by the revision list's origin and departure entries [S22]; and a surname formed from the patronymic in 18th-century statements [S25]. Link each change through post, parish and the father's name. Published literature on Ural clergy surname changes is a lead, not proof. [S16] Also expect spelling drift in surnames between the metrical book, gazette and register. [S16]
- Before you call a man missing, check two things. First, look for dismissal from clerical status (directory section, step 2). Then follow him as a civil official through marriages, jury lists and the seminary book, linking by patronymic, seminary year and age. [S18] Second, check teaching posts outside the parish staff. A priest employed only as a law teacher at a gymnasium or other secular school was not on a church staff, so look in the provincial address-calendars [S20] and the Памятные книжки of the Orenburg educational district. [S6] Parish priests also taught in secular schools, so a teaching post does not by itself mean a second man. [S10] If both checks fail, test a misrecorded given name or patronymic as the next hypothesis. [S20]

## 7. After 1917, record each entry as a dated observation

- Metrical books from 1919–1920 still name the serving priest. [S23]
- Use the regional lists of people deprived of voting rights (лишенцы), arranged by okrug, district, village soviet and year: ГАСО ф. р-88, оп. 9 — д. 2, т. 1 (1924); д. 33, т. 2 (1925); д. 235 (1929). [S16], [S23], [S24] Record the list, volume and folio, and quote the stated before-and-after occupation as written. [S24]
- For the 1930 and 1937 cases, a participant pointed to ГААОСО in Yekaterinburg and advised bringing documents that prove kinship. [S8] A memorial-book arrest entry with matching name, birth year and village is still only a candidate: one participant found a full namesake who was a different man. [S17]

## Output

1. A diocese and deanery timeline for each parish, with dates and the custodian archive for each series.
2. A service chronology with one row per source: source type, edition or issue and date, page, post, stated age with its reference date, and the level inspected.
3. A household table from the clergy registers, revision lists and confession lists, with full locators.
4. A namesake and surname-change register: the candidates, the linking evidence (post, parish, father, school year, household), conflicts, and status.
5. A series-coverage list: which years of each register, confession and revision series were searched, which are missing, and which remain unrequested.
