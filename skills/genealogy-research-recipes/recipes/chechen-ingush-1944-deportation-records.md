# Trace a Chechen or Ingush family through the 1944 deportation and special settlement

Use this recipe when a Chechen or Ingush family lived in the Chechen-Ingush ASSR before 1944, when a line runs through the 1944 deportation and special settlement in the Kazakh or Kirghiz SSR, or when names and village names in documents differ from the ones the family uses. It prevents six failures: placing a 1944–1957 event in a republic that did not exist then, searching only one name of a renamed village, splitting relatives or merging strangers by surname and spelling, predicting a file's absence from Grozny's war losses, asking only one end for a special settler's file, and reading a miss in a partial online list as proof that nobody was deported. The custodian routes in step 5 are those of the Chechen Republic. For an Ingush family, establish the Republic of Ingushetia's custodians separately; this recipe does not cover them.

Link out for the shared steps:

- **Dated place names, and translating a later name back to the name at the event:** [historical place-name resolution § 3](historical-place-name-resolution.md).

- **Capturing and registering the family's papers:** [family collection intake and capture](family-collection-intake-and-capture.md).

## Inputs to gather first

Gather these before searching:

- every form of each person's name seen so far, with the document it came from, and the form the family uses
- each event's date, and whether it falls before the deportation of 23 February 1944, during 1944–1957 or after the restoration of 9 January 1957
- the home village and district as the family names them, and as each document writes them
- the place of settlement as written: SSR, oblast, raion, selsoviet, and kolkhoz, sovkhoz or enterprise
- what the family already holds: a rehabilitation certificate, a certificate of eviction in 1944, and papers from the exile period
- any WWII service, and any repression before 1944
- whether the family is Chechen or Ingush

## 1. Read what the family already holds

- **Ask first whether the family holds a rehabilitation certificate or a certificate of eviction in 1944.** Both are issued by the custodians in step 5 [S12]. Read each one for the places it names before you request anything new.
- **Read exile-period papers (1944–1960s) for the place of settlement.** Use them to identify the custodian at the place of settlement, which is the route to the special-settler file (step 6).
- **Treat one dated paper as one sighting.** It places the person there on its date, not for the whole span of residence.
- **Register each scan as an image of the original, with every place as written.** Capture mechanics belong to the family-collection recipe.

## 2. Fix the jurisdiction at each event's date

- **Date each event against the sequence of units:**
  - **Until 7 March 1944:** the Chechen-Ingush ASSR. The deportation of Chechens and Ingush began on 23 February 1944 [S5], [S6].
  - **7–22 March 1944:** the decree of 7 March 1944 abolished the ASSR. Its core became Grozny okrug (Грозненский округ) of Stavropol krai [S2].
  - **22 March 1944 to 9 January 1957:** Grozny oblast (Грозненская область) of the RSFSR, formed by decree of 22 March 1944 [S2], [S3].
  - **From 9 January 1957:** the restored Chechen-Ingush ASSR within the RSFSR [S4].
- **Check whether the district went to a neighbour in 1944.** The decree of 7 March 1944 transferred [S2]:
  - **to the Dagestan ASSR:** the Vedeno, Nozhai-Yurt, Sayasan and Cheberloy districts, the Kurchaloy and Sharoy districts except their north-western parts, and the east of the Gudermes district
  - **to the North Ossetian ASSR:** the town of Malgobek, the Achaluki, Nazran and Psedakh districts, the Prigorodny district except its south, and the west of the Sunzha district
  - **to the Georgian SSR:** the Itum-Kali district, the west of the Sharoy district, and the south of the Galanchozh, Galashki and Prigorodny districts

  Expect a 1944–1957 document about one of these places to name the receiving republic, not Grozny oblast.
- **Record the jurisdiction exactly as the source gives it.** Do not convert it to the unit you expect for that date; a mismatch is a finding.
- **For a special-settlement event, record the SSR, oblast, raion, selsoviet, and kolkhoz, sovkhoz or enterprise.**
- **Do not carry a district across 1944 or 1957 by its name.** Resolve each district from an administrative-division handbook dated to the event, using the dated-name rule in historical place-name resolution § 3.
- **Do not date a family's return from the 1957 restoration.** Date each person's return from dated records at both ends.

## 3. Search both names of a renamed place

- **Search both the old and the temporary name of a village.** Places in Grozny oblast were renamed after the deportation. Two sources agree on one pair: Shali became Mezhdurechye (Междуречье) in 1944–45 and got its name back in 1957 [S7], [S8]. Accounts disagree on the date of the renaming decree; one gives an RSFSR decree of 23 February 1945 for Grozny oblast's districts and settlements [S9].
- **Expect the temporary name in documents made while it was in force.** A passport or military paper issued before the name came back can give it as the birthplace. Record the place as written.
- **Confirm every other pair before you use it.** Check it against the RSFSR Presidium renaming decrees of 1944–45 and the act that restored the name. Do not assume that every name came back in 1957. Then translate the name back with historical place-name resolution § 3.
- **Search places of settlement under their later names too.** Kazakh regional authorities rename villages and rural districts by joint akimat and maslikhat acts, which Adilet, the official legal database (adilet.zan.kz), publishes [S10]. Search a place of settlement under the name in the family's document and under each later name from those acts. Treat an encyclopedia's account of a name's history as a lead to the act. For Kyrgyzstan, find the renaming source separately.

## 4. Build name query lanes before searching

- **Treat a surname difference between Chechen relatives as a question to test, not a separator.** Establish from the family's own documents when each branch's surname became fixed and how it was formed. Then apply the different-names test in evidence/proof judgment.
- **Where the surname may have changed, also search by given name and patronymic without it.** Use the fields the source's current search form offers.
- **For each given name, search every lane:**
  - the family's own form, for example Мохьмад
  - the same form with хь, Ӏ, аь and оь dropped or replaced by Russian letters
  - the standard Muslim form of the same name in Russian spelling, for example Магомед, Магомад or Мухаммад
  - a compound name hyphenated, joined and re-vowelled, for example Абдул-Керим, Абдулкерим and Абдул-Карим
  - the хаджи (hajji, the pilgrim's title) element as -хаджи-, -хажи-, -ходжи- and -ходжа-, in compound names and in surnames
- **Merge two forms only when date, place and family cluster agree.** Keep each form verbatim with the document that gave it.

## 5. Ask the Chechen Republic's custodians

- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: request --> A request to these custodians sends names and family details, and asks for eviction, rehabilitation, repression or ZAGS records about family members who may still be living. Answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first, ask only for what the family question needs, and send only the proof the custodian asks for.
- **Route each request by type.** The Archive Directorate of the Government of the Chechen Republic lists these custodians on its services page (arhiv-chr.ru/gosudarstvennyj-uslugi, checked 2026-11-02) [S12]:

  | Request | Custodian |
  | --- | --- |
  | Certificate of eviction in 1944 (справка о выселении) | Archive Directorate of the Government of the Chechen Republic |
  | Rehabilitation certificate (справка о реабилитации) | MVD information centre (ИЦ МВД) for the Chechen Republic |
  | Repression before 1944 | FSB Central Archive (ЦА ФСБ России) and the MVD's Main Information and Analytical Centre (ГИАЦ МВД России) |
  | Birth, marriage or death record | ZAGS archive of the Chechen Republic |

- **Treat repression before 1944 as its own episode.** The eviction route does not cover it. Use the repression-episodes recipe for its file classes.
- **Do not promise an online or foreign route.** The page links a general Gosuslugi service for archival-certificate requests, but it does not say that these particular requests can be filed there. It says nothing about applicants abroad [S12]. Record both as open.
- **Do not predict a file's absence from Grozny's war losses.** The Archive Directorate's official history page (checked 2026-11-02) reports [S11]:
  - the fighting of 1994–96 and 1999–2000 destroyed more than 90% of the documents of the republic's National Archive
  - of 12 district archives, 4 were fully and 4 partly destroyed
  - part of the collection of personnel documents of former special settlers (Коллекция документальных материалов по личному составу бывших спецпереселенцев) survives: 122,058 storage units out of a former 257,905 special-settler files, about 47%

  Ask the Archive Directorate about the specific file, and search at the place of settlement in parallel (step 6).
- **Outside the republic, read GARF's special-settlement fonds for context, not for one family's file.** The State Archive of the Russian Federation (ГАРФ) holds f. Р-9479, the special-settlements department of the USSR NKVD and MVD and its predecessors (1,279 storage units, 1930–1959), and f. Р-9401, the USSR NKVD/MVD, including the "Special Folders" of its Secretariat. Its guide describes decrees, orders, reports, statistics and correspondence on deportation and special settlement; some files name individuals, such as the 1952–1958 conclusions on removing special settlers from the register. Neither fond is a route to an individual deportee's case file [S23].

## 6. Search the place of settlement

- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: request --> A request at either end, to the Kazakh Archive of the President, to the Kazakh legal-statistics committee or to a ZAGS office at the place of settlement, sends names and family details and asks for special-settler, repression or birth records about people who may still be living. Answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before you send it, and follow the record's access rule.
- **Do not assume the file went back to Grozny, or that it stayed in exile.** A 2022 study of the special settler's personal file reports that only a small part of the documents was sent to the archives of the restored Chechen-Ingush, Kabardino-Balkar, Karachay-Cherkess and Kalmyk autonomies in 1967–68, and that the rest stayed in the Kazakh and Kirghiz republics [S14]. Yet Grozny's special-settler collection once held 257,905 files (step 5) [S11]. Neither tells you where one family's file is. Ask both ends.
- **In Kazakhstan, ask the Archive of the President first.** In 2022–2023 its Almaty centre for the study of political-repression materials received more than 700,000 storage units from the special archives of the MVD, the National Security Committee (КНБ) and the Prosecutor General's Office, including criminal cases and registration cards [S19]. The Prosecutor General's Office reported in September 2023 that about 251,000 special-settler files had been transferred there [S21]. Its "Архивная справка" (archival certificate) page has a separate procedure and sample form for requests about victims of political repression in 1920–1950 [S13]. The procedure asks for the place of deportation or resettlement and for documents proving identity and kinship, and the sample form accepts a foreign citizen's passport [S20].
- **Also ask the Prosecutor General's Committee on Legal Statistics and Special Accounts (КПСиСУ).** It issues archival certificates and copies from its own archives as a state service (gov.kz service 3035), and its archives hold special-settlement registration. Persons under 16 were not registered personally in 1939–1955, so a child has no record of their own there [S22].
- **For a birth in exile, search the civil-registration (ZAGS) records of the place of settlement.**
- **In Kyrgyzstan, establish the custodian separately.** This recipe has not checked one.
- **For the file classes at the place of settlement, use the repression-episodes recipe;** for workplace and school files there, use the Soviet personal-file recipe.

## 7. Read Kazakh online lists for what they cover

- **Cite e-memory.kz as the site describes itself, and date the citation.** It presents itself as the portal of Kazakhstan's State Commission for the Full Rehabilitation of Victims of Political Repression, and lists the commission's project office and the Valikhanov Institute of History and Ethnology as contacts [S15]. The decree that set up the commission (No. 456 of 24 November 2020) lost force under Decree No. 438 of 22 January 2024 [S16].
- **Read a miss as absence from this list only.** It is a partial list for 1920–1950: 214,696 people as of 2026-11-02, 50,907 of them in the «Сталинские депортации» (Stalin deportations) category. The site says the list does not fully include the victims the commission identified [S15].
- **Search each category separately.** The home page offers no search across categories; the deportation category has its own search page (/search/stalin/) [S15]. Search every category that could fit the person, and record each search.
- **Test two hits for a shared origin before you count them.** Open List carries records sourced from regional departments of Kazakhstan's National Security Committee (ДКНБ РК) [S17], and e-memory.kz names no source for its entries [S15]. Compare an e-memory.kz hit with an Open List hit before counting them as two sources, using the independence test in evidence/proof judgment.

## 8. Check WWII service in the Chechen memory book

- **Use the printed book as its own source.** The Chechen Republic's memory book «Память. 1941–1945 гг.» (Memory. 1941–1945) was published in Nalchik in 2010 and presented in the Chechen parliament on 6 May 2010 [S18]. It lists every participant found, not only the dead, under 20 districts of the Chechen-Ingush ASSR and the city of Grozny, with districts and names in alphabetical order; it also covers Chechens who went to the front from other regions [S18]. Find a copy with the published-book recipe. Read the section for the person's district first, then browse the others. Bound the negative to the sections, pages and name forms checked.
- **Trace an OBD Memorial hit to the book.** Read the hit's source field. If it names a memory book of the Chechen Republic, find that entry in the printed book, and count the card and the book entry as one source, as the repression-episodes recipe's source-scope rule requires.

## Close with a deportation packet

Return:

- **Jurisdiction rows:** each event with the unit as written and the unit at that date; for a settlement event, the SSR, oblast, raion, selsoviet, and kolkhoz, sovkhoz or enterprise.
- **Place-name rows:** each renamed place with both names, the act that confirms the pair or "unconfirmed", and where each name was searched.
- **Name lanes:** every form with its document, and where each lane was searched.
- **Family papers:** each item with its date, the places it names and the custodian it points to.
- **Custodian rows:** each request with its custodian, the date the route was checked, whether the user sent it, and the reply's exact wording.
- **List searches:** site, category, search date and result, and any shared-origin group.
- **Open routes:** Ingush custodians, the Kyrgyz custodian, applicants abroad other than through the Kazakh form, and whether the Kazakh repression procedure handles special-settler files, which the archive holds but the procedure does not name.

The model assembles this packet. The researcher decides identity and kinship, and the user decides whether any request is sent.
