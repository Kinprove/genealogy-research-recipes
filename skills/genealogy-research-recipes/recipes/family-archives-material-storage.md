# Store family papers, books, photographs and textiles by material

Open this recipe when inherited documents, albums, photographs or textiles need safer physical storage. Record what was kept together before rehousing any item.

## Separate loose paper and newsprint

According to the presenter’s advice, house family papers in archival folders and separate retained newsprint from other originals. [S1†]

Use acid-free, lignin-free folders and boxes large enough to support loose records. House oversize sheets flat if they open without resistance. Leave brittle or tightly rolled material supported as found pending conservation advice. [S3]

If a news clipping came out of a Bible, photograph or note where it lay before moving it. File the original separately in flat housing and keep the clipping locator with the Bible. A flat copy can preserve the association without placing a stiff sleeve inside the binding. [S3], [S4]

## Match the enclosure to the photograph or film

According to the presenter, choose boxes that fit the prints or film and preserve the grouping of albums or originals. [S1†]

Use sleeves or folders that pass the Photographic Activity Test for prints. Choose a box that supports the size and format, and use archival pages in albums. Identify a film negative’s base before choosing a sleeve, and use suitable paper enclosures for nitrate, acetate or glass plate negatives. Preserve labels when moving slides or negatives into new housing. [S3], [S5]

## Support books and textile folds

According to the interview, use an enclosure for a family Bible or album on a stable shelf, protect the spine during removal and keep household adhesive tape off a tear. [S2†]

Box fragile Bibles and albums. For shelving, support sound volumes upright and lay oversized or weak volumes flat. Grip both sides near the middle when removing a book. For a torn or unstable binding, protect it in its box and choose professional conservation for treatment. [S6], [S7], [S8]

According to the presenter, cushion a folded quilt or garment with suitable tissue when boxing it. [S1†]

Box a folded family textile in a cool, dark storage area. Pad garment sleeves and unavoidable folds of a dress or quilt with unbuffered acid-free tissue. [S9]
