## Goal

How an AI assistant (or a genealogist using one) can apply AI to DNA/genealogy work **reliably** — what to delegate freely, what to never trust, and the prompt patterns that actually work.

## The mental model (get this right or everything downstream breaks)

AI is a **language-prediction system, not a reasoning authority** — it produces *plausible* output, which is not the same as *correct* output, and it has no access to records it hasn't been given (or can't retrieve). Therefore: **every AI output is a hypothesis to verify, never a fact to file.** "AI lies" is the wrong frame — it's non-intentional; it confidently fills gaps. (Denyse Allen, 2026-01)

## What to delegate freely (AI is genuinely good at these)

- **Restructuring** scattered notes / transcriptions into tables, outlines, timelines, drafts.
- **Transcription** of documents — genuinely useful, but accuracy varies with the handwriting and image quality; *correct-and-verify* against the image, never trust a transcription blind. (Denyse Allen)
- **Assembling citations from complete inputs** you supply (see prompt-craft). (Family Locket, 2025-02)
- **Brainstorming research strategy**, historical/legal context, and *draft* proof-argument prose. (Denyse Allen; Family Locket)
- **Surfacing hypotheses** — comparing Ancestry vs 23andMe reference panels, or cross-referencing tree surnames against ethnicity regions, to *raise* patterns and anomalies **to investigate** (not to conclude — see the ethnicity never-trust). (Family Locket, 2026-03)
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> Match screenshots name living DNA matches and show their shared cM, so giving them to an AI tool moves living people's data; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first.
- **Front-ending mechanical prep** — e.g. turning Ancestry match screenshots into a structured cM/relationship table to start a Leeds chart. (Family Locket, 2025-02)

## What to never trust or delegate (verify, or don't)

- **Record retrieval / existence.** A bare LLM (no verified retrieval/browsing) cannot fetch an actual birth record or confirm a source exists; if pushed, it *invents* one. A tool-enabled assistant that *can* browse may surface a real catalog entry — but treat anything it returns as unverified until you inspect the actual record. Supplying the record makes it genuinely useful, but verify the output against the source even then. (Denyse Allen)
- **Citations from a bare link.** Citation format depends on source-specific fields (author, source type, publication, access) that a URL alone doesn't carry — supply the fields or the citation will be wrong. (Family Locket & Denyse Allen)
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> Entering a tree and several matches into WATO puts living matches' cM and tree details into an outside tool; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first.
- **cM → relationship odds.** Never ask the LLM to compute relationship probabilities from a cM number — it *fabricates* them. Route a *single* cM figure to the **Shared cM Project / DNA Painter's Shared cM Tool** (point cM → the *range* of candidate relationships); if you have a tree plus multiple matches to place, that's **WATO** — a *different* DNA Painter tool that scores hypothesis slots, not a cM→relationship lookup. Either way, always ask for **all** candidate relationships, never a single point answer. (Half vs full relationships are frequently indistinguishable by cM alone.)
- **cM as closeness under endogamy.** Endogamy / pedigree collapse **inflates** shared cM, so the same cM implies a closer relationship than reality — the AI has no idea your lines are endogamous unless you tell it, and even then it can't quantify the correction. **Never let an AI add cM across relationship paths as though the paths were independent** — this is **non-additivity**: when a match descends from the same ancestral couple through more than one path (pedigree collapse), the realized shared DNA is the **union** of the paths' identical-by-descent (IBD) regions, so its expected once-counted cM is **at least the strongest single path's expected figure and at most the naive sum**. Those regions can overlap, so the sum is only an *upper bound*, not the expectation, unless disjointness has been established. For an actual figure, route it to a **pedigree-aware kinship / IBD-simulation tool** that states its IBD-counting convention, rather than hand-combining path estimates. Do not apply one maximum-cM cap to every relationship: full siblings and identical twins can share IBD2 regions, and vendors count IBD differently. (Estes, 2024-03-28)
- **Auto-clustering swallowing genealogically distinct lines.** In a shared-match / in-common-with (ICW) network, a match that bridges two clusters is a genuine edge, not noise, and a clustering algorithm merges the two real family clusters into one. One explanation is a match related to the target through **more than one line** (see the next bullet for the others). Never trust an AI's auto-clustering output blindly; when two clusters look suspiciously fused, the standard fixes are to **raise the cM threshold, sub-cluster, and manually resolve the multi-relationship match**. If you exclude that match to re-cluster on single-line matches, reinstate it afterwards: dropping it loses real signal, and forcing it into a single line repeats the clustering error. Label it **multi-line** once you have identified **both** relationship paths, and where segment data exists, [evaluate each interval separately](#untangle-multiple-paths-without-discarding-the-useful-evidence) and retain unresolved ancestral assignments. (Family Locket, RLP 225, 2022-10-31)
- **A match that bridges two clusters is not proof of two paths to the tester.** It may descend from both ancestral couples, but it may instead share separate ancestry with members of the other cluster that does not involve the tester, or both clusters may draw on one endogamous population. Until both paths to the tester are documented, do not merge the clusters or label the match as multi-line; record the bridge with all three explanations open. The [linked-cluster discussion](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/myheritage-dna-theories-of-family-relativity) lists these explanations.
- **Ethnicity estimates as conclusions.** They're probabilistic and re-versioned by vendors; let AI surface *hypotheses* about admixture/migration, never conclude an ancestral origin. (Family Locket, 2026-03) **Never let an AI flag an ethnicity-% discordance as an NPE, and never let it declare "no NPE" from ethnicity % either** — ethnicity percentages alone can neither establish nor rule out a non-paternity event (NPE) or a lab problem. A parent/child regional-percentage discordance has two co-existing sources, so do not let an AI dismiss it as "mostly estimator noise": (a) the percentages are **model-dependent statistical estimates** (reference panel, category boundaries, algorithm version) that vendors revise over time, and (b) **real biology** — a child inherits only a recombined half of each parent's autosome, so regional proportions are genuinely **not conserved** across a generation even with a perfect estimator. Route the parentage question instead to relationship-specific DNA evidence — not total shared cM alone (parent-child and full-sibling totals overlap), but **total cM plus chromosome coverage plus the IBD1/IBD2 (FIR/HIR) pattern**, evaluated against the competing close-relationship alternatives. Reserve any "confirms/excludes parentage" conclusion for **appropriately identified samples**; where it matters, use a purpose-built chain-of-custody parentage test. (Estes, 2017-07-02; Ancestry Support; MyHeritage Help, 2025-11-16)
- **A kit from a transplant recipient.** Before reading unexpected matches, ethnicity or predicted relationships as misattributed parentage or a sample swap, ask whether the tester has had a bone-marrow or stem-cell transplant. The [transplant remark](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1rjrcHOGygI) notes that a consumer kit from such a tester may resemble the donor's DNA. Flag the result as possibly not the tester's own genome. Do not suggest a different consumer sample type as a workaround, and do not use the explanation to dismiss a discrepancy without asking.
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: contact --> Asking a documented relative to take a targeted DNA test approaches a living person about a possible parentage question; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first.
- **A documented line read as confirmed, or as broken, from the match list alone.** A documented relative's presence among the matches does not confirm the documented relationship: check the amount against both that relationship and its half alternative. An amount more typical of the half relationship opens a possible break on either tester's line, or on both, while the documented full relationship with unusually low sharing stays possible. Work outward one generation at a time, looking for matches through each ancestor's mother and father; matches to descendants of a distant couple do not by themselves confirm every link below it. A proposed line with no matches is not by itself proof of misattributed parentage. It is a clue, and a stronger one when it comes with another anomaly, such as low sharing with a known relative. First record the line's family sizes over several generations, whether it descends from recent immigrants from a population the database samples poorly, and whether its known descendants have tested or attached trees. Test each pattern before declaring a parentage break. For a half-typical amount, check whether the matches shared with that relative include collateral relatives of both proposed common ancestors or of only one. For a line with no matches, target-test a documented relative from that line, such as a descendant of the ancestor's sibling; the result can help confirm or refute a break. Legacy Tree Genealogists' articles on [the half-relationship check](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/using-dna-genealogy-tools-centimorgans), [closest matches](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/how-to-understand-your-closest-autosomal-dna-test-matches), [signs of misattributed parentage](https://www.legacytree.com/?p=80153), [checking a known pedigree](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/6-reasons-why-taking-an-autosomal-dna-test-can-bring-new-insights-into-your-known-ancestry) and [searching match lists](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/searching-sorting-and-filtering-dna-match-lists) motivate these checks.
- **A tested close cousin who matches your relatives but not you.** First confirm that the comparison exists: both kits are in the same database, both take part in matching, and neither match is hidden. If so, the pattern raises a possible break on either tester's line, and matching your relatives does not localize it. At second cousin or closer, where detectable sharing is near-certain, a confirmed zero is a strong signal that the documented relationship is wrong or that a kit is not its tester's own; beyond that, genuine cousins can share no detectable DNA, so read the missing match only as a clue. Under endogamy, those relatives may also match the cousin through another line. Locate the break with relationship-specific DNA evidence before recording misattributed parentage. The [endogamy talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xaa5FcbZYb8) names this screen.
- **DNA-estimated relationships as fact.** A tool's "estimated relationship" is a cM-based guess and routinely differs from the real one — AI parroting it will mis-group matches. (Family Locket, Leeds video)
- **Final evidentiary judgment.** Proof validation is human work; AI drafts, you rule. (Denyse Allen)
- **Source-type classification without explicit rules.** Ask AI to classify sources as *original / derivative / authored* and it will (a) confuse them with *primary / secondary* (which classify the *information*, a different axis), and (b) invent its own logic — unless you define the rules first. (Family Locket, 2026-03; the framework is Elizabeth Shown Mills methodology, so this is a real, checkable failure mode.)

## Inspect supplied region views before planning a locality search

For a reported region/place discrepancy, retain the provider, result date/version, exact
label and the map or geographic description actually supplied. Check the displayed extent
before comparing the label with a modern country name; unavailable map detail stays
unavailable. The [Ancestry region-description example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=59wkUV3C3iM)
supplies this observation step.

If the supplied community view includes a Timeline, preserve the selected date interval,
map and accompanying narration. Compare its place/time leads with separately documented
family locations, keeping conflicting records and alternative localities in the search
worklist. The [Journeys example](https://familytreemagazine.com/dna/why-you-should-pay-attention-to-your-ancestrydna-journeys/)
uses successive time views to suggest where to search records. Tree-fed pins and agreement
with a known line are not independent validation or confidence calibration. Treat the
display as a source of documentary search leads, not proof of a personal migration route,
birthplace, parentage or exclusion of an ancestor.

## Endogamous matches: separate prioritization from ancestral attribution

These autosomal tactics address Ashkenazi and other endogamous pedigrees; a threshold chosen for one purpose does not establish a relationship or an ancestor.

- **Check ancestry segment by segment, even for a documented cousin.** A first cousin of Diamond’s mother was related through the maternal grandfather, yet some shared 7–10 cM segments overlapped lines mapped to the maternal grandmother and others fit the known path [N13]. Keep the relationship and leave ambiguous segments unassigned. For Ashkenazi chromosome mapping, Diamond proposed a 10 cM minimum, or 15 cM to reduce misattribution at the cost of losing some valid segments. Record the chosen threshold as a mapping decision.
- **Where a parent or grandparent has tested, check each segment's side against that relative.** Treat only a documented cousin's segments that overlap the regions the focal tester shares with that relative as candidates for the documented line, and leave the rest unassigned; a side assigned to one segment does not carry over to the match's other segments [N44], [N45].
- **Under endogamy, check both sides before placing a match or reading its total.** A match can share DNA through both the father's and the mother's side, and the sharing from both raises the total. A known cousin's shared match on one side therefore does not settle the side. Before placing the match, check whether it also appears among tested parents or known relatives on the other side. If it does, record both sides as open, and do not read the combined total as one closer relationship [N85], [N86].
- **Use filtered totals to prioritize investigation, not as corrected relationship probabilities.** Keep the original total and segment list; alongside them record the longest segment and a separately labeled total after a chosen minimum-segment filter. Dyer describes 10–15 cM filters for endogamous analysis and ranking by longest segment together with total sharing. Compare which matches remain promising, without treating an excluded segment as disproved. Do not feed the filtered sum into ordinary Shared cM/WATO probabilities as an endogamy correction: filtering changes the input without supplying a matching probability model or eliminating multiple inheritance paths. [N14] (for WATO assumptions see `unknown-parentage-wato.md`).
- **Record average segment length as a labelled screening flag, not a correction.** Where a report gives total cM and segment count, divide the total by the count. Take both from the same report and filter, and store the result beside the vendor's values and the longest segment. Use it only to rank matches within one project for follow-up: under endogamy, many small segments from distant paths pull the mean down. It does not replace an available longest segment or feed Shared cM/WATO probabilities, and a high average does not rule out multiple paths [N40], [N41].
- **Keep looking when a distant path leaves a long shared segment unexplained.** One pair reported 385 cM in total and a 60 cM longest segment despite having found only a seventh-cousin path [N42]. Keep the documented path, search the other lines for a more recent shared ancestor and leave the segment’s source open.
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: contact --> Approaching a living descendant for targeted testing shares the research question and asks for their DNA; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first.
- **Look for a documented descendant whose later branch married outside the community.** Dyer relays Paul Woodbury's term *genetic pioneers*: such a branch can help distinguish the target connection from competing endogamous sharing. Select by documented descent, not a low ethnicity percentage. Weigh that advantage against generational distance and the chance the descendant inherited useful DNA; do not automatically prefer a much more distant tester. This is a targeted-testing lead, not certification of a single relationship path or restored WATO assumptions. General coverage across relatives belongs to `unknown-parentage-wato.md`; small-segment validity and pileup checks belong to `xdna-phasing-judgment.md`. [N14].
- **Locate the multi-path source before discounting.** A recent double relationship or pedigree collapse strongly raises sharing for an identifiable set of matches traceable to a specific couple. Population endogamy raises sharing broadly. Tell the two apart. Also record which ancestral line carries the endogamous origin. When only one parent's or grandparent's line is affected, matches placed on the other lines need not carry that caution, but establish the placement first. Neither label supplies a numeric correction (see the non-additivity bullet above) [N43].
- **For an Ashkenazi pair, also read the total against an endogamous reference, and keep its limit.** The Shared cM Project's ranges come from a general population. The Ashkenazi reference presented in the JewishGen talks groups shared cM for documented relationships, in separate tables by the two testers' Ashkenazi fractions (for example both fully Ashkenazi, or one fully and one half). Record which table fits the pair, read its ranges as observed sample values (see the range check under predictor outputs below), and keep the candidates from each reference labelled side by side. Do not turn its excess over the Shared cM Project into a correction factor: the submissions are self-reported and sparse at distant relationships. A population table describes general endogamy, not a specific recent collapse. Where the testers' parents or grandparents were first cousins or otherwise doubly related, model those paths in a pedigree-aware tool (non-additivity above) [N84], [N85].

### Untangle multiple paths without discarding the useful evidence

**Choose a match's role before deciding whether to include it.** A double cousin can be unsuitable for a single-path odds calculation yet useful on a chromosome map; a weak match to the focal tester can help check a stronger match's pedigree. Keep the master evidence set and make the narrower working selection explicit. For tied placements and WATO inclusion rules, use [unknown-parentage-wato](unknown-parentage-wato.md#when-two-hypotheses-survive-decide-what-would-separate-them).

1. **Map the better-resolved matches first.** Start with documented matches for which no second path is known and check phase and attribution. Overlay the double cousin’s intervals onto that map to propose a source for each overlap. Verify transmission on the same parental copy before assigning the interval [N15].
2. **Do not extend an ancestral label across the longer segment's overhang.** Even if the overlap is securely assigned to couple A, the extra interval in a double cousin need not come from A: recombination can put DNA from the other shared line immediately beside it. Keep the supported interval and the unresolved extension separate. For an unassigned interval, record the actual alternatives, such as “couple A or couple B,” without declaring that list exhaustive when other paths remain possible. Check half relationships and remarriages before choosing the labels: a convenient combined family label can accidentally include a spouse who is not an ancestor of both testers. Adjacent painted segments do not settle the gap. [N15].
3. **If subclustering stays fused, change the seed branches.** In Kotter’s Jones–Smith case the network stayed fused after three descendants through other children were added to nine doubly connected descendants [N16]. Rebuild a smaller network from the three descendants’ own shared-match lists and keep the doubly related group in the master record. Document which second connection the selection avoids, leave other paths open and keep the case unresolved until independent evidence distinguishes the families.

   **Record each clustering run's settings with its roster.** For endogamous auto-clustering, three filters can break up one giant cluster: average segment size (total ÷ count from one report), a minimum largest segment, and a minimum sharing among the shared matches themselves [N50]. Membership shifts between threshold runs [N51], so store each run's preset or thresholds and keep runs as separate views. Keep both the weighted and unweighted totals when the report supplies them [N52]. A close relative such as a half-uncle may exceed the tool's upper cM limit. Cluster that relative's shared-match list on its own rather than raising the ceiling, which tends to yield large, sparse clusters [N53]. Presets and limits are heuristics or tool defaults, and a smaller cluster is not more correct. Subclusters remain shared-match groupings that may connect through another path.
4. **Use secondary matches to check the primary match’s tree.** A weak match to the focal tester can share enough with a key cousin to help distinguish that cousin’s branches. Label the secondary match’s role and check repeated mismatches against documents for a skipped generation or descent through a different spouse [N17]. Audit which documented branch anchors fall below the clustering cutoff and which new members lack a documented place. In the Smithville analysis, known branch anchors below 100 cM were deliberately included and labelled [N18].

   **Check a match's tree one branch point at a time.** Find other tested descendants on that tree and compare each pair's shared cM with the relationship their trees imply, recording the result per branch point rather than for the whole tree. A fit is consistent with the two connecting at about that generation. It does not rule out the other relationships the same total allows, and it does not show that the named ancestor is the right person; check the names against records. The [branch-point walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9rSab4b9EI) shows the method.
5. **Use fully identical regions to test a documented double relationship or a total that fits several close relationships.** When a match is recorded as related through both parents, check whether the total and, where reported, the fully identical (IBD2) regions fit both paths. Do not explain a low total away. A fit to only one path is a reason to re-examine the documents, not proof that a link is wrong. Where one total fits half siblings, double first cousins and three-quarter siblings, the patterns differ: half siblings should show no sustained fully identical regions, double first cousins usually some, and three-quarter siblings more and often larger ones on a higher total. Get expected figures from a pedigree-aware tool (non-additivity above); pedigree collapse or endogamy can add IBD2. The [double first cousin answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmxhJsw6R-I), [three-quarter sibling explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P6dnJ2CoCXE) and [half-sibling comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g9CL2JK6aEU) set out these patterns.

The practical output is a short role table: **match → documented paths → use in this view → unresolved interval/relationship → next check**.

### Compare families across branches and generations

Use this when the question is **how two families connect**, including several related people whose placement is unresolved. The workflow works with match exports, chromosome browsers, tree software, or a connected analysis tool; no particular platform or account is required. Dyer's concrete coverage tactic is to revisit a match with a medium-length longest segment against siblings and cousins: a longer segment there can make that match a better research lead. The steps below apply that tactic together with the attribution and multiple-path limits above. (Dyer, 2023-01-05; Diamond, 2022-01-30)

1. **Build one family map before ranking pairs.** Inventory all relevant tested relatives on both sides, including tested ancestors, siblings and descendants. Record known parent links, generations and every documented path to each candidate ancestral couple. Keep one person record with its separate kits and paths: two vendor kits are two measurements of one tester; a descendant through two children of a couple belongs to both branches. Neither creates two independent confirmations. Record untested branches and unavailable comparisons so the visible subset is not mistaken for the whole family.

   Before reading matches through a couple, research the couple's collateral marriages, especially for a family that stayed in one small area for generations. Check whether husband and wife were related to each other, and whether the husband's siblings married the wife's siblings or cousins. A match descending from such a marriage can share DNA with the tester through two lines, and until those marriages are in the paper tree the second path stays invisible (Neill, Genealogy Tip of the Day, 2023-04-11 [N95]). Build complete trees for the matches as well and check them for a second shared couple (Elder, Family Locket, 2022-09-02 [N96]).

   Before deriving expected sharing or pedigree collapse, label each parent link biological, adoptive/step or unknown. A cousin path through an adoptive parent predicts no shared DNA, and a documented adoption excludes that link when counting shared biological ancestors. Keep the social relationship in the pedigree [N54]. Model independently established identical twins as one genetic person with several partners. Descendants of different twins then share like half-relatives one degree closer: second cousins are genetically like half first cousins. Keep the documentary label and record the genetic equivalent used. The equivalence applies only to identical twins and becomes harder to detect at greater distances [N55]. A sharing excess is not evidence of twinning.
2. **Keep each pair's measurements together.** Record the selected kit pair, source, result date/version, genome build, chromosome scope and known detection/reporting filters alongside autosomal total cM, segment count, largest segment and the length list when available. Keep X separate. Do not join an imported total to another kit's, filter's or older run's longest segment. Distinguish an unavailable/uncomputed comparison, an empty source-limited segment view, a computed result with no segments meeting the stated filter, and an X-only result. Unknown values stay unknown. A setting called “endogamy” does not establish the filter used for a stored result; read that result’s available filter metadata.
3. **Compare the same match across relatives.** Inspect the full segment pattern, including informative longer tracts in an older relative or sibling that are shorter or absent in the focal descendant. A descendant need not inherit that tract; absence from a display may also reflect coverage or filtering. Do not exclude a branch from that absence alone. Check that coordinates are comparable; require transmission/phase evidence before calling overlaps the same inherited tract. Until then, label the shared inheritance unresolved. Treat confirmed copies of one inherited tract in relatives as dependent evidence. A long segment or a documented cousin still does not assign it to a particular ancestral couple. Many short segments and a high total neither quantify a probability of endogamy nor rule out a real distant connection.
4. **Require a coherent placement for the related people.** Full siblings must retain the same parents; a niece/nephew's extra generation must follow the correct sibling. Reject combinations of individually high-ranked placements that violate those known links. Use a joint pedigree-aware model only if it supports the case’s endogamy, actual multiple paths and dependencies; check its documented assumptions and output. If unavailable, compare explicit family hypotheses qualitatively. Do not multiply separate pair/run probabilities, invent a long-segment bonus, shift every relationship one category outward, or impose a universal generation cutoff. A displayed largest segment is not evidence that the scorer uses it: inspect the scoring contract or component output for that analysis run and report an unavailable contribution as unknown. WATO's selection and model limits remain in `unknown-parentage-wato.md`; a family inventory does not make its assumptions hold.
5. **Preserve the baseline and test what drives the result.** In separate analysis copies, compare the full observed family, omission of one counterpart branch, and views emphasizing tested ancestors versus descendants. Keep multiply descended people labeled with every path: removing a branch must not silently turn them into single-path testers. Record the included people, kits, filters, candidate set and model for each view. These are sensitivity checks, not independent replications. Explain what changes when evidence is withheld, including lost coverage; do not pick the subset merely because it favors the preferred answer. Relative ranks or percentages from different candidate/cohort sets are not directly comparable probabilities.

Keep research priority separate from statistical support. Finish with **the leading research hypothesis, its material limitation, and the next discriminating check**; put the pair matrix and sensitivity details in a supporting artifact. Existing authorization to inspect a dataset covers the necessary bounded reads; keep hypothetical analyses separate from edits to the real tree and respect the user's stated mutation limits.

Build the comparison roster from the union of the selected relatives' own source-labelled
match shortlists, rather than only the focal tester's largest match in each descendant branch.
Deduplicate repeated person/kit entries while retaining distinct assays, which tester nominated
each candidate and unavailable pairs. Then apply the same family and pair controls above.
The [relative-shortlist example](https://familylocket.com/rlp-206-proving-a-parent-child-link-using-ancestry-thrulines-part-3/)
supplies this selection tactic; several relatives nominating one candidate do not create
independent confirmations or waive WATO's assumptions.

For a question about recurrence across the research ancestor's documented child branches, an
optional working view can list candidates nominated by testers in at least two of those
branches. Keep the full union, single-branch candidates and unavailable pairs; a candidate seen
through only one tester can still be relevant. Recurrence orders documentary checks; it
neither proves the ancestor nor counts as independent evidence. The [multi-tester comparison](https://familylocket.com/rlp-248-rlp-with-dna-10-research-logs/)
and [Dyer project](https://familylocket.com/rlp-251-florence-matilda-creer-a-favorite-photo/)
examples motivate this view.

**Check that sharing is coherent down the generations.** Sometimes a tester shares clearly more with a match than the parent or grandparent on whose side the match was placed. If so, do not keep the placement by default. First confirm that both figures share a reporting basis: sites differ in whether they phase against tested parents, and Ancestry's weighted and unweighted amounts can differ. Then look for the match in the other parent's list. If it appears there, record a possible second path without assigning the whole amount to either side. A small excess may be an anomaly, and known cousins can show the pattern. The [live answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ohRdRpBm-qY) and [other-parent example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xv62kjXVi3g) describe this check. Appearing in both lists does not identify or measure the second path.

### Fictional worked comparison

All people and measurements here are invented. Ada, Boris and untested Dorian are full siblings; Irma is their tested mother. Celia is Dorian's tested daughter. In the other family, tested Mara, Niko and Owen descend from the candidate couple Ezra–Mina through three different children. Dana is Mara and Niko's tested child and therefore reaches Ezra–Mina by two paths. Mara has two vendor kits, A and B; use A consistently below and retain B as an alternative measurement.

The five computed rows are autosomal segment lists from one fictional analysis snapshot, one genome build and a stated 7 cM reporting floor. That floor is an example setting, not a research recommendation. Coordinates are available in this invented input: Irma's 32 cM tract overlaps Ada's 14 cM and Boris's 24 cM tract; Celia's two reported tracts are elsewhere. The overlaps are not yet phased. The table is an excerpt: all other cross-family comparisons are unavailable in this fictional input and remain recorded as gaps in the full matrix.

| Selected pair | Reported segment lengths, cM | Total / count / largest | Research reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Celia–Mara A | 9, 8 | 17 / 2 / 9 | No reported tract in that interval; this alone does not exclude Dorian's branch. |

A candidate connecting Irma to Ezra–Mina must place Ada, Boris and Dorian one generation below Irma and Celia two below her. Separate “best” placements making Ada and Boris children of different people fail that constraint. Dana stays one tester with both descent paths, and Mara B adds no new descendant. Preserve all of these observations when checking branch/generation sensitivity; do not turn overlapping intervals into three independent confirmations.

**Handoff:** The Irma–Mara connection merits investigation, but its ancestral source and the connecting pedigree remain unresolved, with incomplete counterpart-branch coverage. Verify transmission of the overlapping tract using the available family kits, then use a documented counterpart branch or records that distinguish the surviving family hypotheses. This is a research priority, not a probability or proof of parentage.

**When the data are partial:** another fictional pair has an imported total of 96 cM over 12 segments, an empty imported segment view, an older report of a 16 cM longest segment, and no effective-filter metadata. Keep the imported total labeled with its stated scope; request the matching segment report and settings within the authorized read scope. Do not splice in the older 16 cM value, infer that raw segments are absent, compute a filtered total, or call the total autosomal if its chromosome scope is unknown. The handoff is “segment-based priority unresolved; obtain a comparable report.”

## Reconcile supplied relationship-predictor outputs

Keep the exact pair, provider/version, input quantity and any ages actually used with each
supplied result. Compare equivalent relationship groups: a grouped prediction and a specific
age-conditioned label have different resolution. If the relationship is independently
documented, record whether it is included and its reported probability, including omission;
otherwise report disagreement only. Omission is not biological exclusion, and related models
do not supply independent confirmations. The [predictor comparison](https://thednageek.com/the-award-for-best-relationship-predictor-goes-to/)
motivates manual output reconciliation, not a reproduced benchmark, ranking or calibration claim.

Before entering an Ancestry total into a Shared cM or other relationship-probability tool, record whether it is the weighted (Timber-adjusted) or unweighted amount. When both are available and differ, run each and keep both candidate ranges labelled by input. Flag any weighted total smaller than that match's reported longest segment as an internal inconsistency; do not treat that weighted total as a corrected figure. The [weighted-versus-unweighted comparison](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=137) is the source for this check.

When birth years are used to choose between relationships with overlapping cM, such as half-aunt versus first cousin, first identify whether each uncertain link passes through a mother or a father. Allow a much wider span of birth years on paternal links: a father's children can be born decades apart, so a paternal route can be off by more than one generation. The [presenter's age screen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o8Mc7UsnTzE) is the source for this screen. Use age as a plausibility screen only: it never establishes a placement, and it should not exclude a paternal-route option on a fixed generation length.

Before using a reported cM range to exclude a relationship, identify what the range describes:
observed sample values or an interval/limit from a named model. Retain the release, relationship
category, sample count and inclusion definition when supplied; leave unavailable definitions
unknown. A sample maximum alone does not establish a biological impossibility. Reopen an
exclusion based only on that maximum and keep the candidate unresolved while checking an
applicable model or discriminating documentary evidence. In the Barsheba Tharp study, 100 cM exceeded the reported 74 cM maximum among 89 half-fourth-cousin submissions; the researcher reopened the half relationship after checking what that sample could establish [N73].

If the supplied comparison spans a probability/model revision, preserve the dated original
outputs and separate the versions. Where available and authorized, compare the same retained
inputs under the revised version; otherwise mark earlier rows unavailable for that comparison
while retaining them in the research record. A 2023 comparison reran available MyHeritage cM Explainer entries after the probabilities changed and removed entries it could not update [N91]. Keep denominators and versions with each result.

For a close match, record segment count with total cM, both from the same kit pair and report. Within roughly first-cousin distance, more segments at the same total lean toward a route through a female line, because female meioses recombine more. Give both numbers to a sex-specific-recombination tool, such as the SegCM tool from DNA Sci that the presenters use, rather than letting the LLM estimate the route. Record the output as a tool-labelled ranking of candidate routes with its inputs, not as the side the match is on. Do not apply it to more distant relationships, and do not combine a count from a different report or filter with this total. The [demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DRQpINI26FU) and [SegCM walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tOKJ0eVgrPQ) show the method.

When a supplied estimate averages the testers’ distances to their most recent common ancestor, double it for an approximate combined path and list possible splits. An average of 4.6 suggests about nine generations in total, consistent with 2+7, 3+6 or 4+5. In one reported GEDmatch comparison, a documented 3+5 path produced a displayed estimate of 3.5 [N69]. Compare the candidates with both pedigrees and leave the split open until records distinguish it.

When comparable supplied mismatches repeatedly centre on one person, audit the entered
generation and parent/spouse paths against documents before interpreting a biological
discrepancy. Correct only a supported input error, retain the old inputs/results and correction
basis, and compare the revised version separately. Do not edit a pedigree to improve fit or
adopt a model’s SD or trial count as a universal threshold. A mismatch concentrated on one tester can be a cue to check for a skipped generation or a different spouse in that tester’s branch [N17].

When a tool supplies several generated trees ranked by odds, as in the [AutoKinship walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LnZ13VCkRfc), keep every tree with its rank, odds and run inputs, and record which documented link excludes any rejected tree. A surviving lower-ranked tree is a research hypothesis, not a confirmed placement.

Before interpreting a supplied two-tester relationship view, check that its stated input
contract fits the proposed pedigree. The [Shared cM example](https://www.yourdnaguide.com/ydgblog/shared-cm-project)
requires both testers to have the same genealogical relationship and generational offset to
the mystery person. Retain each actual pair measurement, including a reported zero distinct
from unavailable or below-floor data. A zero-to-one plotting workaround belongs only in a
labelled display copy; it cannot replace the measured zero in evidence or likelihood inputs.
This input check supplies neither a joint probability model nor a biological exclusion.

## Read ethnicity estimates as dated, version-bound observations

Treat each ethnicity readout as one kit measured under one dated model. Record the provider, whether the kit is native or uploaded from another vendor, the algorithm version, any active confidence setting and the provider's own region labels. A native test and an upload of the same person can differ on one platform, and a version or setting change is not new ancestry evidence. Before calling two readouts discordant, map nested or differently named regions onto each other. Phrase results as DNA shared with a reference population, not an ancestral country. When an update says a category 'split into' new regions, keep old and new values as separate observations; do not redistribute the old percentage arithmetically. The [upload-versus-native comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hw1YmGKAwOw), [cross-provider label comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=alx8-1I7Ato) and [version-change review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_Qzi6THU1jM) motivate this.

**Keep the displayed range with the headline percentage.** Where a provider shows a range for a region, record it beside the most-likely value from the same result version. Also note any segment the chromosome view leaves unassigned. A range reaching zero means that version's model does not rule out the region being absent. Before calling it impossible for a child to show a larger share than the transmitting parent, compare the two ranges. If the same person has a second kit on that platform, record its score and range as a sign of run-to-run variation. Point differences inside wide, overlapping ranges stay unresolved; they do not by themselves show an inheritance error or hidden ancestry. A region missing from the list is not a measured zero, because small components may be hidden. The [range-comparison report](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=852), [region-range discussion](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=76) and [duplicate-kit comparison](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=954) are user reports.

**Check the parent-side estimate against tested relatives.** A per-parent ethnicity view estimates the half transmitted to the child [N27]. Keep ‘parent 1’ and ‘parent 2’ unassigned until relatives identify the sides. Compare each region with each tested parent’s or grandparent’s own result at the same provider and record where the labels disagree [N23], [N24], [N26]. In a repeat test by the same person, the overall estimates were similar but the estimated division between parents changed [N22]. Agreement among full siblings or overlap with a side-specific relative adds support without establishing phase [N25].

**Use a confirmed parent's estimate as an arithmetic control, not an origin finding.** This applies where parent and child have results from the same provider and model version, and parentage is established by relationship-specific DNA (see the 'Ethnicity estimates as conclusions' bullet above). The child carries about half the parent's genome. A category at P% in the parent therefore implies roughly at least P − 50% in the child. A child or sibling far below that floor shows that one or both estimates are inconsistent. It does not identify which estimate is wrong, give true proportions or bear on parentage. Small shortfalls are within approximation. The [parent-child MyOrigins comparison](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=146) and [sibling floor calculation](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=140) motivate this check.

Where a grandparent has also tested, run the painting check interval by interval. Use the grandparent–grandchild one-to-one segments to mark which intervals of the child's copy from the intermediate parent came from that grandparent. Then compare the child's painted label on each interval with the label painted on the same grandparent-side copy of the parent. In one family comparison, an interval labelled France in the parent was painted Northern Italy in the daughter [N62]. Flag a transmitted interval if the labels disagree or neither parental copy carries the child’s label. Record each mismatch as a painting or phasing inconsistency, not new ancestry. The parent's copy labels are themselves inferred, so a mismatch does not show which label is right.

**Keep community and Journey assignments apart from region percentages and from matches' communities.** Label each observation with the product that produced it. A Journey for a place whose region percentage is missing, or the reverse, is not by itself a contradiction. A list of communities common among the closest matches describes those matches; do not add it to the tester's own result. Before relying on a community parent tab, compare it with tested parents or children and with close matches documented on each side. Record any community a child holds that neither tested parent shows. Save each dated assignment set: a community dropped or renamed in an update may reflect changed criteria, not evidence against the paper trail. The [parent-split discussion](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=519), [UK community update thread](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=661) and [Journeys-versus-regions discussion](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=1629) report such anomalies. Neither product confirms the other or establishes an origin.

**Read an unexpected community assignment as two hypotheses.** Community, group and recent-location assignments rest on DNA shared with other testers, though not in the same way: AncestryDNA builds communities from the network of matches among its testers, while MyHeritage defines Genetic Groups from reference kits of known origin (for 23andMe's displays, see recipe 7 of [23andme-match-research](23andme-match-research.md)). Either way, an unexpected assignment can come from relatives who left the region, by emigration or forced removal: a tester in the origin country may be grouped with those relatives' descendants abroad although the tester's own ancestors never lived there. It can also come from misattributed recent ancestry. Keep both open. Where the provider lets you list the matches who share the assignment, check their documented lines. Route any parentage question to relationship-specific DNA evidence (see the 'Ethnicity estimates as conclusions' bullet above). The [admixture introduction](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/introduction-to-genetic-admixture) names both hypotheses; the [communities white paper](https://www.ancestrycdn.com/support/us/2024/02/communitieswhitepaper2024.pdf) and [Genetic Groups page](https://education.myheritage.com/article/genetic-groups-on-myheritage/) describe the two providers' methods.

**Trace a region through matches, not the label or percentage.** An expected population missing from an estimate does not disprove it, and no estimate establishes descent from a tribe or community; documents do. Look for matches with substantial ancestry from the region whose trees converge on one couple, or who also match a documented cousin. That shared couple's branch becomes the research lead. A trace (~1%) region with no such cluster may simply be wrong. Absence among one line's relatives is only a soft clue. Mark branches without placed matches as untested. Recurrence among related cousins is dependent evidence, and under endogamy shared matches may point to several lines. Suggest Y-DNA or mtDNA only for an all-male or all-female path. The [shared-cousin example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t3D0iReQG_s), [region-sharing example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e3ixxQev7YQ), [trace-ancestry answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SBtWjTCpPpo), [relatives check](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3fuhnRtg5I), [sibling-line screen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7IdlI59m8YI) and [negative-evidence caution](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vtICddQnrbU) motivate this.

**Find matches on the painted interval.** A match’s ancestry breakdown describes the person. It cannot identify the source of the DNA shared with you or exclude a parental side [N69]. When a provider supplies coordinates for a small region, record its build and find matches overlapping that interval on a compatible build [N68]. Compare their documented ancestries. Common ancestry in one region gives a lead, while overlap alone leaves the parental copy and the transmitting ancestor open, especially under endogamy.

Do not turn a small ethnicity percentage into one ancestor at one generation. First list the alternatives: misassignment from a closely related reference population, a false positive, and several distant contributors whose shares add up. If one recent source is plausible, keep a generation range. For multi-ancestor hypotheses, bound the total by adding each ancestor's low and high inheritance values, not averages. A 2020 review of a 23andMe timeline reads its 'most recent ancestor' generation as a wide date window resting on a fixed generation length; check the current display. The output only chooses which pedigree generations to complete; it places no ancestor. The [multiple-ancestor ranges](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gThnugZcEUc), [3% answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MlsU45miWKA), [6% example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CPkieGCVhd0) and [timeline critique](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-mZSGxxs5-0) motivate this.

**Compare the locations and versions of painted regions.** Record the provider, version, label, coordinates and reported range for the same interval across releases, providers or tested relatives [N61], [N62]. Flag a lower bound of zero and assignments spanning a whole or half chromosome. One contributor suggested smoothing after a whole chromosome was labelled Cornwall [N65]. Leave the mechanism open and check the region against matches and documents before attributing a subgroup or an ancestral home.

## Prompt-craft that practitioners found works (the reusable core)

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> Screenshots, transcriptions, family maps and pair matrices given to an AI can carry living testers' names and DNA data; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first.

1. **Provide complete inputs, don't expect inference.** Give the citation fields / the transcription / the screenshot — not "here's a link, cite it."
2. **Supply the citation template.** Give the model the required fields in order, instruct it to apply that template to the supplied record, then check the output for omitted fields and invented values [N2].
3. **Prompt-chaining for complex concepts.** Teach it bottom-up: define the terms first (what *is* original vs derivative vs authored), *then* ask it to classify. [N3].
4. **Define the rules or it invents them.** Any domain logic (source analysis, grouping criteria) must be stated explicitly first.
5. **Keep the working data outside the chat.** If the conversation reaches a context limit, start a fresh chat with the current roster, rules and completed work [N4].
6. **Specificity drives quality.** The more precise the ask and the format, the better the output.
7. **Separate an endogamous match's research priority from its segment assignments.** Request original and filtered metrics with the filter stated, the documented relationship, and which individual segments remain unassigned.
8. **For a family connection, supply both family maps and the pair matrix.** Ask for placements that preserve the known relationships, dependent observations, missing comparisons, sensitivity to branch/generation coverage, and one next discriminating check. Do not request a separate best parent for each sibling or an AI-computed combined probability.
9. **For a multiply related match, ask for interval and role decisions.** Separate supported overlap from unresolved overhang; name the actual alternative ancestral paths; distinguish a cluster seed, a pedigree-validation helper and a scored focal match. Require a reason for each working inclusion or omission.

## Worked mini-examples (workflow logic, not clicks)

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool, export --> These workflows export match cM and segment data to files and hand match screenshots, CSVs and GEDmatch coordinates to AI tools and DNA Painter; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first.

- **Leeds chart** — AI builds the match table (names, cM, tentative relationship) from Ancestry screenshots; *you* do the color-coding. The Leeds Method uses matches sharing **90–400 cM** to separate the four grandparent groups. The 400 cM ceiling is meant to **exclude first cousins** (who tie to two grandparents); still exclude any *known* match who shares two grandparents independently, rather than trusting the cap. The human-judgment step is spotting the **removed first-cousin descendants (1C1R, 1C2R)** that fall *inside* the 90–400 window but connect to two lines and skew the four-way split — **second cousins are the foundation you keep, not exclude.** AI front-ends; human judges. (Leeds Method = Dana Leeds, July 2018; 90–400 cM — *verified*.)
- **A match wired into two color groups at once** — the same Leeds chart (or any ICW cluster) occasionally shows one match tangled into two of the four grandparent colors, because it may be related to the target through both lines at once (a shared ancestral couple reached two ways, or a second marriage; see the bridging-match bullet above for the other explanations). Don't have the AI silently pick a side or average the two. Pull that one match out, re-cluster on the rest, and reinstate it **flagged as belonging to both lines** once you've worked out — from the tree, not the cM — the two paths that put it there; where segment data exists, [evaluate each interval separately](#untangle-multiple-paths-without-discarding-the-useful-evidence) and retain unresolved ancestral assignments. (Family Locket, RLP 225, 2022-10-31; Dana Leeds, 2018-08-23)
- **DNA source citations** — build reusable templates (who/what/where/when + the match's cM & segment fields) → export CSV → hand to AI with "use these exact fields." Then **check every output** for missing fields. (Family Locket, 2025-02)
- **Painting a GEDmatch match** — AI helps translate GEDmatch one-to-one coordinates into DNA Painter segments; the *judgment* step it can't own is assigning the segment to the **paternal vs maternal** side and the right ancestral couple — get that wrong and the whole chromosome map is corrupted. (Family Locket, 2025-04)

## The pitfalls worth internalizing (the least-obvious, most-LLM-usable part)

- Confidently wrong on dates/names/places, and it may *argue* when corrected.
- Loses track across a long multi-turn session.
- Treats a DNA-estimated relationship as the real one; ignores endogamy inflating cM.
- Conflates *original/derivative/authored* (source type) with *primary/secondary* (information type).
- Fabricates citations/records when it lacks the underlying fields.
- Silently resolves an ambiguous compound relationship phrase. A chain such as "my maternal paternal great-grandfather" can be parsed starting from different parents. Before computing a relationship or expected sharing, state which reading the analysis assumes. Where the readings diverge, draw or describe each one and ask the user to confirm. The [presenter's double-relationship walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BCvednIm-24) names this ambiguity. Do not choose the reading that best fits the DNA.
- Assigns a match to a line because its tree carries one of the user's surnames or places, even a rare one. The shared DNA can come through a different common couple. Keep the search hit as a lead, check whether the match also shares with documented cousins on that line before attributing it, and treat that overlap as support, not proof. The [match-list search caution](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/searching-sorting-and-filtering-dna-match-lists) names this confirmation bias.

## Verification & privacy discipline

- Every AI-produced claim → **verify against the relevant evidence** — prefer *original* records and *primary* information where available, and correlate independent sources — before it enters a tree or report. Use AI to *think faster*, never to *decide*.
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> Pasting anyone's DNA or match data into an AI or other external tool moves living people's data; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first. **De-identify before you share data with any third-party tool, not just before publishing.** Strip direct identifiers before pasting a third party's DNA or match list into a cloud AI (or any external tool) — though raw DNA is inherently re-identifiable, so this is *pseudonymization*, not true anonymization. **Get the person's consent before processing their genetic data — make it a default, not a step you weigh** (it's the community norm, and legally required in many places). De-identify living individuals in anything public as well.

Inspect the actual calculation, table or screenshot intended for the recipient: masking names
in a tree view need not mask generated output. Keep the private original and the separately
redacted derivative distinct. The [dated Banyan output example](https://www.yourdnaguide.com/ydgblog/banyandna)
supplies this verification trigger; it does not validate current controls or anonymity.

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: contact --> Consent to analyze a match does not cover
naming that person in a shared or published report; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md`
before asking for that permission. When the researcher asks, state the documented or
hypothesized connection with its uncertainty, how the name or data will appear and where the report will be shared; leave out
sensitive findings about other people, such as unexpected parentage. Offer an excerpt limited
to that person's own data or a redacted preview. Record the actual response and any limits
with that report version. No reply is not permission: anonymize or omit instead. The [permission-request example](https://familylocket.com/rlp-250-rlp-with-dna-12-whats-next/)
supplies this preparation step; it does not settle legal sufficiency or guarantee anonymity.
