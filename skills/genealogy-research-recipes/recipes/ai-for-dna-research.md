# Recipe: Using AI reliably in DNA genealogy research

## Goal

How an AI assistant (or a genealogist using one) can apply AI to DNA/genealogy work **reliably** — what to delegate freely, what to never trust, and the prompt patterns that actually work.

## The mental model (get this right or everything downstream breaks)

AI is a **language-prediction system, not a reasoning authority** — it produces *plausible* output, which is not the same as *correct* output, and it has no access to records it hasn't been given (or can't retrieve). Therefore: **every AI output is a hypothesis to verify, never a fact to file.** "AI lies" is the wrong frame — it's non-intentional; it confidently fills gaps. (Denyse Allen, 2026-01)

## What to delegate freely (AI is genuinely good at these)

- **Restructuring** scattered notes / transcriptions into tables, outlines, timelines, drafts.
- **Transcription** of documents — genuinely useful, but accuracy depends on the handwriting, language, script, and image quality (and the model); *correct-and-verify* against the image, never trust a transcription blind. (Denyse Allen)
- **Assembling citations from complete inputs** you supply (see prompt-craft). (Family Locket, 2025-02)
- **Brainstorming research strategy**, historical/legal context, and *draft* proof-argument prose. (Denyse Allen; Family Locket)
- **Surfacing hypotheses** — comparing Ancestry vs 23andMe reference panels, or cross-referencing tree surnames against ethnicity regions, to *raise* patterns and anomalies **to investigate** (not to conclude — see the ethnicity never-trust). (Family Locket, 2026-03)
- **Front-ending mechanical prep** — e.g. turning Ancestry match screenshots into a structured cM/relationship table to start a Leeds chart. (Family Locket, 2025-02)

## What to never trust or delegate (verify, or don't)

- **Record retrieval / existence.** A bare LLM (no verified retrieval/browsing) cannot fetch an actual birth record or confirm a source exists; if pushed, it *invents* one. A tool-enabled assistant that *can* browse may surface a real catalog entry — but treat anything it returns as unverified until you inspect the actual record. Supplying the record makes it genuinely useful, but verify the output against the source even then. (Denyse Allen)
- **Citations from a bare link.** Citation format depends on source-specific fields (author, source type, publication, access) that a URL alone doesn't carry — supply the fields or the citation will be wrong. (Family Locket & Denyse Allen)
- **cM → relationship odds.** Never ask the LLM to compute relationship probabilities from a cM number — it *fabricates* them. Route a *single* cM figure to the **Shared cM Project / DNA Painter's Shared cM Tool** (point cM → the *range* of candidate relationships); if you have a tree plus multiple matches to place, that's **WATO** — a *different* DNA Painter tool that scores hypothesis slots, not a cM→relationship lookup. Either way, always ask for **all** candidate relationships, never a single point answer. (Half vs full relationships are frequently indistinguishable by cM alone.)
- **cM as closeness under endogamy.** Endogamy / pedigree collapse **inflates** shared cM, so the same cM implies a closer relationship than reality — the AI has no idea your lines are endogamous unless you tell it, and even then it can't quantify the correction. **Never let an AI add cM across relationship paths as though the paths were independent** — this is **non-additivity**: when a match descends from the same ancestral couple through more than one path (pedigree collapse), the realized shared DNA is the **union** of the paths' identical-by-descent (IBD) regions, so its expected once-counted cM is **at least the strongest single path's expected (mean) figure and at most — never above — the naive sum**. Those regions can overlap and aren't guaranteed disjoint, so the sum is only an *upper bound*, not the expectation, unless disjointness has been established — independence does not license addition. (This is *realized* shared cM; Wright-style **kinship coefficients** do sum valid path-pair contributions with the proper inbreeding corrections, but that's a different quantity — don't convert it to cM without modelling IBD1/IBD2 and the reporting convention.) For an actual figure, route it to a **pedigree-aware kinship / IBD-simulation tool** that models identity states and the tool's IBD-counting convention explicitly, rather than hand-combining path estimates. Treat any apparent "ceiling" on shared DNA as holding only within a *named* calculation model — expected shared cM under one testing tool's cM map / IBD-counting convention, not a biological constant — and keep the **haploid** autosomal map length distinct from the **diploid** amount that can actually be identical-by-descent; read any bound against that tool's IBD1/IBD2 counting convention, because IBD2-capable relationships (full siblings' fully-identical regions, identical twins) and differing vendor conventions defeat a single "two people can never share more than X cM" rule of thumb. (Estes, 2024-03-28)
- **Auto-clustering swallowing genealogically distinct lines.** In a shared-match / in-common-with (ICW) network, a match related to the target through **more than one line** links two otherwise-distinct lineages, so a clustering algorithm merges two real family clusters into one — a genuine bridging edge, not noise. Never trust an AI's auto-clustering output blindly; when two clusters look suspiciously fused, the standard fixes are to **raise the cM threshold, sub-cluster, and manually resolve the multi-relationship match** — optionally excluding that one match and re-clustering on single-line matches only. Treat exclude-and-recluster as a *diagnostic* step, not an automatic delete: dropping a real match loses real signal, and forcing it into a single line repeats the same clustering error — reinstate it **labeled as the multi-line match it genuinely is**, once you've identified **both** relationship paths, and where segment data exists, [evaluate each interval separately](#untangle-multiple-paths-without-discarding-the-useful-evidence) and retain unresolved ancestral assignments. (Family Locket, RLP 225, 2022-10-31)
- **Ethnicity estimates as conclusions.** They're probabilistic and re-versioned by vendors; let AI surface *hypotheses* about admixture/migration, never conclude an ancestral origin. (Family Locket, 2026-03) **Never let an AI flag an ethnicity-% discordance as an NPE, and never let it declare "no NPE" from ethnicity % either** — the same caution cuts **both ways** on parentage: ethnicity percentages alone cannot diagnose an NPE, or a lab problem, in either direction, so a parent/child regional-percentage discordance is **not by itself** evidence of a non-paternity event (NPE), nor **by itself** proof that no such event occurred. A discordance has two legitimate co-existing sources with **no universal ordering between them** — don't let an AI rank one as "primary" (a fixed "it's mostly estimator noise" framing teaches it to dismiss a real discrepancy): (a) the percentages are **model-dependent statistical estimates** (reference panel, category boundaries, algorithm version) that vendors revise over time, and (b) **real biology** — a child inherits only a recombined half of each parent's autosome, so regional proportions are genuinely **not conserved** across a generation even with a perfect estimator. Route the parentage question instead to relationship-specific DNA evidence — not total shared cM alone (total cM does not uniquely separate all close relationships — parent-child and full-sibling totals overlap — and cannot establish sample identity), but **total cM plus chromosome coverage plus the IBD1/IBD2 (FIR/HIR) pattern**, evaluated against the competing close-relationship alternatives, reserving any "confirms/excludes parentage" conclusion for **appropriately identified samples** — and, where it matters, a purpose-built chain-of-custody parentage test. (Estes, 2017-07-02; Ancestry Support; MyHeritage Help, 2025-11-16)
- **A kit from a transplant recipient.** Before reading unexpected matches, ethnicity or predicted relationships as misattributed parentage or a sample swap, ask whether the tester has had a bone-marrow or stem-cell transplant. The [transplant remark](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1rjrcHOGygI) notes that a consumer kit from such a tester may resemble the donor's DNA. Flag the result as possibly not the tester's own genome. The source does not establish which sample types or tissues are affected, so do not recommend a substitute sample from it, and do not use the explanation to dismiss a discrepancy without asking.
- **DNA-estimated relationships as fact.** A tool's "estimated relationship" is a cM-based guess and routinely differs from the real one — AI parroting it will mis-group matches. (Family Locket, Leeds video)
- **Final evidentiary judgment.** Proof validation is human work; AI drafts, you rule. (Denyse Allen)
- **Source-type classification without explicit rules.** Ask AI to classify sources as *original / derivative / authored* and it will (a) confuse them with *primary / secondary* (which classify the *information*, a different axis), and (b) invent its own logic — unless you define the rules first. (Family Locket, 2026-03; the framework is Elizabeth Shown Mills methodology, so this is a real, checkable failure mode.)

## Endogamous matches: separate prioritization from ancestral attribution

These autosomal tactics address Ashkenazi and other endogamous pedigrees; a threshold chosen for one purpose does not establish a relationship or an ancestor.

- **A documented cousin does not identify the source of every shared segment.** In Lara Diamond's example, her mother's first cousin connected through the maternal grandfather, yet some shared 7–10 cM segments overlapped the maternal grandmother's mapped lines. Other small segments agreed with the documented connection. Preserve the known relationship, but leave ambiguous segments unassigned instead of painting every overlap onto that couple. For Ashkenazi chromosome mapping, Diamond recommends a 10 cM minimum and describes 15 cM as reducing misattribution at the cost of losing some valid segments. These are her mapping heuristics, not universal detection thresholds or proof that a retained segment came from the named couple. (Diamond, 2022-01-30)
- **Where a parent or grandparent has tested, check each segment's side against that relative.** Treat only a documented cousin's segments that overlap the regions the focal tester shares with that relative as candidates for the documented line, and leave the rest unassigned; a side assigned to one segment does not carry over to the match's other segments. The [grandparent overlay example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JKa92d6na5c) and [multi-segment answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u0_x0Ce46Gs) motivate this check without validating further assignments.
- **Use filtered totals to prioritize investigation, not as corrected relationship probabilities.** Keep the original total and segment list; alongside them record the longest segment and a separately labeled total after a chosen minimum-segment filter. Dyer describes 10–15 cM filters for endogamous analysis and ranking by longest segment together with total sharing. Compare which matches remain promising, without treating an excluded segment as disproved. Do not feed the filtered sum into ordinary Shared cM/WATO probabilities as an endogamy correction: filtering changes the input without supplying a matching probability model or eliminating multiple inheritance paths. (Dyer, 2023-01-05; WATO assumptions: `unknown-parentage-wato.md`)
- **Record average segment length as a labelled screening flag, not a correction.** Where a report gives total cM and segment count, divide the total by the count. Take both from the same report and filter, and store the result beside the vendor's values and the longest segment. Use it only to rank matches within one project for follow-up: under endogamy, many small segments from distant paths pull the mean down. It does not measure inflation or establish endogamy. It does not replace an available longest segment or feed Shared cM/WATO probabilities. A high average does not rule out multiple paths. The [presenter's 10 cM rule of thumb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k88TGwqAJlU) and the [average-segment answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkWh21CIHes) give unvalidated project cut-offs, not general thresholds.
- **A found distant path does not explain a long segment.** Suppose a tree search finds a distant documented connection to a match whose longest segment is long. Keep searching for a more recent shared ancestor rather than letting 'endogamy' explain away the difference. Endogamy can raise totals and occasionally preserve a long segment, so this is a research priority, not an exclusion of the distant path or proof of a closer one. The [presenter's example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MJu82Zub5Ag) (385 cM, 60 cM longest, a seventh-cousin path) motivates this step. His endogamy-severity ranking and cM levels come from his own experience and are not validated figures.
- **Look for a documented descendant whose later branch married outside the community.** Dyer relays Paul Woodbury's term *genetic pioneers*: such a branch can help distinguish the target connection from competing endogamous sharing. Select by documented descent, not a low ethnicity percentage. Weigh that advantage against generational distance and the chance the descendant inherited useful DNA; do not automatically prefer a much more distant tester. This is a targeted-testing lead, not certification of a single relationship path or restored WATO assumptions. General coverage across relatives belongs to `unknown-parentage-wato.md`; small-segment validity and pileup checks belong to `xdna-phasing-judgment.md`. (Dyer, 2023-01-05)
- **Locate the multi-path source before discounting.** A recent double relationship or pedigree collapse strongly raises sharing for an identifiable set of matches traceable to a specific couple. Population endogamy raises sharing broadly. Tell the two apart. Also record which ancestral line carries the endogamous origin. When only one parent's or grandparent's line is affected, matches placed on the other lines need not carry that caution, but establish the placement first. Neither label supplies a numeric correction (see the non-additivity bullet above). The [presenter's distinction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-oxvx5DrcmQ) is qualitative, with no thresholds or worked case.

### Untangle multiple paths without discarding the useful evidence

**Choose a match's role before deciding whether to include it.** A double cousin can be unsuitable for a single-path odds calculation yet useful on a chromosome map; a weak match to the focal tester can help check a stronger match's pedigree. Keep the master evidence set and make the narrower working selection explicit. For tied placements and WATO inclusion rules, use [unknown-parentage-wato](unknown-parentage-wato.md#when-two-hypotheses-survive-decide-what-would-separate-them).

1. **Map the better-resolved matches first; overlay the multiply related ones afterward.** Start with documented matches for which no second path is known, retaining the usual phase and attribution checks. Dyer's double-cousin example then uses those earlier assignments to propose origins for individual overlapping intervals. An overlap generates an assignment hypothesis; it does not establish transmission on the same parental copy or prove the ancestral source. (Dyer, 2022-09-24)
2. **Do not extend an ancestral label across the longer segment's overhang.** Even if the overlap is securely assigned to couple A, the extra interval in a double cousin need not come from A: recombination can put DNA from the other shared line immediately beside it. Keep the supported interval and the unresolved extension separate. For an unassigned interval, record the actual alternatives, such as “couple A or couple B,” without declaring that list exhaustive when other paths remain possible. Check half relationships and remarriages before choosing the labels: a convenient combined family label can accidentally include a spouse who is not an ancestor of both testers. Adjacent painted segments do not settle the gap. (Dyer, 2022-09-24)
3. **If subclustering stays fused, change the seed branches.** Build descendancy through other children of the target ancestor and seek matches without the identified second connection. Construct a smaller shared-match network starting from those matches. In Kotter's case, nine known descendants all carried the same Jones–Smith complication; adding three alternate-child descendants to the large network still left it fused. Starting again from those three descendants' shared-match lists made the narrower investigation usable. Keep the doubly related group in the full record. “No identified second connection” is a selection rationale, not proof of statistical independence. The case was still unresolved. (Kotter, 2022-10-01)

   **Record each clustering run's settings with its roster.** For endogamous auto-clustering, three filters can break up one giant cluster: average segment size (total ÷ count from one report), a minimum largest segment, and a minimum sharing among the shared matches themselves ([endogamy clustering walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x-eIdnER5uk)). Membership shifts between threshold runs ([threshold demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lTboZ5q10Nk)), so store each run's preset or thresholds and keep runs as separate views. Where a vendor reports [weighted and unweighted totals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R0Vfw_RjKzM), keep both. A close relative such as a half-uncle may exceed the tool's upper cM limit. Cluster that relative's shared-match list on its own rather than raising the ceiling, which tends to yield large, sparse clusters ([AutoKinship Q&A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LnZ13VCkRfc)). Presets and limits are heuristics or tool defaults, and a smaller cluster is not more correct. Subclusters remain shared-match groupings that may connect through another path.
4. **Use secondary matches to check the primary match's tree.** A person sharing little DNA with the focal tester but much more with a key cousin may help distinguish that cousin's maternal and paternal branches. Give the person the role “validate cousin's pedigree,” rather than demanding strong focal sharing or counting them as another confirmation of the focal hypothesis. In *Power-Up Your DNA Analyses*, comparisons among testers exposed a skipped generation and descent through a different wife. Check such patterns against documents and other pairs before changing a tree; one unusual pair is not a pedigree verdict. In *Struggles with Smithville*, the author also retained documented branch anchors below the chosen clustering cutoff. Audit which known anchors an automated cluster omits and which new members lack a documented place. (The DNA Geek, 2024-07-01; 2025-10-30)
5. **Use fully identical regions to test a documented double relationship or a total that fits several close relationships.** When a match is recorded as related through both parents, check whether the total and, where reported, the fully identical (IBD2) regions fit both paths. Do not explain a low total away. A fit to only one path is a reason to re-examine the documents, not proof that a link is wrong. Where one total fits half siblings, double first cousins and three-quarter siblings, the patterns differ: half siblings should show no sustained fully identical regions, double first cousins usually some, and three-quarter siblings more and often larger ones on a higher total. Get expected figures from a pedigree-aware tool (non-additivity above); pedigree collapse or endogamy can add IBD2. The [double first cousin answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmxhJsw6R-I), [three-quarter sibling explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P6dnJ2CoCXE) and [half-sibling comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g9CL2JK6aEU) give no thresholds. This is a screen against documents, not a verdict.

The practical output is a short role table: **match → documented paths → use in this view → unresolved interval/relationship → next check**.

### Compare families across branches and generations

Use this when the question is **how two families connect**, including several related people whose placement is unresolved. The workflow works with match exports, chromosome browsers, tree software, or a connected analysis tool; no particular platform or account is required. Dyer's concrete coverage tactic is to revisit a match with a medium-length longest segment against siblings and cousins: a longer segment there can make that match a better research lead. The steps below apply that tactic together with the attribution and multiple-path limits above. (Dyer, 2023-01-05; Diamond, 2022-01-30)

1. **Build one family map before ranking pairs.** Inventory all relevant tested relatives on both sides, including tested ancestors, siblings and descendants. Record known parent links, generations and every documented path to each candidate ancestral couple. Keep one person record with its separate kits and paths: two vendor kits are two measurements of one tester; a descendant through two children of a couple belongs to both branches. Neither creates two independent confirmations. Record untested branches and unavailable comparisons so the visible subset is not mistaken for the whole family.

   Before deriving expected sharing or pedigree collapse, label each parent link biological, adoptive/step or unknown. A cousin path through an adoptive parent predicts no shared DNA, and spouses related only through an adoption add no collapse ([Tanner's adoption example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KmUK-Xpx55o) reports no DNA comparison, so a documented adoption removes an expected path; it is not DNA evidence). Model independently established identical twins as one genetic person with several partners. Descendants of different twins then share like half-relatives one degree closer: second cousins are genetically like half first cousins. Keep the documentary label and record the genetic equivalent used. The [twin example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH9B2ySZLUA) concerns identical twins only, and the presenter notes the effect becomes hard to see at more distant levels. A sharing excess is not evidence of twinning.
2. **Keep each pair's measurements together.** Record the selected kit pair, source, result date/version, genome build, chromosome scope and known detection/reporting filters alongside autosomal total cM, segment count, largest segment and the length list when available. Keep X separate. Do not join an imported total to another kit's, filter's or older run's longest segment. Distinguish an unavailable/uncomputed comparison, an empty source-limited segment view, a computed result with no segments meeting the stated filter, and an X-only result. Unknown values stay unknown. A setting called “endogamy” does not establish the filter used for a stored result; read that result’s available filter metadata.
3. **Compare the same match across relatives.** Inspect the full segment pattern, including informative longer tracts in an older relative or sibling that are shorter or absent in the focal descendant. A descendant need not inherit that tract; absence from a display may also reflect coverage or filtering. Do not exclude a branch from that absence alone. Check that coordinates are comparable; require transmission/phase evidence before calling overlaps the same inherited tract. Until then, label the shared inheritance unresolved. Treat confirmed copies of one inherited tract in relatives as dependent evidence. A long segment or a documented cousin still does not assign it to a particular ancestral couple. Many short segments and a high total neither quantify a probability of endogamy nor rule out a real distant connection.
4. **Require a coherent placement for the related people.** Full siblings must retain the same parents; a niece/nephew's extra generation must follow the correct sibling. Reject combinations of individually high-ranked placements that violate those known links. Use a joint pedigree-aware model only if it supports the case’s endogamy, actual multiple paths and dependencies; check its documented assumptions and output. If unavailable, compare explicit family hypotheses qualitatively. Do not multiply separate pair/run probabilities, invent a long-segment bonus, shift every relationship one category outward, or impose a universal generation cutoff. A displayed largest segment is not evidence that the scorer uses it: inspect the scoring contract or component output for that analysis run and report an unavailable contribution as unknown. WATO's selection and model limits remain in `unknown-parentage-wato.md`; a family inventory does not make its assumptions hold.
5. **Preserve the baseline and test what drives the result.** In separate analysis copies, compare the full observed family, omission of one counterpart branch, and views emphasizing tested ancestors versus descendants. Keep multiply descended people labeled with every path: removing a branch must not silently turn them into single-path testers. Record the included people, kits, filters, candidate set and model for each view. These are sensitivity checks, not independent replications. Explain what changes when evidence is withheld, including lost coverage; do not pick the subset merely because it favors the preferred answer. Relative ranks or percentages from different candidate/cohort sets are not directly comparable probabilities.

Keep research priority separate from statistical support. Finish with **the leading research hypothesis, its material limitation, and the next discriminating check**; put the pair matrix and sensitivity details in a supporting artifact. Existing authorization to inspect a dataset covers the necessary bounded reads; keep hypothetical analyses separate from edits to the real tree and respect the user's stated mutation limits.

Build the comparison roster from the union of the selected relatives' own source-labelled
match shortlists, rather than only the focal tester's largest match in each descendant branch.
Deduplicate repeated person/kit entries while retaining distinct assays, which tester nominated
each candidate and unavailable pairs. Then apply the same family and pair controls above.
The [relative-shortlist example](https://familylocket.com/rlp-206-proving-a-parent-child-link-using-ancestry-thrulines-part-3/)
supplies this selection tactic; several relatives nominating one candidate do not create
independent confirmations or waive WATO's assumptions.

For a question about recurrence across the research ancestor's documented child branches, an
optional working view can list candidates nominated by testers in at least two of those
branches. Keep the full union, single-branch candidates and unavailable pairs; a candidate seen
through only one tester can still be relevant. Recurrence orders documentary checks; it
neither proves the ancestor nor counts as independent evidence. The [multi-tester comparison](https://familylocket.com/rlp-248-rlp-with-dna-10-research-logs/)
and [Dyer project](https://familylocket.com/rlp-251-florence-matilda-creer-a-favorite-photo/)
examples motivate this view.

**Check that sharing is coherent down the generations.** Sometimes a tester shares clearly more with a match than the parent or grandparent on whose side the match was placed. If so, do not keep the placement by default. First confirm that both figures share a reporting basis: sites differ in whether they phase against tested parents, and Ancestry's weighted and unweighted amounts can differ. Then look for the match in the other parent's list. If it appears there, record a possible second path without assigning the whole amount to either side. A small excess may be an anomaly, and known cousins can show the pattern. Neither the [live answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ohRdRpBm-qY) nor the [other-parent example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xv62kjXVi3g) gives a threshold. Appearing in both lists neither identifies nor measures the second path, and the pattern never shows the match is unrelated.

### Fictional worked comparison

All people and measurements here are invented. Ada, Boris and untested Dorian are full siblings; Irma is their tested mother. Celia is Dorian's tested daughter. In the other family, tested Mara, Niko and Owen descend from the candidate couple Ezra–Mina through three different children. Dana is Mara and Niko's tested child and therefore reaches Ezra–Mina by two paths. Mara has two vendor kits, A and B; use A consistently below and retain B as an alternative measurement.

The five computed rows are autosomal segment lists from one fictional analysis snapshot, one genome build and a stated 7 cM reporting floor. That floor is an example setting, not a research recommendation. Coordinates are available in this invented input: Irma's 32 cM tract overlaps Ada's 14 cM and Boris's 24 cM tract; Celia's two reported tracts are elsewhere. The overlaps are not yet phased. The table is an excerpt: all other cross-family comparisons are unavailable in this fictional input and remain recorded as gaps in the full matrix.

| Selected pair | Reported segment lengths, cM | Total / count / largest | Research reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Irma–Mara A | 32, 14, 9 | 55 / 3 / 32 | Investigate the long tract and its transmission. |
| Ada–Mara A | 14, 9 | 23 / 2 / 14 | Check the overlap with Irma; do not assign its ancestral source from coordinates alone. |
| Boris–Mara A | 24, 9 | 33 / 2 / 24 | Another family observation, with possible dependence on the Irma/Ada tract. |
| Celia–Mara A | 9, 8 | 17 / 2 / 9 | No reported tract in that interval; this alone does not exclude Dorian's branch. |
| Ada–Niko | Nineteen segments of 8 | 152 / 19 / 8 | The larger total alone does not establish a closer relationship than the Mara connection. |
| Celia–Owen | Comparison unavailable | Unknown / unknown / unknown | Missing coverage, not measured zero. |

A candidate connecting Irma to Ezra–Mina must place Ada, Boris and Dorian one generation below Irma and Celia two below her. Separate “best” placements making Ada and Boris children of different people fail that constraint. Dana stays one tester with both descent paths, and Mara B adds no new descendant. Preserve all of these observations when checking branch/generation sensitivity; do not turn overlapping intervals into three independent confirmations.

**Handoff:** The Irma–Mara connection merits investigation, but its ancestral source and the connecting pedigree remain unresolved, with incomplete counterpart-branch coverage. Verify transmission of the overlapping tract using the available family kits, then use a documented counterpart branch or records that distinguish the surviving family hypotheses. This is a research priority, not a probability or proof of parentage.

**When the data are partial:** another fictional pair has an imported total of 96 cM over 12 segments, an empty imported segment view, an older report of a 16 cM longest segment, and no effective-filter metadata. Keep the imported total labeled with its stated scope; request the matching segment report and settings within the authorized read scope. Do not splice in the older 16 cM value, infer that raw segments are absent, compute a filtered total, or call the total autosomal if its chromosome scope is unknown. The handoff is “segment-based priority unresolved; obtain a comparable report.”

## Reconcile supplied relationship-predictor outputs

Keep the exact pair, provider/version, input quantity and any ages actually used with each
supplied result. Compare equivalent relationship groups: a grouped prediction and a specific
age-conditioned label have different resolution. If the relationship is independently
documented, record whether it is included and its reported probability, including omission;
otherwise report disagreement only. Omission is not biological exclusion, and related models
do not supply independent confirmations. The [predictor comparison](https://thednageek.com/the-award-for-best-relationship-predictor-goes-to/)
motivates manual output reconciliation, not a reproduced benchmark, ranking or calibration claim.

Before entering an Ancestry total into a Shared cM or other relationship-probability tool, record whether it is the weighted (Timber-adjusted) or unweighted amount. When both are available and differ, run each and keep both candidate ranges labelled by input. Flag any weighted total smaller than that match's reported longest segment as an internal inconsistency; do not treat that weighted total as a corrected figure. The [weighted-versus-unweighted comparison](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=137) rests on one documented case. It does not establish which input is generally better, and genuine endogamy can justify down-weighting.

When birth years are used to choose between relationships with overlapping cM, such as half-aunt versus first cousin, first identify whether each uncertain link passes through a mother or a father. Allow a much wider span of birth years on paternal links: a father's children can be born decades apart, so a paternal route can be off by more than one generation. The [presenter's age screen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o8Mc7UsnTzE) uses illustrative ages, not sourced limits. Treat age as a plausibility screen only. It never establishes a placement, and it should not exclude a paternal-route option on a fixed generation length.

If the supplied comparison spans a probability/model revision, preserve the dated original
outputs and separate the versions. Where available and authorized, compare the same retained
inputs under the revised version; otherwise mark earlier rows unavailable for that comparison
while retaining them in the research record. Do not pool them as an unchanged model.
The [reported mid-comparison revision](https://thednageek.com/the-relationship-predictor-comparison-a-first-peek/)
supplies this trigger, without authorizing a new benchmark or establishing a provider ranking.

For a close match, record segment count with total cM, both from the same kit pair and report. Within roughly first-cousin distance, more segments at the same total lean toward a route through a female line, because female meioses recombine more. Give both numbers to a sex-specific-recombination tool, such as the SegCM tool from DNA Sci that the presenters use, rather than letting the LLM estimate the route. Record the output as a tool-labelled ranking of candidate routes with its inputs, not as the side the match is on. Do not apply it to more distant relationships, and do not combine a count from a different report or filter with this total. The [demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DRQpINI26FU) and [SegCM walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tOKJ0eVgrPQ) use illustrative counts. They validate no distributions, thresholds or endogamy performance, and do not show that a vendor's segment counting matches the tool's reference data.

When a supplied tool reports one estimated number of generations to the most recent common ancestor, as GEDmatch does, read it as an average over both testers, not each tester's own distance. Doubling it gives an approximate combined path length. List the splits that fit, including unequal ones (for 4.6, roughly 2+7, 3+6 or 4+5) before comparing it with the documented pedigree. The [generations-to-ancestor discussion](https://forum.molgen.org/index.php/topic,1298.0.html) includes a documented case the estimate placed closer than reality. Treat the value as an estimate: it identifies no split and neither confirms nor excludes a documented route.

When comparable supplied mismatches repeatedly centre on one person, audit the entered
generation and parent/spouse paths against documents before interpreting a biological
discrepancy. Correct only a supported input error, retain the old inputs/results and correction
basis, and compare the revised version separately. Do not edit a pedigree to improve fit or
adopt a model's SD/trial count as a universal threshold. This applies the
[person-centred input example](https://thednageek.com/power-up-your-dna-analyses/) within the
existing coherent-placement and baseline rules above.

When a tool supplies several generated trees ranked by odds, as in the [AutoKinship walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LnZ13VCkRfc), keep every tree with its rank, odds and run inputs, and record which documented link excludes any rejected tree. A surviving lower-ranked tree is a research hypothesis, not a confirmed placement.

Before interpreting a supplied two-tester relationship view, check that its stated input
contract fits the proposed pedigree. The [Shared cM example](https://www.yourdnaguide.com/ydgblog/shared-cm-project)
requires both testers to have the same genealogical relationship and generational offset to
the mystery person. Retain each actual pair measurement, including a reported zero distinct
from unavailable or below-floor data. A zero-to-one plotting workaround belongs only in a
labelled display copy; it cannot replace the measured zero in evidence or likelihood inputs.
This input check supplies neither a joint probability model nor a biological exclusion.

## Read ethnicity estimates as dated, version-bound observations

Treat each ethnicity readout as one kit measured under one dated model. Record the provider, whether the kit is native or uploaded from another vendor, the algorithm version, any active confidence setting and the provider's own region labels. A native test and an upload of the same person can differ on one platform, and a version or setting change is not new ancestry evidence. Before calling two readouts discordant, map nested or differently named regions onto each other. Phrase results as DNA shared with a reference population, not an ancestral country. When an update says a category 'split into' new regions, keep old and new values as separate observations; do not redistribute the old percentage arithmetically. The [upload-versus-native comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hw1YmGKAwOw), [cross-provider label comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=alx8-1I7Ato) and [version-change review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_Qzi6THU1jM) motivate this. They neither rank providers nor validate any region.

**Keep the displayed range with the headline percentage.** Where a provider shows a range for a region, record it beside the most-likely value from the same result version. Also note any segment the chromosome view leaves unassigned. A range reaching zero means that version's model does not rule out the region being absent. Before calling it impossible for a child to show a larger share than the transmitting parent, compare the two ranges. If the same person has a second kit on that platform, record its score and range as a sign of run-to-run variation. Point differences inside wide, overlapping ranges stay unresolved; they do not by themselves show an inheritance error or hidden ancestry. A region missing from the list is not a measured zero, because small components may be hidden. The [range-comparison report](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=852), [region-range discussion](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=76) and [duplicate-kit comparison](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=954) are user reports. They calibrate no range, measure no general retest variability and do not show which value is right.

**A parent-side split or ethnicity painting is not phasing.** A phased per-parent view estimates the half each parent passed on, not that parent's own estimate. Where sides are labelled only parent 1 and parent 2, do not name mother and father from the view itself. Before using a painted region or side assignment to place a segment or match, compare it with each tested parent's or grandparent's own result from the same provider. Record any region put on a side that person's result does not support, comparing presence rather than percentages. Overlap with a side-specific relative's segment is only an indication. In one [resubmitted-sample answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QVx5JDd9E8s), the same DNA got a different parental division. Consistent splits across tested full siblings are better support, but not proof. The [painter check](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6v3WMsOdZ-g), [parent-kit check](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=huP99-Ry0lM), [family side-view check](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fVmFXH9y2sI), [overlap caution](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gj-aDOUv2Y4) and [phased-view review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ez0wg_JcjC0) establish no error rate or current tool behaviour.

**Use a confirmed parent's estimate as an arithmetic control, not an origin finding.** This applies where parent and child have results from the same provider and model version, and parentage is established by relationship-specific DNA (see the 'Ethnicity estimates as conclusions' bullet above). The child carries about half the parent's genome. A category at P% in the parent therefore implies roughly at least P − 50% in the child. A child or sibling far below that floor shows that one or both estimates are inconsistent. It does not identify which estimate is wrong, give true proportions or bear on parentage. Small shortfalls are within approximation. The [parent-child MyOrigins comparison](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=146) and [sibling floor calculation](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=140) motivate this check.

Where a grandparent has also tested, run the painting check interval by interval. Use the grandparent–grandchild one-to-one segments to mark which intervals of the child's copy from the intermediate parent came from that grandparent. Then compare the child's painted label on each interval with the label painted on the same grandparent-side copy of the parent. A [chromosome-by-chromosome painting audit](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=954) found transmitted intervals relabelled and regions painted with no matching source copy. Record each mismatch as a painting or phasing inconsistency, not new ancestry. The parent's copy labels are themselves inferred, so a mismatch does not show which label is right.

**Keep community and Journey assignments apart from region percentages and from matches' communities.** Label each observation with the product that produced it. A Journey for a place whose region percentage is missing, or the reverse, is not by itself a contradiction. A list of communities common among the closest matches describes those matches; do not add it to the tester's own result. Before relying on a community parent tab, compare it with tested parents or children and with close matches documented on each side. Record any community a child holds that neither tested parent shows. Save each dated assignment set: a community dropped or renamed in an update may reflect changed criteria, not evidence against the paper trail. The [parent-split discussion](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=519), [UK community update thread](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=661) and [Journeys-versus-regions discussion](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=1629) report such anomalies but do not document how assignments are made. Neither product confirms the other or establishes an origin.

**Trace a region through matches, not the label or percentage.** An expected population missing from an estimate does not disprove it, and no estimate establishes descent from a tribe or community; documents do. Look for matches with substantial ancestry from the region whose trees converge on one couple, or who also match a documented cousin. That shared couple's branch becomes the research lead. A trace (~1%) region with no such cluster may simply be wrong. Absence among one line's relatives is only a soft clue. Mark branches without placed matches as untested. Recurrence among related cousins is dependent evidence, and under endogamy shared matches may point to several lines. Suggest Y-DNA or mtDNA only for an all-male or all-female path. The [shared-cousin example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t3D0iReQG_s), [region-sharing example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e3ixxQev7YQ), [trace-ancestry answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SBtWjTCpPpo), [relatives check](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3fuhnRtg5I), [sibling-line screen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7IdlI59m8YI) and [negative-evidence caution](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vtICddQnrbU) motivate this. None proves an origin or identifies an ancestor.

**Use matches overlapping the painted segment, not a match's displayed breakdown.** A match's ethnicity breakdown, or the populations you have in common, is that match's own estimate, not the makeup of the shared DNA. Do not place a match on a parent's side because its breakdown fits that parent's population. Where a provider paints a small region onto segments, record their coordinates and build. Then ask which matches overlap exactly there, on any platform that shows segment data. If those matches' documented ancestry points to one population, that is a lead on the segment's source. Overlap does not show shared descent on the same parental copy, and under endogamy many matches can share a segment through population background. The [segment-located match example](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=1065) is one family's anecdote. The [match-ethnicity correction](https://forum.molgen.org/index.php/topic,1298.0.html) shows that a breakdown neither excludes a side nor identifies the shared segment.

Do not turn a small ethnicity percentage into one ancestor at one generation. First list the alternatives: misassignment from a closely related reference population, a false positive, and several distant contributors whose shares add up. If one recent source is plausible, keep a generation range. For multi-ancestor hypotheses, bound the total by adding each ancestor's low and high inheritance values, not averages. The cited presenter interprets a vendor timeline's 'most recent ancestor' generation as a wide date window resting on a fixed generation length; verify that interpretation against the actual provider and result version. The output only chooses which pedigree generations to complete; it places no ancestor. The [multiple-ancestor ranges](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gThnugZcEUc), [3% answer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MlsU45miWKA), [6% example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CPkieGCVhd0) and [timeline critique](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-mZSGxxs5-0) motivate this. Their range chart was on screen and is not reproduced here.

**Read the painting before the label.** A component may carry different names across vendors, versions, or a parent and child. Before calling the results conflicting, compare its painted chromosome location with the vendor's earlier labels for it, and record provider, version and label against that segment. For an unexpected component, note whether it sits on a few segments or fills whole or half chromosomes, and whether its range starts at zero. In one discussion, whole-chromosome blocks were read as smoothing that filled stretches with no better assignment, while the broader group might still be right. Treat either pattern as a reason for caution, then test the region through matches as above. The [cross-vendor painting comparison](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=954), [parent–child relabelling example](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=76) and [ethnicity-update discussion](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=1629) document no algorithm. A shared location shows neither which label is right nor where an ancestor came from, and a high lower bound does not prove a subgroup.

## Prompt-craft that practitioners found works (the reusable core)

1. **Provide complete inputs, don't expect inference.** Give the citation fields / the transcription / the screenshot — not "here's a link, cite it."
2. **Templates + hard constraints.** "Use these exact template fields; do not alter the structure." Generic requests produced partial/non-matching output; explicit template + "apply *this* template" fixed it. (Family Locket, 2025-02)
3. **Prompt-chaining for complex concepts.** Teach it bottom-up: define the terms first (what *is* original vs derivative vs authored), *then* ask it to classify. (Family Locket, 2026-03)
4. **Define the rules or it invents them.** Any domain logic (source analysis, grouping criteria) must be stated explicitly first.
5. **Manage the context window.** Long chats degrade and hit limits even on paid plans — split into fresh chats and reload the current state rather than one endless thread. (Family Locket, Leeds video)
6. **Specificity drives quality.** The more precise the ask and the format, the better the output.
7. **Separate an endogamous match's research priority from its segment assignments.** Request original and filtered metrics with the filter stated, the documented relationship, and which individual segments remain unassigned.
8. **For a family connection, supply both family maps and the pair matrix.** Ask for placements that preserve the known relationships, dependent observations, missing comparisons, sensitivity to branch/generation coverage, and one next discriminating check. Do not request a separate best parent for each sibling or an AI-computed combined probability.
9. **For a multiply related match, ask for interval and role decisions.** Separate supported overlap from unresolved overhang; name the actual alternative ancestral paths; distinguish a cluster seed, a pedigree-validation helper and a scored focal match. Require a reason for each working inclusion or omission.

## Worked mini-examples (workflow logic, not clicks)

- **Leeds chart** — AI builds the match table (names, cM, tentative relationship) from Ancestry screenshots; *you* do the color-coding. The Leeds Method uses matches sharing **90–400 cM** to separate the four grandparent groups. The 400 cM ceiling **excludes most first cousins** (who tie to two grandparents) — but it's not a clean guarantee: first cousins do occasionally fall in the 301–400 cM band, so independently exclude any *known* match who shares two grandparents rather than trusting the cap. The human-judgment step is spotting the **removed first-cousin descendants (1C1R, 1C2R)** that fall *inside* the 90–400 window but connect to two lines and skew the four-way split — **second cousins are the foundation you keep, not exclude.** AI front-ends; human judges. (Leeds Method = Dana Leeds, July 2018; 90–400 cM — *verified*.)
- **A match wired into two color groups at once** — the same Leeds chart (or any ICW cluster) occasionally shows one match tangled into two of the four grandparent colors, because it's related to the target through both lines at once (a shared ancestral couple reached two ways, or a second marriage). Don't have the AI silently pick a side or average the two. Pull that one match out, re-cluster on the rest, and reinstate it **flagged as belonging to both lines** once you've worked out — from the tree, not the cM — the two paths that put it there; where segment data exists, [evaluate each interval separately](#untangle-multiple-paths-without-discarding-the-useful-evidence) and retain unresolved ancestral assignments. (Family Locket, RLP 225, 2022-10-31; Dana Leeds, 2018-08-23)
- **DNA source citations** — build reusable templates (who/what/where/when + the match's cM & segment fields) → export CSV → hand to AI with "use these exact fields." Then **check every output** for missing fields. (Family Locket, 2025-02)
- **Painting a GEDmatch match** — AI helps translate GEDmatch one-to-one coordinates into DNA Painter segments; the *judgment* step it can't own is assigning the segment to the **paternal vs maternal** side and the right ancestral couple — get that wrong and the whole chromosome map is corrupted. (Family Locket, 2025-04)

## The pitfalls worth internalizing (the least-obvious, most-LLM-usable part)

- Confidently wrong on dates/names/places, and it may *argue* when corrected.
- Loses track across a long multi-turn session.
- Treats a DNA-estimated relationship as the real one; ignores endogamy inflating cM.
- Conflates *original/derivative/authored* (source type) with *primary/secondary* (information type).
- Fabricates citations/records when it lacks the underlying fields.
- Silently resolves an ambiguous compound relationship phrase. A chain such as "my maternal paternal great-grandfather" can be parsed starting from different parents. Before computing a relationship or expected sharing, state which reading the analysis assumes. Where the readings diverge, draw or describe each one and ask the user to confirm. The [presenter's double-relationship walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BCvednIm-24) names this ambiguity without offering a parsing rule. Do not choose the reading that best fits the DNA.

## Verification & privacy discipline

- Every AI-produced claim → **verify against the relevant evidence** — prefer *original* records and *primary* information where available, and correlate independent sources — before it enters a tree or report. Use AI to *think faster*, never to *decide*.
- **De-identify before you share data with any third-party tool, not just before publishing.** Strip direct identifiers before pasting a third party's DNA or match list into a cloud AI (or any external tool) — though raw DNA is inherently re-identifiable, so this is *pseudonymization*, not true anonymization. **Get the person's consent before processing their genetic data — make it a default, not a step you weigh** (it's the community norm, and legally required in many places). De-identify living individuals in anything public as well.

Inspect the actual calculation, table or screenshot intended for the recipient: masking names
in a tree view need not mask generated output. Keep the private original and the separately
redacted derivative distinct. The [dated Banyan output example](https://www.yourdnaguide.com/ydgblog/banyandna)
supplies this verification trigger; it does not validate current controls or anonymity.

Consent to analyze a match does not cover naming that person in a shared or published
report. When the researcher asks, state the documented or hypothesized connection with its
uncertainty, how the name or data will appear and where the report will be shared; leave out
sensitive findings about other people, such as unexpected parentage. Offer an excerpt limited
to that person's own data or a redacted preview. Record the actual response and any limits
with that report version. No reply is not permission: anonymize or omit instead. The [permission-request example](https://familylocket.com/rlp-250-rlp-with-dna-12-whats-next/)
supplies this preparation step; it does not settle legal sufficiency or guarantee anonymity.
