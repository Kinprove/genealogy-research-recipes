# Search candidate jurisdictions for a missing marriage

Use this recipe when a marriage is not found in the county where the couple lived. It replaces a single miss with a dated, ranked list of candidate jurisdictions, each with a reason, a law and date basis, the record series searched and its result. A verified original that resolves the marriage ends the search. Otherwise the search is not finished, and the gap is not reported, until every ranked candidate has been searched or recorded as unavailable. To decide what an absence means, use [evidence/proof judgment](evidence-proof-judgment.md). For the probate version of a widened court search, see [probate case inventory](probate-case-document-inventory.md).

## Record the first miss with its limits

Log the residence-county search as one row: jurisdiction, office, record series (licence, bond, register, return), years covered, index or image, and outcome. Set the date window from evidence. Start with the years from about age 18 to the first child's birth, and widen the window when the evidence calls for it [S12], [S13]. A miss in this row is only a "no hit" for that series and those years. Do not write "no marriage" or "never married." Non-marriage is a real possibility, but it is a hypothesis to test after the search, not a conclusion drawn from one county [S10], [S11].

Before searching, record which series should contain an entry if the couple married in the jurisdiction and why, following the suggestion from the available excerpt [S47]. Weigh the absence of an expected entry only after checking recording practice, survival and search coverage. A miss in a neighbouring county searched as a long shot stays a search result [S48].

Before searching, confirm which county the residence lay in at the marriage date, not today. Jurisdiction depends on when as well as where [S15]. The cut-off part of Conway County, Arkansas, described below, was itself part of Perry County from 1840 to 1873.

Then confirm which civil marriage records were kept there at that date. Some states began marriage licences late, but the start of licensing is not the start of civil recording. Check what the place actually kept before reading a no-hit:

- **Pennsylvania:** county marriage licences, issued by the clerk of the Orphans' Court, began on 1 October 1885 under an act of 23 June 1885 [S17], [S19]. Earlier, a registration law of 1852–1855 had county Registers of Wills record marriages. The State Archives holds the 1852–1854 returns of 49 of the 64 counties [S18]. Philadelphia's City Archives holds civil marriage records from July 1860 to December 1885 [S20].
- **New York:** statewide marriage registration began in 1880–81, and the state marriage index starts in 1881 [S21]. Licences were required from 1 January 1908 and were issued by city and town clerks, not counties [S23]. New York City keeps its own marriage records outside the state index, and Albany, Buffalo and Yonkers marriages before 1908 are with those cities' clerks [S22].
- **South Carolina:** marriage licences, issued by the county probate judge, began on 1 July 1911 [S24].

For a marriage before licensing, list the civil series that survive for that place and date, such as registers, returns, bonds or city records. Search church registers, minister records and newspapers alongside them, not instead of them. A civil no-hit counts only for a series that covered that place and date.

When an early American marriage has no licence or return, do not write it off as a lost record or as a sign that no marriage took place; find out whether the jurisdiction then used banns, read in church or posted publicly, or marriage bonds [S28]. Do not assume licences only came later: North Carolina's 1715 act let couples buy a licence or have banns published, and in Virginia giving bond was the step for obtaining a licence [S29], [S30].

Where banns were used, check period local newspapers for printed banns or reports of their reading [S63], [S65] and the sponsoring church’s bulletins for printed banns [S64], [S66]. Log the publication date and any stated banns date separately. Use the named church to look for a ceremony entry before recording a marriage date.

For a marriage in present-day Marion County, West Virginia, check when the county formed and look for bonds and ministers’ returns before Virginia’s 1853 registration series [S56], following the suggestion from the available excerpt [S53]. For dates before Marion formed in 1842, use the location to choose its parent county, Monongalia or Harrison [S54].

For an 1842–1852 Marion County marriage, browse the early pages of the county court marriage series on West Virginia State Archives microfilm. The holdings list dates the marriages from 1842 and the separately listed marriage index from 1853 [S55 @2026-11-22].

## Keep residence, licence and ceremony places apart

Record each partner’s residence county, the ceremony county and the issuing county separately. Search both residence counties and the ceremony county if the issuer is unknown [S12], [S13]. For an older marriage, establish where a licence could be obtained under the law in force on the marriage date.

In Pennsylvania today a licence issued in any county can be used anywhere in the Commonwealth. Follow the officiant’s return to the issuing county [S35 @2026-11-19], [S17 @2026-11-19].

Rank the bride's residence county first among the residence rows. Couples usually married where the bride lived [S1], [S5]. That sets the order of the search. It does not let you skip the groom's county or the ceremony county.

After a residence-county miss, search marriage notices and newspaper abstracts for each partner’s residence at the likely marriage date, even if you began in their later home. Add the reported or planned venue’s jurisdiction to the candidate list and use the ceremony date and officiant to search civil returns or church registers. For a planned wedding, resolve relative dates against the issue date and keep the venue and day provisional until you find a record of the ceremony. The example from the available excerpt points to Burkittsville, Maryland, even though the notice describes both partners as Loudoun County residents [S40].

Search immigrant-language newspapers for elopement reports and add the reported destination as a lead to the licence or ceremony record [S59]. Preserve the geographic precision of the report. A transcribed 1894 report that names only Dakota leaves both North Dakota and South Dakota as possible jurisdictions [S59], [S60].

For Loudoun County, Virginia, search Patricia B. Duncan’s *Democratic Mirror* and *Mirror* abstracts for 1857–1879 [S41] or the *Mirror* abstracts for 1891–1899 [S42].

Follow the abstract to its original newspaper issue. For the Leesburg *Mirror*, check drawer 12 (1865–1902) in the Thomas Balch Library microfilm index [S43 @2026-11-20].

## Rank adjacent and reachable courthouses by period access

List every adjacent county, including those across a state line. Rank them by how easily someone could reach each courthouse at the time, not by straight-line distance:

- Mark the residence, county seats, river crossings and rail stops on a map close to the marriage date. The David Rumsey collection supplied the 1924 map in Lowe’s Warren County search [S6], [S36].
- Check which courthouse was reachable from the couple’s side of a river. Castillo uses the Arkansas River between part of Conway County and its seat at Morrilton to suggest a search in neighbouring Perry County when high water blocked the crossing [S15].
- Compare routes for the season of the marriage. Lisson suggests a longer overland route to a Virginia courthouse could have been safer for Surry County, North Carolina, families during winter rains or the spring thaw [S11], [S9].
- Follow the railway in both directions across county and state lines. After searching north as far as Louisville, Lowe reported finding the Warren County couple’s bond at Mitchellville in Sumner County, Tennessee, the rail stop just south of the border [S6], [S36].
- Add the couple's church. People travelled long distances to marry in their preferred church [S15]. Find the couple's denomination and congregation, and add that church's county as a candidate.

After a miss in the neighbouring counties, add a larger city the couple could reach by period routes, especially when there is no known Gretna Green for the place and date, following the advice from the available excerpt of each talk [S47], [S49]. Give the city its own jurisdiction row.

For a Northern Virginia search with a plausible route to Washington, DC, browse the licences and certificates for the relevant years in FamilySearch’s *District of Columbia, Marriages, 1811–1950* [S50 @2026-11-22].

## Compare the marriage law in force at the marriage date

For the home state and each neighbouring state, record the rules at the marriage date: consent age, waiting period, blood test, residency, bond and fees. Cite the edition you used. One presenter uses the Census Bureau marriage-and-divorce reports on Google Books and HathiTrust. He notes that not every annual report includes marriage statistics [S7], [S8].

- Distinguish the report’s publication year from the dates covered. The Census Bureau’s 1909 report covers 1867–1906 [S37]. Lowe used a Kentucky–Tennessee difference in parental consent requirements as a lead [S7]. Check the applicable statutes on both sides for each partner’s age, sex and marriage date before ranking a crossing on that basis.
- Check a high-rate border county against a period map and the registers. Lowe pointed to Brooke County, West Virginia, between Ohio and Pennsylvania, in a table from the 1922 report [S7], [S8]. A high marriage rate alone cannot identify the couple’s residence or motive.
- Use lists of elopement destinations, also called Gretna Greens, to generate candidates for the home county [S4], [S10], [S11], [S14]. The examples include Niagara Falls, Ontario, for New Yorkers and Dillon, South Carolina, for North Carolinians [S10]. Include the date in a search for a local destination and verify its use in that period [S16].

- Use Cornell’s state marriage-law table to locate statutes. The table cites a chart in the 1999 World Almanac and reports subsequent statute checks without dating individual entries [S27 @2026-11-19]. Retrieve the version of the statute in force at the marriage date.

Do not suggest modern resort venues by default. Las Vegas, Hawaii or Mexico belong on the list only when the period, the couple's location and the travel options make them plausible [S1]. A high-rate county or an easier law is a reason to search a place. It is not evidence that the couple married there [S10].

For a Pennsylvania search in the 1910s–1930s with a plausible elopement route, add Elkton in Cecil County, Maryland, following the suggestion from the available excerpt [S49]. Check period routes into Elkton, including the Philadelphia, Baltimore and Washington Railroad [S51].

In the Maryland State Archives guide, use Cecil County Circuit Court’s marriage record MSA CM352 (1886–1928) and consult its bride index MSA CM353 (1928–1935) or groom index MSA CM354 (1928–1977) for the relevant years [S52 @2026-11-22]. Carry an index hit to the underlying marriage record.

## Add counties from the family timeline

After a miss in the later home county, use dated personal property tax lists to trace each partner’s earlier residence. Keep same-name entries provisional until you can link them to the partner. When a tax-list lead like the one from the available excerpt places either partner in an earlier county during the marriage window, add that county to the ranked list [S44]. Search that county’s marriage register, bonds and returns for the overlapping years.

For a 1787 lead in Shenandoah County, Virginia, check the personal property tax list on reel 315 [S45 @2026-11-21]. Search the 1781–1850 marriage register on reel 65 and the marriage bonds on reels 68 (1772–1788) and 69 (1789–1791) for the years in the marriage window [S46 @2026-11-21].

Map where siblings and close relatives lived at the likely marriage date, including the counties where other family members had married [S6]. Lisson reports locating an ancestor’s wedding in her sister’s home in another county after checking the home county and known elopement destinations [S14]. Add a relative’s county even when the couple never lived there.

Match each partner’s service or work postings to the marriage window. Lefler reports that a Tennessee groom’s March 1951 Air Force enlistment and August air police training at Camp Gordon, Georgia, put him within reach of Aiken, South Carolina, where the couple married that August [S5]. Search across the state line from the posting and use the installation’s name at the time [S5], [S14]. For catalogue and map searches, the Army dates the change from Camp Gordon to Fort Gordon to 1956, the Fort Eisenhower name to 2023 and the return to Fort Gordon to June 2025 [S39].

## Investigate the county where the record should be

When independent evidence points to one county, ask its clerk, historian or archive which returns actually entered the official series. Lisson relays a reader’s report of a justice who left town with his register unfiled [S9]. Search for a minister’s or circuit rider’s papers in denominational archives, museums and ArchiveGrid, and check church records and newspapers where county papers were lost [S9], [S11].

If a marriage is missing from the alphabetical section of a register, check the last pages for a separate block of ministers’ returns before logging a no-hit, following the suggestion from the available excerpt [S53]. Search any separate return section for the full date window and distinguish it from the alphabetical section in the search log [S56].

When a licence or bond survives without a return, identify whether it is a register copy or a loose paper [S61]. For a register copy, determine whether the jurisdiction recorded ceremony details in that volume or in a separate return series at the time. For a loose paper, check for a return attached to the document, elsewhere in the packet or filed with the officiant’s returns. Allow for a separated or lost return when weighing the absence [S61], [S62].

For a missing Fairfax County, Virginia, marriage, use Marty Hiatt and Craig Roberts Scott’s *Implied Marriages of Fairfax County, Virginia* (1994) as a finding aid, following the lead from the available excerpt [S49], [S69], [S70]. Follow the entry’s reference to the underlying deed, will or other record and cite that record for what it says about the relationship.

Search the Periodical Source Index (PERSI) by place and subject for a published transcription, then obtain the article and follow its citation to the journal or register [S38 @2026-11-19]. In the providers’ archived descriptions, ACPL staff compile PERSI, and this copy is the larger one (September 2026) [S25 @2026-11-19]. Findmypast described its version in September 2025 as an index with fewer entries but images of some articles [S26 @2026-11-19].

## Carry each hit to the original and close the list

For each candidate, record:

- the jurisdiction
- why it was added (residence, ceremony, access, church, law, relative, posting)
- the law edition and date relied on
- the series and years searched
- the access route
- the outcome: **hit**, **no hit**, **not searched** or **unavailable**

Keep "not searched" and "unavailable" visibly separate from "no hit."

Check that a hit is the right couple before accepting it, then follow it to the bond, licence, return or register itself. Do not stop at an index miss. Ancestry tends to hold indexes, while FamilySearch holds more original images, many of them unindexed [S12], [S13]. Browse the FamilySearch images for the county and years before recording "no hit."

When entries in two jurisdictions give the same couple and date, compare both, using the fuller entry to recover details missing from a damaged one, as illustrated by the comparison from the available excerpt [S57]. Identify each record’s type and read the officiant’s return for the ceremony location [S58]. If the location is still unclear, use the officiant and date to check the church register and keep the venue unresolved.

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: request --> A request for loose marriage papers sends the couple's names, the marriage date and the county to the clerk's office or archive, and the papers can hold ages, a parent's consent and blood-test results. A 20th-century marriage can concern a couple or parents who are still living. Answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md`, including whether either spouse may be living and what access rules the office applies to recent records, before sending the request.

Ask each county's clerk or archive for its loose marriage records as well as the bound books, both for a register hit and before recording "no hit" there. These are the papers of the licensing process, filed apart from the bound volumes and rarely online [S32 @2026-11-12]. They can include affidavits, parental consent letters for an underage bride or groom, bonds and blood-test results [S32 @2026-11-12], [S33 @2026-11-12]. The Houston County, Tennessee archivist reports that most of that county's packets, 1871–2010, also hold a copy of the licence [S33 @2026-11-12]. Bonds are documents separate from the licence, and guardians' consent affidavits, most common in the South and on the frontier, sit among town and county records [S34 @2026-11-12].

Following the advice from the available excerpt, inventory the licence, bond and banns even when a return survives, and weigh them alongside dated evidence that the couple lived as spouses when the return is missing [S67]. Look for witness affidavits about the relationship in records such as a widow’s pension file [S68]. Keep the ceremony date and place unresolved until the evidence establishes them.

A licence does not show that banns were read: in English law a special licence could dispense with publication, so check the local rule before assuming banns for a licensed marriage [S28], [S31].

Compare the bondsman’s signature with the officiant’s name and the fee notation before treating him as a family associate. In Lowe’s account of the Mitchellville bond, F. M. Groves served as bondsman and justice of the peace and was listed as paying for the bond [S6], [S36].

Close by naming any candidates left unsearched after a verified hit or recorded as unavailable, the reason each remains, and any record-keeping gaps found. Hand the interpretation of any remaining absence to the evidence recipe.
