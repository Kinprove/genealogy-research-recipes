# Search historical newspaper sections for family evidence

Use this recipe when a search has found the right newspaper but skipped the parts of the issue likely to name the person. Choose the section that fits the question before rerunning the name search.

## Find people in lists and social columns

Read list and table entries as well as long notices for engagements, weddings and milestone anniversaries. For a wedding, capture named attendants or witnesses and the place as separate newspaper observations to compare with the marriage record. [S1], [S2]

When no photograph survives, check local wedding coverage for descriptions of what the bride or attendants wore. Record the clothing as described for that occasion. [S8], [S9]

For a missing associate, browse society columns for meetings, club events and private gatherings. Copy the guest and organizer names with their roles and the event’s date. Party attendance gives a social link to investigate. [S1], [S2]

If the first profile ties the person to a conference or award, search for the event itself in the host city and in publications serving the sponsoring religious or civic community. Coverage may be filed under the event or organization. [S1]

For a deceased twentieth-century immigrant, browse local reports of school sports, academic prizes and amateur tournaments, along with legal notices of sales or marriages. [S5]

When U.S. obituaries and community announcements leave a famine-era immigrant’s Irish origin unresolved, look for family names and Irish hometowns in reports on Irish organizations and St. Patrick’s Day parades. [S7]

## Use the classifieds to recover the right kind of lead

Trace a family business through repeated advertisements. Compare dated address and opening-hour entries across issues so a single ad supplies one dated observation. [S1], [S2]

For a suspected move, compare advertisements in the old and new towns. Record each advertised address with its issue date and leave the actual move date open. [S4]

When an immigrant disappears from the expected destination, browse the classifieds for *Information Wanted* and *Missing Friends*. Search for both the advertiser and the missing person. The Boston *Pilot* used this genre for Irish immigrant notices. Use stated origins and migration details as leads to the records for each person. [S1], [S3]

## Follow probate notices to the estate

When a will is hard to find, check the local paper’s legal notices for probate or estate notices. Use the named court and any hearing date to guide the search for the will or other estate papers. [S6], [S10]

## Reconstruct an unusual death away from home

Search the news columns in towns near the incident as well as the deceased person’s home paper. Keep the initial account and follow-up reports as dated observations. Record their witnesses, sequence and conflicting details separately. [S1]

To test an oral account about a deceased ancestor, search the name as it would appear in print, including any title, with an incident keyword from the story. Read each promising article for the event it describes. In the reported example, a trolley-accident search turned up a refugee-arrival article. [S4]

## Extend the search beyond the local paper

Search Washington, D.C.’s *National Intelligencer* for congressional lists, appointments, military news, and marriage and death notices. Include it for deaths outside the District, according to the presenter’s guidance. [S11†], [S12] When using Joan M. Dixon’s *National Intelligencer Newspaper Abstracts*, select the volume covering the year and use the abstract to locate the issue. [S12]

When the *National Intelligencer* credits a reprint to another newspaper, as the presenter reports for a death notice, follow the credit to the source printing. [S11†], [S14]

For a War of 1812 military death, browse surviving newspapers from the person’s locality for casualty lists and battle reports. Extend the search to the *National Intelligencer* and *Niles’ Weekly Register*, two titles the presenter reports searching. [S13†], [S14], [S15], [S16] Check which casualties the report names and which it gives as totals. [S14], [S15]
