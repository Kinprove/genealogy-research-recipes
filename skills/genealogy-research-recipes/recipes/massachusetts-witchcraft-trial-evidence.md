# Reconstruct a Massachusetts witchcraft case from its records

Use this recipe for a named person in a Salem-area witchcraft accusation. Determine whether they were an accuser, witness, accused person or petitioner before describing the case. Attribute each allegation to its speaker and record what the court did with it.

## Read the examination and testimony

Open the Massachusetts Archives trial collection for the relevant warrant, examination, deposition and other surviving records. Separate a witness's claimed apparition from a witness's account of the accused in person and from the accused's confession. Check which type of evidence the court heard and where the record shows it being accepted or acted on. [S2 @2026-11-22]

According to the presenter in the available excerpt, test whether children reporting apparitions heard suspicions from adults or other children first [S1†]. Date the statements and look for shared information before treating them as independent corroboration.

Read an examination for any claimed bodily marks as an examiner's observation and a period belief. For a confession, check the conditions of the examination and compare any later statement by the person about coercion, fear or retraction. The declaration of the Andover accused gives their account of how fear and questioning affected their confessions. [S2] [S3]

## Verify the outcome

When a later account calls an execution a burning, verify the disposition for the individual. For Salem, distinguish an execution by hanging, the recorded death by pressing, and deaths in custody. Search for the record of the outcome even if a sentence survives. [S4]

## Return the evidence

Give the date, originating court or authority, record kind, speakers and what each reported, the conduct of the examination, the last confirmed outcome and a locator for each record. Preserve differences between the examination and any later petition.
