# Record a family interview or inherited account with its speaker, prompter and chain

Use this recipe when you are about to interview a relative, when someone hands you a recording of a family conversation, or when an inherited account is about to go into a tree. An inherited account can be a story everyone repeats, a note or an "as told by" typescript. A supplied file does not require a new interview. The common mistake is to turn the recollection straight into tree facts and fill its gaps. That means rebuilding an answer nobody could hear from the question that was asked, or crediting a detail the prompter supplied to the speaker. It also means polishing an uncertain name into a quotation, or collapsing a relayed or retyped account into one witness.

This recipe covers prompting, recording, attributing and preserving spoken accounts only.

- Weigh what an account can prove with [evidence/proof judgment](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/evidence-proof-judgment.md).
- Cite the recording and each copy with the [source citation record](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/source-citation.md).
- Log sessions, and searches for a missing original recording, in the [research log](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/research-documentation.md).
- For the papers, photographs and media an account arrived with, use Family collection intake and capture. To identify or date a photograph used as a prompt, use Family photograph attribution and dating. To date a written family paper, use Family papers and Bible register dating.

## Preserve the source first

Preserve the source recording and its stated context before making an edit or transcript. If you were given a file, work from that file.

## Agree the session and choose a bounded prompt

For a new recording, agree with the participant on the topic, recording and intended use, including any limits on sharing. Use a bounded prompt such as a photograph, object, recipe or experienced event. With a photograph, ask about the vehicles, buildings, place and season before asking who the people are, and never supply a desired identity [S2]. After an important question, stay silent and let the participant remember [S3], [S11]. Respect a decision not to discuss the topic. Relatives raised in one household can have experienced the same events differently: record each relative's version as that person's own account [S2].

If the participant names someone in a photograph, record it as their identification, with the basis they give. Never compare faces or assign identity by appearance yourself. Resemblance is never evidence by itself.

Use a census page the participant would remember as a neighbourhood prompt: with the recording running, show them their own entry, read out the neighbours listed before and after it, and ask what they recall [S4]. Link the page and question to the actual response, and keep the census and the recollection as separate sources. Page adjacency alone establishes neither kinship nor shared residence.

## Record the answers, not only the questions

Place the microphone on the relative answering, not on the interviewer [S3]. Keep each recording to a few minutes, splitting a long interview into short recordings, and replay each one right after making it to check audibility and completeness [S1]. If an answer was not recorded or cannot be heard, record that limitation; never reconstruct it from the question. For supplied recordings, mark inaudible or missing passages and keep any available session notes separate.

## Record who prompted each answer

Record who prompted each answer. A familiar sibling or other close relative can help a participant with memory difficulties by cueing ("do you remember when…") [S11]. When that happens, note who was present, the cue's wording or recording offset and the response that followed. Details introduced only by the prompter remain the prompter's account. On revisits, record which session first supplied each detail. Flag details that appear only after repeated pressing as possibly reconstructed. Stop when the participant strains to be helpful, note that you stopped, and corroborate such details with documents [S10].

## Record the chain of a relayed or inherited account

For a relayed or inherited account, record its chain. Note the speaker, whom they heard it from, the occasion, their approximate age then and how that teller could know [S5]. Add any interviewer and date, and each later note, typescript, duplicate, photocopy or retyping, marking unseen links and the generation you hold [S6]. Asking about the oldest person remembered from childhood can draw out such accounts [S9].

An "as told by" typescript is a received transcript: its recording stays an open search, and a copy note is not the interview date [S7], [S6]. Differing versions are derivatives, not corroboration. A written version is one dated telling, so do not edit other tellings toward it [S8]. Relayed content, teller intent and remembered dates still need documentary support [S5], [S6].

## Keep the recording, transcript and interpretation apart

Retain the speaker, recording date and context when known, prompt or item reference and actual audio or video file. Distinguish spoken testimony from your transcript, paraphrase and later interpretation. Use recording offsets for quoted or uncertain passages so they can be replayed. Preserve the original alongside edited extracts; mark uncertain readings rather than polishing them into definite names or quotations.

Connect the recording to its photo or object and session notes through stable references. Keep those links in a portable register or sidecar if the application does not export them. After the requested export, open a sample audio file and its associated item and verify that speaker/context, required notes and links survived. Never assume a single image download carries the memory: in a 2022 demonstration of one app, downloading a photograph did not bring its attached audio, and comments did not travel when a photo was shared [S1]. If the interface or account is not available to you, say so, and do not describe download or export controls you have not seen.

## Hand over a packet, not tree facts

Deliver the requested source files and notes with the observed export gaps and attribution. A correctly recorded recollection remains an account to assess, not an automatic tree fact. If a narrative draft is requested later, keep recorded words, remembered speech and written quotations distinguishable; creating the memory packet does not require writing a story.

## Return

- **The source recordings, unedited**, each with its stated context and stable reference.
- **A session register** with one row per session:
  - speaker and everyone present
  - date and context, when known
  - the agreed topic, intended use and any limits on sharing
  - the prompt or item reference
  - the file and the recording segments
  - any note that you stopped, and why
- **A statement record** with one entry per statement:
  - the speaker
  - the prompter, with the cue's wording or recording offset
  - the session that first supplied it
  - its layer: spoken testimony, transcript, paraphrase or interpretation
  - recording offsets for quoted or uncertain passages
  - uncertain readings marked as uncertain
  - a "possibly reconstructed" flag where it applies
- **A chain record** for each relayed or inherited account. Give each teller, their source, the occasion, their age then and how they could know. List each later copy, with unseen links marked and the generation you hold. Log a missing original recording as an open search.
- **Observed export gaps.** List the audio, comments, notes or links that did not travel.
- **No tree facts.** Hand over each recollection as an account to assess.
