# Recover a Buenos Aires passenger arrival

Use this recipe when a deceased person may have arrived by sea at Buenos Aires. Gather name forms, a date range, embarkation port and ship if known. An index miss leaves the arrival open until you check the surviving books for that voyage.

## Search the CEMLA index

Search CEMLA by passenger name. Record the declared marital status, arrival date, ship and embarkation port when shown. According to the community guide, check for gaps before reading a miss [S1† @2026-11-22]. CEMLA describes damage to books and uneven coverage. Its FAQ says the 1933–1937 results principally cover Italian and Spanish nationals and include some months and years for other nationalities [S2 @2026-11-22].

## Carry the hit or miss to the original books

For 1882–1937 sea arrivals at Buenos Aires, search the Archivo General de la Nación passenger books by name, arrival range or ship. Open the register image when supplied. For a miss with a known voyage, use the vessel and arrival date as retrieval keys for the books [S3 @2026-11-22]. Record the book and page or image locator separately from any transcript or download.

## Check later records

For a later voyage, check the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones’ historical records service and ask which records survive for the year before selecting a certificate. Its admission or residence certificate and its movement certificate answer different questions [S4 @2026-11-22] [S5 @2026-11-22].

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: request --> Before sending the request, check the passenger’s consent where required and identify the passenger and requester details and proofs that would go to the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones.

Submit a migration record request only after confirming coverage, eligibility and the identity or kinship documents the service requires.
