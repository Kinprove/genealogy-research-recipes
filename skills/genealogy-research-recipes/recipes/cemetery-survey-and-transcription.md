# Survey a cemetery and produce a relocatable transcription

Use this recipe to survey a cemetery or an entire section and produce a reusable transcription. Use the same marker identifier on the map, inscription and photograph. For investigating a particular burial, use the burial evidence recipe.

## Lay out the survey and number the markers

Sketch the boundaries, access paths and fixed landmarks. Divide the site into recognizable sections and rows. Choose a starting corner and record the direction of each pass. Assign each physical marker a unique identifier even if it names several people. Put that identifier on the sketch, transcription and image labels [S2].

According to the available interview excerpt, label both ends of a row with its first and last monument numbers and speak the same marker identifier into any audio recording [S1†].

## Reconcile the new survey with earlier readings

Check each completed row against the sketch and labelled photos before leaving the section. Record inaccessible ground and illegible markers as separate limits on coverage [S2].

According to the available interview excerpt, compare a resurvey with the previous version to account for new, broken or missing stones [S1†]. Carry a vanished marker forward under the earlier survey’s date and locator. Give any new inspection date its own status so the old text is never mistaken for a new reading.

## Index and deposit the finished survey

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: publish --> The receiving society or library can make the inscription index, maps and images available to readers. Clear the privacy gate in the skill for any inscriptions, images or notes identifying people who may be living. Keep a local copy of restricted material.

Index the names to marker identifiers and redraw the finished map with the same labels and a north arrow. Spot-check that a second person can use the map to locate a sampled marker [S2]. According to the available interview excerpt, Gorr usually gives the finished survey to a local genealogy society or library [S1†]. If the user chooses to deposit the finished survey with a local genealogy society or library, confirm that it accepts the material.
