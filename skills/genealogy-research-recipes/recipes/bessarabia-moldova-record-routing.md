# Route a Bessarabian or Moldovan record by date, district and bank of the Dniester

Use this recipe before you search for a birth, marriage, death or census entry from Bessarabia, today's Moldova or the left bank of the Dniester. It sends each event to the state, district and archive of its own date. It prevents six failures: routing by the word "Bessarabia" or by today's border, reading Ясский уезд (Yassy uezd) as the Romanian city of Iași, sending a post-1940 search for the north of Khotin or for the south to Chișinău, looking for a left-bank event in Bessarabian books, reading a FamilySearch name-search miss as an absence, and correcting a Romanian or Cyrillic spelling instead of searching both.

It covers the period routing, the left bank, the 1941–1944 Transnistria fonds, ANA fond 211 for metrical books, the revision lists and Romanian–Cyrillic name lanes. Link out for the shared steps:

- **Parish candidates, the consistory copy, delo and leaf span, margin notes:** [Russian Orthodox metrical-book recovery](russian-orthodox-metrical-book-recovery.md).
- **A Jewish entry:** the registering rabbinate and its substitutes in [Russian-Empire Jewish metrical-book recovery](russian-empire-jewish-metrical-book-recovery.md); JewishGen's Bessarabia revision-list fields (previous-revision age, former household number) in [JewishGen search and family reconstruction](jewishgen-search-family-reconstruction.md), sections "Ages" and "Further pivots".
- **Chaining a household across revisions:** [revision-list household chaining](russian-revision-list-household-chaining.md).
- **Old Rite registers and the other Old Believer series:** [Old Believer and sectarian record recovery](old-believer-sectarian-record-recovery.md).
- **A village you cannot place, or one with several names:** [historical place-name resolution](historical-place-name-resolution.md).
- **Frame versus leaf, composite volumes and the inspection ledger:** [archival image coverage verification](archival-image-coverage-verification.md).
- **Browsing an unindexed FamilySearch image group:** [FamilySearch practical workflows](familysearch-practical-record-workflows.md), workflow 3.
- **The recording office's form of a name as its own search lane:** [corpus-seeded name search](corpus-seeded-name-search.md).
- **A German colonist recorded abroad as born in "Germany" or "Russia":** [Russian-Empire emigrant origin recovery](russian-empire-emigrant-origin-recovery.md).

## Inputs to gather first

Gather these before searching:

- the event type and approximate year
- the village under every name form seen (Russian, Romanian, Soviet), each with the record it came from
- the district exactly as the record writes it (uezd, județ or raion), with that record's date
- which bank of the Dniester the village lies on
- the confession: Orthodox, Old Rite, Lutheran, Roman Catholic or Jewish
- any archive cipher (fond, opis, delo) or FamilySearch image group already seen

## 1. Fix the state and district on the event date

- **Route by date and district, not by the word "Bessarabia".** The same village was recorded by different states:
  - **Russian Empire, until 1918:** the Bessarabian oblast, later guberniya, divided into uezds [S1].
  - **The south only, 1856–1878:** the Treaty of Paris gave Izmail and Cahul uezds to the Principality of Moldavia [S1], [S2]. Russia regained them in 1878 as one Izmail uezd [S1].
  - **Romania, 1918–1940:** Bessarabia joined Romania in 1918 [S1], [S3].
  - **Soviet, 1940–1941:** Soviet forces occupied Bessarabia in June 1940, and the Moldavian SSR was created on 2 August 1940 [S3].
  - **Romania, 1941–1944:** Romanian rule returned as the Bessarabia Governorate; in the south it ran from 19 July 1941 to 25 August 1944 [S4]. East of the Dniester, Romania ran the Transnistria Governorate (step 3).
  - **Soviet again from 1944:** in the south from late August 1944 [S4].
- **Do not apply the late uezd list to earlier years.** The eight uezds Akkerman, Bălți, Bender, Izmail, Kishinev, Orhei, Soroki and Khotin are the state after 1887. Before 1856 the south had separate Izmail and Cahul uezds; from 1878 it formed one Izmail uezd; and the name Bălți uezd dates only from 1887 [S1].
- **Read Ясский уезд (Yassy uezd) as the Bălți district before 1887.** A decree of 6 March 1887 renamed Ясский уезд, whose seat was Bălți, to Белецкий (Bălți) uezd [S1], [S5]. In a pre-1887 Bessarabian record, Ясский уезд means this district, not the Romanian city of Iași. The ANA opis labelled "Iași" needs the same check (step 4).
- **For the south in 1856–1878, do not expect the Russian imperial series to cover the event.** Check the ANA opisi for Cahul and Izmail year by year (step 4). Record a year with no section as a jurisdiction gap, not as a lost book.
- **Before choosing a successor archive, find which side of the 1940 line the village fell on.** Two parts went to the Ukrainian SSR, not the Moldavian SSR:
  - the northern part of Khotin uezd, into Chernivtsi oblast, formed on 7 August 1940 [S6];
  - the south, largely the historical Budjak, into Akkerman oblast, formed on 7 August 1940 and renamed Izmail oblast in December 1940. It merged into Odesa oblast on 15 February 1954 [S2], [S4], [S7].

  For a Soviet-era record from those parts, search the Ukrainian successor archives (Chernivtsi or Odesa oblast), not Chișinău. The Izmail oblast's archival section was wound up in February 1954 after the merger [S7]. Fix the village under its 1940 name with the place-name recipe first.

## 2. Look for a left-bank event outside the Bessarabian books

- **Do not search Bessarabian books for an imperial-era event east of the Dniester,** apart from the Tiraspol exception below. The stretch of the left bank that later formed the Moldavian ASSR lay in Podolia guberniya (Balta uezd) and Kherson guberniya (Tiraspol and Ananiev uezds) [S8].
- **Search the Podolia-consistory books for Balta uezd** in the Khmelnytskyi oblast archive (f. 315, the Podolia consistory). For some parishes they are in the Odesa oblast archive, the Mykolaiv oblast archive (f. 484 in the catalogue entries seen) or TsDIAK in Kyiv (f. 224) [S10], [S11].
- **Search the Kherson-consistory books** (Tiraspol and Ananiev uezds) in the Odesa oblast archive. Its published catalogue covers both the Kherson and the Podolia consistory [S10]. Then work parish by parish with the Orthodox metrical-book recipe.
- **Check ANA as well for Tiraspol uezd.** ANA fond 211 has op. 23 "Tiraspol" for 1879–1910 and 1911–1923 [S9], although the uezd lay in Kherson guberniya [S8]. Search both op. 23 and the Odesa archive for a Tiraspol-uezd parish. The ANA page does not say which consistory sent these books, so do not state one; take it from the book's own title.
- **From 1924 to 1940, route left-bank events to the Moldavian ASSR.** It lay inside the Ukrainian SSR and was formed on 12 October 1924 from parts of the former Balta, Tiraspol and Ananiev uezds. Its capital was Balta, then Tiraspol from 1929. In 1940 its western part joined the Moldavian SSR [S3], [S8]. Fix each village's unit for the event date. Do not infer membership of the ASSR from a later Moldovan or Ukrainian address.
- **Expect Latin script in ASSR documents of 1932–1938, and Cyrillic before and after** [S8], [S13]. Read each document's script from the document itself.

## 3. For 1941–1944 east of the Dniester, add the Transnistria Governorate fonds

- **Expect Romanian files for land that was in the Ukrainian SSR before and after the war.** The Romanian Transnistria Governorate held the land between the Dniester and the Southern Bug: parts of today's Vinnytsia, Odesa and Mykolaiv oblasts and the Moldovan left bank. It was created under the German–Romanian agreement signed at Bendery (Tighina) on 30 August 1941. Its centre was Tiraspol, then Odesa from October 1941, and it ended in March 1944 [S12].
- **Search its fonds at the State Archive of Odesa Oblast (DAOO)** [S12]:
  - R-2242, Main Registry of the Governorate, 1941–1944, mainly Romanian;
  - R-2264, Labour Directorate of the Governorate, 1941–1944, Romanian and Russian;
  - R-2357, Ovidiopol county prefecture, 1941–1943, German, Romanian and Russian.
- **Use them for residence, labour and persecution, not for a vital event.** They are administrative files, largely about the persecution and forced labour of Jews, not civil registers [S12].
- **Search the fond prefix in both scripts.** EHRI writes it as Latin P-, its rendering of Cyrillic Р- [S12].

## 4. Search Bessarabian metrical books in ANA fond 211 by opis

- **Search ANA fond 211, "Registrele parohiilor din Basarabia" (parish registers of Bessarabia).** Its table runs from 1811 to 1923, although the page title says 1812–1923 [S9]. It holds Orthodox, Lutheran, Roman Catholic, Old Rite and Jewish (mozaică) registers, mostly in Russian and Romanian, with some Polish, Hebrew and Latin [S9]. So route a Bessarabian crown-rabbinate, Lutheran or Catholic register here by its district opis, not only through JewishGen. Read each register's language from the register.
- **Choose the opis by district and years.** Each opis is linked from the ANA page as a file [S9]. The page listed these on 2026-11-02; take the current numbers from the page:

  The labels are Romanian. Tighina is Bender and Cetatea-Albă is Akkerman, as the page itself shows. "jud." marks a county (județ) and "or." a town (oraș), so op. 3 is the Chișinău district and op. 11 the town.
- **Do not file op. 12 under the Romanian city.** The page labels op. 12 only "Iași" (1840–1892) [S9]. Its years overlap the Bessarabian Yassy uezd (step 1). Read the opis file's title and the books' headings to fix which district it covers.
- **Op. 12 aside, two opisi lie outside Bessarabia:** op. 23 Tiraspol (Kherson guberniya, step 2) and op. 24 Cobadin, Constanța county, Romania, 1920–1935 [S9].
- **Read the south against the 1856–1878 cession.** The Cahul opisi stop in 1852 and 1856, and the Izmail op. 20 starts in 1871 [S9]. Check op. 20 for 1871–1878 before you call those years absent.
- **Check the Khotin opisi for the north too.** Op. 19 and op. 34 are Khotin [S9], although the north of the uezd went to Chernivtsi oblast in 1940 [S6]. Look up a northern parish in them before you turn to Chernivtsi.
- **Read an opis's years as a section range.** They do not prove that each year or parish survives. Check each opis file's own title dates against the site table, then the delo list for the parish and year.
- **After 1923, look outside fond 211.** The fond stops in 1923, op. 24 aside [S9]. A later Romanian-period event needs another fond.
- **Browse FamilySearch "Moldova, Church Books, 1811–1936"; do not name-search it.** The collection (1943763) offers image browsing and no name index, with images from the state archive in Chișinău [S14]. A name-search miss there tests nothing. Browse by locality and year, and map each image group to its fond 211 opis and delo through the book's title page. The collection runs to 1936, past the end of fond 211, so do not assume every image group is fond 211.

## 5. Search revision and census lists by fond, not by name

- **Search Bessarabian revision lists (ревизские сказки) mainly in ANA fond 134.** It is the Bessarabian Treasury Chamber (Camera de stat a Guberniei Basarabiei, Russian Казённая палата). Its own dates are 1824–1917, and it has three inventories [S15]. Most revision lists sit in its opis 2 [S17], [S18].
- **Look for a list from before 1824 outside fond 134.** The fond starts in 1824 [S15]. Other ANA fonds hold lists too, fond 3 among them [S17], [S18].
- **Browse FamilySearch's Moldova poll-tax collection; do not name-search it.** "Moldova, Poll Tax Census (Revision Lists) and Census Lists, 1796–1917" (collection 1985804) shows images only, from the Moldovan national archives [S16], and has no index [S17]. Its poll-tax (revision) lists cover 1796–1859. Its family, resident, population, colonist and ethnic-group lists cover 1818–1917 [S16]. Browse by volume.
- **Do not read a browse label as a full archive cipher.** Before you map a volume back to ANA, read its fond, opis and delo on the volume's title page, or match it against the ANA inventory.
- **Do not stop at JewishGen.** Its Bessarabia revision-list database covers Jewish communities [S19], so a JewishGen-only search cannot find a non-Jewish household. For a Jewish household, use the JewishGen recipe for that database's fields.
- **Chain the household across revisions** with the revision-list recipe.

## 6. Generate Romanian and Cyrillic name forms as query lanes

- **Search each form as its own lane; never correct the source.** Keep the literal form in the name register, with its record and period.
- **Add the letter lanes from Romanian to Cyrillic:** ș→ш, ț→ц, j→ж, ce/ci→че/чи, che/chi→ке/ки and ghe/ghi→ге/ги [S20], [S13]. For ge/gi, search the Russian-style дже/джи, and also ӂе/ӂи, written with the Soviet Moldovan letter for ge/gi from 1967 [S20], [S13].
- **Treat ă→э and â/î→ы as the Soviet Moldovan Cyrillic standard,** as in mână = мынэ [S20]. It was official in the Moldavian ASSR before 1932 and from 1938, and in the Moldavian SSR from 1940 until the law of 31 August 1989 restored the Latin script [S13]. Do not assume these values in an imperial Russian entry. There, read the form as written and add lanes with other vowels.
- **Cross the 1918 and 1944 lines with given-name lanes.** The same man can appear under a Romanian form in a 1918–1940 or 1941–1944 record and under a Russian form before 1918 or after 1944 (step 1). Even fond 211's registers are written in both Russian and Romanian [S9]. Build each Russian–Romanian pair from the person's own records or a name dictionary. A matched pair is a query, not an identity.
- **Read a Тодор (Todor) form as a lead to the Bulgarian colonies of southern Bessarabia.** Тодор is the most frequent male name in a study of Bessarabian Bulgarian names in Bolgrad district [S21]. Bulgarian colonists settled in southern Bessarabia [S22]. Use the form to add colony parishes to the search, not as proof of origin.

## Close with a routing packet

Return:

- **Jurisdiction row:** the village under each name form, its district (uezd, județ or raion) and the state on the event date, the bank of the Dniester, and the side of the 1940 line.
- **Series rows:** for each event, the series (fond 211 opis, fond 134 opis, Podolia or Kherson consistory, Transnistria fond, or a Soviet record), the archive, the locator down to delo and leaf, and the status: searched, found, not found or not online.
- **Image-group rows:** the FamilySearch collection and image group, its title-page heading, the opis and delo it maps to, and the frames inspected.
- **Gaps:** jurisdiction gaps (the south in 1856–1878), Romanian-period events after 1923 outside fond 211, section ranges not checked year by year, and unresolved labels such as op. 12.
- **Name register:** every literal form with its record and period, and every lane searched, with where.

The model assembles this packet. The researcher decides identity and kinship.
