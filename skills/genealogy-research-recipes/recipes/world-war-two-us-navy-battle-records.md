# Verify U.S. Navy participation in a World War II battle

Use this recipe when a Navy ancestor or family account names a World War II action such as Midway. Establish the person’s assignment and the ship’s or unit’s action separately.

## Establish assignment on the battle date

If a World War II draft card is the starting point, establish the branch from service evidence. A miss in the World War II Army Enlistment Records leaves possible Navy service unresolved. For enlisted Navy service, use Navy muster rolls. [S7], [S9]

For an enlisted sailor, find the vessel or station in the World War II muster rolls, including the records around and after the battle date. Read changes, transfers and absences before concluding the sailor was aboard. Start from the service record if the ship is unknown. [S1], [S2], [S4]

Search for the sailor by name in MyHeritage’s *United States, World War II Navy Muster Rolls, 1938-1949*. [S5 @2026-11-21], [S6 @2026-11-21] If the index returns no match, keep the assignment unresolved and use the National Archives’ muster-roll guide to locate rolls for a known vessel or station around the battle date. [S2 @2026-11-21]

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> The sailor’s name goes to the selected service, Ancestry or Fold3, as the muster-roll search query.

If you need another name index, search for the sailor by name in the *World War II Navy Muster Rolls* at Ancestry or Fold3. [S7 @2026-11-22], [S10 @2026-11-22], [S11 @2026-11-22]

Use the enlistment date and any recorded enlistment place on the roll to distinguish same-name hits. Take the ship or station from the roll heading and keep the enlistment date separate from the date the sailor reported for duty. [S8], [S13], [S14]

For each posting, record the ship or station, the date the sailor reported or the change occurred, and the date of the roll. Sort the postings by reporting or change date before deciding which assignment spans the battle. Leave gaps in the dated evidence unresolved. [S7], [S8]

If a report of changes records a transfer to a naval hospital, enter the transfer date and destination before deciding whether the sailor was aboard on the battle date. [S15], [S16]

For an officer aboard ship, look in the deck log for officer lists and changes of assignment. Use the same deck log’s daily entries to track movements and any named casualties or personnel changes. [S2], [S3]

For Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941, use the *Pearl Harbor Muster Rolls* entry in the National Archives’ digitization guide to locate the ship’s rolls and check changes around the attack. [S7 @2026-11-22], [S11 @2026-11-22] For a reported death, scan the Pearl Harbor National Memorial’s U.S. Navy casualty list for a ship or station lead. [S12 @2026-11-22]

## Establish the ship or unit’s action

Use the ship’s deck log and the command’s war diary or action report for the battle dates. Correlate the unit’s activity with the person’s assignment, then check named casualty or missing reports for a claim of injury, death or disappearance. Attribute an individual role in the action only when the records describe it. [S1], [S3], [S4]

When a service file names a Navy action, use the geographic area and the battle or campaign name to locate the folder in *World War II by battle or campaign*, a subseries of *World War II casualty lists and related records, 1941–52* in the Bureau of Naval Personnel records (RG 24). Look for the sailor in the folder’s casualty lists and compare any identifiers with the service file. If the battle folder is missing or yields no match, check the *Comprehensive list by operation* under the operation name or *World War II by ship* under the vessel name. Keep casualty status unresolved if the records do not identify the sailor. [S17], [S18]

## Trace evacuation after a wound

For a reported wound at Iwo Jima or Okinawa, look for the sailor’s evacuation transfer in the casualty records or the assigned ship’s deck log. If a record identifies a hospital ship, follow that ship’s deck log from the transfer date through disembarkation. Use the port and disembarkation date to narrow the search for a named admission at a receiving facility. If the destination is unclear, check Guam, Saipan and Tinian as leads. Confirm the destination only when a record ties the sailor to the receiving facility. [S17], [S18], [S19]
