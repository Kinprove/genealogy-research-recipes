# Trace a U.S. merchant seaman through protection papers and voyage records

Use this recipe for an early U.S. merchant seaman identified by name, vessel, port or a protection
reference. Use [military service routing](us-military-service-type-record-routing.md) for Navy service
and [federal finding aids](us-national-archives-finding-aids-and-microfilm.md) to resolve a
series or microfilm locator.

## Find the citizenship papers

Start with the port that created the record and the date of the voyage or application. According to
the presenter, check customs records for early protection applications [S1† @2026-11-22]. In RG 36,
use Entry UD 60 to identify the particular port’s holdings. Look separately for an application, proof
of citizenship, certificate register and quarterly abstract. Read any witness statements or
attachments for a stated birthplace, citizenship and relationship [S2 @2026-11-22].

According to the presenter, use Ruth Priest Dixon’s indexes to get to the underlying application
[S1†]. For Philadelphia, use the Dixon and Eberly index for 1796–1823 and its supplement through 1861.
Carry the port, application or certificate number and date to the original record when available [S2],
[S3].

For an application from 1916–1940, check RG 41, Entry UD 159, National Archives Identifier 2788575.
Use the port to check the regional exceptions for Philadelphia and Boston. The application can carry a
birth date and place, vessel, rating, signature, photo and citizenship attachments [S2 @2026-11-22],
[S7 @2026-11-22]. According to the presenter, this later application series is the place to look for
attached photos [S1† @2026-11-22]. Use documentary identifiers to test identity and record any
personal recognition as an attributed claim.

## Follow the vessel or an impressment lead

According to the presenter, start with a known home-port clue for crew lists [S1† @2026-11-22]. Follow
it to the customs district or port that received the voyage record. Use the vessel, port and arrival
or clearance date to check the relevant RG 36 or RG 41 holdings. Read the series scope before treating
an absent listing as a miss [S4 @2026-11-22].

Use the seaman’s port clue to identify the shipping commissioner’s port for the voyage. In the
shipping articles, search by the vessel and voyage date. Start with the New York series for 1872–1937
if the voyage fits. Check the dates and filing arrangement separately for another port
[S5 @2026-11-22]. According to the presenter, shipping articles are a separate search from crew lists
[S1† @2026-11-22].

For reported impressment, check the Department of State’s RG 59 series. Use M2025’s indexes and
registers of applications for release for 1793–1802. For dates outside that span, check the other RG
59 series in the finding aid. Keep the date of the application for release separate from the
impressment date [S6 @2026-11-22]. According to the presenter, this State Department route can
supplement the protection search [S1† @2026-11-22].

Return one row per document with the creating port, date, vessel, locator and the person’s role. Keep
a planned posting separate from a documented voyage. Use [evidence
judgment](evidence-proof-judgment.md) for conflicting identities.
