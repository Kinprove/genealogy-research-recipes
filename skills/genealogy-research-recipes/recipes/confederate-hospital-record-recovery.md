# Recover Confederate hospital records behind a Civil War service entry

Use a dated wound, illness or transfer in a Confederate service record to identify the place and possible hospitals. Search the hospital’s own records as well as the service record.

## Trace the treatment route in the service file

According to the presenter, the medical cards in the service jacket can supply separate stops along a soldier’s treatment route. [S9†]

Look for Confederate volunteer medical cards inside the full compiled military service record (CMSR) jacket. Use the National Archives digitization list to identify the microfilm publication for the soldier’s state or organization, then follow a listed Fold3 or other provider link if available. Read every medical card, including any grouped in an inner envelope. [S10 @2026-11-22], [S11 @2026-11-22]

Put the dated entries in event order. Log the origin, destination and record locator for each transfer, including any transfer point, hospital steamer or prison named in the file. Use the next recorded admission to check where the patient went after a stop in transit. [S12]

## Identify the hospital and series

According to the presenter, begin a Charlottesville search with the General Hospital and use the Confederate preliminary inventory and Beers’s guide to look for records by hospital. [S1†]

Check Elizabeth Bethel’s Preliminary Inventory 101 and Henry Putney Beers’s guide for hospital records listed under Record Group 109. Search the hospital name and the place. Use the dated service entry to narrow the likely register runs, then compare the runs with the gaps in surviving hospital records. [S2 @2026-11-18], [S3 @2026-11-18]

For a transfer to the general hospital at Point Lookout, Maryland, search under Hammond General Hospital. For a bare Point Lookout entry, use the dated record to distinguish the hospital from the nearby Camp Hoffman prison. [S13]

For a Richmond entry naming the Receiving and Wayside Hospital, check General Hospital No. 9 at 17th and Grace streets, listed under both names in January 1864. For a bare Wayside entry, check the date and address before assigning a hospital number. The September 1863 Richmond directory lists Wayside Hospital as No. 12 and Receiving Hospital as No. 9. [S14], [S15]

According to the presenter’s advice, check the Confederate inventory when a Union soldier was treated at a Confederate hospital. [S16†]

For a Union patient, search by treating hospital in Chapter VI of Preliminary Inventory 101 (RG 109). At Richmond General Hospital No. 21, check the Union patient registers by ward and date. The Ward D register for 1863–1865 is listed in volume 165 and its copy in volume 162. [S17 @2026-11-22]

For a Union prisoner, also check RG 249, Records of the Commissary General of Prisoners. Use its Danville prison hospital register for a Danville admission in 1863–1865. [S18 @2026-11-22]

## Find the named patient

According to the presenter’s advice, use the General Hospital patient index and the two register volumes for a named Charlottesville admission. [S1†]

For Charlottesville General Hospital, use the separate patient index in Chapter VI, volume 216, and follow its entry into the register volumes 214 and 215. Record the admission entry with its own volume and page. [S3 @2026-11-18], [S4 @2026-11-18]

When opening a hospital’s records, distinguish a patient register from case accounts or statistical returns. A name search in a statistical return supplies no named patient coverage. Use a dated register entry to tie treatment to a patient and a unit. [S2], [S3]

Robert J. Driver Jr.’s Prince Edward County volume covers Farmville Hospital deaths, according to the presenter’s book roundup. [S7†] For a death at Farmville General Hospital from April 1862 through April 1865, look up the name in the volume’s separate hospital death list (ISBN 9780788426971). Check the death list even if the county soldiers’ roster has no entry for the patient. [S8]

## Search related papers at other custodians

Alongside the Charlottesville register, check the James Edgar Chancellor papers at the University of Virginia for the 1862–1863 surgical cases. For Louisiana State Museum leads, check RG 326 at item level for surviving furlough, discharge and orders material for the relevant hospital. Keep these series separate when recording which dates and patients you searched. [S5 @2026-11-18], [S6 @2026-11-18]

## Search the published medical and surgical cases

For a wound or illness, open the six-part scan list for *The Medical and Surgical History of the War of the Rebellion* at McGill’s Maude Abbott Medical Museum. Search a local copy of the OCR text for the soldier’s name in each relevant part. If the text search misses, browse the chapter for the disease or wound location. Limit any negative finding to the published cases and parts you searched. [S19 @2026-11-22], [S20 @2026-11-22]

Carry alternate initials and surname spellings from any CMSR cross-reference card into the published-case search. Compare the case’s company, regiment, hospital and admission date with the service file before accepting a variant-name match. [S21], [S22]

Record the part, volume, printed page and case number. Read through the treatment, later dated entries, any autopsy and any cited illustration. Record the final disposition the case gives. In Part II, Volume I, case 443 records apparent improvement on 15 October 1863 and death on 23 October (p. 188). [S19], [S22]
