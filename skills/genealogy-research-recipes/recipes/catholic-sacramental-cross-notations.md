# Follow Catholic baptism–marriage cross-notations between parishes

Use this when a Catholic baptism or marriage index hit, or an image of the entry, gives you a parish and a register date range, and you need the other sacrament's entry in a different parish. Indexes usually keep only names, the date and perhaps an origin. The marriage entry's statement of where each party was baptized, and the marriage note later added to the baptism entry, appear only on the original page. Read both directions. To weigh a later note against the original statement, use [evidence/proof judgment](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/evidence-proof-judgment.md) (§ Follow record versions and test disputed dates). For AI transcription of margins, use [AI for documentary records](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/ai-for-documentary-records.md) (§ Worked mini-examples). If the baptism parish is in Ireland, open the images through [Irish Catholic parish image recovery](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/irish-catholic-parish-image-recovery.md).

## Know the rule: Ne temere, Easter 1908

The papal decree *Ne temere* (dated August 1907) took effect on Easter Sunday, 19 April 1908. Its article IX §2 requires the pastor who performs a marriage to notify the pastor of each party's baptism parish, either directly or through the bishop's office (the episcopal curia), and requires the marriage to be noted in the book of baptisms. This is why marriage entries state baptism parishes and why baptism entries carry marriage notes. In one presenter's words, after Easter 1908 "you may begin to see" these notes [S2]. Another presenter describes the marriage priest writing to the baptism priest [S1].

Another presenter describes an undated order from the Vatican requiring priests to record "all of their sacraments" in the baptism register [S4]. The documented rules list specific events, not all sacraments. *Ne temere* names only marriage. In the 1983 Code of Canon Law, canon 535 §2 lists confirmation, marriage-related changes of status, adoption, sacred orders and perpetual religious profession, among others, and canon 1682 §2 requires a declaration of nullity to be noted in the baptism register. So a baptism register's remarks column can also carry confirmation, religious profession or ordination entries [S5].

## Do not stop at the index

Indexes can leave out the baptism-parish statements and the marriage notes, so never treat an index as complete. They give the names, the date and perhaps an origin, while the priests' notes stay on the page [S1]. Findmypast's Philadelphia baptism search fields, for example, include no marriage field. Go from every index hit to the original register image before you decide whether a cross-notation exists. Read the whole entry, the margins and every column of the page, including the far-right remarks column [S1], [S5].

## Marriage → baptism

1. In the original marriage entry, copy word for word each party's stated baptism parish or church. Also copy any wording such as "baptism confirmed at…" [S1].
2. Copy non-Catholic churches as well. In one example, the marriage priest noted that a groom was baptized or born (the presenter could not tell which) at a Methodist church in another Pennsylvania town. The presenter then treated that church as a new record source [S3]. Keep the uncertain wording as written. Do not settle it as baptism or as birth.
3. Open that parish's baptism register for the stated person and period. Look in the margin or the remarks column for a later marriage note [S4], [S5]. The baptism parish may be overseas. In one example, a man baptized in County Cork in 1877 married in Philadelphia, and his Irish baptism entry carries the marriage [S4].
4. The marriage may have taken place in a third parish. Ideally the bride's baptism parish, the groom's baptism parish and the marriage parish all refer to each other, but not always [S1]. Treat each of the three as a separate retrieval target.

## Baptism → marriage (when the marriage is missing)

1. Open the person's baptism entry in the baptism parish. Read the margin and the far-right column. In a Philadelphia register on Findmypast's Catholic Heritage collection, the presenter read that column's heading as "record of marriage, religious profession or other subdiaconate, remarks" [S5]. The same register also records confirmation [S5].
2. Copy from the note the spouse, church, date, witnesses and officiant, where these are given. In the presenter's example, a 1919 baptism entry gives a 1943 marriage at the Cathedral of Saints Peter and Paul, Philadelphia, with witnesses and officiant [S5]. Another presenter's example has a baptism note giving the spouse and the place of marriage [S2].
3. Use the note only to find the actual marriage entry in the named church. Then search for a civil marriage registration for that place and date [S5].
4. If the online image does not identify the officiating church, record that gap and name the diocesan archive to ask. In one example the presenter planned to contact the Archdiocese of Philadelphia [S3]. The archdiocese's Catholic Historical Research Center (CHRC) takes research requests by form for records 75 or more years old. Newer records, and all certified copies, come from the parish. Philadelphia archdiocesan baptisms and marriages before 1920 are on Findmypast.

## Treat the note as later and derivative

- A marriage note on a baptism entry was written later, from a report sent by the marriage parish. It is not the marriage record. Record it as its own observation: the page and image locator, the date of the note and the hand, where these can be seen, and its literal text. Do not merge it into the original baptism statement [S4], [S1].
- Keep the baptism date, any birth date given and any marriage note as separate dated observations [S2].
- Read a missing note by date:
  - **Marriage before Easter 1908:** a blank margin is expected and tells you nothing.
  - **Marriage after Easter 1908:** a blank margin is weak negative evidence. It does not show there was no marriage. Practice was "ideally, not all the time", priests' detail varied [S1], and one presenter does not know how widespread the practice is [S5]. Use the blank to search the other parishes and civil records. Do not conclude anything from it.
- Witnesses and godparents named in either entry are leads for associates or kin. They are not proof of a relationship [S4], [S5].

## Return a linked pair or a documented gap

For each person, return:

- the marriage entry's stated baptism parish (literal);
- the baptism entry's marriage note (literal, with its date or hand if visible);
- the register, page and image locator for each entry;
- which direction was searched, which registers or pages were inspected, and whether the marriage falls before or after Easter 1908;
- any uncertain baptized-or-born wording, left as written;
- follow-up requests still open: the named marriage church, a diocesan archive or the parish, a civil registration, or another denomination's church.

A note found without its marriage entry is still only a lead.
