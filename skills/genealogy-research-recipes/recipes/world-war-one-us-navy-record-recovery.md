# Trace a U.S. Navy sailor’s assignment and death in World War I

Use this recipe for a U.S. Navy sailor with a First World War ship or station clue, or for a sailor reported to have died during the war. Match the vessel by name, type and date before attributing a ship’s activity to the sailor.

## Find the right muster-roll run

For a World War I U.S. Navy vessel or shore unit, use RG 24, *Muster Rolls of Ships and Shore Establishments, 1898–1939*, PI-123 Entry 134, NAID 572847. Choose the 1898–1930 segment and browse by vessel or shore unit. A miss in the separate 1938–1949 World War II collection leaves World War I assignment open. [S2 @2026-11-22] [S5 @2026-11-22]

Read the rolls for the dates around the event. Log each dated report, change of assignment or transfer naming the sailor and continue in the receiving ship or station’s rolls. [S2]

## Identify the vessel for the date

According to the presenter, compare a vessel’s type and dated names, operators and roles to link its successive designations and distinguish same-named ships [S1†]. Search under the name used on the event date and retain later names and designations as cross-references.

## Follow a death into the casualty records

According to the presenter, check the published Navy death roster for the individual [S1†]. Use *Officers and enlisted men of the United States Navy who lost their lives during the World War* for deaths from April 6, 1917, through November 11, 1918. Record each entry under its own event date and use any named ship or hospital for the next search. [S3]

For naval medical documentation, check RG 52’s medical certificates and casualty lists, then check its registers of patients when the report names a naval hospital. Record the hospital and the record series independently of the ship assignment. [S4 @2026-11-22]
