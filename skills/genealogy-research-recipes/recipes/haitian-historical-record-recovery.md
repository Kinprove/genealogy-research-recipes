# Find historical Haitian civil records and Saint-Domingue property claims

Use this recipe for historical records of deceased people in Haiti. Start with the locality and approximate date of the event, or the place and claimant named in a Saint-Domingue property lead.

## Choose the Haitian civil series

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> A civil-index query sends the deceased subject’s name and any locality or year entered to the Haitian Genealogy Association or FamilySearch. Apply the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before submitting a query.

Search the chosen index with the deceased subject’s name and the minimum locality or year needed. Keep information about living relatives local.

Start with the Association de Généalogie d’Haïti’s *Archives d’Haïti* index and the FamilySearch collection *Haiti, Civil Registration*. Check the locality and event year in each collection before treating a search miss as meaningful. For a FamilySearch miss, look up the locality and act type in its catalogue and check the listed volumes before broadening the name search. Use a hit to obtain the act locator and identify its creator. [S1 @2026-11-22] [S2 @2026-11-22] [S3 @2026-11-22]

## Trace a Saint-Domingue property claim

For a former owner or heir connected with confiscated property, check the alphabetical owner and heir sections of Oliver Gliech’s index, then trace the lead to the French commission’s *État détaillé des liquidations*. Use the BnF catalogue’s 1828–1833 publication listing to identify the volume. Record the named party’s capacity, the property and the commission’s disposition separately. ## Look for sources outside the civil series

Use the University of Florida’s Caribbean collections guide to follow the Haitian material into the Digital Library of the Caribbean. Search by locality and document type, and log whether a result is a record, a publication or contextual material. Keep these holdings separate from the civil registration coverage you checked. Return the locality, dates and series checked, the exact act or volume locator, and the evidence still missing.
