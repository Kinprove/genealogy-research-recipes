# Find historical U.S. Navy treatment in ship, station and hospital records

Use this recipe for a nineteenth-century Navy illness, injury, hospitalization or transfer when the
question names a person, approximate date and likely vessel, station or hospital. If the vessel or
station is unknown, establish it from [military service
records](us-military-service-type-record-routing.md). For a later treatment episode, record the treating facility and dates.
Verifying where its clinical files are held requires a separate search.

## Choose the ship, station or hospital series

Search the treating ship, station or hospital as well as the sailor. According to the presenter, check
RG 52 for ship or shore-station medical journals and a separate patient list [S1† @2026-11-22]. Use
§52.3.2 to match the date to the ship journals (1813–1910), station journals (1812–1912) or patient
lists (1830–1889). Check separately listed hospital admission papers for the relevant dates
[S4 @2026-11-22].

For Philadelphia, according to the presenter, start with the hospital’s RG 24 admission and discharge
register [S1† @2026-11-22]. Use §24.8.2 to locate the 1867–1917 series at the National Archives at
Philadelphia [S2 @2026-11-22], [S5 @2026-11-22]. Search by admission or discharge date and capture the
register entry as its own document.

## Compare centrally filed and hospital-held papers

According to the presenter, search separately for papers sent to the Bureau of Medicine and Surgery
and papers kept by the treating hospital [S1† @2026-11-22]. Use RG 52’s general records and individual
hospital listings to find the relevant journal, patient abstract or admission papers. For
Philadelphia, check the regional RG 52 holdings alongside the RG 24 register [S3 @2026-11-22],
[S4 @2026-11-22].

Return the sailor’s stated assignment, date interval, treating institution, each series and locator,
and dated admission, discharge and transfer observations. Record an unsearched series or missing date
span as a coverage limit. Use [federal finding aids](us-national-archives-finding-aids-and-microfilm.md)
when you have a series reference but no image.
