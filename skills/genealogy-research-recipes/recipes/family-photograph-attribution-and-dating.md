# Attribute and date an inherited family photograph from captions, provenance and imprints

Use this recipe when someone asks who is in an inherited family photograph, or when it was taken. The common mistake is to treat an inherited label, a resemblance or a single date clue as the answer: a caption of unknown authorship becomes a fact, a sitter is named because they look like a known relative, and one studio range or one hairstyle becomes "the date". Instead, record each identification as an attributed claim with its basis, turn every clue into an earliest or latest bound, and test an attribution against the documented lifetime of the person named, never against faces.

This recipe covers attribution and dating of an image already in hand. Capturing the object, its scans and its inheritance chain belongs to Family collection intake and capture; long recollections belong to Family interviews and oral tradition.

- For restored, colorized or AI-generated images as evidence, use [AI for documentary records](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/ai-for-documentary-records.md).
- Identity rulings that combine these claims belong in [evidence/proof judgment](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/evidence-proof-judgment.md).
- To read the directory series behind a studio's working span, use [city-directory observations](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/city-directory-observation-workflow.md).
- Cite each copy and its holder with [source citation](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/source-citation.md).

## Record each identification as an attributed claim

Use this when an image has a caption, inherited identification or new information from a relative. Preserve the attribution and its basis; do not identify a person by their face or compare faces across photographs. A name for every face is not a prerequisite [S1].

1. Start with the original object or best available received copy. Retain its source/holder, copy or inheritance chain, album/page context and literal captions or reverse inscriptions. Keep an old inscription, a later relative's label and your own contextual interpretation as separate statements. Record who supplied each identification and their basis of knowledge when available. A label without a known author stays an unattributed label [S1], [S2], [S3].
2. Transcribe struck-through, overwritten or corrected caption words as part of the literal reading. Mark the cancelled text, its replacement and the writer when known. Keep a cancelled name as a lead to test, for example as a nickname of the same sitter, as in [S4]; do not settle what it meant without the writer's own explanation.
3. When a reverse states an age at a dated occasion, keep the inscription literal and add any implied birth date beside it as your marked calculation: for example, one year and eight days on 19 November 1909 implies 11 November 1908, if the inscription is accurate. The derived date is a search lead, not a recorded birth [S5].
4. Ask about the image. Useful prompts include what occasion is remembered, where/when it was taken, who supplied the photograph and how the contributor knows. Keep an unanswered question open. When a relative attributes a name, record their words and source; familiarity or a machine suggestion does not turn that account into a confirmed identification [S6], [S1]. Record the user's own recognition the same way, as an attributed claim with its basis. Resemblance is never evidence by itself.
5. For remote feedback, a marked copy or worksheet can keep the image reference, existing caption and contributor's new account together. Preserve each response separately; do not overwrite the original caption with the newest guess. Longer recollections belong to Family interviews and oral tradition. Prepare material for the user's chosen sharing scope; this request alone does not authorize sending or publishing it [S6].
6. For a numbered group photograph, obtain the available full image and its associated name key. Bind each actually marked position to the literal key entry, retaining the image/key source relationship and any missing or ambiguous positions. Keep a contributor's newer attribution separate. Weigh the key by who made it and when: a key made with the image is strong evidence of who sat where, while a later or unattributed key is a claim like any other label. An absent key never authorizes substituting resemblance [S7].

## Record the context and look for other copies

Record non-biometric context relevant to the question: an inscription, a stated occasion, location, photographer/imprint or independently supported format/date information. Preserve contradictions with a proposed attribution rather than resolving them from appearance. For related copies, retain their sources and what inscriptions or provenance they add [S6], [S1].

To seek other prints:

1. Ask distant and collateral branches, heirs of the relevant household goods and lines present at a photographed event [S8], [S9].
2. Search the literal studio name and town [S10], check published family genealogies for reproductions of the whole image [S11], and reverse-search a cropped copy for exact matches [S12].
3. Record each copy's holder or host, uploader, displayed date, apparent generation and caption writer. Make observations from the copy nearest the original.
4. Another copy's caption is its source's attribution. Count matching captions across trees, books and sites as one attribution until someone shows an independent basis: wrong identifications are copied quickly. The earliest upload points to a holder, not a maker or sitter [S12]. A place found for one album item is a lead for its neighbours, not a finding. These are leads to ask, never resemblance matches.

## Bound the date from the photographer's imprint

1. Transcribe a photographer's imprint literally, including a partial name or address, any telephone number and the proprietor, with uncertain letters marked. Record whether the mount was trimmed for an album, separately from captions [S13], [S15], [S17].
2. Identify a candidate studio from the combined mark. Keep same-named studios elsewhere, branches, a possible publisher and a successor as separate candidates [S17].
3. Trace that exact firm name and address through dated city directories, censuses or a photographer index, noting editions an index omits. Dated advertisements or maps can corroborate [S13], [S14]. Record the edition, section or page consulted and the exact listing, and compare the observed address, telephone and proprietor between the available dates. A studio missing from an edition you have confirmed covers its trade and street is a negative result for that year; a missing entry in an edition of unknown coverage is only an omission [S17].
4. Before using a studio's working period as a date bound, record each source's range separately, with its citation: lexicon, regional reference, dated collection cards, AI/dealer summary. For a period photographer directory or studio database, record the entry consulted and the place and working years it states [S18]. Do not exclude a date because one summary ends earlier. In one studio-period check, a lexicon and later dated cards extended an AI-reported range [S19]; in a family-photo dating discussion, three sources gave different ranges [S20]. Typography on dated comparison cards gives a range, not a year [S19].
5. When a mount names only the photographer, other photographs from the same studio can supply its address. Match the repeated studio set (props such as a column or flower stand) against an addressed comparison card, and record which card and which features were compared. The comparison card's address applies to that card's period; a studio may have moved or reused its set elsewhere. Keep the recovered address as studio context. Nearness to barracks or an institution is a hypothesis about the sitter's affiliation, not an identification [S21].
6. Compare a supported studio place and date with documented residences to shortlist candidate lines [S15].
7. For a relative's review, copy the scans rather than moving the files into imprint-town folders, so the album order stays intact [S16].

Read the result as a bound on the studio's use of that imprint. An original print on the studio's own mount falls inside the working span; a reproduction or a successor's print can fall later. Keep studio observations apart from what they imply for the original exposure, a later print or reproduction, the mount stock and the annotation [S17]. A studio town is not the sitter's residence [S15]. Never infer the sitter, an event or the photographed place from the business mark alone. Shared imprints show no kinship; never group by resemblance. When the records do not establish a bound, keep the window unresolved.

## Turn every other date clue into a bound

Treat date clues as ranges or one-sided bounds. Record conflicting features instead of dropping them. This recipe supplies no dating table: take each format's or style's introduction date from a cited reference.

- Keep the exposure date and the processing/printing date distinct [S22].
- A print size, border or format cannot predate its introduction, so it gives an earliest date. It can stay in use for years, so it gives no latest date [S22].
- Clothing or hairstyle gives an earliest date. For an older sitter it gives no latest date, because older people kept earlier styles [S22].
- A dress, long hair or pink/blue coloring does not establish a child's sex. Long skirts marked a child's age: boys wore them until they were breeched, commonly between four and seven [S23].
- An old-system house number bounds the image only where dated directories show the change [S24].
- A licence plate's year bounds the photograph only approximately, because next year's plates could go on sale before the year began. The plate is a lead to the vehicle's owner, not to who is pictured [S25].

**Uniforms, insignia and medals.** For a uniformed portrait, keep the full image and make linked detail crops of the cockade, belt buckle, shoulder straps, badges and awards. Date each regulated element from its cited introducing order or a medal's establishment, and treat that as an earliest bound for the picture. Where an order set a later date for wear, take the earliest bound from that date: in [S21], a 1907 order set one uniform pattern for wear from 1909. Do not turn an abolition order into a latest bound: in the same discussion, a portrait dated after the cited abolition still showed the emblem [S21]. A jubilee medal's eligibility rule limits which candidates could have worn it; keep that apart from the photo-date bound. Record each order or statute as cited. A worn medal does not prove whose award it was.

## Test an attribution by documented-lifetime consistency

Test an inherited, profile or published identification for consistency, never by resemblance.

1. Compare the named person's documented life dates with the independently supported date of the sitting itself, not of a later print, copy or composite: a montage made around 1920 could add a brother killed in the First World War [S17]. A sitting that could not have happened in that lifetime is a misattachment to record. A same-named person of another generation becomes the next candidate to test, not a conclusion [S22].
2. For a dated occasion, calculate each proposed person's age from documented birth information.
3. For a group, compare number, sex and broad life stage with a documented household. Do not read a child's sex from dress, hair or coloring.
4. A fit shows consistency, not identity. A conflict other than the lifetime test in step 1 reopens the attribution [S13], [S22], [S26].

**Named sitters as date limits.** When a group photograph's sitters are named by a caption or an attributed account, and their birth years are documented independently, use them as date limits. A named child shown sets an earliest possible date. A documented sibling missing from the image may suggest a latest one. Record each limit with the identification and the document it rests on. An absent child may simply have been elsewhere, and one wrong attribution moves the whole range. Do not assign names from appearance to make the limits fit. In the dating discussion that motivates this check, the identifications stayed uncertain [S20].

**Keep the two directions apart.** A date bound derived from a naming cannot then test that same naming. If the earliest date rests on a named child's birth year, the image fitting that date says nothing for the child's name. Test a naming only against bounds that do not depend on it, and mark in the date table which bounds rest on which namings.

## Return

Return the attributed observations, conflicting claims, linked sources and next check needed. A historical/contextual check may assess an attribution, but it does not authorize a face-based identification. Keep edited/AI-altered copies linked as derivatives and use the unaltered available source for documentary observations [S1], [S6].

- **Attribution register.** For each identification:
  - the literal wording, with cancelled words marked
  - who supplied it, when, and their basis
  - an "unattributed label" flag where the author is unknown
  - numbered-key positions bound to their key entries
  - derived dates marked as your calculations
- **Date-bound table.** For each clue:
  - its source
  - what it bounds: exposure, print, mount or annotation
  - its direction: earliest, latest or range
  - whether it rests on a naming
  - the resulting window, left open where the records do not close it, with conflicts kept
- **Studio crosswalk.** The literal imprint, candidate studios kept separate, each dated listing with its edition and page, each working-period source with its range, and omissions and uninspected editions.
- **Copies register.** Each copy's holder or host, uploader, displayed date and caption writer, the copy used for observations, and edited or AI-altered copies linked as derivatives.
- **Consistency result** for each proposed person: fit, reopened (another conflict) or misattachment, with the documents used.
- **Next check** and the questions still open.
