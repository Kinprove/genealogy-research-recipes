# Trace descent from a Declaration of Independence signer

Open this recipe when the candidate signer is known and you need to identify a descent line or recover the evidence cited for it. Treat a compiled descendant line as a lead to prove one parent–child link at a time.

## 1. Find the Leach compilation

Search for Frank Willing Leach’s *A genealogy of the signers of the Declaration of Independence*. Follow the society’s FamilySearch catalogue route or use Microfilm 4498 at the Library of Congress. Record the edition, reel or image group and page actually used [S2 @2026-11-22] [S3 @2026-11-22]. Check the film’s access in the user’s account.

Use the signer’s name and state to select the relevant part and trace the candidate branch. Leave the relationship open when a descendant is missing from the compilation. The Library of Congress guide documents gaps [S2] [S3].

## 2. Follow the society application index

Use Frederick Wallace Pyne’s 2004 *Signers of the Declaration* index with the three accompanying CDs. Choose the index access point that matches the signer’s name, a deceased member’s name or that member’s number. Retrieve the cited application and its attachments. Treat the 2004 publication as a historical set and leave later applications outside its coverage [S4].

## 3. Reconstruct the evidence for each generation

Based on the available excerpt, the presenter suggests using the Leach manuscript and the application files together when assembling the descent [S1].

Align each candidate generation across the two sources. Record the supporting document, the link it actually establishes, and whether the two works rely on the same evidence. Use [published book discovery](published-book-catalogue-and-full-text-discovery.md) for alternate holdings, [FamilySearch record workflows](familysearch-practical-record-workflows.md) for film access, and [evidence and proof judgment](evidence-proof-judgment.md) for the unresolved links.
