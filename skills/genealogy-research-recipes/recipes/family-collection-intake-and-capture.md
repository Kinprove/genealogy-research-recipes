# Take in, capture and hand over an inherited family collection

Use this recipe when you help take in, inventory, plan the capture of, or hand over an inherited family collection. That covers boxes, albums, loose photographs, papers, and scans someone has already made. It applies to a physical collection, a capture plan or existing scans, and it never requires you to see or handle the originals. The common mistake is to optimize for tidy files. The model renames files by person, re-orders items, scans fronts only, imposes one resolution or format, treats a received crop or a stitched assembly as the master, and assumes an export kept the captions. Each of those destroys part of the record that later identification depends on: who held the item, the order it arrived in, its reverse, and the surfaces nobody has inspected yet.

This recipe covers intake, capture and hand-off only. It never prescribes physical treatment.

## Bound the assignment and record what arrived

1. Work with the requested subset. Keep a record of where it sits in the larger collection, but do not expand the assignment.
2. Before reorganizing, record what arrived, who supplied it, when and how it was received, and who holds the originals when known. Keep a short collection note alongside the files. Link the source email or the relative's note rather than silently paraphrasing it into established facts. An unknown creator, holder or date stays unknown [S1], [S2].

## Give every item a stable reference tied to its place

Give items stable references that connect their physical location and received grouping to their files. Preserve album and page order, and link individual captures to their parent page. Capture every page in sequence, blank pages included. If a folded item opens safely, capture it closed and opened, and link both views to the same page or item. Do not lift or remove adhered material to finish the sequence [S3].

Keep existing files and the received order while preparing the packet. Do not detach mounted items, dismantle albums or prescribe physical treatment. When access would require intervention, record the affected surface as inaccessible and continue with the material that is safely available. An inaccessible reverse is not a blank reverse [S3], [S4].

When the work moves on to photograph, papers or interview work, connect it back through these same stable item references.

For a small task, an item reference, source and location, the files with their roles, the original's dimensions and format when accessible or stated, and a gaps note are enough. Record the original's physical dimensions and format whenever the original is accessible or its holder states them: a digital file's pixel size does not reveal the original's size, and size and format help date a photograph. Never infer object size from a resized image, and never ask anyone to handle a fragile original merely to fill a field. Add album position or capture settings when they are relevant [S2].

## Keep each statement about an item separate

Keep four kinds of statement distinguishable: a received caption, a relative's account, an observed object detail and an interpretation [S1], [S2], [S5].

Never compare faces, and never assign identity by appearance. When the user or a relative recognises someone, record that recognition as their attributed claim, together with its basis. Resemblance is never evidence by itself.

## Plan capture from the media actually present

Inventory the media and their observed condition before proposing capture [S4]. Prints and mounted photographs go on a flatbed. Slides and negatives need a scanner built for film and slides. Never put an old photograph through an automatic feeder [S6]. Record the equipment actually available, the intended reuse and the settings chosen for each material, rather than one resolution or file format for everything. If suitable capture is unavailable, leave an explicit next action. Do not mail originals; arrange copying where the original is, locally or at the holder's home [S6].

## Capture every accessible surface and the page around it

For loose photographs you can safely reach, capture front and reverse and bind both to one object reference. Copy inscriptions literally and keep them separate from your interpretation. Mark each side as missing (not present), uninspected (reachable but not yet examined) or inaccessible (reachable only by intervention). For an album whose items are stuck down, such as a magnetic album, capture the whole page rather than lifting items, and keep that full page beside any individual crop. Save the last item or page actually completed, and the views still remaining, so another session can resume [S3], [S4].

## Keep received files, derivatives and versions apart

Keep the full initial capture or received file. Make crops, resized copies and edits as separate derivatives. If a supplied image is already enhanced or cropped, describe it as received; do not claim that an untouched master exists. Record the source and role of each file. A new format extension cannot recover detail that the input lacks [S2]. An authored display or scrapbook page is also a derivative. It does not replace the source photograph, and it does not establish who is depicted.

If an original has been restored, damaged or otherwise altered, ask whether photographs or scans exist from before that change, as well as after it. Record each copy as a separate version, with its date, its maker, and whether it was made before or after the change. Do not treat the current poor image as the document itself [S7].

Treat date, author, location or contact values embedded in a received scan as assertions by whoever digitized or later edited it [S8]. Record who set them, when known, and their basis, such as whether a date is an estimate, beside the original's inscriptions before using them as the photograph's creation date or maker.

## Bind the parts of an oversized item

If an oversized item is captured in portions, bind each part to the item and to the position it covers. Keep the parts beside any assembled working derivative. At the joins, inspect text, lines, color and retained edges for continuity or missing extent. Record any unresolved seam instead of calling the assembly complete or lossless [S9].

## Look for a missing item under another association

If a known photo, document or recording is missing from the expected person's view, check its associations with the spouse and other close relatives first; one author found her misplaced items often attached to a spouse [S10]. Then inspect a collection-wide media list if one is available, using the item's retained object details. Mark the item either as found under another association or as not located, and never record one as the other. An association does not establish the identity of the person depicted or recorded.

## Record what a recipient actually agreed to take

Contact the receiving society or archive before handing anything over, and record what it actually agrees to take, including limits on scope. Expect duplicates of what it already holds to be refused. Material that nobody has accepted stays unresolved. A named recipient alone does not establish an arranged transfer or legal authority [S11].

## Check the hand-off from the delivered copy

Open a representative delivered item and its companion files from the location they were delivered to. Check that the item reference leads to its page or context, its accessible reverse and its source note. Check that captions and other required metadata survived the actual export. Where the software allows it, also embed dates, sources, captions, places and people in each file's own metadata so they travel with the image [S12]. When an application drops those fields, preserve them in an accompanying register or plain-text sidecar file and record the gap [S1]. A passed check covers only the item you opened.

## Return

Close with what was captured, how to find its source, the actual stopping position and the remaining gaps. A readable scan establishes neither an identity nor complete coverage.

The packet holds:

- **A collection note:** what arrived, who supplied it, when and how, who holds the originals, and links to the supplier's own messages. Unknown values stay marked unknown.
- **An item register**, one entry per stable reference, with:
  - physical location, received grouping, and parent page or position
  - each file and its role: initial capture, received file, derivative, part, or a version made before or after an alteration
  - the status of each surface: captured, missing, uninspected or inaccessible
  - literal inscriptions, kept apart from any received caption, relative's account (with who gave it), observed detail and interpretation
  - the original's physical dimensions and format when accessible or stated by the holder; album position and capture settings when relevant
- **Version notes:** for an altered original, each copy's date and maker and whether it predates the alteration. For embedded scan fields, who set them and whether a date is an estimate.
- **Assembly notes** for oversized items: the parts, the position each covers, and any unresolved seams.
- **A capture plan with a progress marker:** media and condition, equipment and settings for each material, explicit next actions, the last completed item or page, and the views remaining.
- **Location results:** each missing item marked as found under another association or as not located.
- **A handover record:** the recipient, what they agreed to take, any exclusions, and the material still unresolved.
- **The hand-off check result:** which delivered item was opened, what it led to correctly, which fields the export dropped, and where those fields are preserved instead.
