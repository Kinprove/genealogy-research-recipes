# Place and date a building in a historical streetscape photograph

Use this recipe when you have the photograph of a historical street or landscape and need to identify one building or establish when it could have appeared as shown. Use [repository photograph search](repository-photograph-collection-search.md) to find additional views and [historical maps](historical-map-record-recovery.md) to resolve a dated street or parcel. Use [family photograph attribution](family-photograph-attribution-and-dating.md) for a person's identity. Facial resemblance supplies no identity evidence. Record a person's recognition as their attributed claim.

## Orient the view

For a panorama, establish the camera position and direction of each frame before assigning names to buildings. Scan the horizon and outer edges at usable resolution. Carry a fixed landmark into any overlapping frame and compare which features appear to either side and in front of it [S3].

Check a legible building number against a dated map or catalogue description, including numbers on structures at the edge. Start with Brander’s 1908 *Berga 13* record for the camera direction [S2]. Kyläkoski locates its number on the small white building at the right edge of the view [S1].

## Test the site and sightline

Match two landmarks in the background to their mapped positions and check which candidate building falls along the resulting line of sight. Keep the match provisional if more than one candidate fits. For the rear Berga building, Kyläkoski tests Berga 14 using Strömsten 1 and Taipale 7 as background landmarks [S1].

Before treating a missing building as a date clue, plot the sightline from the camera to its site. Check the frame edge and intervening terrain, vegetation and structures. A hidden site supplies no evidence that its building was absent. Kyläkoski reports that a rock hides the largest Berga building in Brander’s National Museum tower view [S1].

## Check function and date against written records

When the view shows a structure the caption leaves unidentified, search the period street or site name in newspapers around the image date. Compare the reported location and use with what the photograph shows. Kyläkoski reads the frames in the Temppeliaukio photograph against the clothes-drying racks described in *Helsingin sanomat* on 22 July 1927 [S4].

Use dated guides, planning histories and reports of changes to bound the visible state of an identified structure [S5], [S6]. Check whether an account describes construction, a conversion or completed demolition. For the Töölö pavilion, find its position on the walk and the occasion for its construction in *Helsinki ympäristöineen*, 1889, p. 81 [S7]. Read the construction and conversion account in Helsinki planning report 2013:2 and compare the pavilion photograph with Fig. 161 on p. 126 for the later view of the site [S6]. Check the replacement landscaping Kyläkoski cites from *Isänmaan ystävä* on 9 July 1897 for a latest bound on removal [S5].

## Return the identification

Give the photo and map locators, the camera position or candidate positions, the anchors visible in each view, and the date bounds with their sources. List the features outside the frame or hidden by obstructions and the candidate identifications still open.
