# Trace a Swedish-realm pulpit notice from order to parish

Use this recipe when a notice or thanksgiving may have been read in a Swedish-realm parish and you need to establish the intended place and date or whether it was actually reported there. Keep the rules and records of the period authority with the locality.

## Find the mandate or circular

Start with the 1800 Finnish *Yhteenweto* for texts prescribed for repeated reading. Find the ordinance or proclamation, then copy the reading season and stated local or realm-wide scope. If the text is missing from the compilation, still search for one-off royal, provincial and local announcements [S1], [S2], [S5].

For instructions sent by the Porvoo cathedral chapter, look up the occasion in A. J. Hornborg's 1872 *Sammandrag* of circular letters for 1725–1829. Copy the dispatch date and identify the occasion named in the instruction. Trace an unclear instruction or absent appointed day to the circular itself [S3], [S4].

## Verify the local reading

A reading order establishes what was prescribed. Establish the local reading separately. Record the event, instruction or dispatch, intended reading, reported local observance and newspaper issue date in separate fields. Search *Posttidningar* for reports from the named parish and compare nearby places. A shared local date is a lead for the intended Sunday. Preserve each report's own geographic scope. If a parish is missing on the expected day, compare that date with the dispatch date and keep delayed delivery open. For a dated comparison, use the Porvoo and Hämeenlinna reports in the 24 March 1746 issue [S3], [S6].

Read the geographic scope from the source's own wording. For a newspaper report of a Stockholm service, keep other localities open until the mandate or another report gives that wider scope. Treat reported battle losses in an observance account as claims requiring independent evidence [S6].

## Return

List the event, dated mandate or circular, stated scope and date of reading, and each parish and issue for which you found separate evidence.
