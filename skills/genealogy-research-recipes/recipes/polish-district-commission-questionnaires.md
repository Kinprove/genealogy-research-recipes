# Recover Polish district-commission questionnaires on Nazi occupation crimes

Use the wartime place, approximate year and reported event to find the postwar form about that community. Resolve same-named villages with [historical place-name resolution](historical-place-name-resolution.md).

## Search by commission, place and event

Use the locality's province at the time of the inquiry to choose the district commission. For the reactivated commissions, use the territorial provision of the 1 September 1965 Temporary Regulations, AIPN GK 162/16, folios 12–13 [S1] [S5].

Start in the [IPN archival inventory for Białystok](https://inwentarz.ipn.gov.pl/archiwa-ipn/bialystok?language_content_entity=pl) for files produced by the Białystok district commission. For another locality, search for the pertinent commission under its Polish creator name, `Okręgowa Komisja Badania Zbrodni Hitlerowskich`, adding the commission city, village or county and `ankiety` or `kwestionariusze`. Include Polish terms for the event, such as `wywózki na roboty przymusowe` for deportations to forced labour and `egzekucje` for executions [S2 @2026-11-22] [S3 @2026-11-22] [S4 @2026-11-22].

Open the file description and read every locality and event heading. Search the village and county across commissions if the local creator's files have no match. IPN Wr 12/7, for instance, was compiled by the Wrocław commission and covers places outside its home province. Record the present branch, fond, reference and recorded date span. A description supplies a file lead and leaves the person's inclusion to be checked in the form [S3 @2026-11-22].

Look for separate crime and forced-labour forms for the same village. For a Klepacze search, use Białystok as the creator lead, 10 June 1968 as the reported compilation date and search separately for the arrests and for forced labour in the Third Reich [S1].

## Extract claims from each form

On the crime form, copy the target person's name, father's given name, stated place and dated allegation where recorded. On a forced-labour list, preserve the maiden and married surnames as labelled and the father's given name. Keep the witness or informant, compiler and subject distinct. If the forced-labour form lacks witnesses, leave attribution open even when a companion crime form names them [S1].

Transcribe the main text and each annotation separately. Record the note's location and retain all plausible referents when a marginal entry sits beside several names. Date an address worded in the present tense to the account that supplies it. Record arrest, escape and subsequent residence as separately dated observations [S1].

Preserve inconsistent statements side by side. In the published transcription of the Klepacze crime form, one passage reports a shooting and also says the men's fate after arrest was unknown [S1]. Carry both statements into [evidence and proof judgment](evidence-proof-judgment.md).

Read `nie wróciła` as a report that the person did not return. A reported failure to return leaves later fate open. Use [Holocaust testimony and source interpretation](holocaust-testimony-source-interpretation.md) for correlating later evidence of survival or death [S1].

## Return the evidence

Return the catalogue locator, each form's date and scope, the exact folio or list position inspected, the target person's recorded identifiers and separately attributed event statements. Record catalogue descriptions as leads when the underlying form is unavailable. Refer to witnesses and other third persons by role in shared summaries.
