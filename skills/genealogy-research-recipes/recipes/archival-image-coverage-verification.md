# Recipe: Verifying archival images, case boundaries and search coverage

Use when an image, transcription, catalogue or name index disagrees with another copy, or when reporting that a case was completely inspected. This recipe verifies the source and the inspected scope; identity conclusions belong to [evidence-proof-judgment.md](evidence-proof-judgment.md).

## Separate the objects before counting them

Keep the archival case/volume, digital file or image group, index record, and person distinct. Record the archive/fund/inventory/case, film or DGS, item or digital part, viewer image, and printed page or leaf/verso in separate fields. Preserve identifiers literally before adding a proposed mapping.

For a transcript, retain its image/leaf binding, version or timestamp, and whether human correction is established. Manually triggering recognition still produces machine text. An unavailable newer version neither makes an older transcript current nor disproves its readings. Identify the text actually consulted and its unchecked relationship to the image.

FamilySearch distinguishes image numbers, record pages, household numbers and family numbers. Its item-grouping instructions allow multiple books on a film and a book split across films. A film's image count therefore cannot establish a particular case's boundaries. [S1, S2]

For the requested item, inspect opening and closing material and representative interior pages. Reconcile its covers, title boards, headings, foliation and entries with the catalogue. A readable copy's own page count is not independent evidence that it contains the whole case. If an inventory, certification or other copy establishes a boundary, cite that evidence; otherwise report inspected pages with completeness unresolved.

When an archive sends copies it selected from a case file, list the archival sheet numbers actually received. The highest number shows only that the file was at least that long; gaps in the foliation mark sheets not supplied. Record the packet as an excerpt with its known minimum extent, not as the case. Request the remaining sheets, citing the sheet numbers already received, and record whether the archive supplied them or withheld them. The [repression-file copy reports](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=64&t=561) motivate this. Sheet numbers do not show what omitted sheets contain or whether they can be released, and an unexplained gap is not evidence that nothing more was recorded.

When reading a case file in a reading room, record three categories by sheet number: sheets inspected, sheets kept in sealed envelopes or otherwise withheld, and sheets shown without permission to copy and noted only by hand. Withheld is not missing, and neither status shows what those sheets contain. Note the viewing date and any further release after a request, because access to the same file can change. Originals released to the family may have been replaced by copies with receipts. The [sealed-envelope reports](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=64&t=561) and a [quoted access regulation](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=64&t=564) motivate this. They do not establish any archive's current practice.

When a supplied archival image package includes a repository-produced HTML companion, retain
it beside the images. It may preserve the archival signature, reported image count, repository
link and received usage statement that image filenames omit. Bind it to the stated unit and
apply the existing extent checks separately. The [NRW archive download description](https://www.compgen.de/2025/10/landesarchiv-nrw-mit-neuen-digitalisaten-und-downloadmoeglichkeiten/)
motivates retaining that source object; a stated count does not establish complete coverage.

## Catalogue disagreements and reading order

If a copy-holder's short catalogue description leaves several possible originating files,
inspect an actually available fuller catalogue representation and the copy's title board.
Keep the copy item/film key and proposed original repository/signature separate; bind only
the supported original unit and inspected copy extent. The [CAHJP copy example](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=153&t=1225)
describes a fuller MARC record, without establishing current fields or controls. A selected-city
copy can be a fragment of the original case; leave an unsupported binding unresolved.

Classify a disputed number before correcting it: archive case, old office reference, volume, act, page or leaf. An old cover and a current inventory can use different identifiers for the same physical object. Preserve both readings and seek their binding; a numerical pattern on neighbouring covers only suggests a mapping. Read struck-out and replacement labels together. A deciphered digit alone does not settle current catalogue attribution.

Treat a fund/inventory/case citation from older notes or a publication as a dated, second-hand lead recorded with its citing source, not as a current locator. If it does not match the current inventory or cover label, check for renumbering, splitting during restoration, or parts moved into separate units. Map it through an archive conversion table where one is available, then read the sheet numbers again and keep the old and new locators. Recheck 'lost' conclusions made under the old numbering. The [renumbered-inventory discussion](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-3-0-00000102-000-0-0-1780133454=), [separated-lists note](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-36-0-00000063-000-0-0-1769266561=) and [secondary-citation caution](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=181&t=4753) motivate this. They do not supply a conversion table.

Check sequence independently from totals. Compare foliation, headings and entries continuing across pages, including transitions between digital parts. Repeated page labels can represent recto/verso, restarted numbering or another section. A complete image set can still be in the wrong order. Do not renumber or merge items from filenames alone.

For an unnumbered verso, inspect the actual preceding recto and surrounding sequence before
assigning a leaf/verso binding; leave it unresolved when that context is missing. A film-end
card directing continuation on another reel leaves that part queued until inspected. Keep
printed page, original folio and viewer frame separate rather than deriving one with a fixed
ratio. The [evacuation-film examples](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=197&t=1199)
sharpen the sequence check without establishing that the reported images are complete.

When an index points into an earlier image set, locate a known entry or heading in both
versions and retain its old/new coordinates before reusing the pointer. Added or removed blank
and duplicate frames can break a fixed offset. Leave the conversion unresolved if the needed
version is unavailable; an absent film field does not prove that no film exists. The
[Lithuanian image-set example](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=164&t=1202)
motivates this version-specific anchor check.

Do not take an image's record type, denomination or jurisdiction from the collection title. Open the image's own citation or information panel and the volume's headings. Record which parishes, chapels and event types it actually contains; a volume can interleave several. When one image differs from its neighbours, compare the adjacent images and identify it by its printed form heading. The [mislabelled register](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jfWVACjBbbM) and [burial-permit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4b4eUWm2s) examples motivate this. They do not establish how common mislabelling is or why an item was filed there.

An inventory line, file title or online catalogue heading can describe only the first document bound into a composite file, use vague wording such as "various villages", carry a wrong year, or lag behind an approved revised inventory. Before ruling a file in or out, inspect it or a published contents list. Record each bound part with its district, year, record type and leaves, and keep the title and inventory version used beside what the file shows. An archive reply based on titles does not establish what a fond lacks. Before reporting a church's year missing, check later-added inventory entries and cases titled for related churches of the same place. The [composite revision-file listing](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-7-0-00000156-000-0-0-1768979291=), [misdated confession-list report](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-7-0-00001243-000-0-0-1778141289=), [catalogue-heading note](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-29-0-00000095-000-0-0-1681915156=) and [inventory-title discussion](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-14-0-00000075-000-0-0-1768980711=) motivate this. None shows how common such errors are.

A scanned or user-transcribed inventory can itself be incomplete. Read its heading notes for sheets skipped during scanning, and note any superseded inventories it lists. Treat a gap in file numbers in such a copy as an uninspected stretch of the inventory, not as an absent file. Confirm against the archive's full current inventory before reporting a file or parish-year missing. The [scanned-inventory transcriptions](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-3-0-00000104-000-0-0-1731557574=) and [their earlier part](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-3-0-00000101-000-0-0-1701788730=) motivate this. They do not show what the skipped sheets listed or whether the missing numbers exist.

## Report coverage as pages × targets

For each assigned page, retain its source locator, target-name set, inspected area, readable portions and findings. Distinguish:

- inspected and readable for the target;
- inspected with a specific unreadable area;
- unavailable or not inspected.

A completed reader session establishes execution coverage, not transcription accuracy. A new surname was not searched merely because its pages occurred in an earlier sweep for other names. Reconcile existing page/target coverage before repeating or expanding work. For a visual-only request, inspect images; OCR candidates can be a separately labelled channel only when compatible with the requested method.

Jumping to the expected date in a chronological roll or image group covers only that span. Before reporting an entry absent, inspect the group's final images for out-of-sequence additions and any roll index, and record in the coverage statement that the end was checked. A roll index is a finding aid; still read the original entry it points to. The [microfilm rule of thumb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Rcm-OakCes) motivates this check. It does not establish how often appended images or indexes occur, or that every digitized image group keeps its roll ending.

In a revision list or other listing of several places, a place need not form one continuous block. It can have a main section, a separate section for those who died or departed since the previous count, or a short later entry under its heading that continues another place's numbering. Before reporting a household absent or counting a place's households, read the case's full leaf sequence and record every leaf range bearing its heading. A compiler's place index is a finding aid; verify its ranges on the images. In the coverage statement, record a missing beginning or end, or missing old-numbered leaves, as ranges in both old and current leaf numbering. The [revision-list leaf discussion](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-14-0-00000057-000-0-0-1768992933=) and [1744 revision-list place index](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-7-0-00001243-000-0-0-1778141289=) motivate this. Each concerns one case, and the index's leaf numbers were not checked against images.

When one parish's registers for successive years sit in different files, keep a per-year row: file locator, the first image of each part (births, marriages, deaths), and what that year's book actually covers and records. Note a part that stops mid-year, poor legibility, a viewer binding that differs from the file label, and the first year a field such as surname or mother's name appears. A field missing before that year, or an event after a part stops, is a coverage gap, not negative evidence. A [metrical-book year ledger](https://rodgersforum.borda.ru/?1-7-0-00001315-000-0-0-1780134215=) motivates this. It covers one parish; its starting years do not transfer elsewhere, and it does not explain the gaps.

In a volume covering several settlements, check the end of each settlement's section, not only the front or back of the volume, for a surname list compiled for that section. Record for each section whether one was found. The [multi-settlement revision-volume tip](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=152&t=36) reports such lists in only some sections of one volume. Treat a list as a finding aid: it does not replace reading the entries. A section without a list is not thereby incomplete, and a name missing from a list is not an absence.

## Adjudicate each field against the image

Open a native-resolution passage with preceding words, the complete entry and any continuation line. Keep the full page for headings and context; a tight surname crop can conceal the given name or patronymic. Distinguish an entry's subject from an owner named inside an address, witness, institution or place.

Before reading a conflict into a field, confirm the image reaches the record's outer edges. If one provider's scan looks cropped, shaved or distorted, open the same record at another provider. Record which image supplied each reading. A second scan of the same original completes the reading; it is not independent corroboration. The [draft-card example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mTyYpO_3uPs), where a cut-off edge hid the stated age, motivates this check. It does not explain the remaining birth-year discrepancy.

Keep literal readings, uncertainty and normalized values separate. Confirmation of a surname does not confirm guessed initials or parentage. A readable patronymic supplies the stated paternal name, not a unique father. A clearly printed impossible date remains a literal reading with normalization unresolved.

When register entries carry numbers in the margin, search the same volume for a section keyed to those numbers, for example at the back, before treating the front entry as complete. Extract both parts with their own image locators and cite them together, keeping each reading bound to its part. The [English Jewish birth-register example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BivX3fFXJRw) motivates this check. It does not establish that other registers use the same layout, and failing to find a keyed section in pages not yet inspected is not evidence that none exists.

When readers or the user disagree, compare the same strokes field by field. Preserve the attributed correction and superseded draft, then update the parent assessment so rejected readings do not return in later summaries. Apply the dependency review in [evidence-proof-judgment.md](evidence-proof-judgment.md) if a corrected field changes an identity hypothesis.

For manual review, provide the exact full source page, citation and neutral question; add a crop as a supplement. Multiple questions can use stable codes. Preparing a review packet does not resolve its questions.

## Scan availability and index coverage are different questions

A catalogue entry, a working scan and a searchable name index establish different things. Verify the actual file and case/part correspondence; a repository page or predicted filename alone does not prove scan availability. A failed retrieval leaves availability unknown.

For JewishGen Ukraine, follow the current Ukraine RD FAQ to its monthly/cumulative upload listings. Compare the actual file reference, years, locality and record type; retain the inspected row and listing date. The FAQ describes periodically updated upload lists, not a guarantee of complete transcription of every case subsection. A missing row does not establish permanent non-indexing. [S3]

An upload for a case does not establish that its supplementary, merchant or other subsection was completely indexed. Resolve that narrower question against a stated coverage scope or the relevant source entries. Index records, people, households and scan pages have different denominators; equal totals do not prove completeness. Preserve the original's locator for each indexed entry, as JewishGen's transcription guidance requires. [S4]

Before ordering or citing a file by an index's fund/inventory/file reference, query an archive holdings database or published inventory by place, year and record type. Compare the returned file number and description with the citation. A file of a different record type at the cited number signals a likely citation error; keep both references until the file is opened. A holdings database lists surviving files and their references, not their entries. A surname search there therefore fails, and a matching file does not show that the person's entry exists. The [Vilnius holdings-database exchange](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=164&t=5924) motivates this from one example.

A compiled reference database or online fond list can omit a fond it never processed, or fail to display one. Before reporting a fond or file as not held, establish whether that finding aid covers the fond at all, and consult the archive's own inventories. A [fond-coverage exchange](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=164&t=5924) motivates this. In it, a database lacked a fond's signatures because the fond was unprocessed, and another user contradicted an apparent jump in the online fond list. It does not show what the fond contains or whether the cited files survive.

A later volunteer surname index to a census can state what its compilers skipped, such as
military lists, households whose first-listed resident was outside the target group, or
attached workforce lists. Keep those omissions with the index; its no-hit is not a search of
the whole census. An index compiled by the original enumerators belongs to the same census:
use it to locate an entry or check a reading, cite it separately and verify the entry on the
census sheet itself. The [Dvinsk census indexing thread](https://forum.j-roots.info/viewtopic.php?f=168&t=289)
motivates both checks without establishing that the enumerators' index survives complete.

Record which sides of loose documents an image copy shows; filming can miss a page or a reverse side, and a published abstract may exclude whole categories of entries, so read a derivative's front matter for stated exclusions. Report an absent page, side or entry as missing from the inspected copy and queue a check of the original or another filming at the holding archive. The [territorial-census example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Eh06QweqW3g), [unfilmed-reverse remark](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t70iQZdquD0) and [parish-register abstract example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7TW9qyShIU) motivate this. None establishes a filming practice or how often omissions occur.

Deliver the literal passage and verified fields, the exact inspected locator, coverage actually achieved and remaining gaps. For OCR retrieval use [corpus-seeded-name-search.md](corpus-seeded-name-search.md); for household reconstruction use [jewishgen-search-family-reconstruction.md](jewishgen-search-family-reconstruction.md).
