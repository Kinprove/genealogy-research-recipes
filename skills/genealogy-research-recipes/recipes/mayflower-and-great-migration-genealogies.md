# Route an early New England pedigree to Mayflower Families or Great Migration sketches

Use this recipe when a family claims a Mayflower passenger or when a named early New England immigrant needs an evidence-based family sketch. Select any Silver Books volume by passenger, child and generation.

## Choose the Mayflower branch and generation

According to the presenter, use the passenger’s Silver Books line as the first compiled check, and consult the Great Migration sketch if details of the immigrant’s family are missing. [S1†]

Use the Mayflower Society’s volume guide to pick the part that covers the child branch and generation. For Desire Howland, search for the separately published Picton Press descendant volume and check the Society’s edition guide for its branch coverage. Look for George Soule in the pink *Mayflower Families in Progress* series and for the relevant Philip Delano lines in that guide’s green softcover sequence. Search a library catalogue by the exact volume or part when you need a copy. [S2]

## Choose the Great Migration arrival series

According to the presenter, choose between the two Great Migration series by arrival year and find the surname within that series. [S1†]

Look up 1620–1633 arrivals in the three volumes of *The Great Migration Begins*, including Mayflower arrivals where a Silver Books entry leaves a gap in the immigrant’s household. For 1634 or 1635, use the seven-volume *The Great Migration* series and select the alphabetical volume for the surname. [S3]

## Test a passenger claim against a sibling line

According to the presenter, compare the immigrant sketches when a surname could lead either to John Howland or to his brother Henry. [S1†]

Work from the established descendant line to the matching child. Use the immigrant’s child list and sibling notes together. For a claimed John Howland descent, compare the candidate child with John’s listed children and investigate the brother’s line separately if the parent-child chain leads there. [S3], [S4]
