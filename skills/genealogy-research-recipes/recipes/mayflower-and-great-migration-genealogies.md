# Route an early New England pedigree to Mayflower Families or Great Migration sketches

Use this recipe when a family claims a Mayflower passenger or when a named early New England immigrant needs an evidence-based family sketch. Select any Silver Books volume by passenger, child and generation.

## Choose the Mayflower branch and generation

According to the presenter, use the passenger’s Silver Books line as the first compiled check, and consult the Great Migration sketch if details of the immigrant’s family are missing. [S1†]

Use the Mayflower Society’s volume guide to pick the part that covers the child branch and generation. For Desire Howland, search for the separately published Picton Press descendant volume and check the Society’s edition guide for its branch coverage. Look for George Soule in the pink *Mayflower Families in Progress* series and for the relevant Philip Delano lines in that guide’s green softcover sequence. Search a library catalogue by the exact volume or part when you need a copy. [S2]

## Check the passenger and later Plymouth voyages

According to the presenter, use Robert Charles Anderson’s *The Mayflower Migration: Immigrants to Plymouth, 1620* to look up a claimed passenger. [S5†], [S6] For a Plymouth Colony settler through 1633, including a later arrival, use Anderson’s broader *The Pilgrim Migration: Immigrants to Plymouth Colony, 1620–1633*. [S3]

If a relative is absent from the Mayflower list, check later Plymouth voyages before ruling out the child branch. [S7] Use the 1623 Division of Land (*Plymouth Colony Records*, 12:5) to check a reconstructed passenger list such as the *Fortune*’s. [S8] Jonathan Brewster came on the *Fortune* in 1621 and his sisters Patience and Fear on the *Anne* in 1623, after their parents’ Mayflower crossing. [S9]

According to the presenter, use Charles Edward Banks’s *The English Ancestry and Homes of the Pilgrim Fathers* for a passenger’s English origins. [S15†], [S10] Look up the passenger under the *Mayflower* (1620), *Fortune* (1621), *Anne* (1623) or *Little James* (1623). Treat the places and relationships in the sketch as leads to verify in English records. [S10]

## Choose the Great Migration arrival series

According to the presenter, choose between the two Great Migration series by arrival year and find the surname within that series. [S1†]

Look up 1620–1633 arrivals in the three volumes of *The Great Migration Begins*, including Mayflower arrivals where a Silver Books entry leaves a gap in the immigrant’s household. For 1634 or 1635, use the seven-volume *The Great Migration* series and select the alphabetical volume for the surname. [S3]

According to the presenter, use *The Great Migration Directory: Immigrants to New England, 1620–1640* to look up immigrants across the full period. [S5†], [S3] When the arrival year is uncertain or you lack a full sketch, search under the likely household head. Use the entry’s origin, migration date, New England residences and bibliography to locate a sketch or the cited evidence. For a 1636–1638 arrival, check the publisher’s Third Series list for a volume that covers the surname. [S3]

## Check early Massachusetts Bay arrivals

According to the presenter, check Anderson’s *The Winthrop Fleet: Massachusetts Bay Company Immigrants to New England, 1629–1630* for a fleet claim. [S5†], [S11] Check the sketch’s arrival evidence for the particular voyage before assigning the immigrant to the 1630 fleet. The book covers the broader company migration of 1629 and 1630. [S11]

According to the presenter, check Banks’s *The Winthrop Fleet of 1630* for a 1630 fleet claim. [S15†], [S12] Look up the immigrant’s name and any English home, then use Banks’s references and Anderson’s sketch to test any vessel assignment before recording it. [S12], [S11]

According to the presenter, look in Alexander Young’s *Chronicles of the First Planters of the Colony of Massachusetts Bay* for people who arrived after the first settlers. [S13†] Within the volume’s 1623–1636 scope, search its index for the person and follow hits to the specific company record, letter or voyage account, such as Francis Higginson’s journal. Cite that document and page. An index miss leaves the arrival question open. [S14]

## Test a passenger claim against a sibling line

According to the presenter, compare the immigrant sketches when a surname could lead either to John Howland or to his brother Henry. [S1†]

Work from the established descendant line to the matching child. Use the immigrant’s child list and sibling notes together. For a claimed John Howland descent, compare the candidate child with John’s listed children and investigate the brother’s line separately if the parent-child chain leads there. [S3], [S4]

## Trace a pedigree in Farmer and Savage

When a pedigree cites John Farmer’s 1829 *A Genealogical Register of the First Settlers of New England*, find the entry the claim relies on. [S17] According to the presenter, James Savage’s *A Genealogical Dictionary of the First Settlers of New England* expands Farmer’s work. [S15†], [S16] Compare what each author actually says and verify the proposed link against the records. For a settler outside Great Migration coverage, use Savage’s four alphabetical volumes for arrivals before May 1692. [S16], [S3]
