# Trace a Philippine family across the 1849 surname decree

Open this recipe when a Filipino surname disappears in older records, or several towns have families of the same name. Match households across the surname change through dated records in the same locality.

## Bridge the surname change within the town

According to the episode’s genealogy adviser, allow for adoption of a different surname around 1849, and keep same-surname families from different places separate until the records connect them. [S1†], [S2]

The 1849 Clavería decree and accompanying *Catálogo alfabético de apellidos* can explain a surname change. Compare neighbouring baptism, marriage and burial entries for the same household and track names, relatives and places on either side of the change. Keep surname retention open. A catalogue match supplies a name lead to test within the locality. [S3], [S4], [S9]

## Find the register behind an index result or miss

Check the town and its parish in both the FamilySearch Catholic church records and the appropriate civil registration collection. A name miss leaves unindexed images and parishes that the collection never acquired to be checked. For colonial-period civil returns, check the parish register separately. [S5 @2026-11-18], [S6 @2026-11-18], [S9 @2026-11-18]

According to Sales’s advice in the interview, continue looking for the church record after an online restriction or miss and start with church and civil indexes where the records are searchable. [S1†]

Follow the access message for the specific image group. For restricted digital images, use the location class it identifies. For physical-only film, verify that the listed institution holds the reel and has a reader before arranging a visit. [S7 @2026-11-18], [S8 @2026-11-18]
