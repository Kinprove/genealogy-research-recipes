# Trace a prisoner’s confiscated personal effects through the Arolsen Archives

Use this recipe when a family wants to know whether a Nazi persecution victim’s confiscated belongings survive, or an effects envelope has surfaced and its owner needs to be identified. A property register entry alone does not establish that the object survives. For the detention and postwar record chain, use [Holocaust register and survivor-list recovery](holocaust-register-and-survivor-list-recovery.md).

## Find the effects or their paper trail

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> The historical prisoner’s name or recorded nationality goes to the Arolsen Archives search.

Search the Arolsen Archives’ effects collection, DE ITS 1.2.9, for the prisoner. Check the item or its catalogue image and the status of any return. Try the name without a nationality filter when a restricted search fails [S2 @2026-11-22], [S3 @2026-11-22].

The published restitution case describes a Belgian prisoner’s effects surfacing among records associated with Dutch prisoners. When a nationality-filtered search fails, also check the documented transfer destination for the effects [S1].

Keep the surviving object, the camp’s property register entry and the ITS individual document envelope as separate records. The last is an archival wrapper for documents. Read the description and check whether it identifies belongings or describes paperwork [S2], [S4], [S6].

## Identify the owner and document the kinship

When the name on an effects envelope is hard to read, start from the birth date as written and cross-check it with the prisoner number, camp or other legible identifiers. Compare any signature or named papers with the detention records before attaching the item to a person [S1], [S3], [S4].

For a return case, identify the captive’s family from documented relationships. If the person had no children, follow siblings’ descendants and other collateral branches using [descendancy gap worklists](descendancy-gap-worklist.md). Treat an online tree as a lead to historical evidence. Apply the [Living people rule](../SKILL.md#living-people) to any living relative [S1], [S3], [S5].

## Work through the archive

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: contact --> The item reference, historical prisoner’s details and any consenting claimant’s details go to the Arolsen Archives restitution staff.

Contact the Arolsen Archives restitution team before starting a volunteer search on a specific item. If a family connection emerges, check it with the team before any approach to the family and let the team arrange the return. Use the consent and disclosure rules in [outreach staging](dna-match-outreach-staging.md) if contact with a living person is proposed [S3 @2026-11-22].

## Return packet

Keep the item reference and return status, owner identifiers with their sources, evidence supporting the kinship, unresolved alternatives and the archive’s next step.
