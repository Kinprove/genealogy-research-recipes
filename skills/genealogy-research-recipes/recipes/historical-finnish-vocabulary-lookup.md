# Resolve unfamiliar words in historical Finnish sources

Use this recipe when an unfamiliar Finnish word in a historical record or publication changes the interpretation of an occupation, object or event. Test a dictionary sense against what the person or object actually does in the passage.

## Select the reference by period and language variety

Use Kotus’s Vanhan kirjasuomen sanakirja for written Finnish from the 1540s through 1810. Try its advanced search for an apparent miss and compare the whole compound with its attested examples. For regional speech use Suomen murteiden sanakirja and compare the sense and its geographic distribution with the place the speaker or writer described. [S1] [S2] [S3]

For a nineteenth-century written word, check the published part of Rapolan 1800-luvun sanasto. Use the dated citations to locate a period example. Leave a missing entry open because the published selection covers part of the collection. [S4 @2026-11-22] [S5 @2026-11-22] [S6 @2026-11-22]

## Test the sense in period context

For a missing or ambiguous headword, search dated newspaper and periodical texts in the Finnish National Library’s Digi. Try the printed inflections and read the page around each candidate hit. Use the named activity, component parts and location to choose the sense. Keep multiple readings if the passage describes only a setting or a list. [S7] [S8]

When a list distinguishes kelkat from jääkelkat, as in the Tampere sidewalk report Kaisa Kyläkoski cites, retain both labels and use the setting to test the iceboat reading. Leave the construction open. [S8]

Use the activity and materials to test the sense of jääkelkka. Kaisa Kyläkoski compares a piece of ice pushed at Teisko with boards, skates and a sail at Näsiselkä. Her downhill example establishes how the sled was used and leaves its construction open. [S8]

## Record the reading

Return the literal phrase, source date and locality, candidate senses and the detail that distinguishes them. Keep unconfirmed readings visible. Use [newspaper section search](historical-newspaper-section-search.md) to follow an event lead and [Scandinavian parish registers](scandinavian-parish-record-recovery.md) to trace the household when the clue comes from a Finnish parish entry.
