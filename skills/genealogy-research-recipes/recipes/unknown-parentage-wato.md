## Goal

Place an unknown person — an unknown parent, adoptee's bio-parent, or an unplaced ancestor — into a tree, using the cM they share with known DNA matches. **WATO ("What Are The Odds?", a DNA Painter tool built by Leah Larkin (the DNA Geek) and Jonny Perl, with the underlying probability calculations derived by Leah Larkin and Andrew Millard) is the odds engine for that placement:** you build the matches' shared tree, propose candidate slots, and it scores each slot against relationship probability distributions. This recipe is the *judgment* around that engine — what a human must decide, what the tool computes, and where the whole thing quietly breaks.

**Where the probabilities come from (get the source right):** WATO's odds are derived from **AncestryDNA-based** relationship probabilities (calculations by Larkin & Millard), *not* from the crowd-sourced Shared cM Project. WATO and WATO Plus offer two probability sets. The **2016** set was simulated by AncestryDNA and described in its 2016 white paper; it is the original WATO's default. The **2020** set, also labelled beta, was compiled from the probabilities Ancestry showed on its site, as documented by Leah Larkin; it is WATO Plus's default. DNA Painter warns that at low cM the 2020 set favours close relationships more strongly than it should, and calls the 2016 set more sensible for small amounts. Only WATO Plus can apply **age-based probabilities**, once you state whether the unknown parent sought is the mother or the father. For every run, record the probability set and whether age-based probabilities were on, and compare scores only within one setup. ([WATO vs WATO plus](https://dnapainter.com/blog/wato-vs-wato-plus/); [WATO plus FAQ](https://dnapainter.com/help/user-guide/wato-plus/faq)) Blaine Bettinger's Shared cM Project data powers a **separate** DNA Painter tool — the "Shared cM Tool" — which you use to turn a cM figure into a *range of candidate relationships*. Two different tools, two different data sources; don't conflate them.

**Age-based probabilities rest on US birth statistics.** WATO Plus can apply the sought parent's age and, as a second option, the other biological parent's age; the data comes from US National Vital Statistics Reports for 1936–2014 (WATO plus FAQ [N51 @2026-11-12]). At launch, the data for both parents' ages covered only 1940 and 1965, a missing year took the closest year, and a source note in the scores table named the data used (Perl, 2024-02-26 [N31 @2026-11-12]). Read that note for each run. For a birth outside the US or outside those years, treat the age prior as a poor fit and compare the run without it. It ranks candidates such as brothers of different ages by how common each age was at a birth, not by DNA, and a likely age is not the actual age (Perl, 2024-01-30 [N54 @2026-11-12]).

## The mental model (get this right or everything downstream breaks)

WATO does **not** find your ancestor. It **ranks where an unknown person could attach to a tree you have already built**, by scoring the observed match-cM against relationship probability distributions. Two facts follow, and skipping either produces confident garbage:

1. **The output is a set of competing hypotheses with *relative* odds — never a verdict.** A single hypothesis you enter arbitrarily proves nothing — the signal is the *ratio* between the candidates, so a "22%" or "score of 8×" only means something against the alternatives on the same tree. (The exception DNA Painter documents: when an *exhaustive* hypothesis set collapses to one surviving feasible slot, that convergence is a genuinely strong result — but it's the exhaustiveness that earns it, not a lone entry.) (Family Locket, WATO Plus, 2025-03-27)
2. **The math is only valid if each match has exactly one relationship path to the target, and the tree's founding couple is correct.** WATO assumes single-path descent. Pedigree collapse / endogamy / a match related through two lines silently invalidates the score — the tool has no way to know and will still rank confidently. (Family Locket, 2025-03-27)

The reframe that keeps you sane: **DNA is a tool that complements your existing research skills, not a separate discipline** — genetic networks sort the matches; targeted DNA testing and/or documents close the case. (Diahan Southard, Your DNA Guide, 2025-11-19)

When interpreting a reported ranking, ask whether pedigree collapse, endogamy or more
than one relationship path could apply to these matches; do not replace the question
with a generic warning. Ask for the other tested hypotheses and identify a relative
whose test could distinguish them before proposing contact through the privacy gate.

## The reasoning arc — the human-owned sequence

This is the spine. WATO only enters at step 4; everything before and after is human judgment.

**Privacy gate before steps 3 and 4:** <!-- privacy-gate: upload, third-party-tool --> WATO is an online DNA Painter tool. Importing a GEDCOM and entering matches' shared cM there gives it living people's names, relationships and genetic amounts. Before entering anything, answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md`: require explicit permission covering this tool and each person's data you enter, and replace living people's names with handles while keeping the identity key local, as item 6 of "Prompt-craft when an AI is in the loop" and [AI for DNA research](ai-for-dna-research.md#verification--privacy-discipline) require. If permission is unclear, do not enter the data.

1. **Cluster the target's matches into ancestral groups.** Use genetic networks / shared-matches (ICW) / a Leeds-style sort to separate which line each match comes from before you trust any of them. A DNA network graph surfaces the *cluster* of related matches; identifying the cluster's likely most-recent-common-ancestor still takes documentary/tree research on top of it. (Southard 2025-11-19; Family Locket RLP 387, 2025-12-08)

   **Check the known side before you filter against it.** Confirm that each tested known relative appears in the target's match list at an amount the Shared cM Tool allows for the documented relationship. A missing or low-sharing known relative makes misattributed parentage of the target, the relative or both a hypothesis to test. Do not use that relative as a known-side filter until the question is resolved (see the named failure modes). Before calling a relative missing, confirm that both kits are in the same company's database (tested there or uploaded) and that the match is visible to you (not hidden or opted out of matching). Legacy Tree's [new-results checklist](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/eight-steps-to-pursue-with-new-autosomal-dna-test-results) motivates the check.

   **For an unknown parent, filter the pool in both directions.** Keep matches shared by at least two descendants of the subject who descend through different children. Then remove matches shared with tested relatives on the known parent's line; what remains are candidates for the unknown side. A Not-In-Common-With list against a half-sibling through the known parent does a similar sort. Where descendants of one mother's children by different fathers have tested, group each line's *unshared* matches by common ancestors in the matches' trees to find each father's line. [Paul Woodbury](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bnghXcCqcQc), [Mary Eberle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gNFNKpvy1no) and [Family History Fanatics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmxhJsw6R-I) motivate this. Exclusion is a clue, not a clean partition. Distant matches, maternal ones included, drop out by chance, sharing depends on vendor thresholds, and endogamy or a double relationship breaks the filter.

   **Under endogamy, filter by amount, not by presence.** Matches from the unknown side also appear as shared matches of known-side relatives, at small amounts, so removing every shared match discards real candidates. Mark known-side anchors: the known-side testers and documented relatives you can place on that line, such as second cousins. Where the amounts are visible, assign a match to the known side only when it shares substantial amounts with several anchors, and record the level you used. Small sharing with the anchors leaves the match in the unknown-side pool. The [JewishGen endogamy talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xaa5FcbZYb8) motivates this.

   **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: contact --> Asking living relatives to take a DNA test discloses your research question to them and asks for their genetic data; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before any approach.

   **A filter covers only the lines its testers share with the target.** Prefer the known parent as the filter tester; failing that, a relative who shares all of that parent's ancestors, such as a half-sibling through that parent or that parent's full sibling. A cousin through only one of that parent's grandparent couples removes matches from that line only. If tested known-side relatives represent only some of that parent's lines, recruit testers from the missing lines; until then, matches from those lines stay in the pool and look like unknown-side candidates. Legacy Tree's [filter-testing advice](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/seven-strategies-to-set-up-your-dna-testing-plans) motivates this.

   **When the unknown parents belong to an ancestor you descend from, sort each cluster by side.** Matches who descend from the ancestor and spouse themselves ('theirs') do not by themselves identify the ancestor's parents, so a cluster made only of them is not progress on the parent question. Matches through the spouse's family ('hers' when the ancestor is a man) answer a different question; the spouse's surname recurring in members' trees is a lead to that side, not proof. Work the clusters that connect through the ancestor's own parents or grandparents. FamilySearch's [DNA Swim School, Part 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9rSab4b9EI) motivates this.
2. **Identify the common ancestral couple of the strongest cluster** — the MRCA the tree will hang from. A useful *heuristic*: two strong matches who share DNA with the target but **not with each other** often descend from two different lines that meet at the target, so their intersection point is a candidate placement. Treat this as a lead, not a law — the two matches could instead sit on *opposite parental sides*, or be genuinely distant on a single line, in which case they never converge on the target. Verify with the tree and additional matches before betting on the intersection. (Kitty Cooper, 2026-01-09)

   **Count the couple's children the matches descend through.** A couple that recurs in match trees is a stronger lead when the matches descend from more than one of its children. When every match descends through one child and the same spouse of that child, the shared DNA can come from that spouse's family instead; keep that family as an alternative. Legacy Tree's [brick-wall case](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/breaking-down-brick-walls-limited-dna-evidence) treats matches through more than one child as a significant sign of a viable lead.

   **Finding step 2's intersection when matches form several groups.** If the target matches two or more groups that each resolve to a different ancestral couple, trace each group's descendants forward, including spouses' families, and look for the marriage (the 'union couple') that joins the lines; that couple's descendants form the candidate pool, narrowed by sex, place and timing. The [BYU FIGG lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bVJc1zapFE) and [GeneaVlogger's Mr. Beat case](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N0W5UpsyJGQ) motivate this. A marriage you find is a hypothesis to check against records and WATO. It does not exclude other connecting paths, and the groups must not share descent through pedigree collapse.

   **Do not hand the cluster's couple to the target automatically.** Shared matches who are second cousins to one another identify their own common couple. A target who shares less DNA with them may connect one generation higher, through that couple's parents. Likewise, a match who does not descend from the target's known grandmother rules out a full relationship only at the same generation. Keep full great-uncle/aunt and full 1C1R hypotheses through an unknown grandfather's own parents, and use the birth years of the tester, her father and the match to separate generations. When the cM allows it, enter each level as a hypothesis and let the scoring and documents decide. The [Mary Eberle Q&A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mN76WbyY2s4) and [GeneaVlogger Q&A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0__vtn3IzSc) motivate this.
3. **Build the known descendancy tree** from that couple down to the present, placing each real DNA match at its documented position. GEDCOM import builds this fast (limit imported candidates to descendants of the selected ancestor). Model half- vs full-sibling relationships correctly — a wrong half/full edge skews every probability downstream. (Family Locket, 2025-03-27)

   **Check which tree a close unexplained match supports before you place the match.** When a close match has a documented tree but no documented connection to the target, work out which documented family the shared matches between the target and that match belong to. If those shared matches are relatives on one of the target's known lines, hypothesize that the match descends from that line and that the error is in the match's tree. If they descend from a couple in the match's tree, test the target's descent from that couple. If they form their own cluster around a couple in neither tree, test whether both trees contain a misattribution. Do not place the match at a documented position the shared matches contradict; give the match a provisional slot and let records and the scoring test it. Legacy Tree's [six signs of misattributed parentage](https://www.legacytree.com/?p=80153) motivates this.

   **Test the close match's own documented parentage.** If chronology rules out the match's documented parent on the relevant side as the shared ancestor (for example, that parent was a child when the person whose parent is unknown was born), reject that placement, not the documented parentage: the match may connect through that parent's own parent instead. If a male match's direct-line Y-DNA test does not match his documented father's paternal-line relatives, check both documented paths and locate the break before treating the match's own parentage as misattributed. Then add hypotheses that place the match on the unknown side, such as a child of the unknown parent and so a half-sibling of the person being researched, alongside every other relationship the cM allows. With the match's permission, cluster the match's own shared matches and build hypotheses from them; they sit generations closer to the unknown parent than the tester's distant matches. Legacy Tree's [unknown-father case](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/identify-unknown-father-1920) motivates this.

   **Run a known-answer control before placing hypotheses.** Before working an unknown placement, rehearse the same workflow on an ancestor you have already documented. Treat that person as unknown, enter the real matches and cM, and check whether the documented slot ranks well against its alternatives. A failure points to tree-building, match-selection or data-entry errors before they contaminate the real case. One passing control does not validate a different tree, especially one with endogamy or multiple paths. The [Eberle case study](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zHwjmnpTz-c) recommends this rehearsal.

   **Check generation alignment before you place matches.** Put known relatives and people from match trees on a generation grid with estimated birth years, then check every parent-child interval. Matches born in similar years need not belong to the same generation. A parent four decades older than a child is not inherently implausible: recheck dates and documentary links, and move a branch down a generation only when they show the interval is wrong. Place a treeless match at the ancestral level its reliable reported relationship implies, and treat that slot as provisional. The [Lisa Lisson session](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1eEJABnbsUs) motivates this step. Its roughly 25-year spacing is an averaging heuristic and does not test any single link.
4. **Place hypotheses** — every plausible open slot where the target could descend — and enter the shared cM between the target and each match already in the tree. WATO scores each slot.
5. **Read the ratios and iterate — do not read a single run as an answer.** When a large tree floods the display, inspect an evidence-bearing branch in a separate working copy. Preserve the full baseline and all plausible alternative placements; a focused view answers a narrower question, and a higher percentage after pruning is not new evidence. Use the tied-hypothesis procedure below before adding or withholding matches. (Family Locket, 2025-03-27, reports branch-focused pruning; the baseline and comparison controls are this recipe's synthesis.)
6. **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: contact --> Recruiting a specific living relative to test discloses the hypothesis and asks for their DNA, so answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before any approach. **Take the top hypothesis forward.** WATO ends the *scoring* step, not the investigation — its output points to the next move, which may be **more targeted DNA testing** (recruit a specific relative to test) *or* **documentary research**, usually both (see the final sections).

## What the tool (and an AI) can carry

- **The odds arithmetic itself** — WATO's whole reason to exist. Never compute cM→relationship odds by hand or by asking an LLM (it fabricates them); route it to **WATO** (for slot odds, given a tree + multiple matches) or **DNA Painter's Shared cM Tool / the Shared cM Project** (to turn a *single* cM figure into the *range* of candidate relationships). Those are two different tools with two different outputs — don't ask one to do the other's job.
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> Pasting a match list, screenshots or a GEDCOM subtree into an AI tool gives it living matches' names, relationships and cM; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first.
- **Mechanical prep an AI is genuinely good at:** turning a match list or screenshots into a clean cM/relationship table; drafting a timeline from census rows; restructuring a GEDCOM subtree into a candidate list; drafting proof-argument *prose* for you to verify.
- **Surfacing candidate intersections** — e.g. duplicate-checking in tree software as a shortcut to find where two floating branches *may* meet (a lead to verify, not a proven junction). (Kitty Cooper, 2026-01-09)
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: link --> Writing living matches' initials and cM into a tree on a DNA site exposes them to anyone who can see that tree; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before adding them.
- **Bookkeeping that saves re-work:** record each match's initials + cM in a systematic slot — Kitty Cooper uses the tree's *Suffix* field (e.g. a match's initials plus its size in asterisks) so it never collides with ThruLines/Hints. (Kitty Cooper, 2026-01-09)

## What to never trust or delegate (the judgment a human must own)

- **A match with multiple relationship paths / endogamy / pedigree collapse.** Such matches violate WATO's single-path assumption — withhold their cM from that calculation, with the reason recorded. Preserve the person, documented paths and observations in the research record; omitting a measurement does not justify deleting a real path from the tree. A possible second line was the reason for withholding one match in the cited case. If essential evidence remains multiply related, use a model whose documented assumptions support the actual pedigree and dependencies, or compare hypotheses qualitatively. Useful segment/cluster roles remain available in [ai-for-dna-research](ai-for-dna-research.md#untangle-multiple-paths-without-discarding-the-useful-evidence). (Family Locket, 2025-03-27)
- **Total shared cM is not proof of a shared *ancestor*.** A total-cM figure can reflect one genuinely shared ancestral segment *or* several unrelated small segments that happen to sum to the same number — WATO scores the total either way, so don't over-trust a placement whose total leans on small fragments. Where segment data are available, check whether all members actually match one another on the same interval; overlap relative to the target alone is not triangulation. Triangulation alone does not validate a small segment or establish its ancestral age or source line. Keep segment validity and attribution to the proposed ancestry as separate questions; a documented genealogical relationship remains useful even when its reported small segment cannot be used as genetic evidence. (Bettinger, 2022-08-07)
- **A cM-based "estimated relationship" as fact.** A tool's predicted relationship (Ancestry/23andMe "estimated relationship") is derived from shared-DNA *amount* alone and routinely differs from the truth — the same cM spans several relationships. Carry all candidates, never the single predicted label.
- **A ThruLine as fact — for a different reason.** ThruLines are inferred from *linked member trees* combined with DNA, so they can faithfully propagate *someone else's tree mistake*. The most instructive failure across the cases reviewed is a *false through-line*: Ancestry's ThruLines proposed a father for the target that *looked* DNA-backed but was wrong, and documents (plus the shape of the DNA) disproved it. In that case the disproven ThruLine proposed **Hopson Milner** as the father, and its apparent DNA support traced back to the target herself rather than to a distinct ancestor. (Family Locket RLP 387, 2025-12-08)
- **The WATO ranking as a conclusion.** The top hypothesis is the *best next thing to test*, not the answer. Every case reviewed that resolved did so with newspapers, wills, census, or a family bible on top of the DNA. (Kitty Cooper; RLP 387)
- **Public-tree identities.** Two women with the same name and birth year get merged into one person constantly; a single bad merge routes your whole hypothesis to the wrong branch. Verify the intermediate people, don't inherit them. (Kitty Cooper, 2026-01-09)
- **A single record's dates.** Census age drift is normal (one subject read 1815, then 1816, 1817, 1818 across enumerations) — triangulate dates across sources before you let one pin a hypothesis. (Family Locket RLP 387, 2025-12-08)
- **Parent names on an adoptee's birth record.** Treat each named parent as a separate claim to test, not a tree fact: DNA may support one and contradict the other, so record each outcome. Check the adoption, amended-certificate and record-sealing rules in force at that time and place; an amended certificate can name the adoptive parents while the court file is sealed. Where illegitimacy carried stigma, a false name may have been given, so a candidate birth or hospital record with a mismatched parent name is not automatically the wrong record. Keep it unresolved until DNA and independent records decide. The [Eberle client case](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yg8KbRoSN8U), [James Tanner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E3we4fIR2qM) and [Denyse Allen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H_EJZccJQfc) motivate this.
- **Non-identifying information as complete or correct.** An earlier release may not be final, because what counts as non-identifying can depend on the person handling the request. Where the jurisdiction allows, request it again, along with the original birth certificate and the adoption file, and record what each release contained. Treat each detail, and what the adoptive family was told, as a clue that may be wrong: a birth mother may have concealed her identity or named the wrong man, and adoptive parents may have been given false background. Use the details to rank candidates; do not exclude one on a single mismatch. Legacy Tree's [adoption research tips](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/successful-adoption-research-with-genetic-genealogy) motivate this.
- **A mutual-consent registry's 'no match' as a negative.** These registries connect an adoptee and a birth parent only when both have filed consent, so 'no match' means only that no matching consent was on file when the registry checked. It does not show that the adoption file or original birth certificate is missing or unobtainable. In some states the registry forms are themselves the route to the original birth certificate, so read which request a refusal answered. Pursue the rest through their own routes, each under its current eligibility rules: the court or county that finalized the adoption, and the state's current original-birth-certificate procedure. Record the registry result, its date and each separate request as their own rows. A birth parent may file later, so note when to check the registry again. A [DNAngels case](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8f_jPZ96-hQ) motivates this.
- **Lean on mid-range matches, not the closest ones.** WATO's discriminating power sits in the mid-range — published as reliable roughly back to a 2nd/3rd-great-grandparent, growing misleading past ~4C1R. A *very close* match is the wrong workhorse for two reasons: (a) it usually **identifies the answer directly** and needs no odds engine, and (b) among several roughly **equidistant** candidate slots it **doesn't discriminate** between them. (Ignore the common backwards rationale that "close ranges overlap" — it's the reverse: total-cM bands overlap worst at *intermediate* degrees like 1C / half-1C / grand-aunt, and even aunt/uncle vs grandparent vs half-sibling are hard to separate by total cM — which is exactly why you carry *all* candidate relationships, never a single label.) (Independence *among* the matches is a separate constraint — see match selection.) (Family Locket, 2025-03-27; DNA Painter WATO docs)

## Match selection dominates the answer (input quality > input volume)

**The right matches matter more than the number of matches** — this is the most repeated lesson across the cases reviewed. (Family Locket, 2024-03-18)

- **Closeness of the question sets the bar.** A closer question needs fewer, closer matches; a distant hypothesis with a weak paper trail needs more matches from *additional independent lines*. A half-sibling match can identify an unknown father in one step; a single first-cousin match often can't, because you still have to exclude the other candidate uncles. (Family Locket, 2024-03-18)
- **Check dependence *among* the matches.** DNA Painter's WATO guidance is to avoid tested descendants more closely related to each other than first cousins. Prefer coverage through **different children** of the ancestral couple, while recording shared descent and additional paths. Parent/child or sibling measurements must not become independent votes; retain them for transmission and pedigree checks, or use a model that handles their dependence. This concerns relationships *among* matches, not how close each is to the target. (Family Locket, 2024-03-18; DNA Painter WATO docs; The DNA Geek, 2022-08-14)
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> Each WATO analysis receives living testers' and matches' cM and tree positions; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before entering them.
- **Several related testers, one match: keep separate analyses.** When same-generation relatives of the target each compare with a match, keep a separate WATO analysis for each focal tester, entering each match once. Retain the related testers' observations for qualitative comparison; do not pool duplicated entries into stronger odds, and never sum testers' cM. The [Eberle case study](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zHwjmnpTz-c) places one match once per tester ('twinning') for four testers (the mother and her three first cousins) in one pooled run. Keep each tester's analysis separate instead: the case does not show how WATO weights the duplicated entries.
- **Mind weak, fragmented segments.** False-match risk is driven mainly by **segment-level** quality — the *longest single segment*, SNP density, phasing, and the platform's own matching threshold — not by a flat total-cM number. A single clean ~20 cM segment is generally a *real* match; the thing to distrust is a ~20 cM *total* stitched from several short fragments, each near the noise floor, which can be identical-by-chance. Do not use unresolved small fragments as genetic corroboration merely because stronger matches support the placement. Retain the documentary relationship and source observations separately.
- **Know whether a displayed cM figure has been filtered.** A 2019 [GeneaVlogger WATO tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ppQfwZHHqNk) describes Ancestry's Timber algorithm as removing segments judged common in the population before Ancestry reports a total; check Ancestry's current documentation. Record whether each figure you enter is filtered. If one low value zeroes out an otherwise supported hypothesis, especially in an endogamous family, check whether filtering caused the zero before you discard the hypothesis. Do not invent a correction or enter a guessed raw figure.
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: upload --> Before any upload of the tester's raw DNA, answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` and get the tester's informed, explicit permission for that database and its matching and visibility settings (see [Ethics and the human layer](#ethics-and-the-human-layer)); permission to work the kit does not cover an upload.
- **Grow the pool across databases.** With that permission, uploading the tester's raw data to a database that accepts third-party transfers surfaces matches, and whole *independent descent lines*, absent from the database you started in. Such databases: **GEDmatch** always; **FTDNA** when its transfer program is open and accepts that file's version (availability and accepted versions change, so check first); **Living DNA**, which accepts free uploads of AncestryDNA, 23andMe, MyHeritage and FamilyTreeDNA (Family Finder) files and shows matches only after the tester opts into its Family Networks (checked 2026-11-06). (MyHeritage no longer accepts third-party raw-DNA uploads, so a tester without an existing MyHeritage kit must test there to reach that match pool; AncestryDNA and 23andMe never accepted uploads.) This is a standard **early** move, and often decisive in burned-county cases where the paper trail is gone.
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: contact --> Asking relatives or a match's older relatives to test, or asking matches for collaborator access with a research summary and diagram, discloses your research and living people's details; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before any approach.
- **Coverage is real: different relatives inherit different segments.** Testing several relatives of the same generation, and asking key matches to share their *full* match list (Ancestry "Collaborator" access), surfaces relevant matches that don't overlap your own DNA at all. A short research summary + a connection diagram makes matches far likelier to share. (Family Locket, 2026-02-12)
- **Recruit a close match's older relatives early, not only after scoring.** An adoptee with no known relatives still has targeted-testing options. When a close match has no other tested close relatives, ask whether the match's parents, aunts, uncles or other older relatives will test. Which of them share DNA with the target shows which branch of the match's tree carries the connection, before you build a descendancy for each side. A relative absent from a match list is missing or below threshold, not zero (see the tie section). If relatives on both sides of the match share DNA, check for a second path. A tested parent and child are one line of evidence, not two votes (see dependence above). Legacy Tree's [adoption research tips](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/successful-adoption-research-with-genetic-genealogy) motivate this.
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> Adding newly found living matches' cM to WATO gives the tool their genetic data and family positions; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first.
- **Search existing match lists after descendancy research.** Once a candidate emerges, build that candidate's descendants forward, limited to names, parents and family positions: no current addresses, phone numbers, emails, workplaces or online accounts, and no people-search services; any contact goes through [DNA match outreach staging](dna-match-outreach-staging.md). Then search every tester's match list for those names, including matches with no tree, which the first sort easily misses. Verify the descent with documents, then add each match's cM per tester (see the multi-tester point above). A large jump in WATO odds is still relative to the hypotheses you built, not proof. In the [Eberle case study](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zHwjmnpTz-c), a strong tree-less match descending from the candidate was in the list throughout and came to light only through descendancy research.
- **Don't over-collect.** Match counts in resolved cases often cluster in the high single digits; more matches from the *wrong* branch resolve nothing. Add matches deliberately to confirm a specific branch, not to bulk up. (Family Locket, 2024-03-18)

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: publish --> A progress brief or connection diagram shared with a match
exposes research findings and living people's tree details; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` first.

For a willing recipient, an optional source-linked progress brief can show the original
objective, actual findings, represented and still-missing child branches, and the specific
contribution being requested. Keep a private identity key separate from a redacted report or
connection diagram. The [test-taker recruitment example](https://familylocket.com/rlp-204-recruiting-test-takers-for-an-autosomal-dna-project/)
motivates this preparation; a private document can provide the same context as a website.

## When two hypotheses survive: decide what would separate them

**Diagnose the tie before adding testers or removing inconvenient matches.** Keep the same candidate set, people, cM measurements, probability model and age assumptions while comparing the two explanations.

1. **Inspect the contribution of each match, not just the final score.** Write the relationship predicted by H1 and H2 beside each observation and inspect the scorer's per-match breakdown where available. For WATO's per-match total-cM calculation, if every scored relationship uses the same distribution under both hypotheses, with the same other inputs and priors, those data cannot distinguish them. If different matches favor opposite candidates, an aggregate tie instead identifies a conflict to investigate. Start with the decisive match's pedigree, half/full edges, generation and possible second path. Perl's worked example shows why this matters: the better-scoring placement was wrong, and other family tests confirmed the lower-scoring one. (Perl, 2022-04-28; The DNA Geek, 2023-09-29)
2. **Add a comparison whose prediction changes.** For each prospective tester, state their documented descent, expected relationship under each hypothesis, and what a match or non-match could distinguish. In the *Jan* analysis, a pair with the same relationship in all three hypotheses was not used to discriminate them. Such a pair can still check the background tree. Prefer a usable contrasting branch over another tester who repeats the same relationship pattern. (The DNA Geek, 2023-09-29)
3. **Treat an absent shared-match entry as an observation with a reporting limit.** Establish which pair would have appeared, the threshold on both legs, and whether the list is complete and accessible. Where the display rules are known, absence means “below the shared-match threshold,” not “shares zero DNA.” If those rules or coverage are unknown, keep it missing. A below-threshold observation helps only where H1 and H2 predict it differently. Do not enter it as 0 cM or multiply absences across related descendants as independent exclusions. The cited 2022 case worked with a 20 cM limit in its view (The DNA Geek, 2022-08-14). On AncestryDNA that limit still applies to the standard shared-match list, which omits a shared match who shares less than 20 cM with the tester; Pro Tools Enhanced Shared Matches lists lower amounts (Estes, 2022-05-12; see [AncestryDNA match investigation](ancestry-dna-match-investigation.md)). Ancestry's current help states no figure, so check the platform's current display rule.
4. **Separate changes to the evidence from changes to the model or denominator.** Record a model change, such as a switch between the 2016 and 2020 probability sets or turning age-based probabilities on or off, as a separate analysis. When relative scores are scaled to the weakest surviving hypothesis, adding a very poor candidate can greatly increase both leaders' displayed scores without improving their ratio to each other. Deleting alternatives can similarly inflate percentages. Compare H1 directly with H2 under the same setup. Inspect a zero's cause before calling it biological impossibility: a zero model likelihood, display rounding and a filtered-out candidate are different states. Do not choose the model or candidate list because it produces the preferred winner. (Family Locket, 2025-03-12; ratio and zero-state distinctions are this recipe's synthesis.)

**Find which rule produced a zero.** In the original WATO, Perl lists three: the cM amounts make the position impossible, birth or death dates make it impossible, or the hypothesis is more than a million times less likely than the top one; a lone hypothesis scores only 0 or 1 (Perl, 2022-04-28 [N7 @2026-11-12]). The WATO plus FAQ gives an impossible hypothesis a zero and every other hypothesis its share of the total score (WATO plus FAQ [N51 @2026-11-12]). So a zero can come from a date you entered, and the million-fold zero is relative to the current leader, not an exclusion. Focus on the hypothesis or open its score calculation and name the relationship or date that caused the zero before you call a slot impossible.

**Fictional selection example:** A, B and C are full brothers. The target's known mother is unrelated to their family; H1 makes A the father and H2 makes B the father. Existing testers are grandchildren of C, with no additional paths. Each is a first cousin once removed to the target under either hypothesis, so another equivalent C-branch tester leaves that symmetry intact. A documented daughter of A by a different, unrelated mother would instead be a half-sister under H1 and a first cousin under H2. Her test could help distinguish the candidates; the C-branch testers cannot. This example describes relationships, not computed odds or a real case.

| Decision | Keep or add | Selection limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A tie persists | A documented tester with different predictions under H1/H2; evidence checking the disputed edge | Do not recruit another equivalent-path tester solely to break this tie; retain existing observations for coverage and pedigree checks |
| A match has a second path | The person and every documented path; separate segment/pedigree work | Withhold their cM from a single-path calculation; never subtract an invented contribution for the other path |
| One branch drives the winner | Audit that branch; compare a recorded leave-one-branch-out copy with the baseline | Omit evidence only for a stated diagnostic purpose, applying the same inclusion rule to every candidate |
| Close relatives repeat observations | Relatives for transmission, coverage or a model that handles dependence | Do not count related measurements or absences as independent votes |

Finish with **why the hypotheses remain tied, the most useful next comparison, and the outcome that would favor each**. If no available observation separates them, leave the choice unresolved. Full family inventories and branch/generation sensitivity belong to [ai-for-dna-research](ai-for-dna-research.md#compare-families-across-branches-and-generations).

### Check what the calculation actually evaluated

Trace both tested people's descent to the proposed common ancestor, including
intervening generations and half/full edges. A person visible in a tree need
not supply a scored observation. When shared-cM observations to both a matching
parent and that parent's child are entered, the original WATO FAQ says only the
parent's observation is used. An untested parent drawn in the tree does not
replace a tested child's observation. When
placing an untested ancestor, represent the tested descendant's path in each
hypothesis; do not score the ancestor as though they supplied the test.
For other pedigree-aware models, audit the route and observations the same way.

**When moving a legacy tree into WATO Plus, check whom each hypothesis represents.**
Preserve the old tree, candidate set, tested people and run inputs before using an
available transfer route. Perl's [2024 migration account](https://blog.dnapainter.com/blog/from-what-are-the-odds-to-wato-plus-in-one-click/)
describes old hypotheses representing the DNA tester, while the Plus hypotheses
represent the proposed unknown parent; that conversion removes the old hypotheses.
Rebuild the plausible alternatives for the intended question and inspect each path
from the proposed parent through intervening generations to the actual tester. The
[introduction](https://blog.dnapainter.com/blog/introducing-wato-a-new-tool-for-investigating-family-mysteries/)
describes generating that trail from the structured question. A copied tree does not preserve hypothesis
meaning by itself; removed alternatives or changed model/age settings are not new
genealogical evidence. Do not compare scores until you know whom each hypothesis
represents and which path it takes.

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: third-party-tool --> Birth and death years, family positions and the research question entered in WATO Plus describe living people in the tree; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before entering them.

**Set up the research question and age limits before you suggest hypotheses.** Each WATO Plus hypothesis is a candidate for the unknown parent, not a place for the tester. Enter the birth year of the person whose parent is unknown: the tester's own when the tester seeks their own parent, otherwise that of the tester's parent, grandparent or other ancestor, never the tester's by default (WATO plus FAQ [N51 @2026-11-12]; Perl, 2025-02-11 [N50 @2026-11-12]; WATO plus guide [N55 @2026-11-12]). The Father, Mother or Parent choice names the parent being sought, not the person of unknown parentage (WATO plus guide [N55 @2026-11-12]). Enter as many birth and death years as the tree supports, and the other biological parent's birth year if it is known: the generator uses ages at the birth, through the **Max and min Ages** settings under **Actions and Settings**, to decide who could be a parent (Southard, 2024-02-23 [N56 @2026-11-12]; Perl, 2024-01-30 [N54 @2026-11-12]; WATO plus guide [N55 @2026-11-12]). At launch the limits were 14 for the minimum, 54 for a mother and 75 for a father (Perl, 2024-02-26 [N31 @2026-11-12]). Read the current values before you trust the suggestions, record them with the run, and widen them when a candidate could fall outside them: a wrong birth year or a narrow limit drops the true placement without warning. **Suggest Hypotheses** stays inactive until the tree holds a match over 40 cM (DNA Painter tutorial [N57 @2026-11-12]).

**Match the generator's settings to what your research has established.** **Stop WATO from adding unknown people to the tree** is off by default, and Suggest Hypotheses then adds full and half-siblings of anyone with matching descendants as possible unknown candidates. Tick it only when research shows the tree already holds every plausible candidate; WATO then tests only people already in the tree. **Allow WATO to add siblings of the root person** is also off by default, so new people are added only as descendants of the root couple. Leave it off when the matches establish descent from that couple, because hypotheses through the root person's siblings cannot then be right. Turn it on when that descent is not established, or when WATO finds no feasible hypothesis among the couple's descendants, and expect the tree to grow (Perl, 2024-02-16 [N58 @2026-11-12]; WATO plus FAQ [N51 @2026-11-12]; WATO plus guide [N55 @2026-11-12]). WATO keeps these settings only in that browser, so record them with each run (Perl, 2024-02-16 [N58 @2026-11-12]).

**When WATO finds no feasible hypothesis, find the cause before you loosen the model.** Add a few hypotheses by hand and open their score calculation to see which relationships rule each one out; the usual causes are an unknown extra relationship that inflates the cM, or a tree error such as a person entered in the wrong generation (Perl, 2021-06-24 [N59 @2026-11-12]). The current guide's warning also points to a very large tree, small matches, the settings above, the age limits, the cM amounts and the birth years (WATO plus guide [N55 @2026-11-12]). WATO Plus's experimental **Flex** setting, meant for endogamy or pedigree collapse, lowers the entered cM amounts by 5 to 50% before scoring (WATO plus FAQ [N51 @2026-11-12]; Southard, 2024-02-23 [N56 @2026-11-12]). Record Flex as a separate model setting. It does not model the extra paths, so a match with a known second path still stays out of single-path scoring (see the multi-path rule above).

Preserve the selected people, exact test comparisons, evidence date and
inclusion rules for the run. A current export cannot reconstruct undocumented
historical inputs. If a past run or component is unavailable, make no score
comparison against it, and do not treat two missing results as equal.

After new evidence arrives, check whether it changed an input the calculation
uses. **An unchanged score is not a verdict on new genealogy.** WATO uses
autosomal total-cM observations. New triangulation may inform ancestral
attribution without changing those totals; a triad involving the target and
one confined to comparison relatives answer different placement questions.
Retain that evidence separately. (DNA Painter original WATO FAQ; Kinprove synthesis)

## Beyond autosomal: Y-DNA, mtDNA, X-DNA as line-narrowing and exclusion tools

WATO scores **autosomal *total* cM only**. **X-DNA is handled separately** (it follows a distinct, restricted inheritance path), and Y-DNA / mtDNA aren't cM-scored at all. Use these as independent tools *around* the autosomal work — they narrow lines and, powerfully, *exclude* them:

- **Y-DNA** — a strong tool for unknown-**father** cases, but it needs two results. First, someone must carry the unknown father's Y: the target himself if male, a brother who shares the target's biological father (a full brother or a paternal half-brother), or a son or other male-line descendant of one of them. A half-brother through the mother, the mother's brother or the mother's father does not carry it. Second, to test a named candidate, compare that result with the candidate himself or a male-line relative of his (his son, a brother who shares his father, or a paternal-line cousin). A candidate's relatives alone cannot test the target's line. Y-matches *often* cluster by **surname** and can hand you a candidate surname before you have a tree — but not always: NPEs, adoptions, name changes and pre-surname-era ancestry break the surname signal, so treat a surname cluster as a strong lead, not a guarantee.
- **mtDNA** — traces the **direct maternal** line (mother's mother's mother…). Because mtDNA mutates slowly, a shared maternal ancestor can lie far outside the genealogical timeframe, so an mtDNA **match** is *supportive/consistent-with* a proposed direct-maternal relationship, not confirmation of it. A resolved **mismatch**, however, is a clean *exclusion* of a direct-maternal-line hypothesis.
- **X-DNA** — its restricted **non-PAR** inheritance pattern can **exclude** candidate paths (a father passes no non-PAR X to a son), narrowing which ancestral line a shared segment can have come from. An absent reported match needs the coverage and comparison-scope checks in [X-DNA judgment](xdna-phasing-judgment.md) before it can support exclusion.

**Remove X-DNA from a vendor total before you enter it.** When you combine matches from several companies, subtract the X amount from every vendor total that includes X-DNA, and record both the original and the adjusted figure, because WATO scores autosomal totals only. Vendors differ and change: the X reporting table in [DNA comparison controls](dna-comparison-controls.md#establish-what-could-have-been-observed) says how five services report X, and each vendor's current documentation decides. The [Mary Eberle WATO session](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kDsqJNtZIaY) motivates this.

## Named failure modes (the least-obvious, most reusable part)

- **A focused tree manufactures a winner.** A percentage increase after pruning can reflect omitted alternatives, not new evidence; compare the surviving leaders against the preserved baseline. (Family Locket, 2025-03-27, pruning case; comparison control synthesized above)
- **The "don't add unknown people as hypotheses" setting** shrinks the search space but can *bias results toward already-known, unmatched children* — know which way you've tilted it. (Family Locket, 2025-03-27)
- **Pedigree collapse** makes one person occupy several tree positions at once; WATO's odds become uninterpretable (same root cause as endogamy inflating cM).
- **Forcing a slot when none fits the cM.** When every candidate placement gives some matches too much DNA and others too little, research every descendant branch of the matches' common ancestor, not only the matches' own lines, and look for marriages between those branches. A match who descends through both branches has a double relationship; withhold that match's cM from single-path scoring (see "never trust," above) rather than adjusting it by a guessed amount. A [Legacy Tree client case](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/dna-doesnt-lie-how-to-accurately-interpret-dna-test-results) motivates this.
- **The false ThruLine** (above) — a member-tree-inferred hypothesis that looks DNA-backed but points at the wrong parent.
- **Treating triangulation as segment validation** — placing a reported small segment on an ancestral line because it triangulates or its bearers share a documented ancestor. Those observations alone resolve neither the segment's validity nor its age and source line (see "never trust," above).
- **Ambiguous cM bands.** ~269 cM in a documented case sat between "2nd cousin" and "half-1C1R" — never let WATO (or an AI) collapse that to a single relationship; carry *all* candidate relationships forward. (Family Locket, 2025-03-27)
- **Same-name conflation** and **single-record date drift** (above) route hypotheses to the wrong branch before WATO even runs.
- **Assuming the documented spouse is the other biological parent.** Once one parental side resolves, research the documented partners. If none of them connect to the remaining match groups, treat that as a red flag and do not force the matches to fit. Locate the other side's convergence on its own, then weigh non-marital conception or adoption against records that place the candidates together in time and place. The fictional [BYU FIGG case](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bVJc1zapFE) motivates this. Co-location only makes a pairing plausible; identification requires correlating DNA and records, with direct confirmatory DNA where possible and appropriate.
- **Expecting a misattributed parent to leave strangers in the match list.** If presumed cousins on one line consistently share about half what the documented relationship predicts, but no unexplained match group appears, consider a biological parent from a family already in the tree. Look for an excess of matches on another known line, and trace that line's descendants for candidates. If the proposed placement creates pedigree collapse or another repeated path to an affected match, withhold that match from single-path scoring (see "never trust," above). A [discussion of halved sharing on one line](https://genarchivist.net/showthread.php?tid=1838) motivates this.
- **Deciding from one pair which relative is off the line.** When proposed close relatives share no DNA, or consistently less than their documented relationship allows, the pair alone cannot show which of them is misattributed, or whether both are. Compare each with other documented descendants of the couple and with collateral relatives of each spouse. Someone who matches both spouses' sides at expected levels supports full descent. Someone who lacks those matches, or lacks one spouse's side and shares consistently low amounts with the other descendants, becomes the non-descent or half-relationship candidate; do not decide half versus full from the pair's cM alone. Without such controls, leave the question open. Non-descent from the couple does not show which link broke, parent-child or grandparent-parent; test the intermediate generation before placing the break. Zero sharing is a strong signal only at second cousin or closer, where detectable sharing is near-certain. A one-sided gap in collateral matches can also mean that side's lines are untested; check who from that side has tested before reading the gap as non-descent. Two Legacy Tree articles, on [proving the exact nature of misattributed parentage](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/proving-the-exact-nature-of-misattributed-parentage) and the [six signs](https://www.legacytree.com/?p=80153), motivate this.

## Prompt-craft when an AI is in the loop

1. **Provide complete inputs, don't expect inference** — hand the AI the finished cM/relationship table and the explicit tree structure, not "here's a match list, figure out the tree."
2. **Never ask for a point relationship from a cM number.** Demand *all* candidate relationships with their odds, and route the actual math to WATO / the Shared cM Tool.
3. **Tell it about endogamy / multiple relationships explicitly** — it cannot detect them and cannot quantify the correction; if you don't say so, it will treat inflated cM as a closer relationship.
4. **Flag the weak-segment and total-cM caveats** — tell it that a total leaning on several short fragments isn't reliable placement evidence, and that total cM alone can't distinguish a shared ancestor from coincidental segments. (Placement in WATO comes from a *documented tree connection plus shared cM*; segment comparisons require their own validity and attribution checks; triangulation is not a WATO prerequisite.)
5. **Prompt-chain the reasoning arc:** cluster → name the ancestral couple → build the descendancy → propose hypotheses. Don't ask for the last step first.
6. **De-identify before you *share data with any third-party tool*, not just before you publish** — WATO, the Shared cM Tool, a cloud LLM, and GEDmatch are all third parties; strip direct identifiers before you paste a match list or upload a kit, and get consent before processing someone else's data (see Ethics). Note raw DNA is inherently re-identifiable, so this is *pseudonymization*, not true anonymization.
7. **For a tie, demand a contrast table before a test recommendation.** List H1/H2 relationships and available score contributions; mark unavailable contributions explicitly. State the proposed tester's different predictions, the reason for each omission, and whether an absent row is missing or a verified below-threshold observation. Do not manufacture odds or tune the input to obtain a winner.

## Ethics and the human layer

De-identifying before you use a third-party tool reduces exposure *inside the tool* — but raw DNA is inherently re-identifiable, so it is never true anonymization, and it does not cover the human stakes of the search.

- **Consent, not just de-identification.** Get the tester's informed agreement before working their kit, and be conservative with third parties who never opted into your search — you are processing *their* genetic data too. Seek consent by default: it is the community norm and, in many jurisdictions, a legal requirement for genetic data.
- **NPE sensitivity.** Unknown-parentage work routinely surfaces *not-parent-expected* events (misattributed parentage, undisclosed adoptions, donor conception). These are family secrets with living stakeholders — handle findings privately and let the people involved control disclosure.
- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: contact --> A first approach to an identified bio-parent or close relative discloses a parentage finding about living people; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before any contact.
- **Making contact is a human act, not a data step.** Identifying a bio-parent or close relative ends the analysis but begins the hardest part: a first approach can be life-changing — welcome or unwelcome — for both sides. Go slowly, lead with the other person's choice to engage, and don't treat a confirmed hypothesis as license to appear on a doorstep.

For a proposed first approach, keep permission to use the test/kit, the family or branch the
recipient wants to explore, and their willingness to hear unexpected findings as separate
choices. Leave unexpressed preferences unknown; kit permission does not settle the other two.
The [collaboration example](https://www.yourdnaguide.com/ydgblog/contacting-african-american-dna-matches)
and [informed-consent discussion](https://www.yourdnaguide.com/ydgblog/informed-consent-for-dna-testing)
motivate this distinction.

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: contact --> A heads-up about unexpected findings discloses sensitive
parentage information to a living person; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before sending it.

If unexpected findings may be discussed, prepare an unsent private heads-up and a short
outline separating supported observations from unresolved interpretations. Let the recipient
choose whether to continue and which channel to use; respect a pause or decline. Send only
with the researcher's explicit authorization. The [communication example](https://www.yourdnaguide.com/ydgblog/dna-test-surprise-unexpected-news)
supports this preparation without promising a particular response.

## Closing the case — and privacy discipline

- **Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: contact --> Recruiting a living relative to DNA-test discloses the hypothesis and asks for their genetic data; answer the privacy gate in `SKILL.md` before any approach.
- **WATO points; DNA testing or documents close.** Treat the top hypothesis as the objective for the next round — which may be recruiting a specific relative to DNA-test *or* a records search: wills that name (or omit) children, marriage and census records, a family bible, newspapers. In the cases reviewed, candidate **elimination** did as much work as the DNA — e.g. ruling out specific Dillard candidates via a will and a family bible. (Family Locket RLP 387, 2025-12-08)
- **Prefer convergent confirmation over elimination.** A candidate father is stronger when separate matches tie to both of his parents' lines. Matches through only one parent's family leave his relatives in that family as alternatives. Narrowing by enumerating one branch is weaker, because adoptions, missing people and record errors hide candidates, so report 'identified children', not a complete set. Full brothers usually remain an unresolved sibling set on autosomal DNA. Use city directories from the conception period to rank them: put each brother's address and employer as those historical directories record them beside the mother's; do not look up anyone's current address, workplace or contact details, or use people-search services. That shows opportunity, not paternity. GeneaVlogger's [Breakthrough review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mKL215bfBGc), [Max Miller case](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9RYQTQzo45I) and [descendancy stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qg_l4olzWYI), and the [Family History Fanatics example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wCYIda-4sfk), motivate this.
- **Set the conception window before you rank candidates.** Estimate a conception window from the birth date and any gestation length recorded in the non-identifying information, not one nine-month date; without a reliable gestation length, allow for a premature as well as a full-term birth. Do not assume conception happened at the birthplace; in adoption cases the two often differ. Compare each candidate's location, life situation and family facts in that window with the non-identifying information, and use the result to rank a sibling set DNA cannot separate (see the non-identifying-information point in "never trust," above). Legacy Tree's [adoption research tips](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/successful-adoption-research-with-genetic-genealogy) motivate this.
- **Work in explicit, revisable phases.** State an objective, test it, and be willing to *reject* it; every case reviewed cycled through wrong candidates before the right one. Being wrong on a hypothesis is the method working, not failing. (Family Locket RLP 387, 2025-12-08)
- **Write it up.** Independent lines and hard-to-document descents need a proof argument, not just a WATO screenshot. (Family Locket, 2024-03-18)
