# Recover Pennsylvania marriage licenses, returns and state copies

Use this recipe when an early Pennsylvania marriage index gives only a couple and date, or when a county marriage could identify parents before civil birth and death registration reached statewide coverage. Search the county that issued the license for each part of the filing.

## Use the county marriage filing before statewide vital records

According to the presenter, use a Pennsylvania county marriage license to investigate family details in the years before statewide birth and death registration, and compare early county entries with their state counterparts. [S1†]

For licenses issued from 30 September 1885, search the issuing county’s marriage docket and license files. Use these as family leads for the years before state birth and death registration began in 1906. [S3 @2026-11-18], [S6 @2026-11-18]

According to the podcast adviser, start a colonial search with a marriage bond when the record series includes one, and look for other marriage filings individually. [S1†]

According to the marriage-record presenter, look for a bond, license and return as distinct records where they were created. [S2†]

Check separately for the application, issued license and officiant’s return or duplicate. Record what the applicants reported and what the officiant certified. Franklin County, for example, describes applications and returns as separate archival series. [S3 @2026-11-18], [S5 @2026-11-18]

## Check the incomplete state copy series

For marriages in 1885–1891, check the state’s incomplete *Record of Marriages* in RG 14 alongside the county record. Compare recorded birthplace, residence and date field by field. The reading room series extends past the dated images linked from its older state scan interface. A miss in the state listing leaves the county search open. [S3 @2026-11-18], [S4 @2026-11-18]

## Treat a bond as evidence of intended marriage

For a period with surviving bonds, find the bond and copy its date, principals and named signers by role. Look separately for a marriage entry or an officiant’s return to establish that the ceremony took place. The State Archives lists surviving Philadelphia County bonds among its earlier materials.
