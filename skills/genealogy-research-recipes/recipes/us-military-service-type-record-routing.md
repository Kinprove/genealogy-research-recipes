# Route pre-1900 U.S. military service by service type before searching or ruling it out

Use this recipe before you search for, or rule out, pre-1900 U.S. military service. The usual failure is to search only federal compiled-service or pension indexes and then report "no service". A related mistake is reading an empty donation-land or bounty-land search as meaningful when the man was never eligible. This recipe works out the probable service type, sends each type to its own record series and custodian, labels what each record actually shows, and keeps same-named men apart.

To retrieve a pension or bounty-land file, use [pension-file recovery](us-military-pension-file-recovery.md) (§1 for custodians, §4 for service the file does not declare). For merge or separate rulings, use [evidence/proof judgment](evidence-proof-judgment.md).

## 1. Fix residence and enlistment place, then classify the service

Before opening any index, record where the man lived and where and when he probably enlisted. Without that, a militia man cannot be told apart from a Continental soldier, and either kind is hard to find [S1].

Classify each service clue into exactly one working type, and record the basis for that choice:

- **Continental or volunteer.** Search the compiled military service records (CMSRs) at the National Archives. CMSRs cover the volunteer army (NARA gives 1775–1902) and do **not** cover regular-army enlistments [S2]. CMSRs also exist for volunteers who served in the 1784–1811 "old war" period (NARA M905) [S4]. Revolutionary CMSRs are reconstructions. A War Department fire on 8 November 1800 destroyed most of the original records, and more were lost in 1814. NARA Record Group 93 was later put together as a substitute, from records transferred by the State, Interior and Treasury Departments (1893–1913) and from purchased and copied material [S2]. An incomplete Revolutionary CMSR therefore does not show that a man did not serve.
- **Militia, state line or ranger.** For state-only service, look in state (and sometimes county) records; NARA does not hold state militia records [S1], [S7]. Militia called into federal service also has CMSRs at NARA (for the War of 1812, federalized militia are in RG 94, index M602), so search both. State archives hold state militia, National Guard and Confederate pension records. Confederate pensions are not at NARA [S7]. Ranger companies are a separate in-between category (see §2).
- **Regular army.** Not in the volunteer CMSRs [S2]. For enlisted men, search the Register of Enlistments in the U.S. Army at NARA. For officers, start with Heitman's *Historical Register* and, for service from 1863, the Commission Branch and ACP files in RG 94 (NARA, *Prologue*, Fall 2002).
- **Loyalist.** Treat this as its own route (§5). Patriot service indexes are the wrong place to look.

- **War of 1812 index hits.** NARA has put the War of 1812 CMSR indexes online at Ancestry and Fold3, but the carded service records themselves (NAID 300392) have not been digitized in full; only select files are free in the National Archives Catalog [S10 @2026-11-16]. One presenter (only transcript excerpts read) warns that the records are not generally on Fold3, Ancestry or FamilySearch [S9 @2026-11-16]. Label an online War of 1812 service-record hit as an index entry, not the CMSR. Look for a scanned file in the Catalog; otherwise list the CMSR as unsearched.

- **War of 1812 regulars.** Most enlisted regulars of the war are in the fifteen Register volumes for 1798–1815. An entry can give age, birthplace, physical description, occupation and enlistment details, and the Adjutant General's Office built the entries from muster rolls, payrolls, inspection and descriptive rolls and other records [S11]. Treat an entry as a derived abstract: cite it, then follow it back to the rolls behind it, as one presenter (only transcript excerpts read) advises [S9].

- **Register online.** The Register (NARA M233) is online at Ancestry, FamilySearch and Fold3 [S12 @2026-11-16].

- **Company books.** For 1812–15, NARA Record Group 98 (Records of United States Army Commands, 1784–1821) holds company books and order books for many regular infantry regiments, the 2nd and 3rd Artillery, the 1st and 3rd Rifle regiments and the Light Dragoons [S13 @2026-11-16]. One presenter (only transcript excerpts read) says these company and descriptive books carry the same kinds of detail as the Register [S9 @2026-11-16]. Search them when the Register entry is thin or the man is missing from it.

## 2. Write each series' eligibility rule before reading a miss

Build a routing table with one row per series: custodian, coverage, eligibility rule, and whether a miss is meaningful. A miss counts only when the man met the eligibility rule **and** the series covers his period and place.

**Privacy gate:** <!-- privacy-gate: request --> The order sends the soldier's identifying service details and the requester's contact, delivery and payment details to NARA. Apply the privacy gate before sending it.

**Order an older Army service record.** When an eligible Army service file is still needed, use NATF Form 86 for the identified organization and prepare a separate form for each organization. Check the form's service limits. It covers federal volunteer service from 1775 through 1902, Confederate service from 1861 through 1865, and Regular Army records retrievable by name for enlisted men who served before October 31, 1912 and officers who served before June 30, 1917. Use the branch-specific routes in §1 for Navy and Marine Corps service and the pension-file recipe for pension or bounty-land applications [S177 @2026-11-23] [S178 @2026-11-23]. Use the Form 86 lead from the available excerpt and apply NARA's branch and date limits [S176 @2026-11-23].

**Revolutionary War search misses.** Follow the search-status distinction from the available excerpt of the Strauss interview [S66 @2026-11-21]. Check the series description and finding aid for surviving coverage. Record holdings without searchable images as unsearched unless you actually examined them, and record losses only where a source identifies the affected records [S67 @2026-11-21].

Limit a negative federal search to the series examined. Follow the repository leads from the available excerpt of the interview [S66 @2026-11-21]. For state-only militia, check the relevant state archive or county records [S67 @2026-11-21]. Check the DAR Library's Patriot Records Project Index as a separate route and carry a hit's source locator to the underlying record [S70 @2026-11-21].

**Mexican War teamsters.** Keep enlisted duty and civilian employment open. For suspected volunteer service, check FamilySearch's M616 index and follow a hit to the CMSR [S75 @2026-11-21] [S90 @2026-11-21]. Treat a miss as leaving civilian employment possible, following the advice from the available excerpt [S83 @2026-11-21]. Test Regular Army enlistment separately with the Register (§1). Congress authorized teamster positions in some Regular Army units in 1847 [S84 @2026-11-21]. Look for a hiring, contract or pay record that identifies the man as a civilian before assigning that status.

- **Pennsylvania donation land.** Check service to the end of the war in the Pennsylvania Continental Line. The State Archives lists ten surveying districts in parts of Butler, Clarion, Crawford, Erie, Lawrence, Mercer, Venango and Warren counties and gives separate claimant, patent and register series [S31 @2026-11-19]. An archivist describes a Line soldier whose service ended at the 1781 mutiny and reports grants to some special ranger companies outside the Line [S3 @2026-11-19]. Check the specific company's eligibility. A missing grant cannot exclude earlier Line or militia service.
- **State bounty land.** Search federal and state grants separately. Bockstruck's published index covers Connecticut, Georgia, Maryland, Massachusetts, New York, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, South Carolina and Virginia [S30 @2026-11-19]. A researcher reports that Massachusetts veterans had to choose between state and federal grants [S4 @2026-11-19]. Check the applicable terms and both sets of records before interpreting a federal miss.
  - Search Lloyd Bockstruck's *Revolutionary War Bounty Land Grants Awarded by State Governments* first. The presenter says it is alphabetical regardless of state. Then go to that state's archive for the record itself [S4 @2026-11-19].
  - Virginia warrants could lie in present-day Kentucky and Ohio (the Virginia Military District). The presenter adds West Virginia [S4]. The Kentucky Secretary of State website has 4,748 Virginia Revolutionary War warrants digitized [S4]. Search where the land lay, not only where the soldier came from.

- **War of 1812 state rosters and society compilations.** Many states published rosters of their troops called to duty in the war [S11]. One presenter (only transcript excerpts read) points to state adjutant-general volumes and to state-level books from the Society of the War of 1812 and the Daughters of 1812 [S9]. Each covers only some service types and units. Write that coverage into the routing table, use a hit to find the original record, and count a miss only inside the stated coverage.

  - **Kentucky.** *Report of the Adjutant General of the State of Kentucky: Soldiers of the War of 1812* (Frankfort, 1891) [S15]. The Kentucky Genealogical Society says men who served in the U.S. regulars or in Virginia units do not appear in it, and that the original state-level records were destroyed after it was published [S16]. A Kentuckian missing from it may still be in the Register (§1) or in Virginia or federal records.

  - **New Jersey and Ohio.** *Records of Officers and Men of New Jersey in Wars, 1791–1815* (Adjutant-General's Office, Trenton, 1909) and *Roster of Ohio Soldiers in the War of 1812* (Adjutant General's Office, Columbus, 1916, catalogued under Ohio militia). These two volumes and the Kentucky report are scanned on the Internet Archive [S15 @2026-11-16], [S17 @2026-11-16], [S18 @2026-11-16].

  - **Virginia militia.** Search *Pay Rolls of Militia Entitled to Land Bounty under the Act of Congress of Sept. 28, 1850* and its companion *Muster Rolls of the Virginia Militia in the War of 1812*.
