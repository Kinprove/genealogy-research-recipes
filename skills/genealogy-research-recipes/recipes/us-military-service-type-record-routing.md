# Route pre-1900 U.S. military service by service type before searching or ruling it out

Use this recipe before you search for, or rule out, pre-1900 U.S. military service. The usual failure is to search only federal compiled-service or pension indexes and then report "no service". A related mistake is reading an empty donation-land or bounty-land search as meaningful when the man was never eligible. This recipe works out the probable service type, sends each type to its own record series and custodian, labels what each record actually shows, and keeps same-named men apart.

To retrieve a pension or bounty-land file, use [pension-file recovery](us-military-pension-file-recovery.md) (§1 for custodians, §4 for service the file does not declare). For merge or separate rulings, use [evidence/proof judgment](evidence-proof-judgment.md).

## 1. Fix residence and enlistment place, then classify the service

Before opening any index, record where the man lived and where and when he probably enlisted. Without that, a militia man cannot be told apart from a Continental soldier, and either kind is hard to find [S1].

Classify each service clue into exactly one working type, and record the basis for that choice:

- **Continental or volunteer.** Search the compiled military service records (CMSRs) at the National Archives. CMSRs cover the volunteer army (NARA gives 1775–1902) and do **not** cover regular-army enlistments [S2]. CMSRs also exist for volunteers who served in the 1784–1811 "old war" period (NARA M905) [S4]. Revolutionary CMSRs are reconstructions. A War Department fire on 8 November 1800 destroyed most of the original records, and more were lost in 1814. NARA Record Group 93 was later put together as a substitute, from records transferred by the State, Interior and Treasury Departments (1893–1913) and from purchased and copied material [S2]. An incomplete Revolutionary CMSR therefore does not show that a man did not serve.
- **Militia, state line or ranger.** For state-only service, look in state (and sometimes county) records; NARA does not hold state militia records [S1], [S7]. Militia called into federal service also has CMSRs at NARA (for the War of 1812, federalized militia are in RG 94, index M602), so search both. State archives hold state militia, National Guard and Confederate pension records. Confederate pensions are not at NARA [S7]. Ranger companies are a separate in-between category (see §2).
- **Regular army.** Not in the volunteer CMSRs [S2]. For enlisted men, search the Register of Enlistments in the U.S. Army at NARA. For officers, start with Heitman's *Historical Register* and, for service from 1863, the Commission Branch and ACP files in RG 94 (NARA, *Prologue*, Fall 2002).
- **Loyalist.** Treat this as its own route (§5). Patriot service indexes are the wrong place to look.

- **War of 1812 index hits.** NARA has put the War of 1812 CMSR indexes online at Ancestry and Fold3, but the carded service records themselves (NAID 300392) have not been digitized in full; only select files are free in the National Archives Catalog [S10 @2026-11-16]. One presenter (only transcript excerpts read) warns that the records are not generally on Fold3, Ancestry or FamilySearch [S9 @2026-11-16]. Label an online War of 1812 service-record hit as an index entry, not the CMSR. Look for a scanned file in the Catalog; otherwise list the CMSR as unsearched.

- **War of 1812 regulars.** Most enlisted regulars of the war are in the fifteen Register volumes for 1798–1815. An entry can give age, birthplace, physical description, occupation and enlistment details, and the Adjutant General's Office built the entries from muster rolls, payrolls, inspection and descriptive rolls and other records [S11]. Treat an entry as a derived abstract: cite it, then follow it back to the rolls behind it, as one presenter (only transcript excerpts read) advises [S9].

- **Register online.** The Register (NARA M233) is online at Ancestry, FamilySearch and Fold3 [S12 @2026-11-16].

- **Company books.** For 1812–15, NARA Record Group 98 (Records of United States Army Commands, 1784–1821) holds company books and order books for many regular infantry regiments, the 2nd and 3rd Artillery, the 1st and 3rd Rifle regiments and the Light Dragoons [S13 @2026-11-16]. One presenter (only transcript excerpts read) says these company and descriptive books carry the same kinds of detail as the Register [S9 @2026-11-16]. Search them when the Register entry is thin or the man is missing from it.

**Revolutionary War records outside the service file.** Follow the alternate routes from the available excerpt of the Strauss interview [S66 @2026-11-21]. For pay, start in Treasury RG 217, §217.7.7, with paymasters' accounts (1777–1781) or the index to later settled Revolutionary War claims (1828–1835), according to which transaction you need [S38 @2026-11-21]. For a wartime diplomatic question, search the Committee for Foreign Affairs and Secretaries for Foreign Affairs papers in RG 360, M247 [S68 @2026-11-21]. For follow-up correspondence from 1785–1790, check the foreign letters in State Department RG 59, M61 [S69 @2026-11-21].

**Privateering commissions.** For a Revolutionary letter of marque, identify the vessel and issuing authority before choosing a record series. Follow Continental Congress commissions and state-issued commissions as separate leads [S34 @2026-11-20] [S35 @2026-11-20]. For the War of 1812, start with the State Department's letters-of-marque correspondence in RG 59, M588, roll 1. Check applications and abstracts returned by customs collectors as well as applications sent directly to the department [S36 @2026-11-20].

**Navy muster and pay rolls.** If the Navy rolls in RG 45 or RG 24 leave a gap [S58 @2026-11-20], check the Treasury series in National Archives RG 217, §217.9.5. Use its card index (1828–1907) to find muster, pay and receipt rolls (1820–1898) [S38 @2026-11-20]. Follow the presenter's advice (only transcript excerpts read) to check the index by ship or shore station even when the series description suggests a gap, then look for the relevant dates in the rolls [S37 @2026-11-20].

For a Coast Survey vessel or revenue cutter that operated with the Navy [S61] [S39], search by vessel name in the Navy muster rolls. Also test the vessel name in the Treasury index when the Navy rolls leave a gap, as the presenter suggests (only transcript excerpts read) [S37].

**Treasury Navy service records.** Follow the lead from the available excerpt of the Treasury talk [S71 @2026-11-21]. For Navy enlisted men or warrant officers, check the separate 1825–1892 service-record series in the Fourth Auditor's Record and Prize Division, RG 217, §217.9.5, even if the ship's payroll turns up nothing [S38 @2026-11-21].

**Marine detachments aboard Navy ships.** Follow the detachment route from the available excerpt of the USS Constitution talk [S79 @2026-11-21]. Search Marine Corps RG 127 by month and ship, using T1118 for 1798–1892. Browse it at FamilySearch or search the Marine rolls at Ancestry. Run a separate Navy muster search for sailors [S81 @2026-11-21] [S80 @2026-11-21].

For the earliest USS Constitution detachment, start the Marine roll search in 1798, the year of the first voyage and the opening year of T1118 [S82] [S77]. Keep the 1797 launch as a separate date in the ship's chronology, following the dating lead from the available excerpt [S79].

If the type is unknown, keep two or more routes open. Do not choose one by default. Expect a man to have service of more than one type. An empty or incomplete CMSR search does not end the search. Check pension and state records for service the CMSR leaves out (pension recipe §4).

**Quaker meeting clues.** Treat a Revolutionary-era Quaker monthly meeting minute that mentions military activity as a claim lead [S28]. Use any place of service named in the minute [S25] to choose the compiled service, pension and bounty-land records to search. Match each series to the probable service type and its eligibility rules (§2) [S29]. Keep the meeting's location separate from the place of service unless the minute connects them.

With a known NARA microfilm-publication number, look it up in NARA's digitization-partner table and follow the listed collection links. Keep the National Archives Catalog Availability Status column separate from the extent of each partner's collection; check the target collection's coverage before reading a miss. Do not assume that a provider's name search indexes the publication number, or that a link to one partner proves the whole series is online. [S23 @2026-11-16](https://www.pricegen.com/7512-2/) [S24 @2026-11-16](https://www.archives.gov/digitization/digitized-by-partners)

**Former Confederates in U.S. Volunteer Infantry.** For service in the 1st–6th U.S. Volunteer Infantry, 1864–1866, look up RG 94 microfilm M1017 in NARA's digitization-partner table and open the listed partner collection, following the suggestion from the available excerpt of the microfilm talk [S73 @2026-11-21] [S24 @2026-11-21]. Search these U.S. volunteer service files separately from the man's Confederate service [S74 @2026-11-21].

**Mexican War volunteers in Mormon organizations.** Follow the publication route from the available excerpt of the microfilm talk [S73 @2026-11-21]. Use the alphabetical volunteer index M616 as a name lead and open the actual compiled service file in RG 94, M351. Follow the M351 collection links from NARA's Mexican War guide or its digitization-partner table [S75 @2026-11-21] [S24 @2026-11-21].

For the Mormon Battalion, choose M351 roll 1 for A–G, roll 2 for H–R, or roll 3 for S–Z [S76 @2026-11-21]. For a physical film search, use the roll list and confirm the viewing location, following the route from the available excerpt [S73 @2026-11-21] [S89 @2026-11-21].

**Battle of Tippecanoe, November 1811.** Look up T1085 in NARA's Microfilm Catalog, following the lead from the available excerpt of the microfilm talk [S73 @2026-11-21]. Use the single roll for both militia and Regular Army muster and pay records [S77 @2026-11-21] [S89 @2026-11-21]. Check current viewing listings and include the Indiana State Library's microform holdings as a separate option [S78 @2026-11-21].

**Mexican War regular Army units.** Follow the unit-roster lead from the available excerpt of the Treasury talk [S71 @2026-11-21]. Search the Second Auditor's *Roster of Officers and Regular Army Units Serving in the Mexican War*, January 1845–January 1850, RG 217, §217.7.3, NAID 2580614 [S38 @2026-11-21] [S72 @2026-11-21]. Carry the unit, commanding officer and any payment date or paymaster named into the search for unit rolls and individual service records.

**Army paymasters' accounts.** Follow the parallel rolls search from the available excerpt of the Treasury talk [S71 @2026-11-21]. If the settled Army paymaster accounts (1815–1861) lack the roll you need, search the separately listed muster rolls and payrolls (1815–1866) in the Second Auditor's Archives Division, RG 217, §217.7.7 [S38 @2026-11-21].

**Colonial Southern militia and Gooch's American Regiment.** Use Murtie June Clark's *Colonial Soldiers of the South, 1732–1774*. In the 1983 edition, start at page 1 for the Maryland Militia and page 125 for Gooch's Regiment, following the chapter leads from the available excerpt [S85] [S86]. Keep a regiment match open for men recruited in Northern colonies too. Rhode Island recruiting correspondence is one route to check colony of enlistment independently of the compilation's title [S87].

**Death during the War of 1812.** For a regular enlisted soldier, inspect the Remarks field in the Register of Enlistments (M233). Read the full page for other death entries only when the question covers other soldiers as well, as the presenter suggests (only transcript excerpts read) [S48] [S49]. Treat a notation in the Register as a lead to the roll or other original record.

For a volunteer, follow the presenter's advice (only transcript excerpts read) to check the full compiled military service record for a death notation or related personal paper [S48] [S10]. Use a silent file as a reason to check the unit rolls.

For a death lead, follow the presenter's advice (only transcript excerpts read) to inspect the original unit muster roll's Remarks field when a published roster is silent [S48 @2026-11-20]. Use the National Archives' RG 94 finding aids to distinguish the volunteer War of 1812 rolls (NAID 654644) from regular-army rolls (NAID 654583) [S50 @2026-11-20]. For regulars, search by arm of service, regiment, company and date [S49 @2026-11-20].

For a regular unit, use the National Archives RG 98 guide to locate surviving company books by regiment [S13 @2026-11-20]. Check the descriptive entries and rosters of men separated from service for a death [S51 @2026-11-20]. Start with the company or regiment and date, following the presenter's advice (only transcript excerpts read) [S48 @2026-11-20].

**Hardy County militia officers.** Follow the presenter's lead (only transcript excerpts read) to search county court minutes and orders for recommendations [S54 @2026-11-20]. For the late 1790s through the War of 1812, start with the Library of Virginia's Hardy County Record Book 4 (1795–1798, reel 3) and use the 1795–1812 index (reel 11) and the later volumes to look for appointments or promotions. Check the overlapping volumes against the dates sought [S55 @2026-11-20].

## 2. Write each series' eligibility rule before reading a miss

Build a routing table with one row per series: custodian, coverage, eligibility rule, and whether a miss is meaningful. A miss counts only when the man met the eligibility rule **and** the series covers his period and place.

**Revolutionary War search misses.** Follow the search-status distinction from the available excerpt of the Strauss interview [S66 @2026-11-21]. Check the series description and finding aid for surviving coverage. Record holdings without searchable images as unsearched unless you actually examined them, and record losses only where a source identifies the affected records [S67 @2026-11-21].

Limit a negative federal search to the series examined. Follow the repository leads from the available excerpt of the interview [S66 @2026-11-21]. For state-only militia, check the relevant state archive or county records [S67 @2026-11-21]. Check the DAR Library's Patriot Records Project Index as a separate route and carry a hit's source locator to the underlying record [S70 @2026-11-21].

**Mexican War teamsters.** Keep enlisted duty and civilian employment open. For suspected volunteer service, check FamilySearch's M616 index and follow a hit to the CMSR [S75 @2026-11-21] [S90 @2026-11-21]. Treat a miss as leaving civilian employment possible, following the advice from the available excerpt [S83 @2026-11-21]. Test Regular Army enlistment separately with the Register (§1). Congress authorized teamster positions in some Regular Army units in 1847 [S84 @2026-11-21]. Look for a hiring, contract or pay record that identifies the man as a civilian before assigning that status.

- **Pennsylvania donation land.** Check service to the end of the war in the Pennsylvania Continental Line. The State Archives lists ten surveying districts in parts of Butler, Clarion, Crawford, Erie, Lawrence, Mercer, Venango and Warren counties and gives separate claimant, patent and register series [S31 @2026-11-19]. An archivist describes a Line soldier whose service ended at the 1781 mutiny and reports grants to some special ranger companies outside the Line [S3 @2026-11-19]. Check the specific company's eligibility. A missing grant cannot exclude earlier Line or militia service.
- **State bounty land.** Search federal and state grants separately. Bockstruck's published index covers Connecticut, Georgia, Maryland, Massachusetts, New York, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, South Carolina and Virginia [S30 @2026-11-19]. A researcher reports that Massachusetts veterans had to choose between state and federal grants [S4 @2026-11-19]. Check the applicable terms and both sets of records before interpreting a federal miss.
  - Search Lloyd Bockstruck's *Revolutionary War Bounty Land Grants Awarded by State Governments* first. The presenter says it is alphabetical regardless of state. Then go to that state's archive for the record itself [S4 @2026-11-19].
  - Virginia warrants could lie in present-day Kentucky and Ohio (the Virginia Military District). The presenter adds West Virginia [S4]. The Kentucky Secretary of State website has 4,748 Virginia Revolutionary War warrants digitized [S4]. Search where the land lay, not only where the soldier came from.

- **War of 1812 state rosters and society compilations.** Many states published rosters of their troops called to duty in the war [S11]. One presenter (only transcript excerpts read) points to state adjutant-general volumes and to state-level books from the Society of the War of 1812 and the Daughters of 1812 [S9]. Each covers only some service types and units. Write that coverage into the routing table, use a hit to find the original record, and count a miss only inside the stated coverage.

  - **Kentucky.** *Report of the Adjutant General of the State of Kentucky: Soldiers of the War of 1812* (Frankfort, 1891) [S15]. The Kentucky Genealogical Society says men who served in the U.S. regulars or in Virginia units do not appear in it, and that the original state-level records were destroyed after it was published [S16]. A Kentuckian missing from it may still be in the Register (§1) or in Virginia or federal records.

  - **New Jersey and Ohio.** *Records of Officers and Men of New Jersey in Wars, 1791–1815* (Adjutant-General's Office, Trenton, 1909) and *Roster of Ohio Soldiers in the War of 1812* (Adjutant General's Office, Columbus, 1916, catalogued under Ohio militia). These two volumes and the Kentucky report are scanned on the Internet Archive [S15 @2026-11-16], [S17 @2026-11-16], [S18 @2026-11-16].

  - **Virginia militia.** Search *Pay Rolls of Militia Entitled to Land Bounty under the Act of Congress of Sept. 28, 1850* and its companion *Muster Rolls of the Virginia Militia in the War of 1812*. The Library of Virginia treats them as the official record of Virginia militia service, and access to the fragile originals is restricted. It also holds militia muster rolls and payrolls left out of the printed volumes (series APA 253a), and says the federal government ultimately paid many of these men, whose service and benefit records are at the National Archives [S14 @2026-11-16]. One presenter (only transcript excerpts read) likewise sends men missing from the volumes to the National Archives [S9 @2026-11-16]. A miss in the printed volumes is not meaningful until APA 253a and the M602 index have also been searched.

  - **Virginia regulars.** The Library of Virginia lists Stuart Lee Butler's *Virginia Soldiers in the United States Army, 1800–1815* among its published indexes [S14]. One presenter (only transcript excerpts read) says Butler pulled the Virginia regulars of the war out of the Register of Enlistments [S9]. Use a hit to find the man's own Register entry (§1).

  - **Tennessee.** *Tennessee Soldiers in the War of 1812: Regiments of Col. Allcorn and Col. Allison* (Penelope Johnson Allen, with the National and Tennessee Societies of the U.S. Daughters of 1812, 1947) is limited by its title to those two regiments [S19]. A miss there says nothing about other Tennessee service.

**Mexican War horse claims.** For mounted service with a clue to the loss of a horse, search the Third Auditor's Claims Division in National Archives RG 217, §217.8.5, beginning with the abstracts of horse claims (1846–1865) [S38 @2026-11-20]. The presenter points to the horse-claim route (only a transcript excerpt read) [S40 @2026-11-20]. Capture the unit, appraisal, loss and replacement details where stated. Use the claim as a lead to the tour and check the service rolls [S41 @2026-11-20].

**Georgia frontier militia claims.** For service on the Georgia frontier in 1792–94, search the five rolls of M1745, *Claims for Georgia Militia Campaigns Against Indians on the Frontier, 1792–1827* [S44 @2026-11-20]. The National Archives lists the publication as fully digitized in its Catalog [S43 @2026-11-20]. Search the Catalog by publication number. Use the M1745 finding aid to locate the voucher, as the presenter suggests (only transcript excerpts read), and open the corresponding roll [S42 @2026-11-20].

**Colonial militia substitutes.** If a man is missing from a colonial expedition's service roll, check whether the local rules let a man chosen for duty furnish a substitute or pay for one. Follow the presenter's lead (only transcript excerpts read) and look for a substitution or fine entry that names him before treating the roll search as conclusive [S45] [S46].

**New Jersey War of 1812 deaths.** Check the muster-roll entries in *Records of Officers and Men of New Jersey in Wars, 1791–1815* for an explicit death remark, as the presenter suggests (only transcript excerpts read) [S48] [S60]. Carry the unit and date to the original rolls if the printed entry leaves details unresolved.

**Virginia militia company to county.** Use the commander index and county-by-county listings in Part III of Stuart Lee Butler's *A Guide to Virginia Militia Units in the War of 1812* to match a known company to a county. The presenter suggests the county route (only transcript excerpts read) [S48] [S52]. Carry the company and county into the state and federal searches. Use this as a unit guide and verify the soldier's service in a roll or service file.

**Maryland privateering in the War of 1812.** Check chapter II of William M. Marine's *The British Invasion of Maryland, 1812–1815* and its appendix for a person or vessel connected with privateering, as the presenter suggests (only transcript excerpts read) [S48] [S53]. Treat a hit as a lead to the commission records (§1).

**Delaware and Maryland militia deaths.** Use a published muster entry to identify the company and date. If the entry is silent about death, inspect the original unit roll's remarks, as the presenter suggests (only transcript excerpts read) [S48 @2026-11-20]. For Delaware, check the Delaware Public Archives' posted 1813 rolls [S62 @2026-11-20]. For Maryland, use the state archive's military records bibliography to find the relevant county volume or unit roll citation [S63 @2026-11-20].

## 3. Label what kind of evidence each record is

Attach exactly one evidence-kind label to every record: **service proof**, **payment**, **levy/fine**, **enumeration** or **claim lead**.

**Gooch's Regiment ration lists.** Follow the record-type lead from the available excerpt of the Gooch's Regiment talk [S85]. If a ship's list records soldiers “borne for victuals only”, label the entry as enumeration of people provisioned on that ship. Carry the ship and date to the relevant service records for any claim about enlistment, duty or home colony [S88].

- **Militia fines and levies in tax lists are levy/fine, never service proof.** Pennsylvania militia fines were charged to men who were not serving. The archivist adds two details. Older men who could not serve were charged too. During the Revolution, some levies fell on a whole township, or on a group within it, depending on whether a volunteer could be found [S3 @2026-11-19]. Look for muster, class or account records instead.
- **A county-history roster can be an enumeration.** In a Georgia county history used to fill an early census gap, the militia roster's notes said that some compilers included women and enslaved people while others omitted them [S5 @2026-11-19]. Transcribe the heading and the counting rule for the particular district. A name establishes inclusion on that list; seek a separate record of service.
- **Pennsylvania militia-loan certificates.** Follow the certificate number on an abstract card marked “militia loan accounts” to the underlying account. An archivist reports that the card can give little more than a name, amount and county, while the account may identify the dates and place of a tour, often frontier or guard duty [S3 @2026-11-19]. The State Archives lists the Revolutionary War Military Abstract Card File as series 13.50, digitized rolls 4444–4497 [S32 @2026-11-19]. Record what the account actually states.
- **State pay vouchers.** The North Carolina State Archives lists Revolutionary War Pay Vouchers as SR.204.29 [S33 @2026-11-19]. Copy the claimant, amount and date and place of the claim where given [S6 @2026-11-19]. Label the payment and inspect the stated reason before identifying a tour of military service.
- **State bounty-land warrants.** Transcribe the service designation and term from the warrant. The 1783 Virginia warrant shown for Hezekiah Lindsay specifies three years in the Continental Line [S4 @2026-11-19]. Enter that term and branch, and check a separate record for any militia service.

**Pennsylvania muster fines.** Transcribe the date and offense. A fine explicitly levied for missing an inactive-duty muster records absence at that muster [S25], [S26]. Keep active duty and combat open. Compare dated fines for the man and his known relatives to spot repeated absences. Leave attendance unresolved on dates with no fine entry [S27]. If you suspect Quaker membership, check the relevant monthly meeting minutes [S28]. Record a reason for an absence only when the records support it.

- **Ohio WPA veterans’ grave-registration cards: claim lead.** Treat an Ohio WPA veterans' grave-registration card's service attribution as a compiled lead, then check the cited unit in service records, rosters and any pension file. In Union County the project crosschecked cemetery names against war records and local rosters before creating cards. When two same-named men receive the same unit or dates, compare each man's residence, life dates and the unit's recruiting context rather than transferring the service to the man whose grave is easiest to find. Keep the burial identification and service identification separate. [S20](https://genealogypants.com/2024/08/14/dont-stop-digging-graves-registration-cards-ohio/), [S21](https://genealogypants.com/2025/07/09/did-my-george-long-serve-in-the-war-of-1812/), [S22](https://www.unioncountyohio.gov/Veterans-Burial-Records)

**Georgia militia vouchers (M1745).** Label the voucher as payment and record the serviceman, company, service interval and amount where stated [S44]. Use the service interval to place the man when the voucher identifies where he served. Following the presenter's examples (only transcript excerpts read), record the later voucher date separately and keep any widow, executor or assignee in the role the voucher gives [S42].

**Colonial militia fines.** If drill rolls are missing, follow the presenter's suggestion (only transcript excerpts read) to check local militia fines for failure to appear [S45]. For colonial Virginia, include county court-martial records in that search. Use the offense as written, since fines also covered other conduct [S59]. For a Quaker, compare the fine entry with the monthly meeting minutes for an explicit refusal to pay or a record of collection [S47].

**Virginia militia nominations.** Label the court's recommendation as a claim lead. Establish the appointment and its date from a record of the governor's action [S57 @2026-11-20]. Follow the presenter's suggestion (only transcript excerpts read) to check the governor's records at the Library of Virginia [S54 @2026-11-20]. Search the Militia Commission Papers, 1777–1858 (misc. reels 6443–6616), and use the Journals of the Council of the State of Virginia (misc. reels 2981–2998) as a rough index into the governor's correspondence [S56 @2026-11-20].

In later Hardy County orders, search for a successor named “in room of” the officer, as the presenter suggests (only transcript excerpts read) [S54]. Record “removed” or “promoted” as the stated vacancy reason and leave the circumstances or new rank open until another record supplies them. Use the later orders and commission records to establish the rank and date at each step from ensign to captain [S55] [S56].

## 4. Read unit designations by roster status

- Retain the class, company and battalion exactly as each roll identifies them, along with the duty status and county. In the Lancaster County example, the same man's active entry was 2nd battalion, 7th company, 8th class, while the inactive entry was 7th battalion, 1st company, 2nd class [S4]. Check the local system before deciding that a change of all three numbers means a different person or a transfer.

Record the roster status next to every designation. Two different number sets do not by themselves mean two men or two units. Do not apply the Pennsylvania renumbering to another colony without checking that colony's own rule.

**Pennsylvania Revolutionary militia.** Use the Pennsylvania State Archives' Revolutionary War Military Abstract Card File, series 13.50, to locate the underlying county and battalion record [S65 @2026-11-21]. Check that record for rank, class, captain's company, battalion and any stated dates [S64 @2026-11-21]. Carry the recorded duty status with the date so that a roster date alone cannot establish a tour.

## 5. Route a suspected Loyalist separately

Search period newspapers for notices of intent to leave the country and for public sales of confiscated property under the person's name [S4]. Then search Ancestry's "UK, American Loyalist Claims, 1776–1835" and, for New York, "New York, Sales of Loyalist Land, 1762–1830". The presenter also names an American Loyalist migration database, and says the claims are the largest of his three collections [S4]. Many claims were rejected or only partly paid, but even an unpaid claim can preserve sworn affidavits about residence and property lost [S4]. Treat a newspaper notice as a claim lead, not as an identification. German auxiliaries are a separate population and are not routed here.

## 6. Separate same-named men without choosing a name-only hit

The failure this section blocks is choosing one soldier from a name-only or name-and-state index result. Build a candidate-separation table with these columns: candidate, unit, where the unit was recruited, militia district, roster status, and voucher amount / date / place claimed. Fill it in as follows:

- List every same-named candidate with birth date, residence at the time of service and recruiting area. In a search for Civil War soldier John Morgan, the researcher used a known birth date and Indiana residence to narrow dozens of candidates to units raised nearby [S8]. Use that list to prioritize searches and retain other units until independent evidence excludes them [S7].
- Use a county militia-district map to place same-named men in different districts [S5].
- Where vouchers survive, compare amount, date and place claimed, man by man [S6].
- Before splitting rows, check roster status (§4). Active and inactive designations that differ can belong to one man.

Each row is a narrowing clue, not an identification. Hand the merge or separate ruling to evidence/proof judgment.

## 7. Return the routing packet

Return:

- the service-type classification and its basis;
- the routing table (custodian, coverage, eligibility rule, whether a miss is meaningful, what was actually searched);
- an evidence-kind label for each record;
- the candidate-separation table;
- handoffs to pension recovery and proof judgment.

Mark every series you did not search as unsearched. Report "no service found" only for the series whose eligibility and coverage fit the man.

For a draft Revolutionary War reference request, record the exact National Archives record group and series or subseries title. Add the entry or National Archives identifier when listed. Use the series description to name the target in the draft, following the advice from the available excerpt of the Strauss interview [S66] [S67].
