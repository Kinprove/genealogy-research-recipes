# Find household-level series in volost, village-society and town-administration fonds

Use this recipe when the metrical books or revision lists for a Russian-Empire peasant or townsperson are missing or stop, especially after the tenth and last revision (1857–1860; its lists are usually dated 1858). The common mistake is to send the researcher only to church registers and revision lists. That misses the local administrative fonds. For peasants these are the volost board (волостное правление) and the village society (сельское общество). For townspeople they are the town administration (городская управа). These fonds hold family lists, call-up lists, inhabitant lists and property lists at household level. Call-up lists under universal military service start in 1874, the year of the military-service statute. Earlier recruit-levy files and lists of lower ranks also survive in volost fonds (GASO ф. 723 оп. 1 д. 17, a recruit levy of 1870–1871, and the second д. 16, lists of lower ranks on the volost register, 1871–1872), so do not stop the search at 1874. [S2] The opposite mistake is to treat an inventory title as verified contents, or to accept a mis-catalogued title. This recipe builds a register of candidate series. Every row stays an inventory lead until the file has been inspected.

Before you start, establish the person's estate and registration society (приписка), and learn to read family and recruit lists, using [Russian-Empire identity attribution](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/russian-empire-jewish-identity-attribution.md). Mark each case as a catalogue lead, inspected or unavailable, and keep titles separate from contents, using [Jewish archival list triage](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/jewish-archival-list-triage.md). Check case boundaries, skipped inventory sheets and image coverage with [archival image coverage verification](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/archival-image-coverage-verification.md). A printed guide or finding aid does not prove that a file is held today ([ELIB reference books](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/elib-ukrainian-archive-reference-books.md)). For reading the locator behind an index hit, use [JewishGen family reconstruction](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/jewishgen-search-family-reconstruction.md). To date a family list from its entries rather than its label, and for any identity ruling, use [evidence/proof judgment](https://github.com/Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes/blob/8b235c47acf62b78cd5a1ccf85f4eb974b2f966a/skills/genealogy-research-recipes/recipes/evidence-proof-judgment.md).

## 1. Pick the fond family by estate, then find every holder

- **Peasants:** search the fond of the volost board for the volost and period, and any separate village-society or village-assembly (сельский сход) fond. In the Sverdlovsk regional archive (GASO), village-society fonds stand on their own. Examples are ф. 721 (the Dolmatovo village assembly under the Lyapunovo volost board, 1914–1919) and ф. 722 (Znamenskoe society, Irbit uezd, 1892–1919). These are mostly assembly-sentence books. ф. 721 also has 1917–1918 minute books of the society's general meetings, and ф. 722 also has 1918–1919 orders and correspondence. [S2]
- **Townspeople (мещане, merchants):** search the town-administration fond. For Yekaterinburg this is GASO ф. 62. Also search the older town fond that holds the town's revision lists and inhabitant lists, which for Yekaterinburg is ф. 8, оп. 1. [S4], [S7]
- **Do not stop at the obvious fond.** In GASO, revision lists for a volost also sit in volost-board fonds and in the county treasury. Two volost-board fonds hold nothing but a single revision list: ф. 205 has 1834 and ф. 209 has 1816. The Irbit county treasury (ф. 674) holds revision lists by volost for 1816, 1834 and 1850. It also holds an inventory list of Irbit town real property for 1911–1915 (дд. 127–128) and a 1915 list of unpaid real-property taxes (д. 132). [S1] A participant in the Shadrinsk archive thread pointed to the Kargapolye volost-board fond (ф. 19) as holding revision lists and possibly other population lists. The participant had not seen the fond or its inventory. [S6]
- **Military-service and election bodies at county or province level** hold parallel person-level files. The Irbit county military-service board (GASO ф. 724) holds pension petitions from named lower ranks of named volosts, 1913–1920, rather than lists. [S2] The Slutsk county military-service board (NIAB ф. 627, оп. 1, д. 11) holds lists of Jews liable for service in 1891. The Vitebsk provincial commission for State Duma elections (NIAB ф. 2558) holds voter-list files. [S10], [S11]
- Look for transcribed inventories before you write to the archive. Forum participants have typed up GASO inventories in the threads. A participant reported that the "Великие описи" project had entered about 11,000 GASO file titles (inv.velikie.org/archive/gaso-ural/), partly duplicating the forum work. [S2]

For towns, add the classes the participants showed:

- **Family lists for the 1874 call-up and later call-up lists:** GASO ф. 62 оп. 1 д. 27а, a family list of Yekaterinburg мещане for military service, 1874, and д. 28, call-up lists 1878–79. [S4]
- **Registration into the town society:** ф. 8 оп. 1 д. 1935а, admission of various persons to the мещане society, 1867. [S4]
- **Inhabitant lists (обывательские списки):** ф. 8 оп. 1 д. 493 (1816–1818) and д. 970 (1844). Their columns cover old resident or newcomer and age, marital status and spouse, children with ages, any house or land and whether it was built, inherited, bought or received as dowry, its town part and number, presence or absence, trade, and civic offices held. [S7] The examples shown are pre-1858. No post-reform inhabitant list was shown, so search the town fond for one but do not assume the series continues.
- **Building and plot ledgers:** ф. 59 оп. 7 д. 353 (1809) lists householders by town part, street and plot number, with the area of each plot. [S7] Another participant then found their own мещане ancestors in the same ledger. [S7]
- **House-construction files:** ф. 62 files 746–813, on houses built or altered in 1875–1911 (step 5). [S5]
- **Voter lists:** see step 4.
- **Town call-up lists outside the Urals:** NIAB ф. 611 (Pinsk town administration), оп. 5, д. 194, a call-up list of Jewish мещане. The participant introduced it as a 1911 example, but the title quoted says 1914. Record the year in the title and note the conflict. [S11]

For each row record: archive, fond, opis, delo (exactly as printed, including letter suffixes such as 27а, 004б), title as printed, start and end dates, sheet count or "not given", and every settlement or society named. Add a status: **inventory-only**, **title questioned** or **inspected**.

## 3. Read the inventory as a list with defects

- **Split combined coverage.** One family list can cover two societies (ф. 680 дд. 29–31). One land-possession record can cover a village with its hamlets (выселки) or farmsteads (заимки), and sometimes only part of a hamlet ("часть выселка", ф. 207 дд. 7–8). [S1] Enter every named place in the row so that a search by hamlet still finds it.
- **Keep repeated files separate.** ф. 212 has two land-possession files for the same group of villages, dated 1895 (д. 5) and 1897 (д. 6). Record both. Do not treat one as a duplicate. [S1]
- **Mark gaps in an annual series.** The ф. 732 call-up lists run yearly but have no file for 1894, 1897 or 1902. [S2] That is a coverage gap in the inventory, not proof that no list was made or that the person was absent.
- **Log defects as they appear.** The defects seen are:
  - an inventory scanned with some sheets skipped (the note at the head of the ф. 725 inventory, an ironworks fond) [S2];
  - repeated delo numbers (ф. 723 has two items numbered 16, ф. 724 two numbered 6 and two numbered 26) [S2];
  - skipped numbers (ф. 683 goes from 031 to 033) [S1];
  - impossible dates ("879" and "00.01.9080" in ф. 683; end dates before start dates, such as ф. 730 д. 1) [S1], [S2];
  - dates given as 00.00.0000 (ф. 730 д. 22, an undated family list) [S2];
  - the "inactive inventory" (недействующая опись) item at the end of most fonds [S1], [S2].

  Copy these as written. Before you order a file with a repeated number, ask the archive which file it means.
- **Inventory dates can be wrong.** A participant in the Shadrinsk archive thread reported files whose actual dates differed from the inventory dates, and leaves bound in from other documents. [S6] Take the date range from the inspected file. Date a family list from its entries, not its label (evidence/proof judgment).
- **A missing sheet count is a question to the archive.** An NIAB inventory gave no sheet counts. A participant advised asking the archive, or someone who works there regularly, for the size and contents. The researcher who then obtained the file found lists of heads of families, a rough village map and a building plan. The file was a case on permitting worship in a village prayer school, ф. 299 оп. 2 т. 5 д. 16988. [S11] Generic titles can hide household lists. Rank them lower, but do not drop them.

## 4. Treat voter lists and anachronistic titles with care

- **Record each list's type separately.** For one uezd in 1906, a participant pointed out that the list of landowners and real-property owners is separate from the list of town voters, and said to check the town list next. [S8] Note which list you actually searched.
- **Content and limits.** Search State Duma voter lists from 1905. The election law dates from August 1905 and the first election from 1906, and the Shadrinsk town administration holds Duma voter-list files dated 1905–1907 and 1905–1909. [S3] They cover only men aged 25 and over, as set by the 1905 election law and kept in 1907. Participants said these lists give full name, religion and residence, and sometimes age. [S9] A surname hit with other given names is only a lead. [S8]
- **Copy every printed field as written.** Shadrinsk town-administration voter lists give rate-sheet (раскладочная ведомость) numbers and a qualification value ("1", "1,3", "4"). The value changed between years for the same man. [S3] Someone in the thread asked what these fields mean, and nobody answered. Record them literally and do not interpret them.
- **Question an impossible title.** The Shadrinsk archive described ф. И-473 оп. 1 д. 633 as State Duma voter lists for 1883–1884. [S3] The State Duma was created in 1905 and first elected in 1906, so this title is wrong as it stands. Record it as **title questioned** and leave the election and contents open until the file is inspected. The same reply also called the 1917 list a Duma list. The researcher found that its full title (д. 2064) is a list of voters for the Constituent Assembly, compiled 2 November 1917 for the elections that began that month. [S3] Read the full title on the file itself.
- **Complaints about exclusion** are a separate series. NIAB ф. 2558 оп. 1 holds such complaints, for example one from 1907 about exclusion from the Polotsk voter list. A complaint like this places a named man in a town at a dated moment. [S10]
- Participants also named other town series to check when metrical books are lost. For Vitebsk these were family lists of мещане and merchants (1882), lists of Jews (1874), voter lists (1912), and the list of persons entitled to take part in the second congress of town voters for the Fourth Duma (1912). They did not give the fond. [S8] Find the fond before you send a request.

## 5. Use published abstracts only to reach the file

A printed reference book describes Yekaterinburg town-administration files 746–813 (GASO ф. 62) on houses built or altered in 1875–1911, with surname and address indexes. [S5] Use the index only to get the delo number, then inspect the original file. Cite the file, not the abstract, for any household fact. Mark it inventory-only until you have seen the original.

## 6. Record inspected entries as household observations

- Copy house, plot, quarter, town part and rate-sheet numbers exactly, including later corrections. In one transcript of the 1809 ledger, plot numbers appear with a bracketed alternative reading ("№1773 [1573]"). Keep both readings. [S7]
- Do not link entries across years by house or plot number alone. Numbering can change between ledgers. The 1816–1818 list gives a bare house number, while the 1844 list gives town part, quarter and number. [S7] A link needs an inspected basis, such as the same owner, the same street and a stated purchase or inheritance.
- Record every person in the household, with the relationships, ages, estate, presence or absence and occupation as written. Cross-reference each household to the matching revision-list and confession-list entries with their own locators. An age or date that does not match ranks the candidate lower. It never excludes them.

## Output: administrative series register

Return one row for each file with these fields:

- archive;
- fond/opis/delo;
- printed title;
- date range;
- sheet count;
- settlements or societies named;
- series class;
- status (inventory-only, title questioned, inspected).

List these separately:

- inventory defects;
- gaps in annual series;
- files you need to ask the archive about (repeated numbers, missing sheet counts).

For inspected rows, attach the household observation. Report "not found" only for files you actually inspected. Survival varies from volost to volost and town to town. A fond with no family list is a coverage gap, not evidence that the family was absent.
