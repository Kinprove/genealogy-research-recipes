# Recover a livestock mark or brand registration

Use when a supported person/locality/period leads to a marks-and-brands index or register.
Carry the index entry to the actual registration and, when necessary, a described surrogate.
This is a non-land registration search; use [land-record recovery](land-index-book-series-recovery.md)
for grants and conveyances and [evidence/proof judgment](evidence-proof-judgment.md) for identity.

## Follow the actual index key

Read the index headings and arrangement before searching by the owner's surname. A design
initial can be the filing key: the teaching example finds an owner under the letter beginning
the brand, rather than the owner's surname. Establish this from the supplied index; do not
generalize that arrangement to every brand book. [S1]

Retain the index entry number, literal owner name, visible brand/earmark representation and
referenced register volume/page separately. An index entry number is not a register page.
Preserve an uncertain reading before choosing the book it points to.

## Match the register or its described copy

Follow the locator to the matching register and verify its title, volume and printed page.
If the original cannot be retrieved, keep that status and inspect an authorized surrogate's
description. Compare the box/reel contents and actual volume/page coverage: a reel can include
overlapping volumes or repeated volume labels. A plausible reel title alone does not resolve
the entry. Keep original-register, film/reel and viewer-image locators linked and distinct. [S1]

From the inspected entry, preserve the registration date, stated community, animal and body-side
wording, drawing and any visible corrections or illegible parts actually present. Retain the
literal reading beside a proposed correction. A brand drawing, registered name and census
post office do not by themselves identify a person or establish ownership or kinship.

## Close with a registration packet

Return the actual index key and inspected extent, linked index/register/surrogate locators,
entry image or permitted transcription, and the remaining identity or copy gap. If the
register remains inaccessible, the located index is still a lead. Name the next described
copy or source extent; do not replace the missing entry with a reconstructed drawing.
