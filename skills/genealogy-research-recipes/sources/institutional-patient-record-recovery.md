## Sources

- **S1:** Genealogy Gems, [Episode 120 - Annie's Ghost with Author Steve Luxenberg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kX8ba2xZhho), published 2016-12-23. Used: the author's argument that court records are kept and open when medical records are not; the Wayne County probate court as the mental-health court in his case; a likely legal proceeding even for a voluntary 1940 public-hospital admission; the post-deceased heir issue.
- **S2:** Genealogy Gems, [Genealogy Gems Podcast Episode 219](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-o8KMcUYHeY), published 2018-07-11. Used: a guest's account of finding Norwich State Hospital's custodian (the Connecticut State Archives), proving descent with linked vital records, the archive revealing three more relative-patients, visits and letters that contradicted family lore, and a New York state hospital's refusal by form letter.
- **S3:** Denyse Allen, [Podcast: Pennsylvania State Archives](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4lzuEyK3Xyc), published 2020-09-26. Used: a Pennsylvania State Archives archivist's description of the 50-year post-death and 75-year record-age thresholds, mixed-date registers, mail-in copy or abstract, and the host's highlights-versus-full-copy choice and record group/file/roll locators.
- **S4:** Denyse Allen, [Pennsylvania State Archives](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RKo2d528iw8), published 2024-07-25. Used: a later version of the same discussion, repeating the thresholds, the register restriction, the copy-or-abstract products and the full-copy request.
- **S5:** Genealogy Gems, [Genealogy Gems Podcast Episode 231 with Lisa Louise Cooke](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vaSTQKvU2z8), published 2019-07-17. Used: counting back from an institutional death certificate's residence length, testing it against the Exeter school's opening and almshouse-transfer history, finding annual reports through the Library of Congress catalogue and WorldCat, and newspapers as a counterweight to self-serving reports.
- **S6:** Family History Fanatics with Amy Johnson Crow, [Get MORE CLUES From Genealogy Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D5weC7CmDlo), published 2022-05-14. Used: state-to-state variation (Illinois judge's order for an 1899 death; Ohio depending on time period), the Ohio History Connection as holder of Columbus State Hospital records, committing-county probate or civil court records, newspaper court recaps, and annual reports as context that rarely names patients.

- **S7:** Yvette Hoitink / Dutch Genealogy, [Quick tip – Was your relative in an asylum?](https://www.dutchgenealogy.nl/was-your-relative-in-an-asylum/), 2022-06-01 (saved publication metadata); saved article reviewed 2026-10-27. Identifies a child's marriage supplements and municipal correspondence about forced admission as possible Dutch discovery sources. Only the saved article was inspected, not an actual supplement, notice, local inventory or patient file. Conditional locator/series checks and separation of incapacity, diagnosis and admission are authored synthesis. No universal consent or notification law, standard document contents, individual admission, survival, current custody or access is established.
- **S8:** Yvette Hoitink / Dutch Genealogy, [Unusual death place case study: Thomas Cammenga](https://www.dutchgenealogy.nl/unusual-death-place-case-study-thomas-cammenga/), 2023-02-24 (saved publication metadata); saved article reviewed 2026-10-27. Describes a dated Leeuwarden resolution abstract concerning payment for institutional placement and referring to a court order and requested authorization. Used for the conditional municipal decision-register target and its supplied references. Only the saved article was inspected, not the original resolution, court or patient file, or a current local inventory. No general payment/notification duty, diagnosis, individual admission, record survival, custody or access is established. This case article is separate from S7's general tip, not independent corroboration of it.

- **S9:** New York State Office of Mental Health, [Requesting Medical Records](https://omh.ny.gov/omhweb/consumer_affairs/medicalrecords/), checked 2026-11-07. Used for: no copies for genealogy searches, the stated routes for relatives of deceased patients, and the page's citation of Mental Hygiene Law §33.13 for confidentiality. This is the agency's policy, not an independent legal ruling on an individual request.

- **S10:** Julianne Mangin, [Researching Your Mentally Ill Ancestor](https://juliannemangin.com/2018/09/13/researching-your-mentally-ill-ancestor/), published 2018-09-13, checked 2026-11-07. Used for: her own retrieval experience and citation of Norwich State Hospital Records, 1904–1996, Connecticut State Library RG021_008. The linked official catalogue at cslarchives.ctstatelibrary.org/repositories/2/resources/585 refused automated access; current catalogue content and eligibility were not verified. Her article and podcast account S2 are not independent corroboration.

- **S11:** Ohio History Connection, [Mental Health Records: An Introduction for Researchers](https://www.ohiohistory.org/mental-health-records-an-introduction-for-researchers/), checked 2026-11-07. Used for: transfer of state-hospital collections to the State Archives, restrictions on collections identifying patients under Ohio Revised Code 5122.31, and checking each collection's catalogue description. This dated guide does not establish a particular Columbus file's survival, present series coverage or the requester's eligibility.

- **S12:** Pennsylvania State Archives, [Request Research – State Hospital Records](https://www.pa.gov/services/phmc/request-research---state-hospital-records--), checked 2026-11-07. Used for: requests processed when the patient has been deceased at least 50 years or the record is at least 75 years old, and variation in records between hospitals. The opened page did not specify which proof-of-death document to send; the recipe tells the requester to ask rather than attributing an unverified document list to this page.

The five-step route, the product labels, the search register and the packet are authored
synthesis. The sources supply the presenters' experiences and the Pennsylvania archive's
described policy; published repository and agency statements add context.
