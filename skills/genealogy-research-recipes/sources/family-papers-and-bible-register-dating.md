**Authored synthesis.** The following is not taken from any one presenter:

- the split into letters, address records and family books
- the separate address and text fields
- the writing-occasion categories
- reading [S7]'s view of many hands as consistency rather than proof
- reading [S11]'s 1909 inscription and shared cover as limits
- the check of whether the register leaves belong to the book, and limiting the before-printing and owner-death tests to leaves that do; the cited pages show register pages removed from Bibles and records copied into new ones, not leaves moved between Bibles
- using the writing medium's first availability as an earliest writing date
- the Return layout

The presenters supply the examples and the failure modes.

**Not verified:**

- the presenters' examples: the letter and its later page [S3], the address-book accounts [S4]–[S6], the Bible examples and the document naming an earlier Bible [S7], [S8], the transcription chain and branch of descent [S10], and the edition, inscription and rebinding conclusions [S11]
- whether the online items described are still available in that form
- any chemical or forensic method for dating ink, layers or hands; the recipe uses only the dates when writing media became available ([S18]–[S21]), and none of the sources supplies a test
- how often a family Bible was reprinted from earlier plates; [S13] documents examples, not a rate
- the current coverage, access and fees of the collections in [S22]–[S28]; the DAR and Ancestry pages themselves could not be opened
