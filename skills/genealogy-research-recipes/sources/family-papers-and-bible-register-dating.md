## Sources

- **S1:** Lisa Lisson, [Tuesday's Genealogy Tip - Christmas Cards](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xGSFosu1SYk), published 2015-11-19. Used: saved Christmas cards, some still in their original envelopes, read for postmark, return address and the address the card was mailed to, separately from the date and family news inside.
- **S2:** BYU Library Family History Center / James Tanner, [Researching Mexico by James Tanner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9v7JgWO4ePE), published 2015-08-24. Used: letters from relatives may mention where a person was from, and their return address may locate the family.
- **S3:** Lisa Louise Cooke's Genealogy Gems, [Genealogy Gems Podcast Episode 238 - The Family History Show](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xzJUics2RDs), published 2020-02-13. Used: a guest's account of an incomplete, unsigned 1910 letter. Its author was first inferred from the addressee's later marriage, until a separated final page found in another file carried a different signature.
- **S4:** Lisa Lisson, [Looking for Your Ancestors in Holiday Records!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Fh0NVYdLyI), published 2021-12-17. Used: address books and Christmas-card lists with old addresses marked out and new ones written beside them, maiden-name entries marked out with married names added, and entries mixing friends and family.
- **S5:** Lisa Lisson, [Finding Ancestors in Holiday Records!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ynu0u5mEVjU), published 2020-12-18. Used: a keeper marking out both people who moved and a person who died, and listed people as part of the ancestor's circle of associates.
- **S6:** Lisa Louise Cooke's Genealogy Gems, [Episode 173 - Inspiring Ideas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3neruIkmbks), published 2016-12-23. Used: address books with years of scratched-out and added names, scribbled updates and arrows, and a speaker's updated entry for a remarried relative kept under the surname she had always used.
- **S7:** Lisa Lisson, [HOW TO ANALYZE THE FAMILY BIBLE | Finding ALL of the Genealogy Clues in the Family Bible](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mdD2f-mzNYY), published 2020-06-13. Used:
  - the title-page printing date tested against entry dates
  - entries before the printing read as probably copied from another Bible
  - one hand versus many hands, with the presenter's view that many hands make near-event recording likelier
  - a transcription signed as accurate that still carried a wrong date
- **S8:** Lisa Lisson, [3 Strategies For Analyzing the Family Bible](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uz8PUlihsTk), published 2018-05-12. Used: an 1853 flyleaf date tested against a couple married in 1854; earlier births on one page in one hand read as copied; a later document naming an earlier family Bible; and the presenter's illustration of a claimed owner whose marriage predates the printing, which the recipe narrows: only a death before the printing rules the owner out.
- **S9:** BYU Library Family History Center / Jerroleen Sorensen, [Solving Puzzles by Correlating Evidence - Jerroleen Sorensen (12 Jun 2022)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MTvFATQbnqE), published 2022-06-15. Used: Bible entries that look written at one time, by one person and with the same ink, treated as derivative, copied or remembered.
- **S10:** GeneaVlogger, [Mr. Beat's Ancestors in the American Revolution](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yCAv9HvyGuQ), published 2021-10-13. Used: an online-tree item carrying a Bible transcription with its transcriber, the person who sent it and the poster; the Bible's descent through a different branch of the family.
- **S11:** Diana Elder, [Effie Kelsey and BitterSweet: A Poem – Researching Material Culture](https://familylocket.com/effie-kelsey-and-bittersweet-a-poem-researching-material-culture/), published 2025-02-02. Used:
  - an inherited book with an undated title page
  - the work's 1858 first publication versus later editions
  - a 1909 inscription in another copy
  - a shared commercial cover design
  - the author's conclusions about the rebinding and the acquisition date

**Authored synthesis.** The following is not taken from any one presenter:

- the split into letters, address records and family books
- the separate address and text fields
- the writing-occasion categories
- reading [S7]'s view of many hands as consistency rather than proof
- reading [S11]'s 1909 inscription and shared cover as limits
- the Return layout

The presenters supply the examples and the failure modes.

**Not verified:**

- the presenters' examples: the letter and its later page [S3], the address-book accounts [S4]–[S6], the Bible examples and the document naming an earlier Bible [S7], [S8], the transcription chain and branch of descent [S10], and the edition, inscription and rebinding conclusions [S11]
- whether the online items described are still available in that form
- any method for dating ink, layers or hands; none of the sources supplies one
