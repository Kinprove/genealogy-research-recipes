## Sources

- **S1:** BYU Family History Library / Joyce Whiting, [Finding Birth, Marriage, and Death Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1LPbDiF6fn8), 2014-03-07. Used for: Utah state registration from 1905; originals at county seats and the state archives; midwives' and physicians' registers under state > county > Vital Records, which may be the only record. The 75-year restriction it states is corrected in the text.
- **S2:** Lisa Lisson, [Let's Talk About Delayed Birth Certificates – Facebook Replay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dTfghdZJlRg), 2020-01-27. Used for: supporting evidence on a delayed certificate (Bible, mother's affidavit, daughter's certificate; school records from a viewer); inferences from the affidavit date and signature; reports that some counties kept only the certificate; the caution not to assume either way for people born just after the mandate.
- **S3:** BYU Family History Library / Maureen Brady, [Chicago Area Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=89uJQ6yxqVA), 2026-08-06. Used for: Cook County birth registers versus certificates as separate series; certificates from 1871, Illinois state-level registration from 1 January 1916; many people without certificates who filed delayed births; delayed births digitized but reportedly restricted.
- **S4:** BYU Family History Library / Laura Lefler, [Southern Research Part II: Proving Relationships with Vital Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PXKxUZZFbnw), 2023-01-23. Used for: county and city records from before the mandate; Raleigh and Wilmington; multiple custodians and the Raleigh phone call; independent cities (Virginia's 38); Virginia births 1853–1896; different online collections on each site.
- **S5:** Lisa Lisson, [5 Reasons You Are NOT Finding Your Ancestor's Birth Record](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_zJ_8UfN4k), 2020-05-06. Used for: searching where the mother's female relatives lived, including across county and state lines; checking collateral lines for a family Bible.
- **S6:** Denyse Allen, [All About 19th Century Vital Records in Pennsylvania](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cRoQrIGYQJ0), date not shown. Used as a near-identical presentation of the S7 segment on Pennsylvania city bureaus and Philadelphia's consolidation.
- **S7:** Denyse Allen, [All About 19th Century Vital Records in Pennsylvania](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g2hjftnjLgQ), 2022-07-14. Used for: start years for city bureaus of health; residents just outside the city; city hall or courthouse custody; society indexes; browsing unindexed county film; the 1854 consolidation.
- **S8:** Denyse Allen, [All About Early Philadelphia Vital Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w2PgteWzpiQ), date not shown. Used for: an interview with the Philadelphia City Archives on its original holdings (holding years corrected in the text), almshouse and Philadelphia General Hospital births, weekly group registers and burial-return death ledgers, and the uncertainty over how far online copies match the originals.
- **S9:** Lisa Lisson, [Delayed Birth Certificates – A Genealogist's Treasure Chest!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W_3wAbRJV6Q), 2019-08-21. Used for: asking the county to search for a delayed application; evidence items as leads; parent-living and married-name clues.
- **S10:** Denyse Allen, [City of Philadelphia Archives](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_RnewkohVvM), 2024-07-25. Used for: holding years restated (corrected in the text); the index → register → cemetery → original sequence for Philadelphia deaths filed by cemetery.

The authority-and-series register, the result vocabulary and the rule against stating absence are authored synthesis. The presenters supply examples of early city registration, separate series, custodians and delayed-certificate contents. They do not supply a general schedule of U.S. start dates.

Not verified:

- Reading's start year(s) and Scranton's start year (the only Scranton date found is deaths from 1878)
- the presenter's statement that Illinois did not require certificates before 1916, and any earlier Illinois county-level reporting requirement
- the Cook County restriction on delayed births and their online status
- a distinct births series within the Philadelphia almshouse and Philadelphia General Hospital records
- the Utah midwives' and physicians' catalog heading
- the claim that the four online providers carry different collections under repository contracts
- current access rules and online availability at providers not named in this recipe
