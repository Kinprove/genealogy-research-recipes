## Sources

The prose, audit matrices, local labels and handoff are original synthesis.
Sources are cited for attribution and methodological support; their text, model
citations and certification rubrics are not reproduced. Checked 2026-10-07.

- Board for Certification of Genealogists, [Ethics and Standards](https://bcgcertification.org/ethics-standards)
  — GPS, documentation and separation of source information from commentary.
  Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- BCG, [Reporting Research in Progress](https://www.bcgcertification.org/skillbuilding-reporting-research-in-progress)
  — bounded objectives, reproducible findings and reporting limitations.
  Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- BCG, [Reasonably Exhaustive: How Do We Know We Are There?](https://www.bcgcertification.org/ten-minute-methodology-reasonably-exhaustive-how-do-we-know-were-there)
  — question-specific assessment of research breadth and remaining evidence.
  Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- BCG, [Proof Statements 1](https://www.bcgcertification.org/ten-minute-methodology-proof-statements-1)
  and [Proof Summaries and Arguments 1](https://www.bcgcertification.org/ten-minute-methodology-proof-summaries-and-arguments-1)
  — context-dependent proof statements and the continuum of writing forms.
  Rechecked 2026-11-06 for the quoted standard: proof statements are used "when at
  least two citations demonstrate that a conclusion's accuracy requires no
  explanation", and conflicting evidence needs more than a proof statement.
  Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- Thomas W. Jones, [The Genealogical Proof Standard: How Simple Can It Be?](https://bcgcertification.org/skillbuilding-the-genealogical-proof-standard-how-simple-can-it-be/)
  (BCG OnBoard, 2010; opened 2026-11-06) — a simple proof whose citations show the
  sources' independent origins and in which no source disagrees with another's answer.
  Counting citations by information stream is this recipe's reading of these sources,
  not a quoted rule. Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- Elizabeth Shown Mills, [QuickLesson 19: Layered Citations](https://www.evidenceexplained.com/content/quicklesson-19-layered-citations-work-layered-clothing)
  and [Source Citations: The Velcro Principle](https://www.evidenceexplained.com/quicktips/source-citations-velcro-principle)
  — consulted versions, source-of-source details and locator ownership.
  Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- Mills, [Negative Evidence vs. Negative Findings](https://www.evidenceexplained.com/quicktips/negative-evidence-vs-negative-findings)
  — absence observations and justified evidentiary interpretation.
  Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- BCG, [Transcribing Source Materials](https://bcgcertification.org/skillbuilding-transcribing-source-materials)
  — distinguish faithful transcription and researcher additions.
  Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- National Genealogical Society, [Guidelines for Sound Genealogical Research](https://www.ngsgenealogy.org/guidelines_sound-research2016-final-30sep2018/)
  — source identification, hypothesis testing and qualification of conclusions.
  Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- Society of Genealogists, [Writing Genealogical Reports](https://sog.org.uk/guides-and-stories/writing-genealogical-reports/)
  — report and proof-summary purposes. Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- FamilySearch, [FamilySearch GEDCOM specification](https://gedcom.io/specifications/FamilySearchGEDCOMv7.html)
  — scope of technical data representation. Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- Nicole Elder Dyer and Diana Elder, [RLP 189: Revisiting Research Logs Again](https://familylocket.com/rlp-189-revisiting-research-logs-again/)
  (2022-02-21) — logs include negative searches and candidate/associate research
  beyond the subject's event timeline. Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- Dyer and Elder, [RLP 101: Proof Arguments — Certification Preparation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rI8F07ph5h8)
  (2020) — practical selection and organization of written proof.
  Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- Dyer and Elder, [RLP 193: Five Takeaways from the DNA Proof Arguments SLIG Course](https://familylocket.com/rlp-193-five-takeaways-from-the-dna-proof-arguments-slig-course/)
  (2022-03-21) — organize proof around reasoning and distinguish it from reporting
  the research process. Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.
- James Tanner (BYU Family History Library), [Untangling a Mess on the FamilySearch Family Tree](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9YdOl_wgvb4), 2024-10-15. Walk-back demonstration in the saved presentation transcript read 2026-11-19. The other presentations formerly grouped here are S1 and S2.
- GeneaVlogger, [Why Everyone is Wrong about Jewish Pirates of the Caribbean](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8AMDbehYYts)
  (2022-07-22). Used for: comparing a published book's quotation with the original source
  wording before accepting an identity claim. Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced. Comparison with the cited Adler text in the presentation transcript read 2026-11-19; the earlier text was not independently inspected.
- James Tanner (BYU Family History Library), [What Are the Oldest Reliable Genealogy Records?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xJpWr2DmpVA)
  (2023-03-08). Used for: checking event places in a tree against when the named town, county
  and state were created. Rights: cited for attribution, not reproduced.

- **S1:** James Tanner, BYU Family History Library, [Evaluating the Accuracy of Entries in the FamilySearch Family Tree](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-mmKGzEd7PE). Pedigree review in the presentation transcript read 2026-11-19.

- **S2:** James Tanner, BYU Family History Library, [Brickwall vs. End-of-Line, Which is it?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i5nCiwPqqCQ). Discussion of surviving records in the presentation transcript read 2026-11-19.

- **S3:** Craig R. Scott, Just Genealogy, [Genealogy as Pastime and Profession, Chapter VI - Royal Ancestry - JG0331](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G1liYml2_-8) (2023-09-23), a reading of Donald Lines Jacobus. Video title and description checked in search results 2026-11-20. The watch page and full transcript were unavailable. In the supplied transcript excerpts, Jacobus describes a chart relying on purported wills he calls fictitious and a photographed pedigree supplying decisive information absent from the original. He describes society acceptance of some examples as his understanding. S4–S6 support the audit procedures independently of these historical anecdotes.

- **S4:** The National Archives, [Wills and Administrations 1383–1858 and Death Duty Registers 1796–1903](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/records/ifa-wills-and-admins-1383-1858-death-duty-registers-1796-1903.pdf). Probate search instructions read 2026-11-20. The guide distinguishes the Prerogative Court of Canterbury’s registered copies and original wills and identifies the search details for locating them.

- **S5:** Elizabeth Shown Mills, [Working with Historical Evidence: Genealogical Principles and Standards](https://historicpathways.com/download/workwthhistevidence.pdf), *National Genealogical Society Quarterly* 87 (September 1999), 165–84. The altered letter example on page 172 read 2026-11-20. Mills describes a scan used to support descent in which one son’s name had been replaced, as revealed by inspecting the original letter. This is a separate case from the photographed pedigree in S3.

- **S6:** Barbara J. Mathews and Darcie Hind Posz, [Meeting Lineage Society Requirements: Part 2, Standards](https://genealogical.com/2021/10/25/meeting-lineage-society-requirements-part-2-standards/) (2021-10-25). Guidance on older applications and evidence standards read 2026-11-20. The authors advise following the citations and checking the documents filed with an application before relying on the lineage.
