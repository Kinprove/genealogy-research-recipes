## Sources

- **S1:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Preserving and Editing Old Photographs: The Overson Collection](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a2I08Q1-Q2A), published 2016-03-22. Used: the presenter's account of his family's glass-negative collection from Eastern Arizona (about the 1880s–1940). He says it went to a university for preservation and was being placed on FamilySearch Memories. Also used: his estimate that group and student shots likely include many local families.
- **S2:** BYU Family History Library / Jeremy Myntti, [Discovering Your Family in Digital Libraries](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aHtewXuH_Jg), published 2023-09-12. Used: the Larson Studio negative collection hosted for the Utah State Historical Society. The presenter gives about 20,000 Utah County photographs from about 1916–1968, a couple of thousand titled "unknown man/woman" and about 1,500 still unidentified. Also used: the recognised 1956 graduation photo and the advice to suggest metadata updates.
- **S3:** BYU Family History Library / Ryan Lee, [Family History Resources in Special Collections](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hJ06E7MLdPw), published 2016-05-31. Used: surname-only search with an image-format filter; searching across the George Edward Anderson, C. R. Savage and other collections; and the presenter's caution that photographs embedded in manuscript collections are often undigitized and must be found through the finding aids.
- **S4:** Lisa Lisson, [Unique Genealogy Finds in the Archives for Your Family History Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jnr2WGm219s), published 2021-11-05. Used: the presenter's account of finding an ancestor's photograph in a Surry County, North Carolina repository's digital collection, reached through a genealogy society site, and of the recorded donor as a contact lead.
- **S5:** Genealogy Gems, [Gathering Strategies to Solve a Genealogy Mystery! Episode 28 Elevenses with Lisa](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9JdhH-O0oY), published 2020-10-08. Used: searching historical-society photos by street rather than exact address, and the presenter's account of a Minnesota Historical Society store photo found through the business name.
- **S6:** Genealogy Gems, [Fabulous Photo Collection: Frith at Findmypast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh_3vjZBt_E), published 2021-09-23. Used: Frith transcripts carrying the photographer's original description, date and location; latitude/longitude that the presenter says the collection added later; and her contrast between landmark and general-scene precision.
- **S7:** Genealogy Gems, [Episode 74 – An Amazing Story Featuring DeadFred](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XB5_KsBKjiU), published 2016-12-23. Used: a mystery photo found by a town-and-state search, then compared with a different family-held photo of the same man.

**Authored synthesis.** The following is not taken from any one presenter: the order of the search, the separate-field record, the coverage log, the "local context only" label, the attribution format, and extending the finding-aid caution beyond BYU. The presenters supply the examples and the failure modes.
