## Sources

- **S1:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [The Anatomy of a Court Case for Genealogists](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BAgypTLrbR4), published 2016-09-15. Used for: recorded judgments and appeals as locators; divorce and injury as flags; the census-in-jail pointer; civil filing stages and service by publication; the search window past death; courts of record and courts not of record; keeping the appellate record separate.
- **S2:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Stand and Deliver: What if your ancestor was a criminal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AyfhLlKm18I), published 2017-11-22. Used for: criminal versus civil classification and the "State v." caveat; arrest as detention; criminal stages; plea frequency; grand-jury records; finding where files were transferred; officer testimony in case files; newspaper limits; full-text case-law search.
- **S3:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Find Your Ancestors in Court Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JrcVzlwtnuQ), published 2025-08-19. Used for: name searches of published case law by jurisdiction; counsel names; following an opinion to the transcript; non-party names in an opinion.
- **S4:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Maximizing Your Use of Google for Genealogists](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yPEzq2OPsBc), published 2016-08-10. Used for: Google Scholar case law as a search of recorded federal and state decisions. His statement that it excludes all trial courts was corrected against a library coverage guide and Google Scholar's help page.
- **S5:** Genealogy Gems, [Episode 205](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zNVSOhlUmgI), published 2017-07-12. Used for: a county archivist's advice that loose court papers are archived apart from the minute books.
- **S6:** Family History Fanatics with The Archive Lady, [Using Manuscript Collections For Genealogy Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o3BGSGILsoQ), published 2022-09-03. Used for: an every-name Stewart County circuit court minute-book index, presented as covering 1821–1834, with roles and volume/page, described as rare.
- **S7:** Genealogy Gems, [Early Virginia Genealogy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H8V4New6FkA), published 2022-07-21. Used for: the chancery index searched by county and name; folders without contents; the FamilySearch Catalog searched by county.
- **S8:** Denyse Allen, [What I Found at the County Courthouse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LCiJ633pXYU), published 2024-07-25. Used for: the lunatics/drunkards index searched under its period term; a negative divorce search at the prothonotary.
- **S9:** Denyse Allen, [What I Found at the County Courthouse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIkcBeJ5d1c), published 2021-08-13. The same account as S8. Do not count S8 and S9 as two independent observations.
- **S10:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Legal Notices for Genealogists](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q5-kKlUKfHs), published 2017-01-19. Used for: reading a file from first to last document; the 30-year probate file example; the sheriff's-sale chain, local variation and post-sale papers.

The following are authored synthesis: the pointer-to-file crosswalk, the stage inventory with explicit gaps, the bounded-negative logging rule and the case register. The sources supply the court-procedure explanations, the retrieval tips and the examples.
