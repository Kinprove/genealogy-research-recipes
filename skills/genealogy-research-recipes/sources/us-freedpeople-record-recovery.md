**Authored synthesis versus sources.** The status table, candidate and per-document registers, explicit versus order-inferred grouping labels, and the coverage log are authored synthesis. The sources supply the record types, search tactics and worked examples. Three points in the gradual-abolition rules come from published state histories, not from S27, which covers Connecticut only: the extension of the birth-cutoff rule to other Northern states, the term-servant reading of a holding, and Connecticut's support liability of former owners and their heirs for people they had freed.

The Freedman's Bank, Last Seen and Enslaved.org entries rest on the custodians' and projects' own pages (S39 to S42, S47, S48); the branch-bounded reading of a miss is authored. The Bureau microfilm numbers and access routes rest on S43 to S46.

**Not covered by the cited sources:** county free-registration books. This recipe makes no claims about their fields. No source shows that the Freedman's Bank claim questionnaires can replace a lost branch register.

Williamson, 57 N.C. 281 (1858), not S37), and the current coverage of the Mapping the Freedmen's Bureau site, how many Bureau images the National Archives Catalog itself carries, and whether Ancestry's Freedman's Bank collection is indexed.
