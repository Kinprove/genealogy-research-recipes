## Sources

Based on recorded talks (reviewed 2026-10-20). The presenters' statements were checked on 2026-10-21 against archive and collection pages. No tax list image was inspected.

- **S1:** Family History Fanatics, [Using Tax Records to Research AROUND The 1890 Census Loss in Genealogy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9jtYj2BEgD4), 2018-10-03. Learn the tax law for that place and year. An absence does not mean evasion or absence from the area. North Carolina poll-tax ages as described by the presenter (white males over 21; over 50 exempt by 1801); the State Library of North Carolina gives freemen and male servants 21+ by 1784. Preambles and headings as scope sources.
- **S2:** Family History Fanatics, [How to Research Tax Records for Your Ancestors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VVlrL4iKUE4), 2020-06-05. Statutes for taxable ages, property and exemptions. Rates from newspapers. Ohio and Michigan volumes arranged by township/range rather than alphabetically, read with a map (presenter's experience).
- **S3:** Genealogy Gems, [Podcast Episode 233](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p4O1Zlol-2k), 2019-09-12. Look up the statute behind unlabelled columns, or use a published summary of tax law (Tennessee example).
- **S4:** Genealogy Gems, [Podcast Episode 218 How to Find Your Family History](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V0he2PdYk2k), 2018-06-14. Pennsylvania "inmates" as renters with taxable personal property. Personal property taxed up to the 1840s (presenter's claim). Colonial lists in the published Pennsylvania Archives, free on Fold3. Later lists at county offices, many microfilmed by the Pennsylvania State Archives in Harrisburg.
- **S5:** Genealogy Gems, [Early Virginia Genealogy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H8V4New6FkA), 2022-07-21. The same interview was reissued in [Podcast Episode 269](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zSx_9MrKP0M), 2022-10-17, so the two are not independent. Separate land and personal-property lists. A man without land can appear as a poll or for an item. The presenter's description of enslaved people listed by first name in county-varying age groups is corrected in the text against the Library of Virginia.
- **S6:** Lisa Lisson, [31 Days of Uncommon Genealogy Tips – Day 20 – Tax Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e3viCt7DJAw), 2019-07-20. Ageing out (over-55 example) as a reason for absence. Consult the state archivist.
- **S7:** Lisa Lisson, [Genealogy Grab Bag of Reader Questions and More!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JY9qiuftib4), 2021-07-16. Left, died or aged out as competing explanations. Exemptions such as ministers. *Estate of* entries. Testing a gap with court and land records.
- **S8:** Lisa Lisson, [Let's Talk Genealogy! – Answering Readers' Questions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5vF5SAYIYU0), 2020-05-08. Follow poll-tax lists for a landless man. Use the liability ages to estimate a birth-year range, not an exact date.
- **S9:** Lisa Lisson, [Substitutions for Your U.S. Census Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jgLVRtRRheM), 2020-01-29. Tax lists cover a mostly male subset. Absence can reflect an exemption.
- **S10:** Lisa Lisson, [15 Strategies to Help You Find a "Missing" Ancestor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pktHqpCrP3g), 2024-03-07. Check whether the person was eligible before reading an absence.
- **S11:** Family History Fanatics, [When Did They Die? Finding Your Ancestor's Death Date](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dck2TkjsKso), 2021-06-19. A later widow entry as a bounding clue. Moved, aged out or died.
