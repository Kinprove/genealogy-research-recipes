## Sources

**Review note for S1–S24:** Recorded talks reviewed on 2026-10-20; the presenters' statements were checked on 2026-10-21 against archive and collection pages. No tax list image was inspected.

- **S1:** Family History Fanatics, [Using Tax Records to Research AROUND The 1890 Census Loss in Genealogy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9jtYj2BEgD4), 2018-10-03. Learn the tax law for that place and year. An absence does not mean evasion or absence from the area. North Carolina poll-tax ages as described by the presenter (white males over 21; over 50 exempt by 1801); the State Library of North Carolina gives freemen and male servants 21+ by 1784. Preambles and headings as scope sources.
- **S2:** Family History Fanatics, [How to Research Tax Records for Your Ancestors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VVlrL4iKUE4), 2020-06-05. Statutes for taxable ages, property and exemptions. Rates from newspapers. Ohio and Michigan volumes arranged by township/range rather than alphabetically, read with a map (presenter's experience).
- **S3:** Genealogy Gems, [Podcast Episode 233](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p4O1Zlol-2k), 2019-09-12. Look up the statute behind unlabelled columns, or use a published summary of tax law (Tennessee example).
- **S4:** Genealogy Gems, [Podcast Episode 218 How to Find Your Family History](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V0he2PdYk2k), 2018-06-14. Pennsylvania "inmates" as renters with taxable personal property. Personal property taxed up to the 1840s (presenter's claim). Colonial lists in the published Pennsylvania Archives, free on Fold3. Later lists at county offices, many microfilmed by the Pennsylvania State Archives in Harrisburg.
- **S5:** Genealogy Gems, [Early Virginia Genealogy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H8V4New6FkA), 2022-07-21. The same interview was reissued in [Podcast Episode 269](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zSx_9MrKP0M), 2022-10-17, so the two are not independent. Separate land and personal-property lists. A man without land can appear as a poll or for an item. The presenter's description of enslaved people listed by first name in county-varying age groups is corrected in the text against the Library of Virginia.
- **S6:** Lisa Lisson, [31 Days of Uncommon Genealogy Tips – Day 20 – Tax Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e3viCt7DJAw), 2019-07-20. Ageing out (over-55 example) as a reason for absence. Consult the state archivist.
- **S7:** Lisa Lisson, [Genealogy Grab Bag of Reader Questions and More!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JY9qiuftib4), 2021-07-16. Left, died or aged out as competing explanations. Exemptions such as ministers. *Estate of* entries. Testing a gap with court and land records.
- **S8:** Lisa Lisson, [Let's Talk Genealogy! – Answering Readers' Questions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5vF5SAYIYU0), 2020-05-08. Follow poll-tax lists for a landless man. Use the liability ages to estimate a birth-year range, not an exact date.
- **S9:** Lisa Lisson, [Substitutions for Your U.S. Census Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jgLVRtRRheM), 2020-01-29. Tax lists cover a mostly male subset. Absence can reflect an exemption.
- **S10:** Lisa Lisson, [15 Strategies to Help You Find a "Missing" Ancestor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pktHqpCrP3g), 2024-03-07. Check whether the person was eligible before reading an absence.
- **S11:** Family History Fanatics, [When Did They Die? Finding Your Ancestor's Death Date](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dck2TkjsKso), 2021-06-19. A later widow entry as a bounding clue. Moved, aged out or died.
- **S12:** BYU Family History Library / Julia Anderson, [Deep South Research: A Beginner's Guide and Case Study](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q9dHUILop_A), 2026-10-02. Rusk County, Texas rolls worked backward from 1860, with capacity notations, adjacency, acreage and original grantee. The presenter describes Texas tax rolls 1837–1910 as indexed on FamilySearch; the collection page shows a selective index (sample years, 231 of 254 counties, about 13% complete).
- **S13:** BYU Family History Library / Laura Lefler, [How to Learn About My Civil War Ancestors Part 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tD9lgibwBRE), 2024-11-19. Last year present in tax lists and censuses. A family still on the taxed land points to death in place.
- **S14:** BYU Family History Library / Richard Miller, [Handling Tough Genealogy Problems with a Visual Canvas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vVWewJI3NgM), 2023-08-10. Jr./Sr. as a descriptor. Section/range/acreage/value as keys for deed tracing. A nominal price is only a lead.
- **S15:** GeneaVlogger, [Building Shane Gillis' Family Tree](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z0wwMYchf2I), 2024-11-02. A Chester County series, as viewed online, jumping from 1838 to 1875. Presence recorded by township (New Garden).
- **S16:** BYU Family History Library / Maureen Brady, [The First Frontiersmen: The Scots-Irish](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fb7YRQOddPI), 2023-02-21. Pennsylvania lists organised by township, with land records first. County guides are finding aids, not records.
- **S17:** BYU Family History Library / Nicky Smith, [Evidence Analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Vp10fU3fTc), 2017-03-04. Taxed 1823–1838, then absent. Moved, too old or died as competing explanations.
- **S18:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Tax Records for Genealogists](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rs_0p8ENBMk), 2017-04-04. Sons appearing as they come of age. Departure dated "almost to the month" (presenter's claim).
- **S19:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Your Ancestors Paid Taxes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3_qHtEvC5Nc), 2025-01-29. A Rhode Island town tax-list disappearance used to time a move to New York.
- **S20:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Overcoming Brick Walls: Resolving the Outcome](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2yVk3RoVGcA), 2021-09-03. Monthly tax records said to time a departure to the month. Search the next place.
- **S21:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Researching New England Town Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bhpOk-A5CeA), 2023-11-06. The last taxpaying year as a bound when no death entry exists.
- **S22:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Researching In-depth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_-q0vqAdMiw), 2015-03-18. Annual rolls as a US stand-in for house registers, bracketing arrival and departure.
- **S23:** BYU Family History Library / Jerroleen Sorensen, [Using Historical Sources To Make Inferences](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BXde-Z4rocM), 2022-06-06. Absence may mean death or being under 21, depending on local law.
- **S24:** Lisa Lisson, [How to Research Your Common Surname Ancestors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2MsRyfJqELI), 2017-10-23. First and last appearance, each read against that year's tax law.
- **S25:** ArkivDigital, [Frågestund på Zoom den 7 februari 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DoRSQ94Hd1M), published 2024-02-09. Used for: parish/year search in *Innehållsförteckningar till mantalslängder* as an administrative bridge into an original roll's parish section; multiple roll results for one year; returning to the first page for period-specific column headings; annual farm tracking with taxable-age and exemption alternatives to vital-event inferences. The interface and example holdings are historical demonstrations, not current coverage, survival, household completeness or a universal tax rule.

The following are authored synthesis and do not come from the videos: the four-status series register, the rule that an index miss is not an inspection, the per-year liability-scope record, the interval format, the rule that neighbour inference depends on list order, the letter-block completion rule, and the delinquent-addendum and newspaper checks. The videos supply the failure patterns and the worked examples. The James Tanner (S18–S20) and Genealogy Gems (S5) segments repeat each other and are not independent support.
