## Sources

Third-party sources are cited references, paraphrased rather than reproduced.
The index-audit, locator and series-resolution schemas are original synthesis.

- **S1:** Aimee Cross,
  [Deeds: How to Use Them in Genealogy Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X8NNeexlV8E),
  24 February 2022. Both index directions, recording versus instrument dates and varied index keys.

  [RLP 49: U.S. Land Records Part 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KvJ5YmeIHvI),
  17 June 2019. Alternative-entry reconciliation, index-reference logging and a misread page range.

  [Treasure Hunt at the Love County Courthouse: Uncovering Land Records](https://familylocket.com/treasure-hunt-at-the-love-county-courthouse-uncovering-land-records/),

- **S4:** BYU Family History Library, John Hendrix,
  [U.S. Land Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gWPPN9DUUSc),

- **S5:** BYU Family History Library, James Tanner,
  [When the Baby was Born, the Mother was There and other Research Rules](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gssWfSl8JwY),

  records were destroyed.

  [AUDIO PODCAST Episode 269 Virginia Genealogy Strategies and 7 Best Websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zSx_9MrKP0M),
  17 October 2022. Deed books as clerk copies of returned originals, and re-recording of surviving
  originals after courthouse fires.
- **S7:** Genealogy Gems,
  [Genealogy Gems Podcast Episode 218 How to Find Your Family History](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V0he2PdYk2k),

- **S8:** BYU Family History Library, Maureen Brady,
  [Family History Research in Illinois and Wisconsin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kFxsAw8HHAs),
  30 January 2026. The presenter describes party-only indexes; verify the actual index's coverage
  before interpreting a negative, and use full-text or document reading for other named people.
- **S9:** BYU Family History Library, James Tanner,
  [Metes & Bounds on Google Earth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gci5qXFBtrQ),

  wife and the date of her examination.
- **S10:** Dana Leeds,

  a current access contract, and present request forms, appointments and retrieval services were
  not verified.
- **S11:** Dana Leeds,
  [NARA Land Records: Discovering Family History Details That Aren’t Online](https://www.danaleeds.com/nara-land-records-discovering-family-history-details-that-arent-online/),

  eligibility, the author's family, kinship or service conclusions, or current digitization claims.

### Instrument-type, mining-claim, house-history and farm-succession sources

- **S44:** FamilySearch Research Wiki, [United States Deeds](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/United_States_Deeds), read 2026-11-12. Used for: deed books containing records relating to property other than land, the listed instrument types, and brothers and sisters signing a deed giving up their claim to property received from their parents. The page does not list divisions among heirs.
- **S45:** FamilyTreeDNA Blog, [Researching the Ancestry of Formerly-Enslaved African American Families, Part 1: Up to the 1870 Brick Wall](https://blog.familytreedna.com/researching-ancestry-formerly-enslaved-african-american-families/), published 2026-06-17, read 2026-11-12. Used for: reading deed books beyond land sales, and the reported 1910 deed dividing a man's land among his heirs. Its general statement that deed books include divisions among heirs is not adopted as a rule.
- **S46:** Michelle Mickelson, Family Locket, [Southwestern United States Research: Key Records and Repositories Part 4 – Mining and Agriculture Records](https://familylocket.com/southwestern-united-states-research-key-records-and-repositories-part-4-mining-and-agriculture-records/), published 2024-02-17, read 2026-11-12. Used for: starting a mining-claim search at the county recorder's office, digitized copies, claim locations leading to maps and land records, and the personal details in miner registrations. Its remark that many such records are online was not tested.
- **S47:** FamilySearch Research Wiki, [Calaveras County, California Genealogy](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Calaveras_County,_California_Genealogy), read 2026-11-12. Used for: the county's "Mining and Land Claims 1852-1931, and Indexes 1850-1892" listed with images among its land records. The catalog entry itself needs a sign-in and was not opened.
- **S48:** Calaveras Heritage Council, [Archives and Organizations](https://calaverashistory.specialdistrict.org/archives-and-organizations), read 2026-11-12. Used for: the Calaveras County Archives in San Andreas holding mining claims with deeds and other county records. Opening hours and finding aids were not checked.
- **S49:** Hermann Hartenthaler, CompGen Blog, [Genealogie und Geografie verknüpft in Ortsfamilienbüchern](https://www.compgen.de/2026/05/zusammenfuehrung-von-genealogie-und-geografie-in-ortsfamilienbuechern/), published 2026-05-03, read 2026-11-12. Used for: the DORIS layer and Wolfgang Zehetner's Haus & Hof as sources for farm names, ownership and manorial lordships. Its description of Haus & Hof as covering Austria is narrowed by S52.
- **S50:** Land Oberösterreich, DORIS, [Hofnamen und Häusergeschichte](https://www.doris.at/themen/geschichte/hofnamen.aspx), read 2026-11-12. Used for: the theme's houses and farms of about 1830 placed on the Urmappe, its farm names, owners and Grundherrschaft, its three source registers and their dates, and its stated gaps.
- **S51:** Land Oberösterreich, [Heimat bewahren: 100.000 historische Namen von oö. Höfen und ihre Geschichte sind nun im Internet abrufbar](https://www.land-oberoesterreich.gv.at/273053.htm), press release published 2022-01-18, read 2026-11-12. Used for: about 100,000 houses and farms at the 2022 launch, prepared by the Upper Austrian State Archives with the state's geoinformation department.
- **S52:** ÖFR – Österreichische Gesellschaft für Familien- und regionalgeschichtliche Forschung, [Haus und Hof](https://oefr.at/haus-und-hof/), read 2026-11-12. Used for: Wolfgang Zehetner's project on Lower Austrian house history, with owners around 1820 and 1787, historical house names, pointers to manorial holdings and communities added regularly. The database itself was not searched.
- **S53:** Karola Kertel, CompGen Blog, [Wie kam der Bauer zu seiner Frau?](https://www.compgen.de/2024/10/wie-kam-der-bauer-zu-seiner-frau/), published 2024-10-12, read 2026-11-12. Used for: undivided farm inheritance in northern Germany, eldest- or youngest-son rules by area, limited shares for the children who left, and the Emsland account of what sons who did not inherit did. Its divided-inheritance examples (Bad Orb, Neckarhausen) are not used.
- **S54:** Austria-Forum, [Anerbenrecht](https://austria-forum.org/af/AustriaWiki/Anerbenrecht), AustriaWiki copy of the Wikipedia article, read 2026-11-12. Used for: Anerbenrecht as inheritance of a farm by a single heir, the youngest-son custom confirmed in Ravensberg in 1669 and held in the Wesermarsch, and the other heirs being paid off well below the real value. Its list of present-day statutory regions is not used for historical custom.
- **S55:** Ulrich Pfister, University of Münster, [Besitzweitergabe und Erbpraxis im ländlichen Raum](https://www.wiwi.uni-muenster.de/wisoge/sites/wisoge/files/downloads/skripte/familie_neuzeit/s02_besitzweitergabe_folien.pdf), lecture slides dated 2016-10-25, read 2026-11-12. Used for: the regions listed for divided and undivided inheritance, the Belm figures on youngest-son succession and the small-farm exception, and the caution that the contrast is schematic.
- **S56:** Riksarkivet, [Lagfartsböcker](https://sok.riksarkivet.se/lagfartsbocker) (database page), read 2026-11-13. Used for: lagfartsböcker as books for rural properties, kept from the 1870s and showing information from about 1875 to 1933; the index of the books not complete; searching the archive's books by the property's name at the time and its parish, or the indexed books by property name.
