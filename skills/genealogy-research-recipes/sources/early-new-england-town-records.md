## Sources

- **S1:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Researching New England Town Records from the 1600s to the present](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bhpOk-A5CeA), published 2023-11-06. Used for: books are not divided by record type, so read every page; possible holdings in special collections; continuing into an adjoining town's book.
- **S2:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Moving Beyond Birth, Marriage, and Death Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JHqvJGhy2d4), published 2023-02-06. Used for: clerks summarizing a family's children at a later date; the need to read the whole record; limits of compiled extracts such as Arnold's.
- **S3:** BYU Family History Library / James Tanner, [Delving into Colonial Town Records](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xh3gTnIEWY4), published 2023-12-13. Used for: most town records as narrative minutes that mention marriages, deaths and land sales; the presenter's view that indexes almost never exist; availability and condition problems.
- **S4:** Genealogy Gems, [Colonial Genealogy Research – How to find early American ancestors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AG1TzZ79buQ), published 2020-11-12. Used for: town guides with parent and daughter towns; the Abington/Bridgewater example; cross-county marriages; the presenter's Torrey example (whose bracket rule is corrected above), the "?" mark, the source-abbreviation guide and the 1962 cut-off; *Founders of Early American Families* coverage.
- **S5:** Genealogy Gems, [Podcast Episode 253 – Finding Early New England Ancestors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UyinOYMxTOI), published 2021-06-04. Used for: a second presentation by the same speaker of the town-guide, parent-town and Torrey material.

The order of steps (jurisdiction first, then whole-volume reading with a coverage register, then Torrey decoding) and the output packet are authored synthesis. The sources supply the reading practice, the parent-town principle with its example, and the Torrey walkthrough. Torrey's notation rules are taken from his key as quoted on FamilySearch, not from the presenter.
