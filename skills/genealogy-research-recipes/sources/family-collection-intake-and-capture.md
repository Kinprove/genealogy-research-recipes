## Sources

- **S1:** Genealogy TV, [How to Create Quick Text Files to go with Image and Document Collections](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QpN2I8mgcQE), published 2018-10-30. Used: a plain-text note kept inside an inherited photograph or document folder, recording where the collection came from, as a basic finding aid for later holders. The presenter's own example works from a cousin's emails about the photographs she gave.
- **S2:** Lisa Lisson, [Tips for Scanning Old Family Photos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BZRieam9j24), published 2019-10-11. Used: notes on who supplied the photographs; scanning uncropped and cropping later for printing or sharing; making copies from a preserved master; and the later discussion correcting an earlier dismissal of physical size: size can help date a photograph, a digital file gives few clues to the original's size, and size or format can go in labels or tags. Excluded: numeric capture settings, and the remark that opening and closing a JPEG degrades it.
- **S3:** Genealogy TV with Melissa Barker, [How to Preserve Old Photos and Scrapbooks: The Archive Lady - Melissa Barker](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11wZOvuGtKs), published 2023-02-24. Used: the creator's original order, digitizing every page including blank ones, and capturing folded items both closed and opened. Not adopted: her rehousing, removal and freezing advice.
- **S4:** MyHeritage / Thomas MacEntee, [Live With Thomas MacEntee! Learn Best Practices and Tips for Scanning Your Photos!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U40HTMoXrbs), published 2020-10-29. Used: an inventory and scanning plan before starting, scanning both sides of photographs, scanning the whole page of a magnetic album, and a note card marking where scanning stopped. Excluded: fixed resolutions, storage estimates and adhesive-removal techniques.
- **S5:** MyHeritage / Andy Likins, [We're live! Andy Likins shares his insights into locating photos of ancestors!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CGajkAEP0H4), published 2021-01-19. Used: asking who identified an inherited photograph and how the item was inherited, which keeps a relative's account apart from a caption and from interpretation. Not adopted: his dating cues and comparing photographs to identify people, which belong to attribution work.
- **S6:** Lisa Lisson, [How To Digitize Old Family Photos - Preserve Your Photos For Future Generations!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EiDoJoxbB2A), published 2019-10-09. Used: a flatbed scanner rather than an automatic feeder for antique photographs; scanners suited to negatives and slides; and having copying done locally or at the holder's home rather than mailing the originals. Excluded: brand and purchasing advice.
- **S7:** GeneaVlogger, [Update on Sephardic Research 2017 - Nunes Vaz and Henriques Pimentel Families (VLOG #19)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1DAj9jarN78), published 2017-11-09. Used: the presenter's account of a historical document whose 1955 restoration went badly, and of the other versions located: a black-and-white photograph from before 1955, a high-resolution photograph from the early 1990s, and scans. It does not compare the readings across versions.
- **S8:** BYU Library Family History Center / Olivia Jewell, [Preserving Family History in Fun & Modern Ways- Olivia Jewell](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5bxw2_C8uAM), published 2019-04-04. Used: metadata stored with an image (filename, date, location, author, a contact email) can be edited after scanning and can change later. It does not show how to tell an estimated value from a recorded one.
- **S9:** Roberta Estes, [RootsTech 2024 Happy Dance – FamilySearch Library and RootsTech Setup](https://dna-explained.com/2024/03/08/rootstech-2024-happy-dance-familysearch-library-and-rootstech-setup/), published 2024-03-08. Used: a large map that no scan station could take, scanned in portions and digitally stitched, with a slight color variation remaining at the joins. This motivates the join check. It does not establish current capture services or validate a stitching method.
- **S10:** Mercedes, Who Are You Made Of?, [What is the Storymaker Studio on Ancestry?](https://whoareyoumadeof.com/blog/what-is-the-storymaker-studio-on-ancestry/), published 2023-03-07. Used: the author's account of documents she could not find because they were attached to an unexpected relative, often a spouse, and of a central view of all tree media. No current gallery, edit, visibility or export behaviour is promised.
- **S11:** Renate Ell, [SOS genealogischer Nachlass](https://www.compgen.de/2022/10/sos-genealogischer-nachlass/), published 2022-10-05. Used: the advice to contact a society or archive before handing material over, and the point that some archives already hold many books, so donors must find another destination.
- **S12:** MyHeritage / Maureen Taylor, [We're Live! Strategies to Enjoy your Photographs With Maureen Taylor!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WPdewUOXn2I), published 2020-11-19. Used: metadata covering dates, sources, captions, locations and people, embedded in the digital file so that it stays with the image. No current product export is promised.

**Authored synthesis.** The following is workflow design, not taken from any one presenter: the stable item references and parent-page links, the surface states (captured, missing, uninspected, inaccessible), the file roles, the progress-marker contents, the join check, the found-elsewhere versus not-located distinction, the handover record and the hand-off check. None of these is a native export header or a platform schema. The display-derivative rule applies the recipe's own derivative control. The presenters supply the examples and the failure modes.

**Not verified:**

- current behaviour of any scanning service, media gallery, export or backup product, including how any platform's gallery shows attached media
- whether any stitching method preserves full extent
