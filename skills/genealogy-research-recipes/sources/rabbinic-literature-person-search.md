## Sources

Public pages were consulted on 2026-11-07 unless another date is given. Where
hebrewbooks.org or nli.org.il refused automated requests, a dated Wayback Machine capture is
cited instead.

- **S1. HebrewBooks advanced search and its help file.**
  [Advanced page, capture 2026-01-23](https://web.archive.org/web/20260123105147/https://hebrewbooks.org/advanced.aspx)
  lists the OCR-search modes (exact phrase, all words, any word, Boolean), fuzzy levels and
  word forms. [Search help, capture 2026-01-23](https://web.archive.org/web/20260123105146/https://hebrewbooks.org/searchhelp.txt)
  documents AND, OR, NOT, parentheses, quoted phrases and `w/N` proximity. The 200-row
  display limit is a Kinprove observation of saved result pages on 2026-10-01; no public
  HebrewBooks text states it.
- **S2. HebrewBooks records.** *Oholei Shem*, Pinsk 1912, 592 pages:
  [HB 36602, capture 2026-10-09](https://web.archive.org/web/20261009043816/https://www.hebrewbooks.org/36602).
  Berl Kohen (Kagan), *Sefer ha-prenumerantn*, New York 1975:
  [HB 46561, capture 2024-02-08](https://web.archive.org/web/20240208081336/https://www.hebrewbooks.org/46561).
  The genealogical *Da'at Kedoshim* (Eisenstadt and Wiener, St Petersburg 1897–1898):
  [HB 6832, capture 2023-12-26](https://web.archive.org/web/20231226001818/https://www.hebrewbooks.org/6832);
  a different, halakhic work with the same title:
  [HB 6812, capture 2026-02-09](https://web.archive.org/web/20260209011439/https://www.hebrewbooks.org/6812).
  Used for the two same-title books and the catalogue's place of printing.
- **S3. *Oholei Shem* entries.** [Hebrew Wikipedia, "אהלי שם (ספר)"](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/אהלי_שם_(ספר))
  and [sample entries](https://oholeschem.wordpress.com/), which are headed by town and give
  each rabbi's birth year and the year he was appointed rabbi.
- **S4. Subscriber-list index.** [JewishGen, "Prenumeranten"](https://www.jewishgen.org/rabbinic/infofiles/prenumeranten.htm)
  describes Kagan's index of subscriber lists by community.
- **S5. Otzar HaHochma.** [Product page](https://www.otzar.org/otzaren/eodot.asp) (OCR
  search, licences, a trial limited to the first pages of each book, printing and saving
  pages as PDF) and [single-user page](https://otzar.org/otzaren/emekuvan-yechidim.asp)
  (guest view of the first pages of each book). The search-field labels, the scope and
  option checkboxes, the explicit no-results message and the one-letter spelling contrast are
  Kinprove observations of the logged-in viewer on 2026-09-15. The closed publisher packages
  and the export limit were observed in the same viewer between 2026-09-15 and 2026-10-25.
- **S6. Dicta library.** [library.dicta.org.il](https://library.dicta.org.il) and its
  [catalogue file](https://files.dicta.org.il/library-1-0/books.json): 1,205 OCR'd books,
  415 of them responsa, each with a text file.
- **S7. Sefaria.** [API reference, v3 texts](https://developers.sefaria.org/reference/get-v3-texts):
  version selection by language and version title, and `fill_in_missing_segments`, which
  fills gaps from other versions. That vowel points in Sefaria can be a modern addition is a
  Kinprove comparison of one Breslov work's Sefaria text, mostly pointed, with its unpointed
  1874 printing.
- **S8. Lubavitch Library.** [chabadlibrary.org](https://chabadlibrary.org/) and its
  [book tree](https://chabadlibrary.org/books/api/main?path=3800000000), where the Tzemach
  Tzedek's responsa are in full text. The index of questioners by town is a Kinprove
  observation of 2026-10-01.
- **S9. National Library of Israel and CAHJP.** [EHRI, Central Archives for the History of
  the Jewish People](https://portal.ehri-project.eu/institutions/il-002781) (merged into the
  National Library of Israel in 2013); Benjamin Lukin,
  [the CAHJP microfilm collection from the former Soviet Union](https://www.jewishgen.org/Ukraine/files/CAHJP-microfilm-collection.pdf),
  2003. That description searches return different sets for Hebrew, Latin and Cyrillic
  spellings is a Kinprove observation of 2026-09-19.
- **S10. Project Zikaron.** [Home page](https://www.zikaron.tourolib.org) and
  [terms of use](https://www.zikaron.tourolib.org/terms-of-use), §3 (no scrapers, robots or
  other automated access).
- **S11. Crown-rabbi era lists.** [JewishGen, "Jewish Religious Personnel in the Russian
  Empire, 1853–1854"](https://www.jewishgen.org/databases/misc/deych.htm), an index to
  G. M. Deych's 1992 book.
- **S12. *Encyclopedia of Hasidism*.** [University of Pennsylvania catalogue record, OCLC
  13175627](https://find.library.upenn.edu/catalog/998628473503681): the personalities
  volumes are by Yitzhak Alfasi.
- **S13. Two men named Nachman.** [Hebrew Wikipedia, "נחמן חזן"](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/נחמן_חזן)
  (R. Nachman of Tulchin, died 1884) and
  [English Wikipedia, "Nachman Goldstein"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachman_Goldstein)
  (the Tcheriner Rav, 1825–1894).
- **S14. Calendar.** [Hebcal, Ta'anit Esther](https://www.hebcal.com/holidays/taanit-esther):
  when 13 Adar falls on Shabbat the fast is held on the preceding Thursday.
- **S15. Naming and priestly status.** [Wikipedia, "Jewish name", Ashkenazi customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_name)
  (a child is named after a deceased relative);
  [Wikipedia, "Kohen"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohen) (status passes from father to son).
- **S16. Further rabbinic reference works.** [JewishGen rabbinic bibliography](https://www.jewishgen.org/Rabbinic/infofiles/biblio2.htm)
  (*Otzar ha-Rabbanim*, 1975, about 20,000 rabbis);
  [JewishGen review of *Me'orei Galicia*](https://www.jewishgen.org/rabbinic/journal/mgalicia.htm)
  (genealogies of Galician rabbinic families arranged by surname);
  Bill Gladstone, [An unbroken chain of Jewish genealogy](https://thecjn.ca/arts-culture/unbroken-chain-jewish-genealogy/),
  *The Canadian Jewish News*, 2017-08-19 (Neil Rosenstein's study of the genealogy of the
  major Ashkenazi rabbinical dynasties).
- **S17. Moreh tzedek as a leading rabbi.** [Kedem auction 27 catalogue](https://bidspirit-images.global.ssl.fastly.net/kedemauctionDocs/430/catalog/Keden_27_WebCat.pdf),
  item 390: a signature as moreh tzedek of Minsk, and Minsk's avoidance of the title av beit
  din after 1813.
- **S18. Dates in Russian-Polish acts.** [JewishGen, Polish records, question 7](https://jewishgen.org/InfoFiles/Poland/Questions.htm):
  acts that give the Julian date first and the Gregorian date second.
- **S19. Approbations.** [Encyclopaedia Judaica, "Haskamah", via Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/haskamah):
  approbations carry biographical and geographical data, and the bibliography cites
  L. Loewenstein, *Index Approbationum* (1923).
- **S20. The Hebrew press.** [Wikipedia, "Historical Jewish Press"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_Jewish_Press)
  (a joint project of the National Library of Israel and Tel Aviv University);
  [Wikipedia, "Hamagid"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamagid) and
  [Wikipedia, "HaMelitz"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMelitz), each linking online,
  searchable editions on that site.

**What is Kinprove's own work.** The procedure, its order and the screening rules, including
the readings of era markers, honorifics, office formulae, kinship words and title-page
dates, are Kinprove synthesis from
its own research in rabbinic and Hasidic books between 2026-05 and 2026-10. The counts in the recipe come from that reading of public books: the surname that
appears once against 121 mentions of "the Rav of <town>" (an English edition of Breslov
works); at least six men called "R. Nachman" (the *Siach Sarfei Kodesh* series); 80 of 81
surname hits that were namesakes; the Yiddish and Hebrew spellings of Uman; the `טש` sweep;
the stem `חותנ`; the closed keyword list that missed hundreds of sections; and book IDs
supplied by a language model that pointed to unrelated books. No family data, private
identifiers or account details are included.
