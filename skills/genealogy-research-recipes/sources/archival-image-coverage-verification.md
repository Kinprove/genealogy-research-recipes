# Sources: archival image and coverage verification

Primary pages opened 2026-10-18; cited as references, not reproduced.

- **S1. FamilySearch, [Image Organize: Guiding Principles and Practices to Divide Groups](https://www.familysearch.org/en/fieldops/article/image-organize-guiding-principles-and-practices-to-divide-groups-external)**, sections 2–5 and 9. Supports item boundaries, opening/closing/interior inspection and exceptions involving several books or films. These are provider organisation rules, not proof that a particular scan is complete.
- **S2. FamilySearch, [Document information in Historical Records](https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/what-is-the-document-information-in-historical-records)**. Defines digital folder, microfilm, image, page, family and household identifiers; provider and original-reference numbers need not coincide.
- **S3. JewishGen Ukraine Research Division, [FAQ](https://www.jewishgen.org/ukraine/TPL_Base.asp?id=13)**, “How do I find out what new records are being added”. Links monthly and cumulative upload lists and says they change monthly. No individual case's current indexing was investigated for this recipe.
- **S4. JewishGen, [Transcription Rules for JewishGen Databases](https://www.jewishgen.org/databases/%24transcription.html)**, Source Indicator and Data Entry. Requires original-record locators and separates conjecture from source transcription. It does not certify a dataset's completeness.
- **S5. BYU Family History Library, James Tanner, [“FamilySearch Part 1 by James Tanner”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Rcm-OakCes)** (published 2014-09-27). Used for the rule to inspect the end of a roll for appended images and a roll index before concluding absence.
- **S6. BYU Family History Library, James Tanner, [“3. Case Studies Live On Air - James Tanner (31 Jul 2022)”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jfWVACjBbbM)** (published 2022-08-01). Used for a collection title contradicted by the image citation and the volume's mixed contents.
- **S7. Denyse Allen, [“AI Finds the Record Trail”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4b4eUWm2s)** (published 2026-08-09). Used for identifying an out-of-place image by its printed form heading against neighbouring images.
- **S8. Genealogy Gems, [“Source Citation for Genealogy - Elevenses with Lisa Episode 60”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Eh06QweqW3g)** (published 2021-06-17). Used for a census page held at the archive but absent from the online images.
- **S9. Denyse Allen, [“Two Must Read History Books for PA Research”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t70iQZdquD0)** (published 2024-07-25). Used for the remark that a digitized copy may show only a document's front.
- **S10. Genealogy Gems, [“Genealogy Gems Podcast Episode 226 (Audio Only)”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7TW9qyShIU)** (published 2019-02-14). Used for a librarian's report that front matter revealed categories of entries excluded from earlier register abstracts.
- **S11. Genealogy Gems, [“Genealogy Records Provenance, Primary & Secondary Sources, Finding Aids”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mTyYpO_3uPs)** (published 2020-12-10). Used for checking a cropped scan's outer edges and consulting another provider's image of the same record.
- **S12. BYU Family History Library, Todd Knowles, [“The Knowles Collection- Todd Knowles”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BivX3fFXJRw)** (published 2019-04-24). Used for marginal entry numbers keyed to a back section of the same birth register.

The page × target ledger, field-specific adjudication, catalogue-conflict handling and completeness checks are workflow synthesis from local archival-review practice. They introduce no historical cutoff, archive-specific holding assertion or measured accuracy claim. Genealogical identity and source weighting remain with the existing proof recipe.
