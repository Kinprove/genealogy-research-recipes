## Sources

- **S1:** BYU Family History Library, Maureen Brady, [Swedish Family History Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4E26fJZm5sY), published 2026-06-05. Used for: generational patronymic change; Persson/Pettersson/Pierson variants; given-name variants; same-name risk and the use of dates, parents, spouses and farms; adult siblings choosing different surnames around the 1880s; the soldier-byname example (Johan Magnus Anderson / John Falk).
- **S2:** BYU Family History Library, Joe Everett, [Scandinavian Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j9iUYQm0_JQ), published 2019-02-05. Used for: smallpox vaccination noted in baptism, confirmation and marriage entries as a discriminator; moving-in and moving-out records; the Jensen warning; the Slangerup example; browsing the register to the known birth date; the destination-first method.
- **S3:** BYU Family History Library, Laurie Castillo, [Why Can't I Find My Family? Digging Deeper on Surnames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qDW8dEp5mN0), published 2022-09-14. Used for: matronymic names for illegitimate children; soldier names; farm names changing with moves or with a son-in-law marrying in; the presenter's 1860 and 1901 Swedish dates.
- **S4:** BYU Family History Library, James Tanner, [Discovering Your Ancestors in Norway](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XyfmOA8kCM8), published 2025-09-17. Used for: researching down to the farm or house; families with repeated parents' and children's names; distrusting lines with no house-census or farm sources.
- **S5:** Genealogy Gems, [Genealogy Gems Podcast Episode 237](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-BtOaR_3Zmk), published 2020-01-07. Used for: the guest's account of patronymic formation; wives keeping their own names; birth dates as near-identifiers; a family surname taken from an army byname; finding an ancestor beside his future wife on a manifest under a "-son" form.
- **S6:** Lisa Lisson, [Scandinavian Naming Patterns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FEJ5gzs_5p0), published 2019-04-26. Used for: older and younger siblings carrying different surnames at the transition; the guest's "mid-1800s" phase-out dating (accurate for Denmark only); farm or occupational additions that last one or several generations; entering the christening-record name as the primary form, with sourced alternates.
- **S7:** Lisa Lisson, [Using DNA of Collateral Lines in Your Genealogy Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fisEw7HXNfo), published 2023-07-21. Used for: finding a probable brother outside the family group on the parents' page of a Norwegian passenger-list transcription by farm name and patronymic; confirming him through a Norwegian baptism and the minister's outgoing record dated before sailing.
- **S8:** BYU Family History Library, Marilyn Thomsen, [Military Research: What and When](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C2_9AVcodcg), published 2024-08-20. Used for: Scandinavian soldiers recorded under a military name; searching by unit and that name, starting with the local muster.
- **S9:** BYU Family History Library, James Tanner, [Finding Your Ancestors in Iceland](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4jhIdvmyFUw), published 2021-04-29. Used for: women listed under maiden names or patronymics in census households; proving the marriage; the presenter's dating of the abandonment of patronymics; Iceland's continued use of patronymics.

The name-form register, the candidate comparison table, the bounded-browse reporting and the origin packet are authored synthesis. The presenters supply the naming mechanisms, the examples and the failure cases. The following were NOT verified:

- Castillo's 1860 start date for Swedish surname changes
- whether vaccination columns appear in a given Swedish or Icelandic parish or period
- the arrangement of moving-out registers outside Norway
- the coverage of provider indexes, and of passenger-list transcriptions other than Norway Heritage
- the spelling of names as spoken in the videos (farm names, the S5 surname, Slangerup's christening name)

No individual identity or kinship conclusion from the examples is adopted.
