## Sources

- **S1:** BYU Family History Library / Dave Obee, [Discoveries in the 1931 Canadian Census – Dave Obee (31 August 2023)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9IHEinKGzs), published 2023-09-02. Used for: de jure versus de facto counting; the absent husband; residents abroad counted at home; listed people who were not in Canada or alive; printed reports showing remote-district enumeration up to about two months off the official date.
- **S2:** Genealogy Gems (Lisa Louise Cooke) with guest Dave Obee, [Episode 203 The Genealogy Gems Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TD88sPoZFPM), published 2017-05-10. Used for: a husband listed in Nova Scotia and Alberta; the guest's 25-year residence span; the death certificate rejected in error; no "divorced" option on some returns; French/English heading differences; pre-census newspaper explanations.
- **S3:** Genealogy Gems, [Episode 154 10 Tips for Breaking Through Your Genealogy Brick Wall](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1pVpwjsp7l4), published 2016-12-23. Used for: the guest's statement that the Canadian census lists those who should have been there; overnight visitors not counted; absence not proving non-residence.
- **S4:** Genealogy Gems, [10 Top Tips for How to Bust Through Your Genealogy Brick Wall](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=33NcRbkm-eQ), published 2013-04-05. This is the same segment as S3 in an earlier release, not independent support.
- **S5:** Genealogy Gems, [4 FAST Search Strategies for the 1931 Canadian Census](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rKMXwO4abnY), published 2023-06-08. Recorded when 1931 was browse-only. Used for: carrying 1926 Prairie census geography and a 1931 city directory forward; sub-district redrawing; title-card browsing; adjacent-sub-district and in-order checking; the partial-match log; image-2, township-column and street-distance screens; the sub-district 11 / image 18 example.

The following are authored synthesis:

- the annotated-observation fields
- reading absence under de jure counting as an informant or enumerator omission
- the rule against using absence or "widowed" as evidence
- recording both heading wordings
- the browse log

The sources supply presenter experience and examples, not statute text or enumerator instructions.
