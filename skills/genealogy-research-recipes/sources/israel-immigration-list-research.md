## Sources

- **S1:** MyHeritage Blog, [MyHeritage Adds 41 Million Historical Records in January 2023](https://blog.myheritage.com/2023/02/myheritage-adds-41-million-historical-records-in-january-2023/), published 2023-02-13, read 2026-11-16; community source. Used for: the collection's Hebrew content, searchable in English and other languages through MyHeritage's Global Name Translation Technology; transcription from images held at the Israel State Archives; tourists and returning Israeli residents in the books; some later immigrants arriving by air. Its statement that the collection holds lists of immigrants from 1919 onwards is narrowed by S2, S4 and S5: the books also list non-immigrants, some lists are missing, and illegal immigrants are not covered. Its pointer to extra information on the images is not used as a separate rule.
- **S2:** MyHeritage Wiki, main contributor Maor Malul, [Israeli Immigration Records](https://www.myheritage.com/wiki/Israeli_immigration_records), read 2026-11-16. Used for: the collection title "Israel, Immigration Lists, 1919–1974"; recording at all seaports, airports and border crossings in a standardized format until 1975; the Jewish Agency keeping the records since 1919; the collection created by scanning Israel State Archives books arranged by the arrival dates of ships or planes; tourists and returning residents; mostly Hebrew data searchable in English and other languages through Global Name Translation; the example of a woman's Hebrew record found from her original name typed in English; a minor recorded under his father's name; no lists of illegal immigrants, with several youth immigration lists.
- **S3:** MyHeritage Wiki, main contributor Maor Malul, [How to search for immigration records in Israel](https://myheritage.com/wiki/How_to_search_for_immigration_records_in_Israel), read 2026-11-16. Used for: entering the first and last name preferably in Hebrew; the search fields (birth year, departure place, arrival date and place, gender, residence, father, mother, spouse); the record page with the transcription above and the scan below; a list heading normally stating whether passengers are immigrants, returning residents or tourists; the category column; the heading's place, date, ship and line; Jewish Agency clerks writing names in Hebrew and often not filling in all the information.
- **S4:** Asaf Elia-Shalev (JTA), The Times of Israel, [Israeli immigration records dating back to 1919 now accessible online](https://www.timesofisrael.com/israeli-immigration-records-dating-back-to-1919-now-accessible-online/), published 2023-01-12, read 2026-11-16. Used for: ship and plane passenger lists in bound volumes at the Israel State Archives; about 60 years of arrivals from 1919; Hebrew as the lists' language and translation making them searchable in English and other languages; some lists missing from the archives and some in poor condition, affecting the scans; IGRA's Rose Feldman on looking for the child with the parents and searching by given name with year of birth or arrival.
- **S5:** MyHeritage Wiki, main contributor David Lukowiecki, [Israeli Deportation Records](https://www.myheritage.com/wiki/Deportation_records_in_Israel), read 2026-11-16. Used for: deportation applications filed by the British for Jewish immigrants who arrived in Mandatory Palestine from 1939 to 1945 without immigration permits; the original scans at the Israel State Archives; the pointer to the Israel Genealogy Research Association website for the names; not all illegal immigrants recorded by the Jewish Agency or caught by the British.
