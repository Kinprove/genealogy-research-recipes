## Sources

- **S1:** Genealogy Gems (Lisa Louise Cooke, with Melissa Barker), [Episode 205](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zNVSOhlUmgI), podcast episode dated 2017-07-11 in the Genealogy Gems feed. Used for the four collections to ask about (vertical files, the finding-aid index for manuscript collections, loose court papers kept apart from minute books, unprocessed records), collections staff won't necessarily offer, and accession description on intake.
- **S2:** Genealogy Gems (Melissa Barker segment), [Episode 210](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TPGm3oHSvw0), published 2017-10-24. Used for unprocessed donations that are not filmed or online, asking by email or phone, viewing that may be refused and most likely requires travel, and keeping such records on the to-do list.
- **S3:** Lisa Lisson (with Melissa Barker), [You can find THAT in the Archives?!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gtyt1Nazxe8). Used for the archivist's cursory inventory of donations, archives not advertising unprocessed collections, and repositories that permit or refuse access.
- **S4:** GeneaVlogger, [Mr. Beat's Native American and Civil War Ancestors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ti2UxgSE-B4). Used for a county library family file with an earlier relative's inquiry and a past librarian's notes, family lore treated as lore, and previously unexamined court-record boxes later indexed.
- **S5:** Lisa Lisson, [Vertical Files + Genealogy = A Great Combination](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2roNEekLO5w). Used for surname files compiled by researchers, photocopied deeds and Bibles bearing citations to the original Bible, following the compiler's sources, treating compiled research as clues, and undated clippings.
- **S6:** Lisa Lisson, [31 Days of Uncommon Genealogy Tips – Day 5 – Vertical Files](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gRhIsBrXHCo). Used for donated material absent from the card catalogue, checking by surname and location, and files being generally on site, with some digitization.
- **S7:** Lisa Lisson, [9 Overlooked Genealogy Finds in Library Vertical Files](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NEtBEUof_tA). Used for deceased researchers' notes deposited in vertical files, margin notes as clues, and possible mistakes.
- **S8:** Lisa Lisson, [7 MORE GENEALOGY TIPS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sfZjbA_2d2Y). Used for the range of holders (local and state libraries, historical societies, state archives) and topical or locality files.
- **S9:** Lisa Lisson, [Vertical Files in the Archives](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7-Stb6pP6Q). Used for vertical files absent from indexed collections and adding them to the research plan.
- **S10:** Lisa Lisson, [More Free Genealogy Resources and Frugal Genealogy Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZBSC-A55lNY). Used for files containing copies rather than originals, oral-history notes, and checking for digitized files.

The four-category ask list and the role of accession inventories are drawn from the presenters'
accounts, which come from their own practice as an archivist and as researchers. The access-state
log, the copy/original labelling, the separate list of originals and the clue list are authored
synthesis. No repository's actual holdings, access policy, processing backlog or digitization status
was verified.
