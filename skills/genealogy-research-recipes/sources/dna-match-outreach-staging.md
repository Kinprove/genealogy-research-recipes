**What is synthesis and what is not verified:** Captured interview transcript read 2026-11-19.

- The following are authored synthesis and are not prescribed by any single presenter: the consent gate as a hard stop, the tier ordering, putting first cousins, aunts and uncles in the sensitive tier by default, the rule that the sensitive tier overrides the message checklist, preferring inline images or links to attachments, and the log format.
- Also synthesis: the firm rule that a request for results access waits for a reply (S6 says only that it may wait until contact is established), and widening S7's screenshot step from close matches to any match, with the fields and date to capture.
- Also synthesis, from the skill's privacy rules rather than any presenter: the helper's drafting and logging role, the limit to contact routes the tester already has, the testing site's messaging or a relative who passes the message on, and the rule not to search for a recipient's contact details or accounts. On channel and identity, S2 supports only the checkable sender identity and the preference for social media over email.
- Also synthesis: widening S10's report on Jewish lineage to any suspected religious or ethnic origin, and widening her English and Spanish templates to the recipient's likely language. S10 names only matches in Spain and Portugal.
- Each source gives one practitioner's advice. None is a professional standard, and none reports measured response rates.

  - any company's privacy terms;
  - whether the second-cousin approach generally succeeds beyond Jones's own account;

### French-origin tester sources

- **S11:** Roberta Estes, DNAeXplained, [Acadian Ancestors and Their DNA](https://dna-explained.com/2024/04/09/acadian-ancestors-and-their-dna/), published 2024-04-09, read 2026-11-12. Used for: descendants of those who emigrated from France supplying the DNA tests for French-origin lines. Its wording that DNA testing is illegal in France is narrowed by S12–S14.
- **S12:** Légifrance, [Article 16-10, Code civil](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000047569317), version in force since 2023-05-21, read 2026-11-12. Used for: genetic examination of a person permitted for medical or scientific-research purposes and, under paragraph III bis, for anti-doping; genealogy is not among them.
- **S13:** Légifrance, [Article 226-28-1, Code pénal](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000024324189), version in force since 2011-07-09, read 2026-11-12. Used for: the €3,750 fine for requesting a genetic examination outside the legal conditions.
- **S14:** Hassan Kohen, Cabinet Kohen Avocats, [Généalogie génétique et enquêtes criminelles : l'Assemblée nationale adopte un dispositif inédit de consultation des bases ADN privées](https://kohenavocats.com/en/genealogie-genetique-enquetes-criminelles-vote-assemblee-2-juillet-2026/), published 2026-07-02, read 2026-11-12. ### Outreach examples and guidance

- **S15:** Cheri Hudson Passey, Family Tree Magazine, [What to Do When You Find Enslavers in Your Family Tree](https://familytreemagazine.com/?p=5459317), community guidance, read 2026-11-18. Passey suggests sharing research with a match whose connection may have arisen through enslavement and making clear that the match owes nothing in return. The recipe uses historical records to guide the offer and omits the article's ancestry-percentage trigger.
- **S16:** The Beyond Kin Project, [The records of slaveholders](https://beyondkin.org/you-have-the-records/), project guidance, read 2026-11-18. Used for the advice to share records and timelines from enslavers' families that can help descendants of enslaved people.
- **S17:** Carl's account as published by Daniella Levy, MyHeritage Blog, [I Was Told My Birth Dad Had Died. A DNA Test Proved He Was Someone Else — Who’s Alive](https://blog.myheritage.com/2023/12/i-was-told-my-birth-dad-had-died-a-dna-test-proved-he-was-someone-else-whos-alive/), published 2023-12-28, community case account, read 2026-11-18. Two matches suggested the same surname, and a relative from the family described the sibling group. The recipe uses that reply to build a candidate list and applies its existing contact limits.
- **S18:** Esther D.'s account as published by Daniella Levy, MyHeritage Blog, [There Was Only Me… Now There’s a We](https://blog.myheritage.com/2022/08/there-was-only-me-now-theres-a-we/), published 2022-08-16, community case account, read 2026-11-18. Esther sent a photo to a match and asked whether the match's mother or grandmother could identify the person. The account supplies the example of asking through a match.
- **S19:** Australian Institute of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Studies, [Photographs](https://aiatsis.gov.au/family-history/family-history-sources/sources-home/photographs), institutional guidance, read 2026-11-18. Used for asking older relatives to identify people in family photos and using their answers alongside other research. The recipe treats a recognition as an attributed claim and excludes resemblance from identity evidence.
- **S20:** Claire Austin, MyHeritage Blog, [Our Fathers Were Brothers: How a DNA Match Brought Us Together](https://blog.myheritage.com/2026/10/our-fathers-were-brothers-how-a-dna-match-brought-us-together/), published 2026-10-21, community case account, read 2026-11-18. Austin shared her father's birth surname, which the match recalled seeing in a family paper. Later records established the relationship in the account. The recipe uses surname recognition as a lead.
- **S21:** Daniella Levy, MyHeritage Blog, [Her DNA Sample Sat in a Drawer for 3 Years. Then She Sent It — and Found a Sister](https://blog.myheritage.com/2022/12/her-dna-sample-sat-in-a-drawer-for-3-years-then-she-sent-it-and-found-a-sister/), published 2022-12-08, community case account, read 2026-11-18. A match drew on an existing private descendants' group to investigate parentage, and two relatives volunteered to test. The case supplies an example of an outreach route. The recipe applies its own opt-in and disclosure rules.

- **S22:** Leah Larkin, [Letter to a Birth Parent](https://thednageek.com/letter-to-a-birth-parent/), 2017-07-19. Published letter and introduction read 2026-11-19.
