## Sources

- **S1:** BYU Family History Library / Bonnie Bossert, [What to Expect When Working with a DNA Helper](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vSnlARwq1-s), session dated 1 April 2026 (date in the video title; upload date not confirmed). Used for: written informed consent, confidentiality, no contact with matches without the tester's permission, and the risk that a first contact may be the only one.
- **S2:** GeneaVlogger, [Answering Questions Live | Genealogy, DNA Testing, History](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aonzO8MDaeY), upload date not confirmed. Used for: contacting by email or social media from an identity that can be looked up, saying concretely what a test would find and why it benefits the recipient, tree snapshots or shared photos, care with attachments, prepaid kits, and frankness about privacy.
- **S3:** Family History Fanatics with Larry Jones, [Larry Jones of DNA Family Trees Shares His Story of How to Handle DNA Surprises](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pI-BFI-19Lg), upload date not confirmed. Used for: approaching a second cousin when a close relative will not engage, and silence as a possible sign of family disruption (the six-brothers anecdote).
- **S4:** Lisa Lisson with Mary Eberle, [Talking DNA with Expert Mary Eberle of DNA Hunters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kgjB_Ok2xhI), upload date not confirmed. Used for: a helper contacting distant cousins with the client's permission, the client contacting a possible birth parent or half-sibling, and the non-accusatory fill-in model letter, identified as Leah Larkin's "[Letter to a Birth Parent](https://thednageek.com/letter-to-a-birth-parent/)" (The DNA Geek, 2017-07-19).
- **S5:** GeneaVlogger (Joe Rossi), [Optimize DNA Results by Contacting DNA Matches - (Quick Genealogy Tip #21)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vimyq2Z0mmw), upload date not confirmed. Used for: naming the matching kits and site, shared cM and longest segment, a surname or town hypothesis, a tree link, keeping the message short, and not assuming the reader is the tester.
- **S6:** Legacy Tree Genealogists, [Five Tips for Contacting Genetic Cousins](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/five-tips-for-contacting-genetic-cousins), published 19 March 2024. Used for: access to test results as one of the useful requests to a genetic cousin, with the suggestion that it may wait until contact is established.
- **S7:** Legacy Tree Genealogists (researcher Michelle), [What Should You Do After Receiving Unexpected DNA Results? 5 Practical Tips for Navigating Family History Surprises](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/unexpected-dna-results), published 8 September 2026. Used for: saving screenshots of matches, shared cM and linked trees before contacting close matches or posting online, because matches may later make profiles private or remove trees.
- **S8:** JewishGen (presenter Gil Bardige), [JewishGen Talks: Help! I Got My DNA Results and I'm Confused -- Part 1.25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0hGonlNLinM), 2026-01-25. Used for: waiting at least a year before writing again to a close match who has stopped replying, and reading a "no, I don't recognise those names" reply as a reply about the recipient's documented tree only.
- **S9:** JewishGen, [JewishGen Talks: Challenges of DNA for Jews: Overcoming Endogamy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xaa5FcbZYb8), 2025-01-22. Used for: checking a match's family tree for someone other than the tester who manages the kit, and contacting that person.
- **S10:** JewishGen (presenter Genie Milgrom), [Introducing the New JewishGen Crypto Jewish Collection with Genie Milgrom](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RD6rtHS53To), 2020-11-11. Used for: short English and Spanish templates, and her report that asking Iberian matches about Jewish lineage got no answers while asking about family information back to the 1400s and 1500s did.

**What is synthesis and what is not verified:**

- The following are authored synthesis and are not prescribed by any single presenter: the consent gate as a hard stop, the tier ordering, putting first cousins, aunts and uncles in the sensitive tier by default, the rule that the sensitive tier overrides the message checklist, preferring inline images or links to attachments, and the log format.
- Also synthesis: the firm rule that a request for results access waits for a reply (S6 says only that it may wait until contact is established), and widening S7's screenshot step from close matches to any match, with the fields and date to capture.
- Also synthesis, from the skill's privacy rules rather than any presenter: the helper's drafting and logging role, the limit to contact routes the tester already has, the testing site's messaging or a relative who passes the message on, and the rule not to search for a recipient's contact details or accounts. On channel and identity, S2 supports only the checkable sender identity and the preference for social media over email.
- Also synthesis: widening S10's report on Jewish lineage to any suspected religious or ethnic origin, and widening her English and Spanish templates to the recipient's likely language. S10 names only matches in Spain and Portugal.
- Each source gives one practitioner's advice. None is a professional standard, and none reports measured response rates.
- Not verified:
  - the model letter's exact wording;
  - any site's current messaging or tree-sharing features;
  - any company's privacy terms;
  - whether the second-cousin approach generally succeeds beyond Jones's own account;
  - the upload dates of S1–S5;
  - the "open at first, then dropped out of sight" detail of the S8 case, which the checked passage does not show;
  - S7's publication date, which is taken from the scraped copy of the page.

### French-origin tester sources

- **S11:** Roberta Estes, DNAeXplained, [Acadian Ancestors and Their DNA](https://dna-explained.com/2024/04/09/acadian-ancestors-and-their-dna/), published 2024-04-09, read 2026-11-12. Used for: descendants of those who emigrated from France supplying the DNA tests for French-origin lines. Its wording that DNA testing is illegal in France is narrowed by S12–S14.
- **S12:** Légifrance, [Article 16-10, Code civil](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000047569317), version in force since 2023-05-21, read 2026-11-12. Used for: genetic examination of a person permitted for medical or scientific-research purposes and, under paragraph III bis, for anti-doping; genealogy is not among them.
- **S13:** Légifrance, [Article 226-28-1, Code pénal](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000024324189), version in force since 2011-07-09, read 2026-11-12. Used for: the €3,750 fine for requesting a genetic examination outside the legal conditions.
- **S14:** Hassan Kohen, Cabinet Kohen Avocats, [Généalogie génétique et enquêtes criminelles : l'Assemblée nationale adopte un dispositif inédit de consultation des bases ADN privées](https://kohenavocats.com/en/genealogie-genetique-enquetes-criminelles-vote-assemblee-2-juillet-2026/), published 2026-07-02, read 2026-11-12. Used for: the firm's citation of an estimate that three million French people have used such tests, which remain prohibited. The bill it describes concerns criminal investigations and is not used.
