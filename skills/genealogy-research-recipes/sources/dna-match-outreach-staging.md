## Sources

- **S1:** BYU Family History Library / Bonnie Bossert, [What to Expect When Working with a DNA Helper](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vSnlARwq1-s), session dated 1 April 2026 (date in the video title; upload date not confirmed). Used for: written informed consent, confidentiality, no contact with matches without the tester's permission, and the risk that a first contact may be the only one.
- **S2:** GeneaVlogger, [Answering Questions Live | Genealogy, DNA Testing, History](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aonzO8MDaeY), upload date not confirmed. Used for: contacting by email or social media from an identity that can be looked up, saying concretely what a test would find and why it benefits the recipient, tree snapshots or shared photos, care with attachments, prepaid kits, and frankness about privacy.
- **S3:** Family History Fanatics with Larry Jones, [Larry Jones of DNA Family Trees Shares His Story of How to Handle DNA Surprises](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pI-BFI-19Lg), upload date not confirmed. Used for: approaching a second cousin when a close relative will not engage, and silence as a possible sign of family disruption (the six-brothers anecdote).
- **S4:** Lisa Lisson with Mary Eberle, [Talking DNA with Expert Mary Eberle of DNA Hunters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kgjB_Ok2xhI), upload date not confirmed. Used for: a helper contacting distant cousins with the client's permission, the client contacting a possible birth parent or half-sibling, and the non-accusatory fill-in model letter, identified as Leah Larkin's "Letter to a Birth Parent" (The DNA Geek, 2017-07-19).
- **S5:** GeneaVlogger (Joe Rossi), [Optimize DNA Results by Contacting DNA Matches - (Quick Genealogy Tip #21)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vimyq2Z0mmw), upload date not confirmed. Used for: naming the matching kits and site, shared cM and longest segment, a surname or town hypothesis, a tree link, keeping the message short, and not assuming the reader is the tester.

**What is synthesis and what is not verified:**

- The following are authored synthesis and are not prescribed by any single presenter: the consent gate as a hard stop, the tier ordering, putting first cousins, aunts and uncles in the sensitive tier by default, the rule that the sensitive tier overrides the message checklist, preferring inline images or links to attachments, and the log format.
- Each source gives one practitioner's advice. None is a professional standard, and none reports measured response rates.
- Not verified:
  - the model letter's exact wording;
  - any site's current messaging or tree-sharing features;
  - any company's privacy terms;
  - whether the second-cousin approach generally succeeds beyond Jones's own account;
  - the upload dates of S1–S5.
