## Sources

- **S1:** BYU Library Family History Center / James Tanner, [Finding Your Ancestors' Descendants - James Tanner (26 Feb 2023)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Q4qYeK5DTg), presented 26 Feb 2023 (date from the title). Used for: starting with people who have few or no sources, the gaps "adult with no recorded spouse" and "deceased with no death date", childless couples, the census-span examples for the U.S. and England, and going back up the line to correct a family that turns out to be fabricated.
- **S2:** BYU Library Family History Center / James Tanner, [How do I do descendancy research on FamilySearch? - James Tanner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RRiZPRbiuQI). Used for: the three gap patterns in a descendancy chart, checking source counts before research, surname-only line research as a cause of missing siblings, the period-dependent view of family size, and possible widow remarriage.
- **S3:** GeneaVlogger, [Building a Descendants Tree for Benjamin Franklin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=33papLposgM). Used for: "sweeping the branches" one generation at a time from oldest to youngest, the risk of skipped siblings, confirming a family through the oldest child across successive censuses, explicit tags for closed families, and Sons of the American Revolution applications as a descendant-line record.

The following are authored synthesis: the worklist fields, the ranking rule, the open/closed/corrected statuses, the census coverage markings and the rule against treating blanks as findings. The talks supply the gap patterns, the census-span examples and the sweep order. No demographic statistic is adopted.
