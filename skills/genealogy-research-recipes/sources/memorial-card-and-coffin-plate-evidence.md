Used for: plates identifying the name and death date; plates in the United States from the early 1840s more often not attached at burial but displayed on the coffin during the funeral and given to the family as a memento, kept on display or put away; the custom peaking in 1880–1899 and dying out by the 1920s; the contrast with England, where plates had to stay on the coffin at burial; the Jones family plates' ages of 80 years 5 months and 20 months 19 days.

This recipe's own contribution is the statement-by-statement transcription, the weighting of birth details as information supplied at a death, the derived-birth-date rule, the unidentified-plate and unknown-provenance rules, the routing of leads to sibling recipes and the return packet. The coffin-plate custom rests on S2 and S6; the card examples rest on S3 and S4. Funeral guest books are left to the family papers and burial evidence recipes, which already cover them.
