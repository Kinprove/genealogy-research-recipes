Used for: the announcement of the "Scotland, High Court Precognitions and Trial Papers" collection as a 138,934-record index of AD14, AD15 and JC26; its description of precognitions as the Lord Advocate's office examining witnesses before the trial for the prosecutor's use. Its name for the custodian, the National Archives of Scotland, is not followed (see S5). It gives no date range, and its statement that the High Court hears the most heinous crimes is narrowed by S2 and S4.
- **S2:** National Records of Scotland, [High Court Criminal Trials](https://nrscotland.gov.uk/learning-and-events/research-guides/high-court-criminal-trials), research guide last updated 2026-02-02, read 2026-11-16. Used for: precognitions as written reports of statements by witnesses and frequently the accused, prepared by the Crown as prosecutor and held in the Lord Advocate's Department records; AD14 (19th century), AD15 (20th century), the JC26 case papers and the JC8, JC9, JC11, JC12 and JC13 minute books; repeat offenders and people deemed criminals "by habit and repute" tried for lesser crimes such as theft or housebreaking in the 19th century; the years catalogued in full and incomplete cataloguing of early 19th-century case papers; the "Search For" and "Reference" fields; Scots crime terms; previous convictions in catalogue entries; victim details left out of cases less than 100 years old, the last being 1905; lost precognitions and trial papers, and trials linked to their minute-book entry; viewing only in the Historical Search Room, the reader's ticket, three days' notice and copies; digitised 19th-century minute books seen through the High Court Criminal Trials database and Virtual Volumes, not online; access status "open" up to 1914.
- **S3:** National Records of Scotland, [Scottish departments](https://nrscotland.gov.uk/learning-and-events/research-guides/scottish-government-records-after-1707/scottish-departments), research guide last updated 2026-02-02, read 2026-11-16. Used for: Crown Office and Lord Advocate precognitions (AD14–15, AD21) from 1797 listed as closed for 100 years.
- **S4:** Scottish Indexes, [Learning Zone – Scottish High Court Criminal Records](https://scottishindexes.co.uk/learninghighcourtadvanced.aspx), read 2026-11-16. Used for: the free index of 1800 to 1916 with over 133,000 records, not all High Court records; the accused and some victims; the full NRS reference in each result; ages and other details from other sources; the "Jane Mitchell alias Scott" example; the court hearing repeat offenders and appeals from lower courts such as the sheriff court; the indictment or criminal libel, evidence and list of jurors in the trial papers; minute books giving the timeline and outcome; research packages.
- **S5:** University of Edinburgh Library, [National Records of Scotland](https://library.ed.ac.uk/research-support/research-data-service/during/discover-and-re-use-data/data-providers-uk/national-records-of-scotland), read 2026-11-16. Used for: NRS formed from the merger of the General Register Office for Scotland and the National Archives of Scotland in April 2011.
