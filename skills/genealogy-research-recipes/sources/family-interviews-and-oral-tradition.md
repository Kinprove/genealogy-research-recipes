## Sources

- **S1:** MyHeritage / Daniel Horowitz, [Recording Memories on the MyHeritage Mobile App](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ba8_hZN35nY), published 2022-07-20. Used: the presenter's advice to keep recordings made in the app to a few minutes, even splitting an interview; the recipe limits that advice to recordings made in the app and follows [S21] and [S23] for interviews on a recorder. Also used: his advice to check a recording right after making it rather than finding hours or days later that it failed or was too noisy, and his 2022 answers that downloading a photograph does not bring its attached audio and that comments do not travel when a photo is shared.
- **S2:** MyHeritage, [Join our Facebook Live about dragging family history information out of your family members!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qTeFZGgSZHw), published 2021-11-09. Used: asking about the vehicles, buildings, place and season in a photograph before asking who the people are. Also used: recipes, objects and events as prompts, respecting relatives who do not want to be recorded, and the presenter's point that people raised in one household experienced things differently.
- **S3:** Family History Fanatics / Andy Lee, [Interviewing Family Members? DON'T DO THIS!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LrvlMbCdiwU), published 2016-11-12. Used: placing the microphone on the person answering, not the interviewer, and waiting through pauses while the person remembers. The recipe also records the interviewer's questions, following [S21] and [S22].
- **S4:** Who is Nicka Smith? (BlackProGen LIVE panel), [BlackProGen LIVE Ep23: Telling the Story Without Boring Your Family](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3VxS2bc_NtA), published 2017-01-26. Used: showing an older relative their own census entry while the recording runs and reading out the neighbours listed before and after. Also used: a panelist's regret at not recording such a session.
- **S5:** Lisa Louise Cooke's Genealogy Gems, with guest Julianne Mangin, [Genealogy Gems Podcast Episode 219](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-o8KMcUYHeY), published 2018-07-11. Used: the guest's questions about inherited stories (how would the teller know, how old was she, who was her source) and hospital records that contradicted the family story.
- **S6:** Family History Fanatics, [Do Genealogy Experts Really Do These 5 Things?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x1j3SIOR9Yg), published 2021-07-03. Used: a story handed down from a grandmother to the presenter's mother, who interviewed her in 1977 and wrote it down. The presenter later copied it several times into her book and tree and typed it up. Also used: a viewer's "trust but verify" comment about relatives' dates, which the presenter endorsed.
- **S7:** Lisa Louise Cooke's Genealogy Gems, [Genealogy Records Provenance, Primary & Secondary Sources, Finding Aids](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mTyYpO_3uPs), published 2020-12-10. Used: an "as told by" family typescript that may come from a taped oral history whose audio had not been found, and the advice to keep looking for the original recording.
- **S8:** Denyse Allen • Chronicle Makers, [Meet the Lenape of Pennsylvania](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Le2bUb856c), published 2024-07-25. Used: a storykeeper's caution that a written version of an oral story comes to be treated as the authoritative one and freezes the telling.
- **S9:** Lisa Louise Cooke's Genealogy Gems, [Episode 201 The Genealogy Gems Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LwjPdTcCxMM), published 2017-03-08. Used: asking elders about the oldest person they remember from childhood.
- **S10:** Lisa Louise Cooke's Genealogy Gems, with author Steve Luxenberg, [Episode 120 - Annie's Ghost with Author Steve Luxenberg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kX8ba2xZhho), published 2016-12-23. Used: the author's account of helpful interviewees straining to fill gaps, and of interviewing one person six or seven times until he judged he was eliciting false or made-up memories. Also used: his decision to stop, and his point that interviews must be supplemented with documents.
- **S11:** Lisa Lisson, [Tips for Documenting Your Family's Oral History](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uckjtsMSlqU), published 2019-09-13. Used: the guest's suggestion that a brother, sister or other family member who has been in the person's life a great deal prompt a person with dementia ("do you remember when"); staying silent for a while after important questions; and stopping when the person tires.
- **S12:** Oral History Association, [OHA Statement on Ethics](https://oralhistory.org/oha-statement-on-ethics/), part of the OHA Principles and Best Practices adopted October 2018, opened 2026-11-06. Used: informed consent obtained before the interview begins, documented in writing with signatures where feasible and otherwise recorded before the interview; no public use until the narrator has given formal authorization; and the narrator's opportunity, whenever possible, to review and approve the recording or transcript.
- **S13:** Oral History Association, [Oral History Best Practices](https://oralhistory.org/best-practices/), part of the same 2018 suite (the complete PDF is the 2025 edition), opened 2026-11-06. Used: a signed legal release, ideally when the interview is completed; the narrator's right to restrict access; and redundant digital copies kept in different places.
- **S14:** Oral History Association, [For Participants in Oral History Interviews](https://oralhistory.org/for-participants-in-oral-history-interviews/), opened 2026-11-06. Used: consent or terms of use given by a recorded or written declaration; the participant's freedom to skip questions, take a break, end the interview, restrict it or withdraw it; and the warning that a recording already shared publicly or archived may not be fully removable.
- **S15:** Oral History Society (UK), [For family historians](https://ohs.org.uk/for-family-historians/), opened 2026-11-06. Used: the Joint Statement of Intent for family oral history recordings, filled in by interviewer and interviewee after the recording and kept with it.
- **S16:** Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, [Introduction to the Reporter's Recording Guide](https://www.rcfp.org/introduction-to-reporters-recording-guide/), page dated 2021-10-28 in its metadata, opened 2026-11-06. Used: U.S. federal one-party consent; states that require every party's consent, some for conversations in person and some for calls; and a call across state lines falling under the stricter state's law. It is a general summary, not legal advice.
- **S17:** Oral History Society (UK), [Dealing with GDPR](https://ohs.org.uk/gdpr-2/), version 5.0, August 2019, opened 2026-11-06. Used: an interview recording as personal data about the interviewee and about identifiable living third parties, and its statement that GDPR does not apply to purely private domestic, personal or family activity.
- **S18:** legislation.gov.uk, [UK GDPR, Article 2: Material scope](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/2), revised version, opened 2026-11-06. Used: Article 2(2)(a), which excludes processing by an individual in the course of a purely personal or household activity.
- **S19:** EUR-Lex, [Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679), opened 2026-11-06. Used: Article 2(2)(c), which excludes processing by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity.
- **S20:** Oral History Society (UK), [Legal and Ethical preparation](https://ohs.org.uk/legal-and-ethical-advice/legal-and-ethical-preparation/), opened 2026-11-06. Used: reaching an adult with cognitive impairment through a carer, relative or friend whose role is carefully defined; accessible explanations; and a friend or long-term associate present at the interview.
- **S21:** Stephen Winick and Peter Bartis, [Folklife and Fieldwork: An Introduction to Cultural Documentation](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/19/66/70/28/2019667028/2019667028.pdf), fourth edition, American Folklife Center, Library of Congress, 2016, opened 2026-11-06. Used: keeping the recording device running throughout the interview; recording the voices of both interviewer and interviewee; lavalier microphones as best for one-on-one interviews; and having more than one way to see that recording is happening, such as the counter and level meter.
- **S22:** Oral History Society (UK), [Equipment](https://ohs.org.uk/for-beginners/equipment/), opened 2026-11-06. Used: a tie-clip or lapel microphone as the best external microphone for one-to-one interviews indoors, and a recorder with two microphone sockets taking one microphone for the interviewee and one for the interviewer.
- **S23:** Baylor University Institute for Oral History, [Introduction to Oral History](https://library.web.baylor.edu/sites/g/files/ecbvkj1806/files/2024-12/intro_manual_2016.pdf), workshop manual, 2016, opened 2026-11-06. Used: a test recording of both voices played back before the interview; placing the microphone between interviewer and narrator; avoiding switching the recorder off and on; and a recorder's automatic split into tracks of an hour or less.
- **S24:** Stephen J. Lofgren, [U.S. Army Guide to Oral History](https://guard.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/U.S.-Army-Guide-to-Oral-History.pdf), U.S. Army Center of Military History, 2006 (copy hosted by the Utah National Guard), opened 2026-11-06. Used: running two recorders during the interview, ideally, so one failure does not lose it. The other guides cited here recommend backup copies, not a second recorder, so the recipe makes the second device optional.

**Authored synthesis.** The following is not taken from any one presenter: the separate layers for testimony, transcript, paraphrase and interpretation; recording offsets; the portable register or sidecar; the export check; the chain fields; prompter attribution; the rule against reconstructing unheard answers; the census-adjacency caution; and the handover format. The presenters supply the examples and the failure modes. Also Kinprove synthesis: the consent and authorization fields in the session register; the rule that a supplied recording with an unknown basis is not deposited or published; the check of the household exemption before sharing; and the rule that a carer's agreement is not the participant's consent and that a recording made without the participant's own informed agreement stays private family memory, which applies [S12]'s authorization rule to doubtful capacity ([S20] describes a carer's supporting role, and no cited page states the rule for dementia).
